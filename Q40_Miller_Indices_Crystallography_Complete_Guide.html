DGMS 2019 Geology • Question 40

Crystallography: Standard Notation of Miller Indices for Crystal Faces

Topic: Geometric Crystallography & Lattice Planes | Primary Reference: Dexter Perkins (Mineralogy, 3rd ed., Ch. 10 & 11)

📝 Question Statement

Q.40. Which one of the following expressions is correct as per notations of Miller indices of a crystal face?

(a) (1 1 1) [or (1 1 0)]
(b) (½ ½ 1)
(c) (1a 1b 1c)
(d) 1 1 1
a (1 1 1) (Correct Answer)
b (½ ½ 1)
c (1a 1b 1c)
d 1 1 1
✓ Correct Answer: (a) (1 1 1) [Miller indices of a crystal face are integers cleared of common fractions, written without axis letters, and strictly enclosed in round parentheses: (h k l)].

🖼️ Textbook Evidence: Dexter Perkins (Chapter 11)

Figure 11.10: Geometry of Miller Indices for Crystal Faces (Dexter Perkins, Mineralogy, 3rd ed., p. 320). Derivation of Miller indices from axial intercepts: (a) A plane intersecting crystallographic axes at intercepts $1a, 2b, 4c$. Taking reciprocals ($1/1, 1/2, 1/4$) and clearing fractions by multiplying by 4 yields the whole-number Miller index (421). (b) A plane parallel to the $c$-axis with intercepts $-1a, 1b, \infty c$ yields the index (1̄10). All crystal faces are designated by whole-number integers enclosed in parentheses without commas or axis letters.
"To determine the Miller index of a crystal face:
1. Determine the intercepts of the face on the crystallographic axes $a, b,$ and $c$ in terms of unit dimensions.
2. Invert the intercepts (take their reciprocals). A face parallel to an axis intersects it at infinity ($\infty$), yielding a reciprocal of 0.
3. Clear fractions to reduce the reciprocals to the smallest whole integers $h, k, l$.
4. Enclose the numbers in parentheses: $(h k l)$. Negative indices are denoted by a bar over the number (e.g., $1̄$)."
— Dexter Perkins, Mineralogy, Chapter 11 (Unit Cells, Points, Lines, and Planes), pp. 319–322

🔍 Comprehensive Option Analysis

(a) (1 1 1) [CORRECT]

Crystallographic Rules of Miller Indices: By international crystallographic convention (established by William Hallowes Miller in 1839):

  • A crystal face is denoted strictly by enclosing the whole integers $(h k l)$ within round parentheses, with no commas and no crystallographic axis letters.
  • For example, the octahedron face in the isometric system has intercepts $1a, 1b, 1c \rightarrow$ reciprocals $1, 1, 1 \rightarrow$ written as (1 1 1) or (111).
  • The expression strictly follows every single rule of Miller index notation.

(b) (½ ½ 1) [INCORRECT]

Miller indices must be whole integers. Fractional notations like $(1/2, 1/2, 1)$ represent fractional intercepts or raw reciprocals before clearing fractions. By rule, fractions must be cleared by multiplying by the least common denominator (which would convert $(1/2, 1/2, 1)$ into the valid Miller index $(1 1 2)$).

(c) (1a 1b 1c) [INCORRECT]

Expressions incorporating the crystallographic axis labels (such as $1a : 1b : 1c$) are Weiss parameters (axial parameters), not Miller indices. Miller indices are dimensionless integers ($h, k, l$) and never contain the letters $a, b,$ or $c$.

(d) 1 1 1 (without parentheses) [INCORRECT]

In crystallography, the type of brackets used defines the physical entity being described. Bare numbers without parentheses fail standard crystallographic syntax and do not specify a crystal face.

📊 Standard Crystallographic Bracket Notation Guide

Bracket Type Example Geometrical Entity Represented Physical Significance
Parentheses: $(h k l)$ (1 1 1) or (1 0 0) Individual Crystal Face or Lattice Plane A single planar surface of a crystal or specific family of parallel atomic lattice planes.
Curly Braces: $\{h k l\}$ $\{1 0 0\}$ or $\{1 1 1\}$ Crystal Form A complete group of symmetry-equivalent crystal faces (e.g., $\{100\}$ represents all 6 cube faces).
Square Brackets: $[u v w]$ $[0 0 1]$ or $[1 1 0]$ Zone Axis / Direction Vector A linear vector or zone axis pointing along a lattice line (e.g., $[001]$ points along the $c$-axis).
Angle Brackets: $\langle u v w \rangle$ $\langle 1 0 0 \rangle$ Family of Symmetry-Equivalent Directions All crystallographically equivalent direction vectors in the lattice.

💡 Key Takeaway & Exam Strategy

Universal Crystallographic Bracket Rules:
  • Face: $(h k l)$ $\rightarrow$ Parentheses, whole numbers only, no commas, no axis letters.
  • Form: $\{h k l\}$ $\rightarrow$ Braces.
  • Zone / Direction: $[u v w]$ $\rightarrow$ Square brackets.
  • Weiss Parameters: Intercepts with axis letters (e.g., $1a : \infty b : 2c$), which are converted to Miller indices by taking reciprocals and clearing fractions.
