DGMS 2019 • SECTION E • QUESTION 30

Sedimentary Dynamics: Bedform Succession with Increasing Bed Shear Stress & Flow Regimes

Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) Junior Scientific Officer Examination 2019 | Physical Sedimentology & Hydrodynamics Guide

📋 Question Statement

Q.30. Which one of the following sequences of sedimentary structures is correct in terms of increasing bed shear?
a (a) Parting lineation, dune, flute mark, ripple
b (b) Ripple, dune, parting lineation, flute mark
c (c) Dune, parting lineation, flute mark, ripple
d (d) Flute mark, parting lineation, ripple, dune
✅ Correct Answer: (b) Ripple, dune, parting lineation, flute mark
⚡ INCREASING BED SHEAR STRESS (τ₀):
Current Ripple ➔ Subaqueous Dune ➔ Parting Lineation (Upper Plane Bed) ➔ Flute Mark (Turbulent Scour)

🔍 In-Depth Option Evaluation & Hydrodynamic Progression

(b) Ripple, dune, parting lineation, flute mark — [CORRECT]

Hydrodynamic Succession with Increasing Bed Shear (τ₀ = ρ Cd U²):

  • 1. Ripples (Lowest Bed Shear): Form just above the threshold velocity for sediment motion in the Lower Flow Regime. Small-scale bedforms (λ < 20 cm, H < 4 cm) where grains saltate over the stoss side and avalanche down the lee slope.
  • 2. Dunes (Moderate Bed Shear): Form at higher velocity and stream power in the Lower Flow Regime. Large-scale bedforms (λ > 0.6–1 m, H > 5 cm) with active flow separation and wake vortices over the crest.
  • 3. Parting Lineation / Upper Stage Plane Bed (High Bed Shear): Upper Flow Regime (Froude number Fr ≈ 1). Dunes are washed out and sheared flat into flat laminae. High-speed and low-speed streaks generated by streamwise turbulent boundary-layer vortices align elongate grains into parting step lineations.
  • 4. Flute Marks (Highest Bed Shear & Extreme Turbulent Scour): Produced by hyper-turbulent erosive vortices at the head of high-energy flows (e.g., turbidity currents or catastrophic surges) scouring directly into the bed before deposition can begin. Basal shear stress exceeds even that of plane-bed deposition!

(a, c, d) Incorrect Permutations — [INCORRECT]

• (a) Starts with Parting lineation (Upper flow regime) followed by Dune (Lower flow regime) — non-monotonic shear order.
• (c) Places Ripple (lowest shear) at the very end after Flute mark (highest shear) — inverted.
• (d) Inverted sequence (from highest to lowest shear: Flute mark ➔ Parting lineation ➔ Ripple/Dune), but the question explicitly demands increasing bed shear!

📚 Direct Textbook Quotations & Citations

Sam Boggs Jr. (2006), Principles of Sedimentology and Stratigraphy, 4th ed., Chapter 4, pp. 83–85:

"Numerous flume experiments have established that under unidirectional flow in sand, the succession of bedforms that develops as flow velocity and bed shear stress increase is: ripples ⟶ dunes ⟶ plane bed (with parting lineation) ⟶ antidunes."
— Sam Boggs Jr., Chapter 4, p. 83
J. D. Collinson & D. B. Thompson (1989), Sedimentary Structures, 2nd ed., Chapter 4 & 5:

"Flutes are produced by localized turbulent scour of a cohesive mud bed by fluid vortices generated in high-velocity, high-shear flows prior to deposition... In terms of boundary shear stress, flute erosion represents the upper boundary of flow power, exceeding the threshold required for plane-bed transport."
— Collinson & Thompson (1989)

🎯 Exam Traps & Rapid Elimination Strategy

⚡ Universal Flow Regime Rule:
  1. Always anchor the two endpoints:
    • Lowest Shear: Ripples (appear immediately when sand starts moving).
    • Highest Shear / Scour: Flute Marks (requires extreme turbulence to scour cohesive mud before sand settles).
  2. Between Ripples and Flutes: Dunes (Lower Flow Regime) come before Parting Lineation (Upper Flow Regime plane bed).
  3. Therefore, order: Ripple ➔ Dune ➔ Parting Lineation ➔ Flute Mark!
