QUESTION 54
Which one of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?
(a) Annular drainages : Develop on eroded dome in alternate resistant/weak sediments
(b) Parallel drainages : Develop on horizontal sediments or homogeneous crystalline rocks NOT CORRECTLY MATCHED (CORRECT OPTION)
(c) Trellis drainages : Develop on alternating weak and resistant tilted sedimentary rocks
(d) Discordant drainages : Develop on river channels cut across geological structures

1. Executive Summary & Answer Verification

The correct option to choose is (b) Parallel drainages : Develop on horizontal sediments or homogeneous crystalline rocks.

In fluvial geomorphology and structural geology (A. D. Howard, 1967; Thornbury, 1969; Arthur L. Bloom):

  • On flat, horizontally bedded sedimentary strata or uniformly resistant, homogeneous crystalline rocks (e.g., massive granite or flat volcanic plateau basalts), streams branch organically and randomly in all directions like the limbs of an oak tree with acute junction angles. This is universally termed a Dendritic drainage pattern (from Greek dendron = tree).
  • A Parallel drainage pattern develops strictly where there is a pronounced, uniform, steep regional slope or regional structural dip (such as steep monoclines, fault-scarp slopes, or narrow coastal plains), forcing both master streams and tributaries to flow parallel to one another down the regional gradient. It does not develop on flat horizontal or massive rocks.

Therefore, statement (b) erroneously assigns the diagnostic geological setting of a dendritic pattern to a parallel pattern, making it NOT correctly matched.

2. Detailed Geomorphological Analysis of Drainage Patterns

2.1 Dendritic vs. Parallel Patterns

  1. Dendritic Pattern (Tree-like):
    • Geological Setting: Horizontal sedimentary beds or completely massive, isotropic crystalline igneous/metamorphic rocks without preferred fracture orientation.
    • Mechanism: Because resistance to erosion is isotropic (uniform in all directions), tributaries join larger streams at acute angles ($<90^\circ$) without any preferred structural alignment.
  2. Parallel Pattern:
    • Geological Setting: Elongated landforms with steep, uniform regional slopes (e.g., coastal plains, steep volcanic cone flanks, or tilted homoclinal blocks).
    • Mechanism: Strong slope gradient overrides minor lithological differences, compelling all stream channels to run parallel or sub-parallel down the dominant slope direction.

2.2 Verification of the Other Drainage Classes

  1. (a) Annular Drainage — Correctly Matched: A ring-like or concentric drainage network that develops on maturely dissected structural domes or structural basins. As concentric bands of alternating resistant (sandstone/limestone) and weak (shale) strata are exposed, streams excavate concentric strike valleys in the softer rock, draining along curved annular paths (e.g., the Black Hills of South Dakota).
  2. (c) Trellis Drainage — Correctly Matched: A rectilinear pattern where major parallel trunk streams flow in longitudinal strike valleys underlain by weak strata, and short subsequent tributaries enter at right angles ($90^\circ$) from adjacent ridges of resistant rock. Diagnostic of folded, tilted sedimentary belts (anticlines and synclines, such as the Appalachian Valley and Ridge province).
  3. (d) Discordant Drainage — Correctly Matched: Refers to a river system that does not conform to the underlying geological structure or topography, cutting transverse gorges across resistant ridges. The two classical genetic mechanisms are:
    • Antecedent Drainage: The river established its course before the tectonic uplift of the ridge or mountain, and eroded its channel downward at a rate equal to or exceeding the uplift rate (e.g., the Brahmaputra, Indus, and Sutlej rivers cutting through the Himalayas).
    • Superimposed (Epigenetic) Drainage: The river established its course on an overlying flat sedimentary cover; as erosion stripped the cover, the river incised blindly into the discordant structures of the buried basement.

Textbook Authority

Arthur D. Howard (1967), Drainage Analysis in Geologic Interpretation: A Summation (AAPG Bulletin):
"Dendritic: Characterized by irregular branching in all directions... indicates horizontal sediments or uniformly resistant, crystalline rocks.
Parallel: Consists of parallel or subparallel streams... caused by pronounced, uniformly steep regional slope or parallel topographic features."
William D. Thornbury, Principles of Geomorphology:
"Dendritic drainage patterns are characterized by treelike branching and develop on rocks of uniform resistance to erosion, such as flat-lying sedimentary beds or massive igneous rocks... Parallel drainage patterns develop on steep, unidirectional structural slopes."
