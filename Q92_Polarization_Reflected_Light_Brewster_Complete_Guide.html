Question 92: Optics & Remote Sensing: Polarization of Reflected Light & Brewster's Law

Official Examination Guide • Plane Polarized Light, Specular Water Reflection & Rayleigh Scattering Polarization
Official Examination Question
Consider the following statements regarding reflected light:
  • 1. The reflected light, vibrating in only one plane, is called polarized light.
  • 2. The light reflected from a water body into the field of view of a camera is polarized light.
  • 3. The light from a blue sky is non-polarized because it is reflected from non-metallic particles in the atmosphere.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 CORRECT ANSWER
(b) 2 and 3
(c) 3 only
(d) 1 only
1. Executive Summary & Core Principle

Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave where the electric field vector $\mathbf{E}$ vibrates perpendicularly to the direction of propagation (Hecht, Optics; Lillesand & Kiefer, Remote Sensing and Image Interpretation):

  • Statement 1 is TRUE: In unpolarized light, the electric field vector vibrates randomly in all possible planes perpendicular to the propagation ray. When the electric field vibrations are confined to a single fixed plane, the light is termed linearly (or plane) polarized light.
  • Statement 2 is TRUE: When unpolarized sunlight reflects off a smooth non-metallic dielectric surface such as a water body ($n \approx 1.333$), the reflected ray is partially to completely polarized. According to Brewster's Law:
    tan(θB) = n₂ / n₁ ≈ 1.333 ➔ θB ≈ 53.1°
    At the Brewster angle of incidence ($\theta_B \approx 53^\circ$), the component of the electric vector oscillating in the plane of incidence (p-polarization) is completely refracted into the water, and the reflected light is 100% plane-polarized parallel to the water surface (s-polarization / horizontal polarization). This reflected polarized light enters camera lenses as blinding glare, which aerial photographers and remote sensing sensors suppress using polarizing filters.
  • Statement 3 is FALSE: Diffuse blue skylight is produced by Rayleigh scattering of unpolarized sunlight by atmospheric gas molecules ($N_2, O_2$). Because molecular dipole oscillation does not radiate energy along the dipole axis, skylight scattered at an angle of 90° relative to the incident solar rays is strongly linearly polarized (reaching 70% to 85% polarization under clear skies). Stating that clear blue skylight is "non-polarized" contradicts basic physics!

Therefore, Statements 1 and 2 are correct, making Option (a) 1 and 2 the correct answer.

2. Mathematical Physics of Polarization by Reflection (Fresnel Equations)

The reflection coefficients for light polarized parallel ($r_\parallel$) and perpendicular ($r_\perp$) to the plane of incidence are given by Fresnel's equations:

r∥ = tan(θi - θt) / tan(θi + θt)
r⊥ = - sin(θi - θt) / sin(θi + θt)

When the angle between the reflected ray and the refracted ray is exactly $90^\circ$ ($\theta_i + \theta_t = 90^\circ$), the denominator $\tan(\theta_i + \theta_t) \rightarrow \infty$, causing $r_\parallel = 0$. Consequently, the reflected beam contains only $r_\perp$ vibrations (vibrating parallel to the reflecting surface), resulting in complete linear polarization.

3. Evaluation of Options
  • Statement 1: CORRECT. Defines linearly plane-polarized light.
  • Statement 2: CORRECT. Reflection from water surfaces is linearly polarized (horizontal glare) according to Brewster's law.
  • Statement 3: INCORRECT. Blue skylight is strongly polarized due to Rayleigh scattering at 90° from the Sun.
Key Takeaway: Light vibrating in one plane = polarized (1); Water reflection = polarized glare via Brewster's Law (2); Blue skylight = polarized by Rayleigh scattering (3 is false). Hence, (a) 1 and 2 is the correct answer.
