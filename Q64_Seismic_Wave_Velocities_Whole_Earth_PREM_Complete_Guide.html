Question 64: Seismic Wave Velocity Distribution Within the Earth (PREM Model)

Complete Velocity Range from Upper Crust to Deep Mantle, Elastic Moduli, and PREM Radial Profiles
Official Examination Question
The velocity of the seismic waves within the earth ranges from
(a) 1 to 4 km/second
(b) 2 to 8 km/second
(c) 3 to 10 km/second
(d) 2 to 13 km/second [CORRECT]
Verified Answer Key

Correct Option: (d) 2 to 13 km/second — Throughout the Earth's interior (from the near-surface crustal layers to the base of the lower mantle), seismic wave velocities span from ~2.0–3.0 km/s (shear waves in shallow crust and sedimentary cover) up to ~13.6–13.7 km/s (maximum compressional $P$-wave velocity at the base of the lower mantle / $D''$ layer, just above the core-mantle boundary).

Figure 64.1 | Radial Seismic Velocity Profile of the Entire Earth: Compressional ($P$) and shear ($S$) wave velocities plotted as a function of depth from the surface (0 km) to the Earth's center (6370 km). In the crust, $V_s \approx 2\text{--}3.5\text{ km/s}$ and $V_p \approx 5\text{--}6.5\text{ km/s}$. Across the mantle, $V_p$ increases progressively from $8.0\text{ km/s}$ at the Moho to a global terrestrial peak of $13.7\text{ km/s}$ at the base of the lower mantle (~2898 km). (Source: John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, 2nd Ed., Fig. 1-3, p. 7; after Kearey & Vine, 1990; PREM model).

1. Elastic Wave Physics & Seismic Velocity Controls

The velocities of body seismic waves propagating through isotropic solid elastic media depend directly on the density ($\rho$) and elastic moduli: bulk modulus ($K$, incompressibility) and shear modulus ($\mu$, rigidity):

  • Compressional / Primary Wave Velocity ($V_p$): $$V_p = \sqrt{\frac{K + \frac{4}{3}\mu}{\rho}}$$
  • Transverse / Shear Wave Velocity ($V_s$): $$V_s = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\rho}}$$

Although density $\rho$ increases downward with depth, the elastic moduli $K$ and $\mu$ increase much more rapidly due to intense self-gravitational compaction and pressure. Consequently, both $V_p$ and $V_s$ increase with depth throughout the crust and mantle.

2. Detailed Layer-by-Layer Velocity Breakdown (PREM Model)

Lower Crust 15 – 35 km 3.4 – 3.9 km/s 6.5 – 7.1 km/s High-grade granulites and amphibolites.

Lower Mantle (Mesosphere) 660 – 2700 km 5.5 – 7.2 km/s 10.7 – 13.5 km/s Bridgmanite + ferropericlase; uniform compaction increase.

3. Comprehensive Option Analysis

  • (a) 1 to 4 km/second: Incorrect. This range corresponds only to surface waves (Rayleigh/Love) or shear waves in shallow unconsolidated sediment. It completely overlooks mantle and core velocities ($>8\text{ km/s}$).
  • (b) 2 to 8 km/second: Incorrect. This represents only the crust and uppermost mantle just beneath the Moho ($\sim 8.1\text{ km/s}$). It omits the entire transition zone and lower mantle where velocities reach nearly $14\text{ km/s}$.
  • (c) 3 to 10 km/second: Incorrect. It omits shallow slow waves ($<3\text{ km/s}$) as well as the deep mantle velocities ($10\text{ to }13.7\text{ km/s}$).
  • (d) 2 to 13 km/second: Correct. The universally recognized whole-Earth body wave velocity range spans from ~2 km/s (near-surface crustal shear waves) up to ~13–13.7 km/s (deep lower mantle compressional waves).
