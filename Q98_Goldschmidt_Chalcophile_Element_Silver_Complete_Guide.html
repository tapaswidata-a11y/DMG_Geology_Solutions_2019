Question 98: Goldschmidt's Geochemical Classification: Chalcophile Silver

Thermodynamic Affinities, Electronic Configurations, and Element Partitioning

Q.98: Which one of the following is a chalcophile element?

(a) Oxygen
(b) Lithium
✓ (c) Silver
(d) Niobium
Correct Option: (c) Silver

1. Principles of Goldschmidt's Geochemical Classification

V. M. Goldschmidt (1922, 1937) classified the chemical elements according to their preferred geochemical affinities and distribution among the four primary phases formed during planetary differentiation:

  • Lithophile Elements ("Rock-loving" / Silicate-loving): Elements with a strong chemical affinity for oxygen; they have outer electron configurations that readily form ionic bonds with O2− and concentrate in silicate minerals and oxides in Earth's crust and mantle.
  • Chalcophile Elements ("Ore-loving" / Sulfur-loving): Elements with intermediate electronegativities that form covalent bonds with polarizable S2−, Se2−, and Te2− ions, concentrating predominantly in sulfide minerals and sulfide melt phases.
  • Siderophile Elements ("Iron-loving"): Elements that form metallic bonds and concentrate in metallic iron-nickel phases (Earth's core).
  • Atmophile Elements ("Gas-loving"): Highly volatile elements concentrated in the atmosphere and hydrosphere.

2. Geochemical Characteristics of Silver (Ag)

Silver ([Kr] 4d10 5s1, atomic number 47) is a quintessential chalcophile element:

  • In igneous, metamorphic, and hydrothermal systems, Ag strongly partitions into sulfide melts and hydrothermal sulfide complexes with partition coefficients Dsulf/sil > 100.
  • Silver forms primary economic sulfide and sulfosalt minerals such as argentite/acanthite (Ag2S), pyrargyrite (Ag3SbS3), proustite (Ag3AsS3), and tetrahedrite-freibergite ((Ag,Cu)12Sb4S13).
  • It also occurs in substantial solid solution in galena (PbS) via coupled substitution: Ag+ + Bi3+ ↔ 2 Pb2+.

3. Systematic Option Evaluation

Option Element Goldschmidt Classification Geochemical Affinity & Natural Occurrence
(a) Oxygen (O) Lithophile / Atmophile Incorrect. Oxygen is the defining ligand of lithophile elements, constituting ~46% of Earth's crust and forming the silicate tetrahedron [SiO4]4−.
(b) Lithium (Li) Lithophile Incorrect. Group 1 alkali metal with high ionic potential; substitutes for Mg2+ in micas and pyroxenes (e.g., spodumene LiAlSi2O6, lepidolite).
(c) Silver (Ag) Chalcophile Correct. Strong affinity for sulfur; concentrates in sulfide ore bodies, sulfosalts, and native silver associated with hydrothermal veins.
(d) Niobium (Nb) Lithophile Incorrect. High Field Strength Element (HFSE, pentavalent Nb5+) that concentrates in oxides (columbite-tantalite [(Fe,Mn)(Nb,Ta)2O6]) and carbonatites/alkaline rocks.

Key Geochemical Takeaway

Goldschmidt's chalcophile group comprises S, Se, Te, Cu, Ag, Zn, Cd, Hg, Ga, In, Tl, Pb, As, Sb, Bi. In mining exploration and ore geology, these elements form the basis of base-metal and precious-metal sulfide mineralization.
