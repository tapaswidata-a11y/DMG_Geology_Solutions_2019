Equilibrium Mineral Assemblages: The Forsterite–Quartz Incompatibility & Phase Petrology

Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) Junior Scientific Officer Examination 2019 | Igneous Petrology & Phase Equilibria Guide

📋 Question Statement

Q.27. Which of the following do NOT constitute an equilibrium mineral assemblage in a rock?
a (a) Quartz and orthoclase
b (b) Forsterite and quartz
c (c) Hornblende and andesine
d (d) Labradorite and augite
✅ Correct Answer: (b) Forsterite and quartz (Chemically incompatible pair)

At equilibrium, the system is split into two mutually exclusive sub-systems: (1) Fo + En (silica-undersaturated) and (2) En + Qtz (silica-oversaturated). 🔍 In-Depth Option Evaluation & Petrographic Analysis

(b) Forsterite and quartz — [DOES NOT CONSTITUTE AN EQUILIBRIUM ASSEMBLAGE • CORRECT]

Chemical Incompatibility: Forsterite (Mg2SiO4) is a highly silica-undersaturated nesosilicate (olivine group). Quartz (SiO2) is pure silica (tectosilicate). In the MgO–SiO2 system, an intermediate chain silicate phase exists: Enstatite (MgSiO3).

The Incompatibility Reaction: Under all geological crustal and upper-mantle conditions (T < 1557°C and P < 10 GPa), forsterite and quartz react completely and irreversibly to form enstatite:

Forsterite–Quartz Breakdown Reaction:
Mg2SiO4 (Forsterite) + SiO2 (Quartz) ⟶ 2 MgSiO3 (Enstatite)
ΔG°reaction < 0 (Thermodynamically spontaneous)

Because the Gibbs free energy of this reaction is strongly negative, a rock can contain either (Forsterite + Enstatite) in silica-undersaturated compositions, or (Enstatite + Quartz) in silica-oversaturated compositions, but NEVER Forsterite + Quartz together in thermodynamic equilibrium!

Note on Fe-rich systems: While Mg-olivine (Forsterite) is incompatible with quartz, Fe-rich olivine (Fayalite, Fe2SiO4) CAN coexist with quartz in highly differentiated ferroan granites and rhyolites because pure ferrosilite (FeSiO3) is unstable at low pressures. The question explicitly specifies Forsterite, making this an absolute thermodynamic incompatibility!

(a) Quartz and orthoclase — [STABLE EQUILIBRIUM PAIR]

Rock Types: Granite, Rhyolite, Granitic Pegmatite, Aplite, Felsic Gneiss.

Phase Relations: Quartz (SiO2) and Orthoclase (KAlSi3O8) form the classic low-temperature eutectic in "Petrogeny's Residua System" (Quartz–Albite–Orthoclase). They crystallize together in mutual thermodynamic equilibrium as the terminal assemblage of granitic melts.

(c) Hornblende and andesine — [STABLE EQUILIBRIUM PAIR]

Rock Types: Diorite, Andesite, Amphibolite (metabasite).

Phase Relations: Hornblende (calcic amphibole) and Andesine (intermediate plagioclase, An30–An50) define the diagnostic mineral assemblage of intermediate calc-alkaline magmas as well as the standard amphibolite facies of regional metamorphism. They are in complete thermodynamic equilibrium.

(d) Labradorite and augite — [STABLE EQUILIBRIUM PAIR]

Rock Types: Basalt, Gabbro, Diabase / Dolerite.

Phase Relations: Labradorite (calcic plagioclase, An50–An70) and Augite (calcic clinopyroxene) form the principal cotectic mineral pair of mafic igneous rocks worldwide, coexisting stably in equilibrium to produce ophitic, subophitic, and intergranular textures.

⚛️ Petrological Foundations: Silica Saturation & Bowen's Reaction Series

1. The Concept of Silica Saturation (Shand, 1927)

Igneous rocks and mineral assemblages are divided into three fundamental categories based on their silica activity:

Category Definition Diagnostic Minerals Forbidden Minerals (Incompatible)
Silica-Oversaturated Contains free primary SiO2 Quartz, Cristobalite, Tridymite, Hypersthene Forsterite, Nepheline, Leucite, Sodalite
Silica-Saturated Exact stoichiometric silica; no free quartz, no unsaturated phases Enstatite, Diopside, Feldspars (Albite, Anorthite, Orthoclase) Quartz + Forsterite together
Silica-Undersaturated Deficient in SiO2; forms silica-poor minerals Forsterite, Nepheline, Leucite, Kalsilite, Melilite Quartz

2. Bowen's Discontinuous Reaction Series

N. L. Bowen (1928) demonstrated that during fractional crystallization of basaltic magmas:

Bowen's Discontinuous Branch:
Olivine (Fo) ⟶ Orthopyroxene (En) ⟶ Clinopyroxene (Augite) ⟶ Amphibole (Hornblende) ⟶ Biotite

• At the peritectic reaction: Forsterite + Liquid ⟶ Enstatite.
• In a silica-rich melt, all forsterite is resorbed before quartz can form.
• In a silica-poor melt, quartz never forms because all silica is consumed.

📚 Direct Textbook Quotations & Citations

John D. Winter (2010), Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, Chapter 7, p. 121:

"Olivine crystals begin to form at 1800°C, they continue to grow with cooling, and then they begin to be consumed back into the melt as a new mineral, enstatite, forms... You might also recognize this olivine ⟶ pyroxene transition as the first step in the 'discontinuous' left side of Bowen's Reaction Series... Because enstatite forms between forsterite and silica, forsterite and quartz cannot coexist in equilibrium."
— Winter (2010), Chapter 7, p. 121
Anthony R. Philpotts & Jay J. Ague (2009), Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, 2nd ed., Chapter 10:

"Because enstatite is intermediate in composition between forsterite and quartz, the reaction Fo + Qtz = 2 En prevents the coexistence of forsterite and quartz at equilibrium in any igneous or metamorphic rock. Rocks containing olivine and quartz are either non-equilibrium mixtures or contain iron-rich olivine (fayalite)."
— Philpotts & Ague (2009), Chapter 10

🎯 Exam Traps & Rapid Elimination Strategy

⚡ Standard Competitive Exam Incompatible Pairs (Forbidden Assemblages):
  1. Forsterite + Quartz: Always incompatible! They react to form Enstatite / Orthopyroxene (Fo + Qtz ⟶ 2 En).
  2. Nepheline + Quartz: Always incompatible! They react to form Albite (Ne + 2 Qtz ⟶ Ab).
  3. Leucite + Quartz: Always incompatible! They react to form K-feldspar (Lc + Qtz ⟶ Kfs).
  4. Kalsilite + Quartz: Always incompatible! They react to form K-feldspar (Kls + 2 Qtz ⟶ Kfs).
  5. Corundum + Quartz: Always incompatible! They react to form Al2SiO5 (Crn + Qtz ⟶ Ky/Sil/And).
