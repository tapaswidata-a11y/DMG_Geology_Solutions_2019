Question 91: Radiometric Quantities in Remote Sensing: Irradiance vs. Exitance

Official Examination Guide • Standard SI Radiometric Terminology, Radiant Flux & Incident Energy Density
Official Examination Question
The amount of radiant energy at wavelengths (λ) incident upon a surface per unit area of that surface is known as
(a) Radiant energy (Qλ)
(b) Radiant flux (Φλ)
(c) Irradiance (Eλ) CORRECT ANSWER
(d) Radiant exitance (Mλ)
1. Executive Summary & Core Principle

In optical physics and remote sensing (Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman, Remote Sensing and Image Interpretation, Chapter 1; Jensen, Introductory Digital Image Processing), radiometric terms describe how electromagnetic radiation is emitted, transferred, and received:

  • Irradiance ($E_\lambda$ or $E$): The measure of radiant flux (or radiant energy per unit time) incident upon a surface per unit area of that surface:
    Eλ = dΦλ / dA
    It is expressed in Watts per square meter ($\text{W}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}$ or spectrally as $\text{W}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}\cdot\mu\text{m}^{-1}$). In Earth observation, solar radiation arriving at the Earth's surface or at the top of the atmosphere is referred to as solar irradiance ($E$).
  • Radiant Energy ($Q_\lambda$): The total energy carried by electromagnetic waves, measured in Joules ($\text{J}$).
  • Radiant Flux ($\Phi_\lambda$): The time rate of flow of radiant energy ($\Phi = dQ/dt$), measured in Watts ($\text{W} = \text{J/s}$).
  • Radiant Exitance ($M_\lambda$, formerly Radiant Emittance): The rate of radiant energy (radiant flux) emitted or leaving from a surface per unit area ($M = d\Phi / dA$, also in $\text{W}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}$).
  • Radiance ($L_\lambda$): The radiant flux per unit projected source area per unit solid angle ($\text{W}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}\cdot\text{sr}^{-1}$). Radiance is the fundamental quantity measured by airborne and satellite sensor detectors.

Because the question specifies the radiant energy per second (radiant flux) incident upon a surface per unit area, the correct term is (c) Irradiance ($E_\lambda$).

3. Evaluation of Options
  • (a) Radiant energy ($Q_\lambda$): INCORRECT. Represents total energy in Joules, not energy per unit area.
  • (b) Radiant flux ($\Phi_\lambda$): INCORRECT. Represents total energy rate in Watts ($\text{J/s}$), not normalized by surface area.
  • (c) Irradiance ($E_\lambda$): CORRECT ANSWER. By definition, irradiance is radiant flux incident upon a surface per unit area ($\text{W/m}^2$).
  • (d) Radiant exitance ($M_\lambda$): INCORRECT. Represents radiant flux leaving or emitted from a surface, not incident upon it.
Key Takeaway: Energy incident upon a surface per unit area = Irradiance ($E_\lambda$); Energy leaving a surface per unit area = Radiant Exitance ($M_\lambda$). Hence, (c) is the correct choice.
