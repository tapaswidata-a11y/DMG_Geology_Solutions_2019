Question 87: Ocean Acidification & Seawater Carbonate Chemistry

Official Examination Guide • CO2 Ingress, Seawater pH Drop & Carbonate Ion Depletion in Marine Calcifiers
Official Examination Question
Carbon dioxide emissions from land entering the oceans:
  • 1. Make oceanic water acidic
  • 2. Make oceanic water basic
  • 3. Help some marine organisms to make their carbonate shells by drawing carbonate from the seawater
Choose the correct option:
(a) 1 only CORRECT ANSWER
(b) 2 and 3
(c) 2 only
(d) 1 and 3
1. Executive Summary & Core Geochemical Principle

The dissolution of anthropogenic carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) emissions into the global oceans triggers fundamental shifts in the marine carbonate equilibrium system (William M. White, Geochemistry, Chapter 6):

  • Seawater Acidification (Statement 1 is TRUE): Atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ dissolves into surface seawater, forming aqueous carbonic acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$), which rapidly dissociates to produce hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$) and bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$):
    CO₂(g) ⇌ CO₂(aq)
    CO₂(aq) + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
    The release of free hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$) directly lowers the pH of seawater (a phenomenon termed Ocean Acidification). Surface ocean pH has already decreased by ~0.1 units since the industrial revolution (representing an approximate 30% increase in $[\text{H}^+]$ activity). Statement 1 is therefore correct, while Statement 2 ("Make oceanic water basic") is completely false.
  • Carbonate Ion Depletion & Calcification Impairment (Statement 3 is FALSE): A common misconception is that adding $\text{CO}_2$ adds carbonate for shells. The excess free $\text{H}^+$ ions react with ambient dissolved carbonate ions ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$), converting them into bicarbonate:
    H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ ⇌ HCO₃⁻
    This reaction consumes and depletes the bioavailable carbonate ions ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$)!
    The saturation state of calcium carbonate ($\Omega$) is given by:
    Ω = [Ca²⁺][CO₃²⁻] / K'sp
    As $[\text{CO}_3^{2-}]$ plummets, $\Omega$ decreases toward undersaturation ($\Omega < 1$). Consequently, calcifying marine organisms (corals, pteropods, coccolithophores, foraminifera, mollusks) find it substantially harder to precipitate their protective shells ($\text{CaCO}_3$), and existing shells undergo chemical dissolution. Statement 3 is thus completely contrary to scientific fact!

Therefore, only Statement 1 is correct, making (a) 1 only the definitive answer.

3. Evaluation of Options
  • Statement 1: CORRECT. Ingress of $\text{CO}_2$ generates $\text{H}^+$ ions, lowering seawater pH and making the ocean more acidic.
  • Statement 2: INCORRECT. $\text{CO}_2$ acts as an acid gas, lowering pH; it never makes water basic.
  • Statement 3: INCORRECT. $\text{CO}_2$ consumes carbonate ions ($\text{H}^+ + \text{CO}_3^{2-} \rightarrow \text{HCO}_3^-$), depleting the carbonate necessary for shell synthesis.
Key Takeaway: $\text{CO}_2$ dissolution releases $\text{H}^+$ (acidification) and consumes $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ (depleting shell-building minerals). Hence, only Statement 1 is correct: (a) 1 only.
