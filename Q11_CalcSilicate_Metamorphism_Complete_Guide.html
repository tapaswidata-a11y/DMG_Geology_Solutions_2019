1. Calc-Silicate Rocks & Cross-Facies Correlation

Calc-silicate rocks are metamorphosed marls or calcareous mudstones/sandstones containing significant amounts of both carbonate components ($\text{CaCO}_3, \text{CaMg(CO}_3)_2$) and silicate detritus (quartz, feldspar, illite/kaolinite clays providing Al, Si, and K). Because they contain both calcium and aluminum, calc-silicates develop distinctive Ca-Al-Mg silicates (zoisite/clinozoisite, grossular, anorthite, diopside, wollastonite) that are exceptionally sensitive to metamorphic grade.

In a landmark classic study, W. Q. Kennedy (1949) established the direct correlation between the progressive mineral zones in calc-silicate rocks and the Barrovian zones in associated pelitic schists of the Scottish Highlands:

  • Biotite Zone Equivalent: Zoisite + Calcite + Biotite + Albite + Quartz
  • Garnet Zone Equivalent: Clinozoisite + Calcite + Hornblende + Oligoclase
  • Staurolite / Kyanite Zone Equivalent: Diopside + Anorthite + Calcite ± Hornblende
  • Sillimanite Zone Equivalent (Peak Grade): Wollastonite + Grossular (+ Diopside + Anorthite)
2. Petrologic Reactions Generating Wollastonite + Grossular

Under high amphibolite-to-granulite facies conditions equivalent to the pelitic sillimanite zone ($T > 650^\circ\text{–}750^\circ\text{C}$), two quintessential decarbonation reactions occur:

Reaction 1: The Wollastonite-Forming Decarbonation Reaction
Calcite reacts with excess quartz to eliminate the Cal-Qtz tie-line and produce wollastonite: Calcite + Quartz = Wollastonite + CO₂      (CaCO₃ + SiO₂ = CaSiO₃ + CO₂)

In regional terranes buffered to moderate-to-high $X_{\text{CO}_2}$, this reaction requires high temperatures ($> 650^\circ\text{–}720^\circ\text{C}$), precisely matching the sillimanite zone.

Reaction 2: The Grossular-Forming Decarbonation Reaction
Calcic plagioclase (anorthite) reacts with remaining calcite and quartz to produce calcic grossular garnet: Anorthite + 2 Calcite + Quartz = Grossular + 2 CO₂ CaAl₂Si₂O₈ + 2 CaCO₃ + SiO₂ = Ca₃Al₂Si₃O₁₂ + 2 CO₂

Alternatively, at high pressures, zoisite breaks down: 2 Zoisite + Calcite + Quartz = 3 Grossular + CO₂ + H₂O.

Because both wollastonite and grossular require the complete breakdown of calcite with quartz and anorthite under peak thermal conditions, the assemblage Wollastonite + Grossular serves as the definitive high-grade calc-silicate marker equivalent to the sillimanite zone of pelites.

Official Exam Question Statement & In-Depth Critical Analysis
Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) 2019 · Junior Scientific Officer Exam · Question 11
Question Statement
Q.11: In metamorphism of calc-silicate rocks, which assemblage can be considered equivalent to the sillimanite zone of pelitic rocks?
(a) Anorthite–pyroxene
(b) Zoisite–calcite–biotite
(c) Diopside–anorthite
(d) Wollastonite–grossular
✓
Correct Answer: (d) Wollastonite–grossular
The high-temperature decarbonation assemblage of wollastonite + grossular defines the peak calc-silicate metamorphic grade, directly equivalent to the sillimanite zone in pelites.
Exhaustive Option-by-Option Critical Analysis
(d) Wollastonite–grossular CORRECT
Why it is uniquely correct: In metamorphic petrology and regional metamorphic zonations (Winter, 2010, Chapter 29; Kennedy, 1949; Bucher & Grapes, 2011), the stable coexistence of wollastonite ($\text{CaSiO}_3$) and grossular ($\text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$) requires high temperatures ($> 650^\circ\text{–}750^\circ\text{C}$) to drive the univariant decarbonation reactions $\text{Cal} + \text{Qtz} = \text{Wo} + \text{CO}_2$ and $\text{An} + 2\text{Cal} + \text{Qtz} = \text{Grs} + 2\text{CO}_2$. This peak assemblage represents the highest calc-silicate zone in regional metamorphic terranes and contact aureoles, directly equivalent to the upper amphibolite-to-granulite facies (the sillimanite zone of pelitic rocks).
(a) Anorthite–pyroxene INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: While anorthite and pyroxene (diopside) can occur across a broad temperature range in calc-silicate rocks, "anorthite–pyroxene" is more typically the characteristic signature of mafic granulites. In calc-silicates, anorthite and diopside form at intermediate grades (staurolite/kyanite zone); at the sillimanite grade, anorthite breaks down with calcite to produce grossular!
(b) Zoisite–calcite–biotite INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: In Kennedy's (1949) classic zonal correlation, the assemblage zoisite + calcite + biotite + albite is explicitly the diagnostic marker of the biotite zone (low-grade greenschist facies, T < 400°C–450°C). Zoisite is a hydrous epidote-group mineral that is completely unstable at the high temperatures of the sillimanite zone.
(c) Diopside–anorthite INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: The diopside–anorthite assemblage corresponds to the middle amphibolite facies (kyanite zone equivalent). Under progressive metamorphism, diopside and anorthite form before wollastonite and grossular appear. Only when temperatures rise further into the sillimanite zone does quartz react with calcite to generate wollastonite and anorthite react with calcite to yield grossular.

8 High-Yield Practice Questions on Calc-Silicates & Metamorphic Zonation
1. In Kennedy's (1949) zonal correlation of metamorphic rocks in the Scottish Highlands, which calc-silicate assemblage corresponds to the biotite zone of pelites?
(a) Wollastonite + Grossular    (b) Zoisite + Calcite + Biotite    (c) Diopside + Forsterite    (d) Periclase + Brucite
Answer: (b) Zoisite + Calcite + Biotite — Low-grade marly rocks develop zoisite and biotite in equilibrium with calcite, matching the pelitic biotite isograd.
2. What is the fundamental decarbonation reaction that introduces wollastonite into high-grade calc-silicates?
(a) Calcite + Quartz = Wollastonite + CO₂
(b) Dolomite + Quartz = Diopside + 2 CO₂
(c) Tremolite + Calcite = Diopside + CO₂ + H₂O
(d) Grossular + Quartz = Anorthite + Wollastonite
Answer: (a) Calcite + Quartz = Wollastonite + CO₂ — The classic univariant decarbonation equilibrium.
3. The presence of grossular garnet in calc-silicate rocks requires the presence of which element in the sedimentary protolith?
(a) Titanium    (b) Aluminum (from detrital clay/shale)    (c) Zirconium    (d) Phosphorus
Answer: (b) Aluminum — Pure carbonate lacks Al; grossular ($\text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$) requires aluminum supplied by detrital clay minerals.
4. In contact metamorphic aureoles of limestone (e.g., Crestmore, California), the innermost, highest-temperature zone adjacent to the pluton is typically the:
(a) Forsterite zone    (b) Monticellite zone    (c) Grossular–Wollastonite–Diopside zone    (d) Chlorite zone
Answer: (c) Grossular–Wollastonite–Diopside zone — The innermost zone represents peak temperatures where silica metasomatism and intense decarbonation yield grossular, wollastonite, and diopside.
5. On an isobaric $T-X_{\text{CO}_2}$ diagram, why do devolatilization reaction curves that liberate both H₂O and CO₂ (e.g., tremolite formation) exhibit a parabolic maximum?
(a) Due to changes in gravity
(b) Because the reaction produces mixed volatiles, reaching maximum thermal stability at a specific fluid composition matching the stoichiometric product ratio ($X_{\text{CO}_2} = n_{\text{CO}_2} / (n_{\text{CO}_2} + n_{\text{H}_2\text{O}})$)
(c) Because calcite melts at low temperatures
(d) Because quartz dissolves into the gas phase
Answer: (b) — Reactions producing both H₂O and CO₂ reach their peak temperature on a $T-X_{\text{CO}_2}$ diagram where the fluid composition equals the ratio in which the gases are evolved.
6. Which calc-silicate mineral is an epidote-group sorosilicate with formula $\text{Ca}_2\text{Al}_3\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}\text{(OH)}$?
(a) Zoisite/Clinozoisite    (b) Wollastonite    (c) Diopside    (d) Forsterite
Answer: (a) Zoisite/Clinozoisite — Zoisite (orthorhombic) and clinozoisite (monoclinic) are hydrated Ca-Al silicates characteristic of low-to-medium grade calc-silicates.
7. In high-grade regional calc-silicate rocks, scapolite commonly forms by replacement of:
(a) Plagioclase feldspar in the presence of $\text{CO}_2$- and $\text{Cl}$-rich fluids    (b) Olivine    (c) Biotite    (d) Pyrite
Answer: (a) — Scapolite (meionite/marialite solid solution) is an alumosilicate containing volatile anions ($CO_3^{2-}, Cl^-, SO_4^{2-}$) that replaces plagioclase at high metamorphic temperatures.
8. The transition from the diopside–anorthite zone to the wollastonite–grossular zone in calc-silicate rocks coincides with which pelitic reaction?
(a) Chlorite = Biotite
(b) Muscovite + Quartz = Sillimanite + K-feldspar + H₂O (or melt)
(c) Smectite = Illite
(d) Kaolinite = Pyrophyllite
Answer: (b) — The second sillimanite isograd (muscovite breakdown to sillimanite + Kfs) occurs at roughly $650^\circ\text{–}700^\circ\text{C}$, matching the formation of wollastonite and grossular in calc-silicates.
