DGMS 2019 Geology • Question 34

The Earth's Lithosphere: Mechanical vs. Compositional Layering

Topic: Geophysics, Petrology & Geodynamics | Primary Reference: John D. Winter (Principles of Igneous & Metamorphic Petrology, Ch. 1)

📝 Question Statement

Q.34. The lithosphere is comprised of

(a) Mantle and outer core
(b) Mantle and asthenosphere
(c) Upper mantle and crust
(d) Continental and oceanic crusts
a Mantle and outer core
b Mantle and asthenosphere
c Upper mantle and crust (Correct Answer)
d Continental and oceanic crusts
✓ Correct Answer: (c) Upper mantle and crust [Specifically the crust plus the uppermost rigid lithospheric mantle above the asthenosphere].

🖼️ Textbook Evidence: Winter (Chapter 1)

Figure 1-11: Rheological and Compositional Subdivisions of the Earth (John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, 2nd ed., p. 29). Cross-section illustrating the fundamental duality of the Earth's upper layers: compositionally, the boundary between the crust and mantle is the Mohorovičić discontinuity (Moho); mechanically, the rigid lithosphere consists of the entire crust plus the uppermost rigid mantle (the lithospheric mantle), resting above the ductile, convecting asthenosphere.
"The lithosphere (from Greek lithos, stone) is the rigid, mechanically strong outer layer of the Earth. It includes the crust and the uppermost solid, rigid portion of the upper mantle. The underlying asthenosphere (from Greek asthenes, weak) is part of the upper mantle, but differs rheologically: it is plastic, ductile, and capable of flow over geologic timescales."
— John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, Chapter 1, pp. 11–12

🔍 Comprehensive Option Analysis

(c) Upper mantle and crust [CORRECT]

Rheological & Structural Architecture: Earth is layered in two distinct ways:

  • Compositional (Chemical) Layering: Based on density and mineral chemistry—Crust (felsic to mafic silicates), Mantle (ultramafic peridotite), and Core (iron-nickel metal).
  • Mechanical (Rheological) Layering: Based on mechanical strength and deformation response—Lithosphere (rigid/brittle plate), Asthenosphere (weak/ductile mantle), Mesosphere (stiff lower mantle), Outer core (liquid), and Inner core (solid).

The lithosphere is defined precisely as the rigid outer shell that moves as coherent tectonic plates. It encompasses the entirety of the crust (both continental and oceanic) plus the uppermost mantle (lithospheric mantle / LID), bounded below by the ~1300°C isotherm that marks the transition into the ductile asthenosphere.

(a) Mantle and outer core [INCORRECT]

The outer core is a liquid iron-nickel metallic alloy extending from 2,890 km to 5,150 km depth. The lithosphere is confined exclusively to the outermost 100–250 km of the Earth.

(b) Mantle and asthenosphere [INCORRECT]

The asthenosphere is the ductile substrate beneath the lithosphere, not a constituent of it. Moreover, the lithosphere contains the crust, which is omitted here, while the lithosphere does NOT include the entire mantle.

(d) Continental and oceanic crusts [INCORRECT]

Continental and oceanic crusts together make up only the Crust. Confining the lithosphere to the crust is a classic student pitfall: the crust is merely the top 7–70 km of the lithosphere. The lithosphere extends down to 100 km (beneath oceans) and up to 200–250 km (beneath ancient continental cratons) because it includes the rigid upper mantle.

📊 Compositional vs. Mechanical Layers of the Solid Earth

Classification Layer Name Typical Depth Composition & Physical State
Compositional (Chemical) Crust (Continental / Oceanic) 0 – 35 km (avg) Granitic to basaltic silicates; rigid, low density (2.7–3.0 g/cm³)
Mantle (Upper + Lower) 35 – 2,890 km Peridotite (olivine, pyroxene, garnet); bridgmanite, ferropericlase (3.3–5.5 g/cm³)
Core (Outer + Inner) 2,890 – 6,371 km Iron-nickel alloy + light elements (S, Si, O) (10–13 g/cm³)
Mechanical (Rheological) Lithosphere 0 – 100/250 km Rigid, elastic, brittle plates: Crust + Uppermost Mantle (LID)
Asthenosphere 100 – 410/660 km Ductile, plastic peridotite; capable of slow solid-state convective flow
Mesosphere 660 – 2,890 km Rigid, solid lower mantle under high lithostatic pressure
Outer Core / Inner Core 2,890 – 6,371 km Liquid outer core (magnetic dynamo) / Solid inner core

💡 Key Takeaway & Exam Strategy

Core Golden Rule:
  • Moho: The compositional boundary separating crust from mantle.
  • LAB (Lithosphere-Asthenosphere Boundary): The rheological/thermal boundary separating the rigid lithosphere (crust + upper mantle) from the ductile asthenosphere.
  • Always remember: Lithosphere = Crust + Uppermost Mantle.
