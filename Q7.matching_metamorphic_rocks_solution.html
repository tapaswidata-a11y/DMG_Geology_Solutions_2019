1. The Spectrum of Metamorphic Environments

Metamorphic rocks are classified on the basis of their texture, mineralogy, protolith chemistry, and geological setting of origin (Winter, 2010, Chapters 21 & 22; IUGS Subcommission on the Systematics of Metamorphic Rocks - SCMR). Four critical classes represent extreme end-members of physical regimes:

  • Blueschist (Subduction Zone High-P/Low-T): Metabasic rocks metamorphosed under high lithostatic pressure and low temperature ($P > 6\text{–}10\text{ kbar}, T = 200^\circ\text{–}450^\circ\text{C}$) in subducting oceanic slabs. Diagnostic sodic amphibole: glaucophane, imparting a distinctive blue color and schistose fabric.
  • Pseudotachylite (Dynamic Frictional Melt in Faults): Glassy, vitreous, or crypto-crystalline material occurring as injection veins and fault-vein fault rocks, generated by extreme localized frictional heating during rapid seismic rupture ($>0.1\text{–}1\text{ m/s}$) along fault planes.
  • Hornfels (Contact / Thermal Metamorphism): Tough, fine-grained, non-schistose rocks formed in the thermal aureoles of igneous intrusions under low confining pressures. Characterized by substantial static recrystallization and polygonal/decussate textures without preferred mineral orientation.
  • Skarn (Hydrothermal Metasomatism): Coarse-grained, zoned calc-silicate rocks resulting from intense chemical mass transfer (metasomatism) between silicate magmas/hydrothermal fluids and carbonate host rocks (limestones or dolomites), richly packed with Ca-Fe-Mg-Mn silicates (garnet, pyroxene, epidote).
2. Authoritative Textbook Definitions (Winter, Ch 22, pp. 500–502)
Blueschist: "A blue-amphibole-bearing metamorphosed mafic rock. Glaucophane, the characteristic blue amphibole, indicates high pressures and low temperatures typical of subduction zones... Most blueschists are foliated (schistose) due to directed tectonic stress during subduction accretion." — John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology (2nd Ed., p. 501)
Pseudotachylite: "A dark, very fine-grained to glassy rock that typically occurs as thin veins or intrusive veinlets in fault zones. It is formed by frictional melting of the wall rock during rapid seismic slip events on faults. When the melt chills against the cold country rock, it forms a vitreous or cryptocrystalline matrix with rounded host clasts." — John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology (2nd Ed., p. 502)
Hornfels: "A type of granofels that is typically fine-grained, non-schistose, and occurs in contact metamorphic aureoles. Hornfelses are tough and splintery, exhibiting substantial recrystallization into equant grains without preferred orientation because deformation is absent during thermal baking." — John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology (2nd Ed., p. 500)
Skarn: "A calc-silicate rock resulting from contact metamorphism and metasomatism of carbonate rocks by hydrothermal fluids derived from an igneous intrusion... Characterized by Ca-Fe-Mg-Mn silicates (such as grossular-andradite garnet, diopside-hedenbergite, epidote, wollastonite) that develop pronounced compositional zones and bands." — John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology (2nd Ed., p. 501)

4. A metasomatic Ca-Fe-Mg-Mn-silicate rock with compositional zones and bands
✓
Correct Match: A – 2,   B – 1,   C – 3,   D – 4
Blueschist is a Na-amphibole schist; Pseudotachylite is vitreous fault melt; Hornfels is non-schistose thermal recrystallization; Skarn is metasomatic Ca-Fe-Mg-Mn silicate rock.
Exhaustive Pairwise Critical Analysis
A → 2: Blueschist ⟷ Na-amphibole containing schistose rock

Why this match is conclusive: Blueschists are defined mineralogically by the predominance of the blue sodium amphibole glaucophane ($Na_2(Mg_3Al_2)Si_8O_{22}(OH)_2$) or crossite/riebeckite, formed by high-pressure, low-temperature metamorphism of basaltic oceanic crust in subduction zones. Because they undergo intense non-coaxial deformation within the subduction accretionary wedge, blueschists almost universally possess a pronounced tectonic foliation and are thus true schistose rocks.

B → 1: Pseudotachylite ⟷ Ultrafine-grained vitreous looking material occurring as thin veins

Why this match is conclusive: Pseudotachylites are fossil earthquakes: they represent rock melt generated by extreme frictional dissipation along fault surfaces during seismic rupture. The generated silicate melt is violently injected into surrounding fractures and rapidly quenched against cold host rock, solidifying into an ultrafine-grained, glassy (vitreous) or cryptocrystalline black material occurring as sharp, thin veins.

C → 3: Hornfels ⟷ A non-schistose substantial recrystallization

Why this match is conclusive: Hornfels is the classic product of thermal contact metamorphism in the static aureoles surrounding igneous plutons. Because differential stress and directed tectonic shearing are minimal or absent during thermal baking, the preexisting rock undergoes substantial static recrystallization to form an interlocking, equigranular polygonal or decussate mosaic that is entirely non-schistose (lacking foliation).

D → 4: Skarn ⟷ A metasomatic Ca-Fe-Mg-Mn-silicate rock with compositional zones and bands

Why this match is conclusive: Skarns (also known as tactites) are formed by intense metasomatic mass exchange driven by hydrothermal fluids infiltrating between a silicate magma (providing Si, Fe, Al) and carbonate rocks (providing Ca, Mg, CO₂). This reactive fluid flow produces a complex suite of Ca-Fe-Mg-Mn silicates (grossular-andradite, diopside-hedenbergite, wollastonite, epidote, bustamite) arranged in sharp, distinct compositional zones and reaction bands.

8 High-Yield Practice Questions on Metamorphic Rock Nomenclature
1. Glaucophane, the defining sodium amphibole in blueschists, belongs to which mineral group formula?
(a) Ca₂(Mg,Fe)₅Si₈O₂₂(OH)₂
(b) Na₂Mg₃Al₂Si₈O₂₂(OH)₂
(c) (Mg,Fe)₇Si₈O₂₂(OH)₂
(d) KAl₂Si₃AlO₁₀(OH)₂
Answer: (b) — Glaucophane is a sodic amphibole with the end-member formula Na₂Mg₃Al₂Si₈O₂₂(OH)₂.
2. Pseudotachylites are distinguished from mylonites primarily because pseudotachylites form by:
(a) Ductile crystal-plastic dislocation creep
(b) Frictional melting during rapid seismic slip
(c) Slow pressure solution mass transfer
(d) Static thermal annealing in an aureole
Answer: (b) Frictional melting during rapid seismic slip — Mylonites form by ductile plastic flow without melting, whereas pseudotachylites are generated by flash frictional melting during fast seismic slip events.
3. Contact metamorphic aureoles surrounding shallow granitic intrusions typically produce which non-foliated rock from pelitic shale protoliths?
(a) Mylonite    (b) Pelitic Hornfels    (c) Blueschist    (d) Gneiss
Answer: (b) Pelitic Hornfels — Shales baked under static conditions in thermal aureoles transform into dense, fine-grained, non-schistose hornfelses (spotted hornfels with cordierite/andalusite).
4. An "exoskarn" refers to a metasomatic skarn developed within:
(a) The intrusive igneous body itself
(b) The sedimentary carbonate host rock adjoining the pluton
(c) Ocean floor basaltic pillow lavas
(d) Mantle peridotite xenoliths
Answer: (b) — Exoskarn forms in the surrounding sedimentary carbonate country rock, whereas endoskarn forms by replacement within the igneous intrusion itself.
5. Under polarizing petrographic microscope, the glassy matrix of an unaltered pseudotachylite appears:
(a) Strongly pleochroic    (b) Completely isotropic (dark under crossed polars)    (c) Highly birefringent    (d) Lamellar twinned
Answer: (b) Completely isotropic (dark under crossed polars) — True volcanic and frictional glass lacks crystalline order and is optically isotropic.
6. Which diagnostic mineral pair in blueschists indicates the complete breakdown of plagioclase at high pressure?
(a) Albite + Anorthite    (b) Jadeite + Quartz    (c) Calcite + Quartz    (d) Chlorite + Biotite
Answer: (b) Jadeite + Quartz — Albite breaks down at high pressure via the univariant reaction: Albite = Jadeite + Quartz, a definitive high-pressure index equilibrium.
7. The decussate texture often observed in hornfelses is characterized by:
(a) Parallel aligned mica flakes defining continuous schistosity
(b) Randomly oriented, interlocking elongate or platy crystals lacking preferred orientation
(c) Alternating felsic and mafic compositional banding
(d) Rounded porphyroclasts with recrystallized tails
Answer: (b) — Decussate texture consists of interlocking prismatic or platy grains (e.g., micas, amphiboles) oriented completely at random due to absence of deviatoric stress.
8. Skarn deposits are world-class economic sources for which metals?
(a) Fe, Cu, W, Sn, Au, Zn, Pb    (b) Exclusively alkali metals like Li and Na    (c) Noble gases    (d) Halite and sylvite
Answer: (a) Fe, Cu, W, Sn, Au, Zn, Pb — Metasomatic skarns host major hydrothermal ore deposits of iron (magnetite skarn), copper, tungsten (scheelite skarn), gold, and lead-zinc.
