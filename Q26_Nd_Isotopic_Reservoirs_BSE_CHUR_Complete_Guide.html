Mantle Reservoirs & Isotopic Geochemistry: The Bulk Silicate Earth (BSE) and Chondritic Uniform Reservoir (CHUR)

Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) Junior Scientific Officer Examination 2019 | Standard Petrology & Geochemistry Reference Guide

📋 Question Statement

Q.26. Which one of the following geochemical reservoirs is defined by the Nd-isotopic composition of chondritic meteorites?
a (a) DM (Depleted Mantle)
b (b) BSE (Bulk Silicate Earth)
c (c) PREMA (Prevalent Mantle)
d (d) HIMU (High-µ Mantle)
✅ Correct Answer: (b) BSE (Bulk Silicate Earth)

🔍 In-Depth Option Evaluation & Geochemical Breakdown

(b) BSE (Bulk Silicate Earth) — [CORRECT]

Definition: The Bulk Silicate Earth (BSE), also termed the Primitive Mantle (PUM), represents the bulk composition of the Earth's silicate portion (crust + mantle) prior to any differentiation into continental crust and residual depleted mantle, but after core formation.

Why Chondritic Nd Isotopes Define BSE: Both Samarium (Sm) and Neodymium (Nd) are Refractory Lithophile Elements (RLE).

  • Refractory: They possess extremely high condensation temperatures in the solar nebula (> 1400 K), meaning they did not volatilize during planetary accretion.
  • Lithophile: They do not dissolve in metallic iron and thus did not enter the Earth's core during planetary core segregation.

Because neither element was fractionated by volatility nor core separation, the whole silicate Earth must have the exact same Sm/Nd ratio as chondritic meteorites. Consequently, the Nd-isotopic evolution of the Bulk Silicate Earth is modeled by the Chondritic Uniform Reservoir (CHUR) (DePaolo & Wasserburg, 1976). Today, CHUR and BSE have an identical 143Nd/144Nd value of 0.512638, defining εNd ≡ 0!

(a) DM (Depleted Mantle / DMM) — [INCORRECT]

Characteristics: Depleted Mantle (DM) is the residue left behind in the upper mantle after the extraction of continental crust over geological time. It is the source reservoir for normal mid-ocean ridge basalts (N-MORB).

Isotopic Signature: During partial melting of the primitive mantle, Nd is more incompatible than Sm (DNd < DSm), so Nd partitioned preferentially into the crustal melt. This left the residual mantle (DM) with a higher Sm/Nd ratio than chondrites. Over 2–3 billion years of 147Sm decay, DM evolved to higher 143Nd/144Nd ratios (0.5131–0.5136), corresponding to strongly positive εNd values (+10 to +12). Hence, DM is distinctly non-chondritic!

(c) PREMA (Prevalent Mantle) — [INCORRECT]

Characteristics: Proposed by Zindler and Hart (1986), PREMA represents a common focal component toward which many oceanic island basalt (OIB) arrays converge from different geographic sectors.

Isotopic Signature: PREMA has 143Nd/144Nd ≈ 0.5128–0.5130 (εNd ≈ +4 to +6), intermediate between DM and BSE, and 87Sr/86Sr ≈ 0.7033. It represents a mildly depleted, well-mixed mantle component (possibly lower mantle or entrained plume material), not the chondritic reservoir.

(d) HIMU (High-µ Mantle) — [INCORRECT]

Characteristics: HIMU derives its name from "high µ" (where µ = 238U/204Pb). It was created by ancient subduction and mantle storage (~1.5–2.0 Ga) of recycled altered oceanic crust (basaltic slabs) that lost Pb during subduction dehydration, resulting in an exceptionally elevated U/Pb ratio.

Isotopic Signature: Characterized by extremely radiogenic lead isotopes (206Pb/204Pb > 20.5–21.5), very low 87Sr/86Sr (~0.7028), and intermediate 143Nd/144Nd (0.5128–0.5129, εNd ≈ +4). It has no genetic or isotopic equivalence to chondritic meteorites.

⚛️ Theoretical Foundations: The Sm-Nd Isotopic System & CHUR

1. Radioactive Decay Scheme

The Sm-Nd isotopic system is based on the alpha decay of 147Sm to 143Nd:

Sm-Nd Alpha Decay Law:
147Sm → 143Nd + α (decay constant λ = 6.54 × 10-12 yr-1, half-life t1/2 = 106 Ga)

(143Nd / 144Nd)present = (143Nd / 144Nd)initial + (147Sm / 144Nd) × (eλt - 1)

2. The CHUR (Chondritic Uniform Reservoir) Concept

In 1976, Donald DePaolo and Gerald Wasserburg introduced the Chondritic Uniform Reservoir (CHUR) model. They analyzed carbonaceous and ordinary chondritic meteorites and demonstrated that their Sm/Nd ratios are remarkably uniform:

Present-Day CHUR Standard Parameters:
(147Sm / 144Nd)CHUR0 = 0.1967
(143Nd / 144Nd)CHUR0 = 0.512638 (normalized to 146Nd/144Nd = 0.7219)

3. The Epsilon Neodymium (εNd) Notation

Because differences in 143Nd/144Nd occur only in the 4th and 5th decimal places, DePaolo and Wasserburg defined parts per 10,000 deviations from CHUR/BSE:

Epsilon Nd Equation:
εNd = [ ((143Nd/144Nd)sample / (143Nd/144Nd)CHUR) - 1 ] × 10,000

• If εNd = 0 → Sample is identical to Bulk Silicate Earth (BSE / CHUR).
• If εNd > 0 → Sourced from Depleted Mantle (DM), higher Sm/Nd ratio (LREE-depleted).
• If εNd < 0 → Sourced from Enriched Reservoir / Continental Crust, lower Sm/Nd ratio (LREE-enriched).

🎯 Exam Traps & Rapid Elimination Strategy

⚡ Rule of Thumb for UPSC Geo-Scientist & DGMS:
  1. Whenever a question connects chondritic meteorites / CHUR to a mantle reservoir, immediately look for BSE (Bulk Silicate Earth) or PUM (Primitive Upper Mantle). They are synonymous in isotope geochemistry.
  2. DM (Depleted Mantle) is always associated with N-MORB, high positive εNd (+10 to +12), and low 87Sr/86Sr.
  3. HIMU is always associated with high 206Pb/204Pb (> 20.5) and recycled oceanic crust.
  4. EM-I / EM-II are always associated with negative εNd, high 87Sr/86Sr, and recycled continental crust / sediments.
  5. PREMA is the common convergence point of OIB mixing arrays (εNd ≈ +4 to +6).
