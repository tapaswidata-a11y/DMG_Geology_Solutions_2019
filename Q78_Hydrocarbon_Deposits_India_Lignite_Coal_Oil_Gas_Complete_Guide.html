Question 78: Indian Hydrocarbon & Fossil Fuel Deposits Matching

Official Examination Guide • Geographic Distribution & Stratigraphy of Indian Energy Resources
Official Examination Question
Match List-I (Hydrocarbon deposit) with List-II (Location):

List-I (Deposit)

A. Lignite
B. Oil
C. Bituminous coal
D. Natural gas

List-II (Location)

1. Kalol, Gujarat
2. Neyveli, Tamil Nadu
3. Jaisalmer, Rajasthan
4. Kamte (Kamptee), Maharashtra
(a) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3 CORRECT ANSWER
(b) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
(c) A-2, B-4, C-3, D-1
(d) A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2
1. Executive Summary & Verification

India's energy resource distribution spans distinctive sedimentary basins across various geological eras—from Lower Gondwana Permian coal measures to Cenozoic rift-margin petroleum basins and Tertiary coastal lignites:

  • A → 2: Lignite in Neyveli, Tamil Nadu: Neyveli hosts India's premier Tertiary lignite deposit within the Miocene Cuddalore Sandstone Formation in the Cauvery Basin / Tamil Nadu coastal plain. Mined by NLC India Ltd., it supplies fuel to giant pit-head thermal stations.
  • B → 1: Oil in Kalol, Gujarat: Kalol is a prolific onshore oilfield situated in the Ahmedabad-Mehsana block of the Cambay Basin, discovered by the Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC) in June 1961. The Middle Eocene Kalol Formation contains multiple deltaic sandstone reservoir pays.
  • C → 4: Bituminous Coal in Kamptee (Kamte), Maharashtra: The Kamptee Coalfield in Nagpur district belongs to the Wardha-Kanhan-Godavari valley Lower Gondwana basin. The Permian Barakar and Motur/Kamthi formations host substantial reserves of non-coking bituminous coal, extracted by Western Coalfields Limited (WCL).
  • D → 3: Natural Gas in Jaisalmer, Rajasthan: The Jaisalmer Basin on the northwestern Indian shelf is a premier gas-producing basin. Discovered by OIL and ONGC, major gas fields include Manhera Tibba, Tanot, Dandewala, and Ghotaru, producing commercial gas from Cretaceous (Pariwar) and Tertiary (Sanu/Bandah) sandstone/limestone reservoirs.

Combining these unambiguous pairs yields the unique matching sequence A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3, which corresponds precisely to Option (a).

2. Detailed Geological & Stratigraphic Provenance of Each Field
Deposit (List-I) Location (List-II) Sedimentary Basin Geological Age & Stratigraphic Unit Key Geological Characteristics
A. Lignite 2. Neyveli (Tamil Nadu) Cauvery Coastal Basin Tertiary / Miocene (Cuddalore Sandstone Formation) Seams up to 20 m thick; high moisture (45–50%), low ash (~6%), calorific value ~2800 kcal/kg; mined by open-cast bucket-wheel excavators.
B. Crude Oil 1. Kalol (Gujarat) Cambay Basin (North Block) Cenozoic / Middle Eocene (Kalol Formation) Extensional rift basin; interbedded sandstones, silts, and coal bands; deltaic reservoir sandstones (K-III to K-X pay zones).
C. Bituminous Coal 4. Kamptee (Maharashtra) Pranhita-Godavari / Wardha Valley Lower Gondwana / Early Permian (Barakar & Kamthi Formations) Non-coking thermal bituminous coal; interbedded with fluvial sandstones and shales; thick coal seams in graben/half-graben fault troughs.
D. Natural Gas 3. Jaisalmer (Rajasthan) Jaisalmer Basin (Shelf Basin) Mesozoic–Cenozoic (Cretaceous Pariwar & Paleocene-Eocene Sanu Formations) Sweet gas (predominantly methane with minor nitrogen); reservoirs in shallow marine to fluvial sandstones and shelf carbonates.
3. Evaluation of Match Combinations
  • (a) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3: CORRECT. All four geographic, geological, and commodity correlations are verified against official GSI, ONGC, and Coal India records.
  • (b) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4: Incorrect. Kalol is an oilfield, not a lignite deposit; Neyveli is a lignite field, not an oilfield.
  • (c) A-2, B-4, C-3, D-1: Incorrect. Kamptee is a coalfield, not an oilfield; Kalol is an oilfield, not a gas field.
  • (d) A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2: Incorrect. Jaisalmer is a gas field, not a lignite deposit; Neyveli produces lignite, not natural gas.
Key Takeaway: Neyveli = Lignite; Kalol = Oil; Kamptee = Bituminous Coal; Jaisalmer = Natural Gas. Hence, (a) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3 is the exact answer.
