Blueschist Facies: Metamorphic Petrogenetic Principles

Blueschist Facies (Lawsonite High-Pressure Marker) provides a fundamental empirical and quantitative tool for deciphering crustal thermal gradients, pressure regimes, and plate tectonic geodynamics. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (a): Lawsonite, CaAl2Si2O7(OH)2·H2O.

Eclogites and blueschists are diagnostic of high-pressure, low-to-moderate-temperature metamorphism developed along active subduction zones (Miyashiro's high P/T facies series). Under pressures >12-15 kbar (>45-50 km depth), low-density plagioclase feldspar breaks down completely into jadeite-diopside solid solution (omphacite) and almandine-pyrope garnet, creating ultra-dense (density > 3.4-3.6 g/cm3) mantle-penetrating oceanic slab residues.

"Lawsonite is stable exclusively at high pressures (>3-4 kbar) and low temperatures (<400°C), breaking down to zoisite + kyanite + quartz at higher temperatures." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Explanatory Answers, p. 390-391); Eskola, P. (1921, The mineral facies of rocks. Norsk Geol. Tidsskr. 6: 143-194); Coleman, R.G. et al. (1965, Eclogites and eclogites. GSA Bull. 76: 483-508)
Governing Metamorphic / Thermodynamic Relationship
$$\text{Eclogite Transition: } \text{Plagioclase} + \text{Pyroxene} \xrightarrow{P > 12\text{--}15\text{ kbar}} \text{Omphacite } (\text{Cpx}_{ss}) + \text{Pyrope-rich Garnet} + \text{Quartz}$$

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: A metabasic rock contains omphacitic pyroxene and pyrope-almandine garnet, with zero plagioclase feldspar. To which metamorphic facies does this rock belong?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (C)
    Mechanistic Rationale: The bimineralic assemblage of omphacite clinopyroxene and pyrope-rich garnet in the absence of plagioclase is the defining diagnostic hallmark of the Eclogite facies.
  • Practice Challenge 2: The appearance of which of the following minerals in basic rocks definitively marks the entry from the amphibolite facies into the granulite facies?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: In metabasites, the prograde dehydration breakdown of hornblende into orthopyroxene (hypersthene) + clinopyroxene + plagioclase marks the fundamental boundary of the Granulite facies.
