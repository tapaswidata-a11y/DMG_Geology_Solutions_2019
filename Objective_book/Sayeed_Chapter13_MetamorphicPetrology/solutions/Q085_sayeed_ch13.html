Metasomatism: Metamorphic Petrogenetic Principles

Metasomatism (Boron Pneumatolysis & Tourmaline) provides a fundamental empirical and quantitative tool for deciphering crustal thermal gradients, pressure regimes, and plate tectonic geodynamics. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (b): Water, boron, and fluorine.

Metamorphic petrology deciphers the thermodynamic transformations, kinetic recrystallization pathways, and structural fabrics that develop when rocks adjust to temperatures and pressures prevailing in Earth's interior. Combining phase equilibria, multi-component geothermobarometry, and isotopic geochronology provides quantitative reconstructions of tectonic burial, orogenic crustal thickening, and unroofing trajectories.

"Tourmalinization occurs when volatile boron- and fluorine-rich hydrothermal fluids from cooling granites metasomatize wall-rock aluminosilicates into tourmaline." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Explanatory Answers, p. 390-391); Winter, J.D. (2014, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology. Pearson); Spear, F.S. (1993, Metamorphic Phase Equilibria and Pressure-Temperature-Time Paths); Ausaf Sayeed (Objective Geology)

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: According to George Barrow's classic metamorphic zonation of the Scottish Highlands, what is the correct progressive sequence of index minerals in pelitic rocks with increasing grade?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (A)
    Mechanistic Rationale: The classic Barrovian progressive sequence in pelites is: Chlorite -> Biotite -> Almandine Garnet -> Staurolite -> Kyanite -> Sillimanite.
  • Practice Challenge 2: Which of the following fault rocks is characterized by dark glassy or cryptocrystalline veinlets formed by frictional melting during rapid seismic rupture?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Pseudotachylyte represents fossil earthquake slip surfaces formed by localized frictional melt generation along high-velocity fault planes during seismic slip.
