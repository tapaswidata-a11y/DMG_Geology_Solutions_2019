Official Examination Question — Question 131
In most crustal sedimentary rocks, what is the relative chemical proportion of potassium (K) to sodium (Na)?
(a) K > Na (Potassium exceeds Sodium)  ✔
(b) K < Na
(c) K = Na
(d) None of the above
PART I
Depositional Mechanics & Theoretical Framework
Hydrodynamics, granulometric equations, and authoritative literature grounding
Sedimentary Geochemistry: Core Sedimentological Principles

Sedimentary Geochemistry (K/Na Fractionation in Weathering) provides a fundamental empirical and quantitative tool for basin analysis and paleoenvironmental reconstruction. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (a): K > Na (Potassium exceeds Sodium).

Sedimentary petrology investigates the physical, chemical, and biological processes governing the genesis, transport, deposition, and lithification of sedimentary rocks. Integrating grain-size distribution, primary structures, detrital mineralogy, and diagenetic cements allows geologists to reconstruct paleoenvironments, paleocurrents, and tectonic provenance across Earth history.

"During chemical weathering, plagioclase breaks down faster than K-feldspar, and Na remains in solution while K is fixed into illitic clays, making sediments rich in K relative to Na (K > Na)." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Explanatory Answers, p. 371-372); Boggs, S. (2009, Petrology of Sedimentary Rocks. Cambridge Univ. Press); Folk, R.L. (1980, Petrology of Sedimentary Rocks. Hemphill Publishing); Ausaf Sayeed (Objective Geology)

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: In an ideal Bouma turbidite sequence, which division is characterized by current-ripple and convolute lamination representing lower flow regime conditions?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (C)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Division Tc of the Bouma sequence represents lower flow regime bedform migration, characterized by small-scale climbing ripple, wavy, and convolute lamination.
  • Practice Challenge 2: According to Carl Ochsenius' Bar Theory, thick accumulations of marine evaporites develop when:
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: The Bar Theory explains thick evaporites via a shallow sill (bar) that permits continuous influx of fresh seawater to balance intense evaporation in a restricted lagoon.
