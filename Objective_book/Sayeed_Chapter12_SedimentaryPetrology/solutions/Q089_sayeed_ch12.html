Evaporite Petrology: Core Sedimentological Principles

Evaporite Petrology (Rann of Kutch Basin Type) provides a fundamental empirical and quantitative tool for basin analysis and paleoenvironmental reconstruction. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (b): Marine marginal evaporite basin / coastal salt marsh.

J. Usiglio (1849) experimentally established the invariant precipitation sequence during solar evaporation of marine brine. Calcium carbonate begins precipitating when seawater volume is reduced by half; gypsum precipitates at 19% volume; halite begins at 9.5% volume; and highly soluble potash/magnesium bitterns (sylvite KCl, carnallite KMgCl3·6H2O) crystallize only when <4% of original brine remains. Ochsenius' (1877) Bar Theory explains thick evaporite deposits via continuous marine replenishment across a shallow sill.

"The Rann of Kutch is inundated seasonally by Arabian Sea storm tides and southwest monsoonal seawater, which subsequently evaporates under arid conditions to form a vast marine evaporite crust." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Explanatory Answers, p. 371-372); Usiglio, J. (1849, Ann. Chim. Phys. 27: 92-107); Ochsenius, C. (1877, Die Bildung der Steinsalzlager. Halle); Sonnenfeld, P. (1984, Brines and Evaporites. Academic Press)

    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (C)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Division Tc of the Bouma sequence represents lower flow regime bedform migration, characterized by small-scale climbing ripple, wavy, and convolute lamination.
  • Practice Challenge 2: According to Carl Ochsenius' Bar Theory, thick accumulations of marine evaporites develop when:
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: The Bar Theory explains thick evaporites via a shallow sill (bar) that permits continuous influx of fresh seawater to balance intense evaporation in a restricted lagoon.
