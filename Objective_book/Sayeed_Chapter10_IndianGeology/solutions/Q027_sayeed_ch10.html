Vindhyan Supergroup: Core Stratigraphic Principles

Vindhyan Supergroup (Bhander Lithology) constitutes an essential component of Indian historical geology and mineral resource exploration. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (d): Partly arenaceous, partly calcareous, and argillaceous strata.

The Vindhyan Supergroup covers >100,000 km² in Central India, spanning Mesoproterozoic to Neoproterozoic time (~1.7 to 0.6 Ga). The Lower Vindhyan (Semri Group) represents shallow-marine carbonate-shale deposition with prolific stromatolites (Conophyton, Colonella) and Chopan porcellanites. The Upper Vindhyan (Kaimur, Rewa, Bhander) is dominated by mature quartz arenites, deltaic shales, and diamondiferous conglomerates (Panna), bounded on the northwest by the Great Boundary Fault.

"The Bhander Group comprises a complete mixed siliciclastic-carbonate sequence: Ganurgarh Shale, Bhander (Nagod) Limestone, Sirbu Shale, and Upper Bhander Sandstone." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Notes, p. 325-328); Auden, J.B. (1933, Vindhyan Sedimentation in the Son Valley); Soni et al. (1987); Chakraborty, C. (2006); Valdiya, K.S. (The Making of India)

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: Which of the following regional structural lineaments defines the northwestern tectonic boundary of the Vindhyan Basin in Rajasthan?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: The Great Boundary Fault (GBF) is an 800-km-long regional thrust fault in southeastern Rajasthan bringing older deformed pre-Vindhyan rocks over the flat-lying Upper Vindhyan Bhander Group.
  • Practice Challenge 2: What is the primary operational criterion distinguishing the Kurnool Group from the underlying Cuddapah Supergroup in Andhra Pradesh?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: The Neoproterozoic Kurnool Group rests with a sharp, regional angular unconformity upon both the deformed Cuddapah formations and the basement granites, initiating with basal diamondiferous Banganapalle conglomerates.
