Indian Lignite Deposits: Core Stratigraphic Principles

Indian Lignite Deposits (Stratigraphic Distribution) constitutes an essential component of Indian historical geology and mineral resource exploration. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (d): All of the above (1, 2 and 3).

The Vindhyan Supergroup covers >100,000 km² in Central India, spanning Mesoproterozoic to Neoproterozoic time (~1.7 to 0.6 Ga). The Lower Vindhyan (Semri Group) represents shallow-marine carbonate-shale deposition with prolific stromatolites (Conophyton, Colonella) and Chopan porcellanites. The Upper Vindhyan (Kaimur, Rewa, Bhander) is dominated by mature quartz arenites, deltaic shales, and diamondiferous conglomerates (Panna), bounded on the northwest by the Great Boundary Fault.

"Major Indian lignite reserves occur across multiple Cenozoic epochs: Paleocene-Eocene Palana Formation in Rajasthan, Mio-Pliocene Cuddalore Formation at Neyveli, and Plio-Pleistocene Karewa beds at Nichahom in Kashmir." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Notes, p. 325-328); Auden, J.B. (1933, Vindhyan Sedimentation in the Son Valley); Soni et al. (1987); Chakraborty, C. (2006); Valdiya, K.S. (The Making of India)

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: Why is the Talchir Formation of the Gondwana Supergroup regarded as an exceptional chronostratigraphic time-marker across the Indian Peninsula?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: The basal Talchir Boulder Bed represents a continent-wide continental tillite deposited by an extensive Permo-Carboniferous ice sheet, providing an unambiguous isochronous chronostratigraphic datum across all Indian Gondwana basins.
  • Practice Challenge 2: Which major tectonic boundary in the Himalaya separates the high-grade metamorphic Higher Himalayan Crystalline Complex (HHC) from the underlying sedimentary Lesser Himalaya?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (C)
    Mechanistic Rationale: The Main Central Thrust (MCT) is a north-dipping ductile shear zone along which the high-grade Proterozoic Vaikrita crystalline thrust sheet was emplaced southward over the low-grade metasedimentary rocks of the Lesser Himalaya.
