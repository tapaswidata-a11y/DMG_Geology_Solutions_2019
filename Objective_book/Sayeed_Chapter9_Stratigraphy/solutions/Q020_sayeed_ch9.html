History of Stratigraphy: Core Stratigraphic Principles

History of Stratigraphy (Origin of Biostratigraphy) constitutes an essential foundation of historical geology and stratigraphic correlation. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (b): S.S. Buckman.

Principles of Stratigraphy integrate lithic, biological, geochemical, and geophysical signals to reconstruct Earth history. Modern chronostratigraphy anchors global chronostratigraphic stage boundaries with Global Boundary Stratotype Sections and Points (GSSPs), defining physical geological time-rock standards.

"Sydney Savory Buckman (1893, 1902) systematically introduced biostratigraphic terminology, refining biozones and epiboles through his ammonite studies." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Notes, p. 296-297); Gradstein et al. Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: According to Walther's Law of Facies, which of the following statements is strictly correct?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Walther's Law states that only those facies that can be observed forming side-by-side in laterally adjacent environments can be superimposed in vertical conformable succession without the presence of a stratigraphic break or unconformity.
  • Practice Challenge 2: Why do altered volcanic ash layers (bentonite beds) serve as superior isochronous chronostratigraphic marker horizons compared to sandstone or limestone beds?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (A)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Explosive plinian volcanic eruptions disperse ash clouds into the upper atmosphere, settling synchronously within days across thousands of square kilometers across marine and continental basins, creating perfect isochronous chronostratigraphic time-markers.
