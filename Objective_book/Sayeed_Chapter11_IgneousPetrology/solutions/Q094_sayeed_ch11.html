Granite Petrogenesis: Core Petrological Mechanics

Granite Petrogenesis (Restite Model) represents a pivotal concept in magma genesis, differentiation, and classification. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (b): Refractory solid residue remaining un-melted at the source region during partial melting.

Igneous petrology deciphers the thermodynamic, chemical, and physical principles that control magma generation in Earth's mantle and crust, melt ascent through lithospheric pathways, and magma crystallization in plutonic, hypabyssal, and volcanic environments. Modern petrology couples multi-component phase equilibria with high-precision trace element and radiogenic isotope geochemistry to constrain lithospheric geodynamics.

"White and Chappell's restite model defines restite as un-melted refractory source minerals (biotite, sillimanite, garnet) entrained within ascending granitic melts." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Notes, p. 344-347); Winter, J.D. (2014, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology. Pearson); Philpotts & Ague (2009); Best, M.G. (2003); Ausaf Sayeed (Objective Geology)

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: A granite pluton contains primary muscovite, cordierite, and almandine garnet, and yields an A/CNK ratio of 1.25. What is its genetic classification?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: An A/CNK ratio > 1.1 combined with diagnostic aluminous minerals (muscovite, cordierite, almandine) definitively identifies an S-type granite generated by anatexis of pelitic metasedimentary crust.
  • Practice Challenge 2: Which suite of geochemical characteristics is uniquely diagnostic of anorogenic A-type granites?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: A-type granites are characterized by high Ga/Al ratios, elevated FeO*/MgO, high halogen contents (F, Cl), and substantial enrichments in High Field Strength Elements (HFSE: Zr, Nb, Y, REE).
