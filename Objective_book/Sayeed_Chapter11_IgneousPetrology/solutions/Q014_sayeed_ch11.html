Granite Petrology: Core Petrological Mechanics

Granite Petrology (A-Type Granites) represents a pivotal concept in magma genesis, differentiation, and classification. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (b): (i), (ii) and (iii) are correct.

Chappell and White (1974) introduced the genetic I- and S-type granite classification for the Lachlan Fold Belt of Australia. I-type granites derive from partial melting of mantle-derived igneous or meta-igneous underplates (metaluminous, hornblende/sphene-bearing). S-type granites originate by anatexis of supracrustal pelitic or psammitic sedimentary rocks (strongly peraluminous, muscovite/biotite/cordierite/almandine-bearing). Loiselle and Wones (1979) subsequently defined A-type granites as anorogenic, anhydrous, and alkaline.

"Coined by Loiselle and Wones (1979), A-type granites are anhydrous, anorogenic, alkaline suites enriched in Fe/Mg, halogens, Zr, Ga, and Nb with low CaO, Al2O3, and Sr." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Notes, p. 344-347); Chappell, B.W. & White, A.J.R. Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: A granite pluton contains primary muscovite, cordierite, and almandine garnet, and yields an A/CNK ratio of 1.25. What is its genetic classification?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: An A/CNK ratio > 1.1 combined with diagnostic aluminous minerals (muscovite, cordierite, almandine) definitively identifies an S-type granite generated by anatexis of pelitic metasedimentary crust.
  • Practice Challenge 2: Which suite of geochemical characteristics is uniquely diagnostic of anorogenic A-type granites?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: A-type granites are characterized by high Ga/Al ratios, elevated FeO*/MgO, high halogen contents (F, Cl), and substantial enrichments in High Field Strength Elements (HFSE: Zr, Nb, Y, REE).
