Layered Intrusions: Core Petrological Mechanics

Layered Intrusions (Skaergaard Differentiation) represents a pivotal concept in magma genesis, differentiation, and classification. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (b): Extreme fractional crystallization of a single pulse of basaltic magma in a closed chamber.

Layered mafic-ultramafic intrusions (e.g., Skaergaard in East Greenland studied by Wager and Brown; Bushveld Complex in South Africa; Stillwater in Montana) demonstrate closed-system fractional crystallization and gravitational crystal settling governed by Stokes' law. Cryptic layering reflects continuous Fe-enrichment in olivine and pyroxenes alongside Na-enrichment in plagioclase as the magma chamber differentiates from base to roof.

"Skaergaard crystallized as a closed-system basaltic magma chamber, generating a textbook succession from Lower Zone (olivine + plagioclase) to Sandwich Horizon." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Notes, p. 344-347); Wager, L.R. & Brown, G.M. (1967, Layered Igneous Rocks. W.H. Freeman & Co.); Irvine, T.N. (1982, Terminology for layered intrusions. J. Petrol. 23: 127-162)

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: In the Forsterite-Silica binary phase system at 1 atm, enstatite melts incongruently at 1557°C into:
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Enstatite (Mg2Si2O6) breaks down peritectically at 1557°C to produce solid forsterite (Mg2SiO4) plus a silica-rich liquid containing ~65 wt% SiO2.
  • Practice Challenge 2: Which of the following textures is specifically diagnostic of komatiitic ultramafic lava flows?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Spinifex texture—comprising long, intersecting skeletal blades of olivine and pyroxene—forms exclusively by rapid thermal quenching of exceptionally high-temperature (>1600°C) ultramafic komatiite flows.
