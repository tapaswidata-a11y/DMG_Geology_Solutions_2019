CIPW Norm: Core Petrological Mechanics

CIPW Norm (Acmite Molecule) represents a pivotal concept in magma genesis, differentiation, and classification. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (c): Acmite (aegirine).

The CIPW normative calculation (Cross, Iddings, Pirsson, Washington, 1902) converts whole-rock chemical analyses into an idealized anhydrous mineral assemblage based on rigorous stoichiometric rules and affinities. Elements are allocated sequentially: P to apatite, S to pyrite, Ti to ilmenite/sphene, then alkalis and alumina to feldspars and feldspathoids. A key thermodynamic boundary is the critical plane of silica saturation: quartz and nepheline are mutually exclusive normative phases.

"Acmite (NaFe3+Si2O6) has the molar formula Na2O·Fe2O3·4SiO2, appearing in the CIPW norm of peralkaline igneous rocks." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Notes, p. 344-347); Cross, W., Iddings, J.P., Pirsson, L.V., Washington, H.S. (1902, J. Geol. 10: 555-690); Cox, K.G., Bell, J.D., Pankhurst, R.J. (1979, The Interpretation of Igneous Rocks)

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: A rhyolite chemical analysis yields normative quartz = 32%, orthoclase = 28%, albite = 26%, and nepheline = 0%. According to Thornton and Tuttle (1960), what is its Differentiation Index (DI)?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Thornton-Tuttle DI equals the arithmetic sum of the normative salic components: DI = Qz + Or + Ab + Ne + Ks + Lc = 32 + 28 + 26 + 0 = 86.
  • Practice Challenge 2: According to Kuno's Solidification Index (SI), which of the following magmas would exhibit the highest SI value?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (C)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Kuno's SI = 100 * MgO / (MgO + FeO + Fe2O3 + Na2O + K2O). Primitive high-Mg melts such as picritic basalts contain abundant MgO (12-20 wt%), yielding the highest SI values (~40-48), whereas evolved silicic magmas have SI < 10.
