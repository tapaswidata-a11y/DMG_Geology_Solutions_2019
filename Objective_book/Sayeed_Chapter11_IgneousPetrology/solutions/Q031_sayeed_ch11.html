Plutonic Bodies: Core Petrological Mechanics

Plutonic Bodies (Batholith Definition) represents a pivotal concept in magma genesis, differentiation, and classification. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (c): Batholith.

Igneous petrology deciphers the thermodynamic, chemical, and physical principles that control magma generation in Earth's mantle and crust, melt ascent through lithospheric pathways, and magma crystallization in plutonic, hypabyssal, and volcanic environments. Modern petrology couples multi-component phase equilibria with high-precision trace element and radiogenic isotope geochemistry to constrain lithospheric geodynamics.

"By international definition, a batholith is an immense discordant plutonic body covering >100 km²; discordant bodies smaller than 100 km² are termed stocks." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Notes, p. 344-347); Winter, J.D. (2014, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology. Pearson); Philpotts & Ague (2009); Best, M.G. (2003); Ausaf Sayeed (Objective Geology)

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: In the Forsterite-Silica binary phase system at 1 atm, enstatite melts incongruently at 1557°C into:
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Enstatite (Mg2Si2O6) breaks down peritectically at 1557°C to produce solid forsterite (Mg2SiO4) plus a silica-rich liquid containing ~65 wt% SiO2.
  • Practice Challenge 2: Which of the following textures is specifically diagnostic of komatiitic ultramafic lava flows?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Spinifex texture—comprising long, intersecting skeletal blades of olivine and pyroxene—forms exclusively by rapid thermal quenching of exceptionally high-temperature (>1600°C) ultramafic komatiite flows.
