Petroleum Geology: Core Economic Geology Principles

Petroleum Geology (Structural Traps in Indian Basins) provides a fundamental quantitative and exploration foundation for mineral and energy resource evaluation. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (d): A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3.

Petroleum accumulations require the spatial-temporal co-occurrence of five fundamental elements: an organically rich, thermally mature source rock (Type I/II/III kerogen in the oil window, 60-120°C); a porous and permeable reservoir rock (sandstone or vuggy limestone); a regional impermeable cap rock/seal (evaporite or thick shale); a structural, stratigraphic, or combination trapping geometry; and favorable migration pathways connecting source kitchen to trap prior to seal breach. In India, Tertiary basins (Assam Shelf, Cambay Rift, Mumbai Offshore, Krishna-Godavari, Cauvery, Barmer) host the primary commercial plays.

"Bombay High is a giant northwest-southeast trending elongated structural limestone dome (2); Cambay Basin features doubly plunging fault-assisted anticlines (1); Assam Shelf contains tilted fault-blocks and drape folds (4); Tripura-Cachar features steep, folded asymmetrical frontal fold-belt anticlines (3)." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Explanatory Answers, p. 417-418); Levorsen, A.I. (1967, Geology of Petroleum. Freeman); Hunt, J.M. (1996, Petroleum Geochemistry and Geology. Freeman); DGH India Annual Hydrocarbon Reports

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: Which of the following organic matter kerogen types is predominantly oil-prone and formed primarily from lacustrine algal and planktonic precursors?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (A)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Type I kerogen has the highest atomic H/C ratio (>1.5) and lowest O/C ratio, derived from algal lipids and highly oil-prone upon thermal maturation in the oil window.
  • Practice Challenge 2: The primary carbonate reservoir horizon that accounts for over 80% of hydrocarbon reserves in the offshore Bombay High field is designated as:
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (C)
    Mechanistic Rationale: The Early Miocene L-III biomicrite carbonate sequence is the principal regional reservoir of Bombay High, characterized by high primary and vuggy solution porosity.
