Hydrothermal Alteration: Core Economic Geology Principles

Hydrothermal Alteration (Phyllic Alteration Definition) provides a fundamental quantitative and exploration foundation for mineral and energy resource evaluation. In Dr. Ausaf Sayeed's Objective Geology, the officially verified answer is firmly established as Option (d): Phyllic (sericitic) alteration.

Waldemar Lindgren's genetic classification establishes a quantitative continuum of hydrothermal ore deposition governed by decreasing temperature and lithostatic/hydrostatic confining pressure away from igneous or metamorphic heat engines. Fluid inclusions and stable isotope thermometry demonstrate that as ascending metalliferous aqueous solutions depressurize and cool, phase separation (boiling), wall-rock sulfidation, and fluid mixing trigger progressive metal precipitation along predictable paragenetic vectors.

"Phyllic (sericitic) alteration forms at 250-350°C under moderate acid conditions, converting feldspars to sericite [KAl3Si3O10(OH)2] + quartz + pyrite." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed (Official Key & Explanatory Answers, p. 417-418); Lindgren, W. (1913, 1933, Mineral Deposits. McGraw-Hill); Graton, L.C. (1933, The hydrothermal depth-zones. Ore Deposits of the Western States); Buddington, A.F. (1935, Econ. Geol. 30: 205-222)
Governing Economic / Thermodynamic Relationship
$$\text{Lindgren (1913, 1933): } \begin{cases} \text{Hypothermal: } T = 300\text{--}600^\circ\text{C}, & P = 1\text{--}3\text{ kbar (deep intrusive)} \\ \text{Mesothermal: } T = 200\text{--}300^\circ\text{C}, & P = 0.5\text{--}1.5\text{ kbar (intermediate depth)} \\ \text{Epithermal: } T = 50\text{--}200^\circ\text{C}, & P \le 100\text{ bar (shallow sub-volcanic)} \\ \text{Telethermal: } T < 100^\circ\text{C}, & \text{far-travelled basinal brines} \\ \text{Xenothermal: } T \approx 300\text{--}500^\circ\text{C}, & P \text{ low (telescoped volcanic)} \end{cases}$$

Advanced Pedagogical Practice Challenges
  • Practice Challenge 1: Which of the following hydrothermal depth-zones is characterized by mineralization deposited by far-traveled, spent solutions at low temperatures in rocks displaying minimal wall-rock alteration?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (C)
    Mechanistic Rationale: L.C. Graton (1933) established the 'telethermal' zone for low-temperature hydrothermal deposits (e.g., MVT lead-zinc) formed far from their igneous heat sources with negligible host-rock alteration.
  • Practice Challenge 2: What physical parameter uniquely distinguishes 'Xenothermal' mineral deposits from standard hypothermal deposits?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Option (B)
    Mechanistic Rationale: A.F. Buddington defined xenothermal deposits as high-temperature ore systems emplaced at shallow depths (low pressure), causing rapid fluid boiling and severe telescoping of mineral assemblages.
