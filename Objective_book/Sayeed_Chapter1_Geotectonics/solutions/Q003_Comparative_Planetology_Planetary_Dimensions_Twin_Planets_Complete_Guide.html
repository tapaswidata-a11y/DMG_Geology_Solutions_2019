Comparative Planetology: Planetary Dimensions & Twin Planets: Fundamental Principles & Mechanics

In planetary science and comparative planetology, Earth and Venus are universally designated as 'twin planets' or sister planets because they exhibit almost identical physical bulk dimensions, mean radii, surface areas, volumes, and bulk densities.

A rigorous comparative review of the geodetic parameters reveals the remarkable dimensional symmetry between the two terrestrial worlds:

  • Equatorial Radius: Earth's equatorial radius is $6,378.1 ext{ km}$, while Venus has an equatorial radius of $6,051.8 ext{ km}$ ($94.9\%$ of Earth's size).
  • Mean Bulk Density: Earth's bulk density is $5.515 ext{ g/cm}^3$, whereas Venus has a bulk density of $5.243 ext{ g/cm}^3$ ($95.1\%$ of Earth's density).
  • Planetary Mass: Venus's mass ($4.867 imes 10^{24} ext{ kg}$) is approximately $81.5\%$ of Earth's mass ($5.972 imes 10^{24} ext{ kg}$).
  • Surface Gravity: Surface gravitational acceleration on Venus is $8.87 ext{ m/s}^2$ ($90.5\%$ of Earth's $9.81 ext{ m/s}^2$).

In contrast, Mars is a much smaller body with a radius of only $3,389.5 ext{ km}$ ($53\%$ of Earth) and barely $10.7\%$ of Earth's mass. Mercury is even smaller ($R = 2,439.7 ext{ km}$, $38\%$ of Earth). Thus, 'size-bearing' (matching dimensional proportions) strictly unites the Earth-Venus planetary pair.

"Radius in Km: Venus = 6,052 km, Earth = 6,371 km. Earth and Venus are near-twins in terms of diameter, mass, and average density, whereas Mars is roughly half their diameter." — Dr. Ausaf Sayeed, Explanatory Note *3, p. 69; William Lowrie, Fundamentals of Geophysics, Ch. 1, p. 4; G.B. Mahapatra, p. 12
Governing Physical / Mathematical Relationship
$$\frac{R_{\text{Venus}}}{R_{\text{Earth}}} = \frac{6052\text{ km}}{6371\text{ km}} \approx 0.95, \quad \frac{\rho_{\text{Venus}}}{\rho_{\text{Earth}}} = \frac{5.24\text{ g/cm}^3}{5.52\text{ g/cm}^3} \approx 0.95$$

(A) Earth-Mars Incorrect Distractor
Why Option (A) is incorrect: Mars ($R = 3,390 ext{ km}$) has barely half the radius and one-tenth the mass of Earth ($R = 6,371 ext{ km}$), exhibiting starkly disparate planetary scaling.
(B) Earth-Venus Correct Verified Option
Why Option (B) is correct: Earth-Venus is the verified answer. Venus has a radius of $6,052 ext{ km}$ compared to Earth's $6,371 ext{ km}$ (a dimensional difference under $5\%$), making them the premier 'size-bearing' planetary pair in the Solar System.
(C) Mars-Venus Incorrect Distractor
Why Option (C) is incorrect: Mars and Venus differ drastically in size: Venus is $1.78 imes$ larger in diameter and $7.6 imes$ more massive than Mars.
(D) Venus-Mercury Incorrect Distractor
Why Option (D) is incorrect: Mercury is the smallest terrestrial planet ($R = 2,440 ext{ km}$), less than half the diameter of Venus ($R = 6,052 ext{ km}$).

Key Examination Takeaway

Earth and Venus possess virtually identical bulk physical dimensions (~95% match in radius and density), earning them the designation of planetary twins.

High-Yield Practice Problems with Worked Solutions
  • Practice Problem 1: Despite their near-identical size, what primary atmospheric factor makes Venus hostile to life?
    ✔ Verified Solution: A 92-bar runaway greenhouse atmosphere composed of 96.5% CO2 with sulfuric acid clouds
    Mechanistic Rationale: Venus's extreme greenhouse effect produces a uniform surface temperature of ~465°C, higher than Mercury's daytime temperature.
  • Practice Problem 2: Why does Venus lack a core-dynamo magnetic field despite having a metallic iron core similar in size to Earth's?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Lack of plate tectonics and slow core convection (along with an extremely slow 243-day retrograde rotation)
    Mechanistic Rationale: Without plate-driven mantle cooling, thermal convection in Venus's liquid core is sluggish, preventing the geodynamo from initiating.
