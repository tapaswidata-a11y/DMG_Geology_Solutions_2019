DGMS 2019 Geology • Question 41

Feldspar Petrology: Antiperthite vs. Perthite Exsolution Textures

Topic: Igneous & Metamorphic Petrology | Primary Reference: John D. Winter (Petrology, 2nd ed., Ch. 6)

📝 Question Statement

Q.41. The exsolution texture in which the host mineral is a plagioclase feldspar and the lamellae are of KAlSi₃O₈ composition is known as

(a) Perthite
(b) Antiperthite
(c) Myrmekite
(d) Inverted pigeonite
a Perthite
b Antiperthite (Correct Answer)
c Myrmekite
d Inverted pigeonite
✓ Correct Answer: (b) Antiperthite [When plagioclase is the host grain and contains exsolution blebs/lamellae of K-feldspar (KAlSi₃O₈), the texture is antiperthite; perthite is the reverse].

🖼️ Textbook Evidence: John D. Winter (Chapter 6)

Figure 6.16: Subsolidus Solvus Phase Relations in the Alkali Feldspar System (John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, 2nd ed., p. 123). As a homogeneous high-temperature feldspar cools below the solvus, difference in ionic radii between $\text{K}^+$ (1.59 Å) and $\text{Na}^+$ (1.24 Å) forces the crystal to unmix into two phases. When the host is K-rich and the exsolved blebs are Na-rich plagioclase, it is termed perthite. When the bulk composition is Na-rich and the host is plagioclase with exsolved K-feldspar lamellae, it is termed antiperthite.
"When the bulk composition is K-rich, lamellae of Na-rich feldspar form in a K-rich host, and the texture is called perthite. When the bulk composition is Na-rich (as in plagioclase), lamellae of K-rich feldspar ($\text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8$) form in a Na-rich/plagioclase host, and the texture is called antiperthite. Exsolution is less common in plagioclase due to the slower coupled substitution of $\text{Ca-Al}$ for $\text{Na-Si}$, making antiperthite especially characteristic of high-temperature granulites and charnockites."
— John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, Chapter 6, p. 123

🔍 Comprehensive Option Analysis

(b) Antiperthite [CORRECT]

Definition & Petrogenesis: Antiperthite is an exsolution intergrowth where:

  • Host Mineral: Plagioclase feldspar (typically oligoclase or andesine, $\text{NaAlSi}_3\text{O}_8 - \text{CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8$).
  • Exsolution Lamellae / Blebs: Potassium feldspar ($\text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8$, orthoclase or microcline).
  • Geological Occurrence: Very common in high-grade regional metamorphic rocks of the granulite facies, charnockitic suites, anorthosites, and alkaline plutonic rocks. At high temperatures (750°–900°C), plagioclase can dissolve several percent of the orthoclase molecule; upon slow cooling, the excess $\text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8$ exsolves as blebs, spindles, or oriented lamellae parallel to $(010)$ or $(001)$.

(a) Perthite [INCORRECT / REVERSE TEXTURE]

Perthite is the opposite: the host is K-feldspar (orthoclase or microcline) and the exsolved lamellae are albite/plagioclase ($\text{NaAlSi}_3\text{O}_8$). It is ubiquitous in granites, syenites, and granitic pegmatites.

(c) Myrmekite [INCORRECT]

Myrmekite is a vermicular (worm-like) intergrowth of quartz enclosed within sodic plagioclase. It is a replacement/metasomatic texture formed along margins where plagioclase invades and replaces adjacent alkali feldspar, rather than an exsolution texture between two feldspar solid solutions.

(d) Inverted pigeonite [INCORRECT]

Inverted pigeonite is a pyroxene texture, not a feldspar texture. High-temperature monoclinic low-Ca clinopyroxene (pigeonite) inverts upon slow subsolidus cooling to orthorhombic orthopyroxene (hypersthene/enstatite) with thick exsolution lamellae of augite (clinopyroxene) oriented along $\{001\}$.

💡 Key Takeaway & Exam Strategy

Memory Rule for Feldspar Exsolution:
  • Perthite: K-feldspar host + Albite lamellae (Think: "P-erthite starts with Potash host").
  • Antiperthite: Plagioclase host + Potash lamellae (The "Anti" or opposite configuration).
  • Host is plagioclase $\rightarrow$ Antiperthite!
