1. Eclogite Facies Petrogenesis & The Destruction of Plagioclase

The eclogite facies represents the pinnacle of high-pressure crustal and mantle metamorphism ($P > 1.2\text{–}1.5\text{ GPa}$, equivalent to depths greater than 40–50 km, and temperatures from 500°C to over 900°C). Eclogites are classically defined as bimineralic metabasic rocks consisting essentially of omphacite (a jadeite-diopside solid solution sodium-calcium clinopyroxene) and pyrope-almandine-grossular garnet, without plagioclase!

"Plagioclase is unstable at the high pressures of the eclogite facies, and its breakdown reactions mark the transition from amphibolite or granulite facies to eclogite facies... Ultimately, anorthite breaks down in the absence of other mineral phases via the reaction: 3 Anorthite = Grossular + 2 Kyanite + Quartz." — John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology (2nd Ed., Chapter 25, p. 579)
The Fundamental High-Pressure Breakdown Reactions of Plagioclase
Because plagioclase feldspar contains both albite ($NaAlSi_3O_8$) and anorthite ($CaAl_2Si_2O_8$), high lithostatic pressure forces both components to collapse into denser, octahedral-aluminum phases: Albite Breakdown:      NaAlSi₃O₈ = NaAlSi₂O₆ (Jadeite) + SiO₂ (Quartz) Anorthite Breakdown:   3 CaAl₂Si₂O₈ = Ca₃Al₂Si₃O₁₂ (Grossular) + 2 Al₂SiO₅ (Kyanite) + SiO₂ (Quartz)

Both reactions have positive $dP/dT$ Clapeyron slopes, with the dense product assemblages (lower molar volume) favored at elevated pressures.

2. Mathematical & Stoichiometric Proof of the Reaction

The reaction given in the exam question is: $$\mathbf{3\text{ Anorthite} = \text{Grossular} + 2\text{ Kyanite} + \text{Quartz}}$$

Let us perform a rigorous element-by-element atomic inventory to demonstrate that grossular must have the stoichiometric formula $\text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$:

Element Reactant: 3 × Anorthite ($3 \times \text{CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8$) Product: 2 × Kyanite ($2 \times \text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_5$) Product: 1 × Quartz ($1 \times \text{SiO}_2$) Residual Required for Grossular Grossular Atom Count ($\text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$)
Calcium (Ca) $3 \times 1 = \mathbf{3}$ 0 0 $3 - 0 = \mathbf{3}$ Ca₃
Aluminum (Al) $3 \times 2 = \mathbf{6}$ $2 \times 2 = 4$ 0 $6 - 4 = \mathbf{2}$ Al₂
Silicon (Si) $3 \times 2 = \mathbf{6}$ $2 \times 1 = 2$ $1 \times 1 = 1$ $6 - (2+1) = \mathbf{3}$ Si₃
Oxygen (O) $3 \times 8 = \mathbf{24}$ $2 \times 5 = 10$ $1 \times 2 = 2$ $24 - (10+2) = \mathbf{12}$ O₁₂

The stoichiometric subtraction yields precisely: $\text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$.

3. Crystal Chemistry of the Garnet Group (Dexter Perkins, Chapter 13)

Garnets are orthosilicates (nesosilicates) belonging to the isometric (cubic) crystal system (space group $Ia\bar{3}d$, point group $4/m\bar{3}2/m$). Their structural formula is written as: $$X_3 Y_2 (\text{SiO}_4)_3 \quad \text{or} \quad X_3 Y_2 \text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$$ where:

  • $X$-site: Divalent cations in 8-fold (dodecahedral) coordination: $\text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Fe}^{2+}, \text{Mn}^{2+}$.
  • $Y$-site: Trivalent cations in 6-fold (octahedral) coordination: $\text{Al}^{3+}, \text{Fe}^{3+}, \text{Cr}^{3+}$.
  • Tetrahedral site: $\text{Si}^{4+}$ in independent $[\text{SiO}_4]^{4-}$ tetrahedra.
"Mineralogists divide garnets into two series: the pyralspites (pyrope, almandine, spessartine) and the ugrandites (uvarovite, grossular, andradite)... Grossular is the Al end member of the ugrandite series, Ca₃Al₂Si₃O₁₂. Like all garnets, grossular forms equant dodecahedral or trapezohedral crystals and has no cleavage." — Dexter Perkins, Mineralogy (3rd Ed., Chapter 13, pp. 410, 412)

Official Exam Question Statement & In-Depth Critical Analysis
Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) 2019 · Junior Scientific Officer Exam · Question 9
Question Statement
Q.9: 3 Anorthite = Grossular + 2 Kyanite + Quartz is a reaction which marks the eclogite facies in metabasic rocks. What will be the composition of grossular in the reaction given above?
(a) Ca₃Al₂Si₃O₁₂
(b) CaSiO₃
(c) (Ca, Mg)Si₂O₆
(d) CaCO₃
✓
Correct Answer: (a) Ca₃Al₂Si₃O₁₂
Grossular is the calcium-aluminum end-member of the ugrandite garnet series with the exact stoichiometric formula Ca₃Al₂Si₃O₁₂.
Exhaustive Option-by-Option Critical Analysis
(a) Ca₃Al₂Si₃O₁₂ CORRECT
Why it is uniquely correct: In mineralogy and petrology (Perkins, 2014, p. 412; Winter, 2010, p. 579), grossular is universally established as the calcic aluminous end-member garnet with chemical formula $\text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$. As proven by the mass balance of $3\text{ Anorthite} = 3\text{ CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8 \rightarrow \text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12} + 2\text{ Al}_2\text{SiO}_5 + \text{SiO}_2$, this formula satisfies stoichiometry exactly.
(b) CaSiO₃ INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: $\text{CaSiO}_3$ is the chemical formula of wollastonite, a calcium inosilicate (pyroxenoid) typical of thermally metamorphosed limestones. Wollastonite lacks aluminum entirely and is structurally and chemically distinct from garnet.
(c) (Ca, Mg)Si₂O₆ INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: $(\text{Ca, Mg})\text{Si}_2\text{O}_6$ represents diopside (specifically $\text{CaMgSi}_2\text{O}_6$), a monoclinic clinopyroxene. Diopside contains magnesium (absent in pure anorthite) and has a single-chain inosilicate crystal structure with a $(\text{Si}:\text{O})$ ratio of $1:3$, whereas garnet is an orthosilicate with an isolated $(\text{SiO}_4)$ ratio of $1:4$.
(d) CaCO₃ INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: $\text{CaCO}_3$ is the formula for the anhydrous carbonate minerals calcite and aragonite. Carbonates contain carbon and lack silicon and aluminum altogether.

8 High-Yield Practice Questions on Eclogite Facies & Garnet Mineralogy
1. In the structural formula of garnet $X_3 Y_2 \text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$, what are the coordination numbers of the $X$, $Y$, and $\text{Si}$ sites?
(a) 4, 6, 8    (b) 8, 6, 4    (c) 6, 6, 6    (d) 12, 8, 4
Answer: (b) 8, 6, 4 — The divalent $X$-site is in 8-fold (dodecahedral) coordination, the trivalent $Y$-site is in 6-fold (octahedral) coordination, and $Si$ is in 4-fold (tetrahedral) coordination.
2. Which clinopyroxene is the diagnostic phase defining the eclogite facies in metabasites?
(a) Diopside    (b) Hedenbergite    (c) Omphacite    (d) Aegirine
Answer: (c) Omphacite — Omphacite is an intermediate solid solution between augite/diopside and jadeite, characteristic of eclogites.
3. The albite breakdown reaction marking the onset of high-pressure blueschist/eclogite conditions is:
(a) Albite = Nepheline + Quartz
(b) Albite = Jadeite + Quartz
(c) Albite + Calcite = Anorthite + CO₂
(d) Albite = Glaucophane + H₂O
Answer: (b) Albite = Jadeite + Quartz — This classic univariant reaction represents the breakdown of sodic plagioclase at high pressure.
4. The green color of the ugrandite garnet uvarovite is caused by the presence of:
(a) Titanium    (b) Chromium ($\text{Cr}^{3+}$) in the octahedral site    (c) Nickel    (d) Copper
Answer: (b) Chromium — Uvarovite has the formula $\text{Ca}_3\text{Cr}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$, imparting a vibrant emerald-green color.
5. Coesite and microdiamonds found as inclusions in eclogitic garnets provide proof of:
(a) Very low temperature    (b) Ultra-high pressure (UHP) metamorphism    (c) Contact aureole baking    (d) Hydrothermal alteration
Answer: (b) Ultra-high pressure (UHP) metamorphism — Coesite requires $P > 2.5\text{–}3.0\text{ GPa}$ (depths $>90\text{ km}$), proving subduction of continental/oceanic crust into the mantle.
6. In garnet nomenclature, the name "ugrandite" is a portmanteau derived from:
(a) Ural, Granite, Diorite
(b) Uvarovite, Grossular, Andradite
(c) Ultramafic, Granulite, Diopside
(d) Underground Anorthite Diopside
Answer: (b) Uvarovite, Grossular, Andradite — The three calcium-bearing end-members form the ugrandite series.
7. In the reaction $3\text{ Anorthite} = \text{Grossular} + 2\text{ Kyanite} + \text{Quartz}$, why does volume decrease ($-\Delta V$)?
(a) Garnet and kyanite have much denser, closely packed atomic structures with higher coordination numbers than plagioclase
(b) Gas is liberated during the reaction
(c) Quartz contracts under heat
(d) Aluminum evaporates
Answer: (a) — High pressure favors dense phases where Al enters 6-fold coordination (kyanite and garnet) rather than 4-fold coordination (anorthite).
8. What is the composition of the garnet andradite?
(a) $\text{Ca}_3\text{Fe}_2^{3+}\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$    (b) $\text{Fe}_3^{2+}\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$    (c) $\text{Mg}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$    (d) $\text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$
Answer: (a) $\text{Ca}_3\text{Fe}_2^{3+}\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$ — Andradite is the calcium-ferric iron end-member of the ugrandite series.
