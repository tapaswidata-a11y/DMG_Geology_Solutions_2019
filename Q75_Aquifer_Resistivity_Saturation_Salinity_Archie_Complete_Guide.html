Question 75: Electrical Resistivity of Aquifers: Saturation & Salinity Controls (Archie's Law)

Geoelectrical Principles, Pore-Fluid Conduction Mechanisms, and Inverse Physical Relationships
Official Examination Question
Consider the following statements regarding an aquifer composed of unconsolidated materials:
1. The resistivity increases with the degree of saturation.
2. The resistivity increases with increase in salinity of groundwater.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only
(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2 [CORRECT]
Verified Answer Key

Correct Option: (d) Neither 1 nor 2 — Both statements assert the exact opposite of fundamental geoelectrical physics. As demonstrated by Archie's Law:
(1) Electrical resistivity decreases as the degree of water saturation increases (because conducting water displaces insulating air).
(2) Electrical resistivity decreases as groundwater salinity increases (because higher ion concentration dramatically increases electrolytic conductivity).
Therefore, both Statement 1 and Statement 2 are false.

Figure 75.1 | Archie's Law Controls on Aquifer Bulk Electrical Resistivity: (a) Resistivity vs. Water Saturation ($S_w$): As pore water saturation increases from dry (air-filled) to 100% saturation, bulk resistivity drops by over two orders of magnitude ($\rho \propto S_w^{-2}$). (b) Resistivity vs. Groundwater Salinity: Dissolved salts supply charge carriers ($\mathrm{Na^+, Cl^-}$); as salinity increases from freshwater (~0.3 g/L) to seawater intrusion (~35 g/L), formation resistivity plummets from ~100 $\Omega\cdot\text{m}$ down to <1 $\Omega\cdot\text{m}$.

1. Geoelectrical Conduction Mechanisms in Aquifers

In unconsolidated sedimentary formations (gravel, sand, silt), common silicate minerals (quartz, feldspar) are electrical insulators with intrinsic mineral resistivities exceeding $10^{12}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$.

Consequently, electrical current in an aquifer flows almost entirely by electrolytic conduction through the interconnected pore fluid. Bulk electrical resistivity is governed by Archie's Law (1942):

$\rho = a \cdot \rho_w \cdot \phi^{-m} \cdot S_w^{-n}$

where:

  • $\rho$ is the bulk electrical resistivity of the formation ($\Omega\cdot\text{m}$)
  • $\rho_w$ is the electrical resistivity of the pore water ($\Omega\cdot\text{m}$)
  • $\phi$ is fractional total porosity
  • $S_w$ is the fractional water saturation ($0 \le S_w \le 1$)
  • $a, m, n$ are empirical constants ($a \approx 1$, cementation exponent $m \approx 1.3\text{--}2.0$, saturation exponent $n \approx 2$).

2. Statement-by-Statement Rigorous Scientific Refutation

Statement 1: "Resistivity increases with the degree of saturation" — INCORRECT

From Archie's empirical equation:

$\rho \propto \frac{1}{S_w^n} \approx \frac{1}{S_w^2}$

  • In dry, unsaturated sands above the water table (vadose zone), pore spaces are filled with air, which is an electrical insulator ($\rho_{\text{air}} \rightarrow \infty$). Bulk resistivity is very high ($1,000\text{ to }10,000\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$).
  • As water saturation ($S_w$) increases, continuous films and channels of conducting electrolyte connect the pore network.
  • When the aquifer reaches 100% saturation below the water table, electrical resistivity reaches its minimum value ($50\text{ to }150\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$ for fresh sand).
  • Therefore, resistivity decreases (not increases) as the degree of saturation rises.

Statement 2: "Resistivity increases with increase in salinity" — INCORRECT

Pore fluid resistivity ($\rho_w$) is inversely proportional to its electrical conductivity ($\sigma_w = 1 / \rho_w$), which is directly proportional to dissolved ion concentration (salinity / TDS):

$\rho_w \approx \frac{1}{\sum c_i \cdot z_i \cdot u_i} \propto \frac{1}{\text{Salinity}}$

  • In freshwater aquifers (low dissolved solids, $\text{TDS} < 500\ \text{mg/L}$), pore water has low conductivity and high resistivity ($\rho_w \approx 20\text{ to }100\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$). The formation displays high bulk resistivity ($\sim 100\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$).
  • In saline aquifers or zones of coastal saltwater intrusion ($\text{TDS} > 10,000\ \text{mg/L}$ to $35,000\ \text{mg/L}$), abundant free ions ($\mathrm{Na^+, Cl^-}$) conduct electricity with minimal resistance ($\rho_w < 0.2\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$). The formation bulk resistivity drops drastically to $ < 5\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$.
  • In electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) and vertical electrical sounding (VES), saline aquifers appear as profound low-resistivity (high-conductivity) anomalies!
  • Therefore, resistivity decreases (not increases) as salinity rises.

3. Summary Table: Physical Controls on Aquifer Electrical Resistivity

Aquifer Property Change in Property Effect on Bulk Resistivity ($\rho$) Physical Mechanism
Water Saturation ($S_w$) Increases ($0 \rightarrow 100\%$) DECREASES ($\rho \propto S_w^{-2}$) Conductive electrolyte displaces insulating pore air; expands cross-sectional conduction area.
Groundwater Salinity (TDS) Increases ($0.1 \rightarrow 35\text{ g/L}$) DECREASES ($\rho \propto 1/\text{TDS}$) Higher concentration of dissociated ionic charge carriers lowers pore fluid resistivity ($\rho_w$).
Porosity ($\phi$) Increases DECREASES ($\rho \propto \phi^{-m}$) More volume available for conducting pore fluid.
Clay Content Increases DECREASES Surface cation exchange capacity (CEC) provides surface electrical conduction.
Temperature ($T$) Increases DECREASES Viscosity of pore water decreases, increasing ionic mobility by ~2% per °C.
