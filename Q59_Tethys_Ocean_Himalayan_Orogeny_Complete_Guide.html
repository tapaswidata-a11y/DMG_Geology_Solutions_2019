Junior Scientific Officer (Geology) | Global Tectonics, Paleogeography & Himalayan Geology
QUESTION 59
The ocean that gave rise to birth of the Himalayan mountains was:
(a) Indian Ocean
(b) Pacific Ocean
(c) Tethys Ocean CORRECT ANSWER
(d) Panthalassa Ocean

1. Executive Summary & Answer Verification

The correct option is (c) Tethys Ocean (specifically the Neo-Tethys Ocean).

In global tectonics and historical geology (Eduard Suess, 1893; Dewey & Bird, 1970; Gansser, 1964):

  • During the Mesozoic Era, the supercontinent Pangea was bifurcated into northern Laurasia (Eurasia and North America) and southern Gondwanaland (India, Africa, Antarctica, Australia, South America) by a vast, triangular equatorial seaway known as the Tethys Ocean.
  • Following the Early Cretaceous breakup of Gondwana (~120 Ma), the Indian Plate broke loose and drifted rapidly northward across the Tethys at extraordinary velocities of 15–20 cm/year.
  • Subduction of the Neo-Tethys oceanic lithosphere beneath the southern margin of Asia (the Lhasa terrane) generated the Gangdese batholith/volcanic arc. At approximately 50–55 Ma (Early Eocene), the ocean basin was completely consumed, initiating the continent-continent collision between India and Eurasia that crumpled the Tethyan marine sediments and Indian crustal margin into the Himalayan Mountain Belt.

Notice the sharp decrease in convergence rate from ~100 mm/yr to ~50 mm/yr at 50 Ma marking the initial continental collision. (Van der Pluijm & Marshak, Earth Structure, Figure 21.2.1, p. 526).

2. Comprehensive Geological & Tectonic Evolution

2.1 Geological Record of the Tethys in the Himalayas

The closure of the Tethys Ocean is directly preserved across the tectonic zones of the Himalayas:

  1. Indus-Yarlung Tsangpo Suture Zone (IYTSZ): The physical tectonic boundary where the Tethys Ocean disappeared. It contains dismembered ophiolites (fragments of Tethyan oceanic crust and upper mantle peridotites, such as the Spontang and Jungbwa ophiolites), deep-sea radiolarian cherts, and chaotic subduction melanges.
  2. Tethyan Sedimentary Sequence (Tethys Himalaya): A continuous, highly fossiliferous marine sedimentary succession ranging in age from the Cambrian to the Eocene, deposited on the northern passive continental shelf of the Indian continent bordering the southern Tethys Ocean. Remarkably, the uppermost limestone cap of Mount Everest (the Qomolangma Formation) consists of Ordovician marine limestone deposited on the shallow floor of the Tethys Ocean, containing marine trilobites, brachiopods, and conodont fossils!
  3. Ammonite Fossils (Saligrams): Black ammonite fossils (Jurassic *Ammonoidea*) found abundantly in the Spiti Shales of the Tethyan Himalaya (Muktinath, Nepal and Spiti, India) represent shallow-marine mollusks that swam in the Tethys Ocean before its destruction.

2.2 Chronology of Tethyan Evolution

Tectonic Phase Geological Period Plate Tectonic Event
Paleo-Tethys Devonian – Late Triassic Equatorial ocean between Gondwana and Laurasia; closed by Cimmerian collisions
Neo-Tethys Opening Permian – Early Triassic Rifting of Cimmerian microcontinents (Tibet, Iran, Turkey) away from northern Gondwana
Neo-Tethys Subduction Jurassic – Late Cretaceous Northward subduction beneath Eurasia, producing the Trans-Himalayan magmatic arc (Ladakh/Kohistan/Gangdese)
Terminal Ocean Closure Early Eocene (~50–55 Ma) Final collision of India with Asia; birth of the Himalayan Mountain Chain
Intracontinental Shortening Oligocene – Present Development of major Himalayan thrusts: MCT (Main Central Thrust), MBT (Main Boundary Thrust), and MFT (Main Frontal Thrust)

3. Evaluation of All Options

  • (a) Indian Ocean — INCORRECT: The modern Indian Ocean was created behind India as it broke away from Antarctica and Australia; it did not close to form the Himalayas.
  • (b) Pacific Ocean — INCORRECT: Located east of Asia and the Americas; unrelated to the collision of the Indian shield.
  • (c) Tethys Ocean — CORRECT: The intervening ancient equatorial ocean whose subduction and collision produced the Himalayas and the Tibetan Plateau.
  • (d) Panthalassa Ocean — INCORRECT: The vast global ancestral Pacific ocean that surrounded the entire supercontinent Pangea during the Paleozoic and Mesozoic.
