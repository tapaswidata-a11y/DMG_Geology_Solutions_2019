Phyllosilicates: Muscovite Crystal Chemistry & Layer Stacking

Topic: Mineralogy & Sheet Silicate Crystal Chemistry | Primary Reference: Dexter Perkins (Mineralogy, 3rd ed., Ch. 13 & 14)

📝 Question Statement

Q.39. Muscovite belongs to which one of the following types of phyllosilicate structures? (o = octahedral, t = tetrahedral)

(a) Dioctahedral t-o-t, interlayer cation, dioctahedral t-o-t type
(b) Dioctahedral t-o-t type only
(c) Trioctahedral t-o-t, interlayer cation, trioctahedral t-o-t type
(d) Dioctahedral t-o type only
a Dioctahedral t-o-t, interlayer cation, dioctahedral t-o-t type (Correct Answer)
b Dioctahedral t-o-t type only
c Trioctahedral t-o-t, interlayer cation, trioctahedral t-o-t type
d Dioctahedral t-o type only
✓ Correct Answer: (a) Dioctahedral t-o-t, interlayer cation, dioctahedral t-o-t type [Muscovite possesses 2:1 (t-o-t) sandwiches with 2 out of 3 octahedral sites filled by Al³⁺, separated by interlayer K⁺ cations].

🖼️ Textbook Evidence: Dexter Perkins (Chapter 13 & 14)

Figure 13.15: Atomic Stacking in Phyllosilicates (Dexter Perkins, Mineralogy, 3rd ed., p. 368). In 2:1 sheet silicates (micas, pyrophyllite, talc), an octahedral sheet ($o$) is sandwiched between two opposing tetrahedral sheets ($t$), forming a rigid t-o-t layer. In muscovite, substitution of $\text{Al}^{3+}$ for $\text{Si}^{4+}$ in tetrahedral sites creates a negative layer charge satisfied by large interlayer $\text{K}^+$ cations situated in 12-fold coordination between successive t-o-t layers.
"In pyrophyllite, talc, and micas, tetrahedral layers surround octahedral layers to produce T-O-T sandwiches (2:1 layers). $\text{K}^+$, and more rarely $\text{Na}^+$ or $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, occupy sites between the sandwiches in micas.

When the octahedral sheet contains trivalent aluminum ($\text{Al}^{3+}$), only two out of every three octahedral sites are occupied to maintain charge balance ($2 \times +3 = +6$); these minerals are dioctahedral (e.g., muscovite, pyrophyllite, kaolinite). When the octahedral sheet contains divalent cations ($\text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Fe}^{2+}$), all three sites are occupied; these are trioctahedral (e.g., phlogopite, biotite, talc)."
— Dexter Perkins, Mineralogy, Chapter 13 (Atomic Structure: Sheet Silicates), pp. 367–368

🔍 Comprehensive Option Analysis

(a) Dioctahedral t-o-t, interlayer cation, dioctahedral t-o-t type [CORRECT]

Crystal Chemistry of Muscovite: The structural formula of muscovite is $\text{K}\text{Al}_2[\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10}](\text{OH})_2$:

  • Layer Architecture (2:1 or t-o-t): One octahedral sheet ($o$) is bonded between two inverted tetrahedral sheets ($t$).
  • Dioctahedral Nature: Aluminum ($\text{Al}^{3+}$) resides in the octahedral sheet. To balance charge, only 2 out of 3 octahedral positions per formula unit are filled by $\text{Al}^{3+}$, leaving 1 site vacant ($2/3$ occupancy $\equiv$ dioctahedral).
  • Interlayer Cation: In the tetrahedral sheets, one out of four $\text{Si}^{4+}$ cations is replaced by $\text{Al}^{3+}$ ($\text{AlSi}_3$ instead of $\text{Si}_4$). This generates a net $-1$ electrostatic deficit per formula unit, which is neutralized by interlayer $\text{K}^+$ cations located between adjacent t-o-t layers.
  • Therefore, the full stacking architecture is: dioctahedral t-o-t ➔ interlayer cation ($\text{K}^+$) ➔ dioctahedral t-o-t.

(b) Dioctahedral t-o-t type only [INCORRECT]

This describes pyrophyllite ($\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_4\text{O}_{10}(\text{OH})_2$). In pyrophyllite, all tetrahedral sites are occupied by $\text{Si}^{4+}$ without substitution, leaving the t-o-t layers electrically neutral. As a result, no interlayer cations exist; the t-o-t sheets are held together strictly by weak van der Waals forces, making pyrophyllite very soft (hardness 1–2).

(c) Trioctahedral t-o-t, interlayer cation, trioctahedral t-o-t type [INCORRECT]

This describes the trioctahedral micas—namely biotite ($\text{K}(\text{Mg},\text{Fe})_3[\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10}](\text{OH})_2$) and phlogopite ($\text{K}\text{Mg}_3[\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10}](\text{OH})_2$). Because they contain divalent cations ($\text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Fe}^{2+}$), all 3 out of 3 octahedral sites are occupied ($3/3 \equiv$ trioctahedral).

(d) Dioctahedral t-o type only [INCORRECT]

This describes kaolinite ($\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_5(\text{OH})_4$), a 1:1 clay mineral consisting of alternating single tetrahedral ($t$) and single octahedral ($o$) sheets without interlayer cations.

💡 Key Takeaway & Exam Strategy

Mnemonic & Structural Classification Rule:
  • Muscovite = Dioctahedral (Al³⁺, 2 out of 3 sites filled) + 2:1 (t-o-t) + Interlayer K⁺.
  • Biotite / Phlogopite = Trioctahedral (Mg²⁺/Fe²⁺, 3 out of 3 sites filled) + 2:1 (t-o-t) + Interlayer K⁺.
  • Pyrophyllite / Talc = Neutral t-o-t (no interlayer cation, soft).
  • Kaolinite = 1:1 (t-o) dioctahedral.
