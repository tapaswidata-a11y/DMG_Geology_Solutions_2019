1. The Genus Pliopithecus: Evolutionary Position Among the Anthropoidea

The fossil primate Pliopithecus (named by French paleontologist Paul Gervais in 1849 from Middle Miocene strata in Sansan, France) represents one of the most famous and historically pivotal genera in tertiary vertebrate paleontology:

  • Taxonomic Classification: Pliopithecus is a quintessential genus of the Anthropoidea (Anthropoids / Simians), belonging to the Superfamily Pliopithecoidea within the Parvorder Catarrhini (Old World higher primates).
  • Geochronological & Geographic Distribution: It flourished during the Early to Late Miocene ($\sim 18-10\text{ Ma}$) and persisted into the basal Pliocene across Europe (France, Spain, Germany, Poland, Slovakia) and eastern Asia (China, e.g., Pliopithecus zhanxiangi).
  • The First Eurasian Catarrhine: Following the closure of the Tethyan seaway and the establishment of the Gomphothere land bridge connecting Afro-Arabia to Eurasia at $\sim 19\text{ Ma}$, Pliopithecus was among the very first anthropoid lineages to migrate out of Africa and successfully colonize the subtropical forested belts of Europe and Asia.
Figure 67.1 (Szalay & Delson 1979 / Fleagle 2013): Cranial and dental anatomy of Pliopithecus antiquus, illustrating complete postorbital closure, catarrhine dental formula ($2.1.2.3$), and the diagnostic "Pliopithecus triangle" on lower molars.
2. Anatomical Hallmarks of the Anthropoidea in Pliopithecus

Pliopithecus exhibits the full suite of diagnostic morphological characters that define higher primates (Anthropoids):

The Anthropoid Cranio-Dental Formula
$$\text{Dental Formula: } \frac{2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3} \times 2 = 32 \text{ Teeth} \quad \left(\mathbf{I}\frac{2}{2}, \mathbf{C}\frac{1}{1}, \mathbf{P}\frac{2}{2}, \mathbf{M}\frac{3}{3}\right)$$

Possesses the definitive catarrhine dental formula with two premolars, identical to modern Old World monkeys, apes, and humans.

  • Complete Postorbital Closure: A solid bony wall (postorbital septum) separates the eye orbit from the temporal fossa, a critical anthropoid feature protecting the eyes during mastication.
  • Fused Frontal Bones: The right and left frontal bones fuse into a single solid frontal plate across the metopic suture.
  • Fused Mandibular Symphysis: The two dentary halves fuse completely at the chin midline.
  • The "Pliopithecus Triangle": A distinctive, narrow, triangular crest or cingulum on the buccal side of the lower molar cusps (talonid basin), characteristic of pliopithecoids.

Official Examination Question
Question 67
Pliopithecus is a genus of —__________ found in the Upper Miocene and Lower Pliocene strata of Europe and possibly Asia.
(A) Anthropoids
(B) Bipedal ape-like animals
(C) Lazarus taxa
(D) None of these
Verified Correct Answer: (A) Anthropoids [Pliopithecus is a celebrated fossil genus of higher primates belonging to the Anthropoidea (specifically stem-catarrhines), possessing an anthropoid skull and dental formula.]
Correct Option: (A) Anthropoids

Detailed Primate Paleontological Forensic Analysis:

  • In primate taxonomy and vertebrate paleontology (Fleagle 2013, Ch. 12; Szalay & Delson 1979; Benton 2014), Pliopithecus is universally classified under the suborder/infraorder Anthropoidea (Anthropoids / Simians).
  • It represents an ancient stem-catarrhine (higher primate) characterized by definitive anthropoid synapomorphies: complete postorbital closure of the eye orbits, fused mandibular symphysis, and a catarrhine dental formula of $2.1.2.3$.
  • It was the first major anthropoid radiation to spread into the Miocene forests of Europe and Asia.
  • Therefore, Option (A) Anthropoids is the exact, scientifically verified classification.
Incorrect Distractor: (B) Bipedal ape-like animals

Detailed Refutation:

  • Pliopithecus was not bipedal; postcranial skeletons (such as Epipliopithecus vindobonensis from Slovakia) prove that it was an arboreal quadrupedal monkey-like climber possessing a short external tail.
  • Bipedalism evolved tens of millions of years later in the hominin lineage (e.g., Sahelanthropus, Ardipithecus, Australopithecus) in Africa.
Incorrect Distractor: (C) Lazarus taxa

Detailed Refutation:

  • A Lazarus taxon is an organism that disappears from the fossil record during a mass extinction only to reappear much later (e.g., the coelacanth).
  • Pliopithecus is a legitimate, well-documented extinct genus of fossil anthropoid primates, not an evolutionary disappearance-reappearance anomaly.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) None of these

Detailed Refutation:

  • Because Option (A) Anthropoids is the precise taxonomic group to which Pliopithecus belongs, Option (D) is false.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Primate Evolution & Anthropoid Paleontology
Problem 1: Dental Formula of Platyrrhines vs. Catarrhines

Contrast the dental formula of New World monkeys (Platyrrhini) with that of Pliopithecus and Old World higher primates (Catarrhini).

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Platyrrhini (New World Monkeys): Dental formula is $\frac{2.1.3.3}{2.1.3.3} \times 2 = 36 \text{ teeth}$ (retains three premolars in each quadrant).
  • Catarrhini (Pliopithecus, Old World Monkeys, Apes): Dental formula is $\frac{2.1.2.3}{2.1.2.3} \times 2 = 32 \text{ teeth}$ (loss of the second premolar leaves two premolars, $\text{P}_3$ and $\text{P}_4$).
Problem 2: The Ectotympanic Tube in Catarrhine Crania

What primitive cranial feature in Pliopithecus demonstrates that it branched off before the divergence of modern catarrhines?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Modern catarrhines (cercopithecoids and hominoids) possess an extended, bony tubular ectotympanic tube leading to the ear canal. In Pliopithecus, the ectotympanic bone is a simple, primitive annulus (ring-like bone) fused to the bulla without a bony tube, proving it is a stem-catarrhine.

Problem 3: The Siwalik Primate *Sivapithecus*

What primate fossil discovered in the Middle Siwalik beds of the Potwar Plateau and Ramnagar (India) is recognized as the direct ancestor of the modern Orangutan (*Pongo*)?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Sivapithecus indicus (and Sivapithecus sivalensis). It shares unique cranial synapomorphies with living orangutans: vertically elongated oval eye orbits, narrow interorbital distance, concave "dished" facial profile, and smooth unsegmented subnasal alveolar plane.

Problem 4: *Gigantopithecus bilaspurensis* in India

What is the significance of the fossil ape Gigantopithecus discovered in the Siwalik Hills of Himachal Pradesh?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Gigantopithecus bilaspurensis (Late Miocene, $\sim 6-8\text{ Ma}$) and its Pleistocene descendant Gigantopithecus blacki represent the largest primates that ever lived (estimated body mass $>250-300\text{ kg}$). They possessed massive jaws with thick, flat-crowned molars adapted for chewing tough fibrous vegetation like bamboo.

Problem 5: Postorbital Bar vs. Postorbital Septum

How does the orbit of a strepsirrhine prosimian (e.g., lemur) differ from an anthropoid like Pliopithecus?

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Strepsirrhines: Possess only a lateral postorbital bar; the orbit remains wide open to the temporal fossa posteriorly.
  • Anthropoids (Pliopithecus): Possess a complete, bony postorbital septum / closure, separating the eye orbit into an enclosed cone that insulates the eye from chewing muscle vibrations.
Problem 6: The Fayum Depression and Anthropoid Radiation

What famous geological locality in Egypt preserves the ancestral Eocene-Oligocene radiation of early anthropoids?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Fayum Depression (Jebel Qatrani Formation), which yielded the iconic stem-anthropoids and early catarrhines Aegyptopithecus zeuxis, Propliopithecus, and Catopithecus ($\sim 34-30\text{ Ma}$).

Problem 7: Brachiation vs. Quadrupedalism in Pliopithecus

Why did early researchers mistakenly classify Pliopithecus as a fossil gibbon (hylobatid)?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Early workers observed that Pliopithecus had relatively slender limbs and short facial proportions similar to modern gibbons. However, complete limb skeletons proved that its intermembral index was $\sim 90-95$ (equal arm and leg lengths typical of quadrupedal tree-climbers), completely lacking the extreme arm elongation (index $>130$) and mobile wrist joints required for true ricochetal brachiation.

Problem 8: Miocene Climate and Primate Extinction in Europe

Why did Pliopithecus and other apes disappear from Europe by the end of the Miocene ($\sim 8-5\text{ Ma}$)?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Messinian salinity crisis and global tectonic cooling caused the warm, subtropical evergreen rainforests of Europe to be replaced by cold, dry, deciduous woodlands and open grasslands. As arboreal fruit-bearing trees disappeared, tropical frugivorous anthropoids like Pliopithecus and Dryopithecus were driven to regional extinction in Europe.
