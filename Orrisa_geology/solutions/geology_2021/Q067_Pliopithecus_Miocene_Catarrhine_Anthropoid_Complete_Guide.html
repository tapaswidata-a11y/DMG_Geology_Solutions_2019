Q67: Primate Paleontology - Genus Pliopithecus: Miocene Anthropoid Primates

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Fleagle (Primate Adaptation & Evolution); Szalay & Delson (Evolutionary History of the Primates)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. The Catarrhine Radiation & Genus Pliopithecus

The evolutionary history of higher primates (Suborder Haplorhini, Infraorder Anthropoidea / Simiiformes) is divided into the New World monkeys (Platyrrhini) and the Old World higher primates (Catarrhini), which includes Old World monkeys (Cercopithecoidea), apes and humans (Hominoidea), and extinct stem groups. Among the most historically important and widely dispersed fossil catarrhines of the Eurasian Neogene is the extinct genus Pliopithecus (described by French paleontologist Édouard Lartet in 1837; Szalay & Delson, Evolutionary History of the Primates; Fleagle, Primate Adaptation and Evolution, 3rd ed., pp. 297–302).

Pliopithecus is recovered extensively from Upper Miocene to Lower Pliocene strata across Europe (France, Spain, Germany, Austria, Poland, Switzerland) and Asia (China). It represents an extinct branch of Anthropoids (specifically stem catarrhines belonging to the superfamily Pliopithecoidea, family Pliopithecidae) that dispersed from Africa into Eurasia following the closure of the Tethyan seaway and the establishment of the Gomphotherium Land Bridge (~18 Ma).

2. Morphological Hallmarks of Pliopithecus

Pliopithecus displays a critical mixture of derived anthropoid specializations and primitive arboreal quadrupedal traits:

  • True Anthropoid Dentition: Possesses the definitive catarrhine anthropoid dental formula: $$\mathbf{2.1.2.3 / 2.1.2.3 = 32\ teeth}$$ (two incisors, one canine, two premolars, and three molars in each quadrant), identical to modern Old World monkeys and apes.
  • The "Pliopithecine Triangle": The lower molars exhibit a unique, diagnostic morphological specialization known as the pliopithecine triangle—a distinct triangular wear facet/crest formed by the convergence of crests on the anterior aspect of the lower molars.
  • Cranial & Postcranial Anatomy: Possesses complete postorbital closure (confirming its status as an anthropoid), forward-facing stereoscopic orbits, and a short snout. Postcranially, it was an agile, arboreal quadruped with a short tail, retaining a primitive entepicondylar foramen on the humerus (a feature lost in hominoids).

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q67. Pliopithecus is a genus of —__________ found in the Upper Miocene and Lower Pliocene strata of Europe and possibly Asia.
(A) Anthropoids
(B) Bipedal ape-like animals
(C) Lazarus taxa
(D) None of these

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "Pliopithecus is a genus of —__________ found in the Upper Miocene and Lower Pliocene strata of Europe and possibly Asia."

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Anthropoids (CORRECT): Pliopithecus is systematically classified within the Infraorder Anthropoidea (higher primates). As a stem catarrhine anthropoid, it displays true anthropoid cranial features (postorbital closure, stereoscopic vision) and the standard catarrhine dental formula (2.1.2.3). Every standard primate paleontology textbook classifies Pliopithecus under the anthropoid catarrhine radiation.
  • (B) Bipedal ape-like animals (INCORRECT): Pliopithecus was strictly a small, quadrupedal arboreal climber and clamberer; it was not bipedal! Obligate bipedalism arose much later among hominins (e.g., Australopithecus, ~4 Ma).
  • (C) Lazarus taxa (INCORRECT): A Lazarus taxon is an evolutionary term for a group that disappears from the fossil record for a long hiatus and then miraculously reappears alive or in younger strata (e.g., coelacanths). Pliopithecus is a standard, extinct fossil primate, not a Lazarus taxon.
  • (D) None of these (INCORRECT): Option A is the accurate taxonomic placement.
