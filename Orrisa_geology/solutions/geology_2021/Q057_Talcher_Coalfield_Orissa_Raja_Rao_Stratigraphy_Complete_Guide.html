1. The Talcher Coalfield: Stratigraphic Framework of C.S. Raja Rao (GSI 1982)

The Talcher Coalfield, situated in the Brahmani-Mahanadi valley in the Angul and Sambalpur districts of Odisha, represents the largest single repository of thermal power-grade bituminous coal in India (holding over 50 billion tonnes of geological reserves). In 1982, Geological Survey of India Director C.S. Raja Rao synthesized the definitive stratigraphic architecture of the basin:

  • Broad Chronostratigraphic Division: Raja Rao classified the sedimentary sequence of Talcher Coalfield into two overarching chronological divisions:
    1. Early Permian Succession: The lower sequence comprising the basal glaciogenic beds and the initial coal-bearing sequence.
    2. Late Permian to Triassic Succession: The upper sequence comprising the Middle-to-Late Permian and Triassic units (Barren Measures, Raniganj, and Kamthi/Mahadeva formations).
  • Subdivisions of the Early Permian: In Raja Rao's official classification, the Early Permian sequence of Talcher Coalfield is specifically and systematically divided into two constituent basal formations:
    Raja Rao (1982) Early Permian Sequence of Talcher
    $$\mathbf{Talchir \text{ Formation}} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{Karharbari \text{ Formation}}$$

    Followed conformably by the main Barakar Formation (Early Permian Artinskian).

2. Lithological Characteristics of the Talchir and Karharbari Formations in Odisha

The two Early Permian formations established in Raja Rao's classification exhibit distinct sedimentological signatures:

  • 1. Talchir Formation: Resting with profound nonconformity on Precambrian Eastern Ghats granulites and granites, it consists of:
    • The basal Talchir Boulder Bed: Unsorted diamictite containing striated and faceted cobbles of quartzite and granite in a green-grey rock flour matrix.
    • Needle Shales: Thinly laminated greenish olive-grey silts and shales displaying pencil-jointing.
    • Varved Silts and Turbidite Sandstones: Lacustrine deposits carrying ice-rafted dropstones. Completely devoid of coal.
  • 2. Karharbari Formation: Represents the pioneering coal-forming episode in the Talcher basin:
    • Composed of poorly sorted, coarse-grained sub-arkosic sandstones, pebbly grits, reworked glacial clasts, and carbonaceous shales.
    • Contains the basal Seam I (Bottom Seam) of Talcher Coalfield, characterized by dull, inertinite-rich, low-volatile bituminous coal.
    • Palynologically and paleobotanically identified by the diagnostic presence of Gondwanidium validum, Buriadia sewardana, and Crucisaccites.

Official Examination Question
Question 57
Raja Rao of GSI in 1982 classified the stratigraphic sequence of Talcher coalfield, Orissa into Early Permian and Late Permian to Triassic. The Early Permian is again classified into which formations ?
(A) Lower Kamthi
(B) Talchir and Karharbari
(C) Damuda Group
(D) All of these
Verified Correct Answer: (B) Talchir and Karharbari [In C.S. Raja Rao's (GSI 1982) authoritative classification of the Talcher Coalfield, the Early Permian succession is specifically subdivided into the Talchir and Karharbari Formations (followed by Barakar).]
Correct Option: (B) Talchir and Karharbari

Detailed Stratigraphic Forensic Analysis:

  • In the official Geological Survey of India memoir Coalfields of India (Vol. II, 1982), C.S. Raja Rao systematically classified the stratigraphic sequence of the Talcher Coalfield, Orissa.
  • The succession was bifurcated into an Early Permian division and a Late Permian to Triassic division.
  • The Early Permian was explicitly subdivided into two diagnostic basal units: the Talchir Formation (representing the glaciogenic tillites and needle shales) and the Karharbari Formation (representing the initial gritty sandstone coal measure hosting Seam I).
  • Therefore, Option (B) Talchir and Karharbari is the exact, authoritative, and scientifically verified answer.
Incorrect Distractor: (A) Lower Kamthi

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Lower Kamthi Formation belongs to the Late Permian to Triassic division of Raja Rao's classification, not the Early Permian.
  • It represents upper barren red ferruginous clastics, making it chronostratigraphically incorrect.
Incorrect Distractor: (C) Damuda Group

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Damuda Group is a major hierarchical group rank that includes the Karharbari, Barakar, Barren Measures, and Raniganj formations.
  • The question asks for the specific lower formations into which the Early Permian was subdivided, not a group name. Furthermore, the Talchir Formation does not belong to the Damuda Group; it forms its own independent group/formation below the Damuda.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) All of these

Detailed Refutation:

  • Because Lower Kamthi is Late Permian-Triassic and Damuda is a group, Option (D) is incorrect.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Odisha Coal Geology & Basin Mechanics
Problem 1: Strip Ratio Calculation in Talcher Opencast Mines

In an opencast coal block of the Talcher Coalfield, a composite Barakar coal seam has thickness $T_{\text{coal}} = 25\text{ m}$. Overlying overburden rock has thickness $T_{\text{ob}} = 75\text{ m}$. Calculate the Stripping Ratio (in $\text{m}^3/\text{tonne}$) assuming coal density $\rho_{\text{coal}} = 1.4\text{ tonnes/m}^3$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

For unit horizontal area ($1\text{ m}^2$):

$$\text{Volume of Overburden} = 1\text{ m}^2 \times 75\text{ m} = 75\text{ m}^3$$ $$\text{Mass of Coal} = 1\text{ m}^2 \times 25\text{ m} \times 1.4\text{ tonnes/m}^3 = 35\text{ tonnes}$$ $$\text{Stripping Ratio} = \frac{75\text{ m}^3}{35\text{ tonnes}} \approx 2.14\text{ m}^3/\text{tonne}$$

Final Answer: The stripping ratio is $2.14\text{ m}^3/\text{tonne}$, indicating an exceptionally favorable opencast mining project.

Problem 2: Distinguishing Karharbari Coal from Barakar Coal

What petrographic and maceral characteristic distinguishes Karharbari coals from Barakar coals in the Talcher basin?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Karharbari coals (Seam I) are dull, hard, compact coals dominated by the inertinite maceral group (fusinite, inertodetrinite, semifusinite $>50\%$) formed by oxidative subaerial peat exposure and wildfires. In contrast, Barakar coals (Seams II-VIII) are banded, brighter coals containing higher proportions of the vitrinite maceral group ($40-60\%$) formed under permanently waterlogged, reducing anoxic swamp conditions.

Problem 3: Geographic Boundaries of the Talcher Basin

State the structural tectonic boundaries of the Talcher Coalfield within Odisha.

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Talcher Coalfield forms the southeastern terminus of the Mahanadi-Brahmani Master Graben. It is bounded on the north by the North Fault scarp against Rengali Province gneisses, on the south and west by Eastern Ghats charnockites and khondalites, and plunges gently towards the west-northwest into the adjacent Ib River Coalfield.

Problem 4: Gross Calorific Value (GCV) Determination

According to India's Ministry of Coal GCV pricing banding, calculate the GCV of a Talcher coal sample containing Moisture $M = 7.0\%$ and Ash $A = 38.0\%$ using the empirical formula: $\text{GCV (kcal/kg)} = 8550 - 150(M + A)$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$M + A = 7.0 + 38.0 = 45.0\%$$ $$\text{GCV} = 8550 - 150(45.0) = 8550 - 6750 = 1800\text{ kcal/kg}$$

Final Answer: $\text{GCV} = 1800\text{ kcal/kg}$ (corresponding to Grade G16 power utility thermal coal).

Problem 5: Palynological Dating of the Talcher Karharbari

Which pollen genus is the definitive biostratigraphic marker for the Karharbari Formation in Talcher?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The radial monosaccate genus Crucisaccites monoletus and Parasaccites korbaensis, accompanied by the appearance of striate disaccate pollen (Faunipollenites), marking the Sakmarian stage of the early Cisuralian.

Problem 6: Structural Asymmetry of the Talcher Half-Graben

How do sedimentary dip angles vary from the southern to the northern boundary of the Talcher Coalfield?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Along the southern boundary, Gondwana strata rest unconformably upon Precambrian basement with gentle northern dips of $5^\circ-10^\circ$. Moving northward toward the master boundary fault, dips increase sharply to $25^\circ-45^\circ$, forming an asymmetric half-graben tilted towards the northern boundary fault.

Problem 7: Groundwater Hazards in Mining Talcher Coal

What hydrogeological hazard is associated with the multi-aquifer system in the Barakar sandstones of Talcher?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The thick, porous Barakar sandstone layers sandwiching coal seams form prolific confined and semi-confined artesian aquifers. Heavy seasonal monsoons recharge these sandstones, generating high artesian pore pressures capable of bursting pit bottoms or causing sudden flooding of opencast sumps.

Problem 8: Fossil Flora of the Overlying Kamthi Formation

What characteristic gymnosperm fossil plant assemblage appears in the Kamthi beds overlying the coal measures in Talcher?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Kamthi beds contain late Permian to early Triassic floral elements, notably Schizoneura gondwanensis, Glossopteris conspicua, and Dicroidium fronds, signifying a climatic drying trend with the onset of seasonal arid conditions.
