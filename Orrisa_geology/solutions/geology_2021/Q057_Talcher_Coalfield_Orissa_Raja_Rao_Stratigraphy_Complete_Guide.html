Q57: Stratigraphy of Talcher Coalfield, Odisha - Raja Rao (1982) Classification

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: K.M. Bangar (Principles of Engineering Geology, p. 338); G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 320)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Regional Framework of the Talcher Coalfield, Odisha

The Talcher Coalfield, situated in the Angul and Dhenkanal districts of central Odisha, forms the southeastern terminus of the prolific Mahanadi Valley Gondwana Master Basin. Covering an area of approximately 1,800 $\mathrm{km^2}$, Talcher holds the largest single coal resource inventory in India (estimated at over 50 billion tonnes), providing thermal feedstock for massive energy infrastructure including the NTPC Super Thermal Power Plants.

The basin is a fault-controlled asymmetrical half-graben, characterized by a prominent East-West trending southern boundary fault where Gondwana sediments are faulted against the high-grade Precambrian metamorphic basement of the Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt (EGMB) and Rengali Province.

2. Raja Rao's 1982 GSI Stratigraphic Framework

In his authoritative Geological Survey of India (GSI) memoir (Coalfields of India, Vol. II: Coal Resources of Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Orissa and Maharashtra, GSI Bulletin Series A, No. 45, 1982), C.S. Raja Rao synthesized the lithostratigraphic framework of the Talcher basin. He partitioned the succession into two major temporal regimes:

  1. Early Permian Sequence:
    • Talchir Formation (Earliest Permian / Asselian–Sakmarian): The basal unit, resting with profound angular unconformity upon the Precambrian granitic and khondalitic/charnockitic basement. It comprises a thick glaciogenic pile of tillites (diamictites), boulder beds, khaki-green "needle shales" (rhythmites), and turbiditic sandstones deposited during the melting of the Late Paleozoic Gondwanan ice sheet.
    • Karharbari Formation (Early Permian / Artinskian): Represents the initiation of fluvial drainage and peat accumulation under cold-temperate post-glacial climatic conditions. Dominated by poorly sorted, immature pebbly sub-arkosic sandstones, gray shales, and thin, low-moisture coal seams (notably Seam I, which yields superior grade thermal coal).
    • Barakar Formation (Early Permian / Kungurian): Composed of cyclic coarse felspathic sandstones, carbonaceous shales, and multiple exceptionally thick coal seams (Seams II through IX, aggregating up to 60 meters in thickness).
  2. Late Permian to Triassic Sequence:
    • Barren Measures / Lower Kamthi Formation (Late Permian): Coarse, ferruginous sandstones and mottled shales, largely devoid of economic coal seams.
    • Upper Kamthi / Mahadeva Formation (Early Triassic): Massive, cliff-forming ferruginous sandstones and red clays marking the transition into Triassic continental regimes.

In Raja Rao's standard classification scheme, the Early Permian is explicitly classified into the Talchir and Karharbari formations (and Barakar Formation), which together represent the foundational, lower coal-bearing succession of the Talcher basin.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q57. Raja Rao of GSI in 1982 classified the stratigraphic sequence of Talcher coalfield, Orissa into Early Permian and Late Permian to Triassic. The Early Permian is again classified into which formations ?
(A) Lower Kamthi
(B) Talchir and Karharbari
(C) Damuda Group
(D) All of these

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "Raja Rao of GSI in 1982 classified the stratigraphic sequence of Talcher coalfield, Orissa into Early Permian and Late Permian to Triassic. The Early Permian is again classified into which formations ?"

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Lower Kamthi (INCORRECT): The Kamthi Formation belongs to the Late Permian to Early Triassic sequence in the Talcher basin. It overlies the Barakar/Barren Measures and is characterized by ferruginous, non-coaliferous or poorly coal-bearing clastics. It does not belong to the Early Permian subdivision.
  • (B) Talchir and Karharbari (CORRECT): In Raja Rao's (1982) GSI Memoir classification of the Talcher Coalfield, the Early Permian succession is systematically classified into the basal glaciogenic Talchir Formation and the overlying coal-bearing Karharbari Formation (alongside the Barakar Formation). These represent the definitive Early Permian (Asselian to Artinskian) lithounits of the basin.
  • (C) Damuda Group (INCORRECT): The Damuda Group is a higher-rank lithostratigraphic unit (a Group, not a formation) that encompasses the Karharbari, Barakar, Barren Measures, and Raniganj formations across the entire Gondwana realm. Raja Rao's specific formation-level classification of the Early Permian in Talcher targets the discrete formations.
  • (D) All of these (INCORRECT): Because the Lower Kamthi belongs strictly to the Late Permian–Triassic sequence, it is impossible for "All of these" to be Early Permian.
