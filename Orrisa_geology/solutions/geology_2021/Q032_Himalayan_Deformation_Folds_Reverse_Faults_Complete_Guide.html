Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

32.1 Tectonic Architecture of the India-Asia Collisional Orogen

In global tectonics and Indian geology (K.S. Valdiya, The Making of India: Geodynamic Evolution, Ch. 5; Gansser, 1964; Ramakrishnan & Vaidyanadhan, Geology of India, Vol. 2, Ch. 29), the Himalayan mountain belt represents the classic, archetypal active continental collision zone (Himalayan-Tibetan orogen). Driven by the ongoing northward indentation of the Indian plate into Eurasia at $\sim 40 - 50\text{ mm/yr}$, crustal shortening is accommodated by a massive foreland-propagating fold-and-thrust belt:

Major Tectonic Boundary / Unit Structural Architecture Dominant Deformational Regime Lithotectonic Association
Main Central Thrust (MCT) Ductile, high-angle shear zone transitioning into crustal ramp Contractional Thrusting + Overturned Folds High-grade Higher Himalayan Crystalline core over Lesser Himalaya.
Main Boundary Thrust (MBT) Regional northward-dipping low-angle thrust fault Contractional Thrusting + Nappes Lesser Himalayan metasediments thrust over the Siwaliks.
Main Frontal Thrust (MFT / HFT) Active southernmost frontal reverse fault Blind thrusts, Fault-bend folds, Fault-propagation folds Siwalik molasse thrust over active Indo-Gangetic alluvium.
Sub-Himalayan Siwalik Belt Fold-thrust wedge Asymmetrical anticlines, synclines, & imbricate thrusts Neogene-Quaternary foreland synorogenic sediments.

32.2 Why Normal Faults are Minor and Restricted

While the South Tibetan Detachment System (STDS) at the crest of the Higher Himalaya exhibits localized orogen-parallel extension due to gravitational collapse of over-thickened crust, the overwhelming bulk of the deformed rocks across the length and breadth of the Himalayan belt forms folds and reverse/thrust faults under regional horizontal compression ($\sigma_1$ horizontal, $\sigma_3$ vertical).

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q32. Rocks are deformed in the Himalayan mountain belt commonly forms which deformational structures ?
(A) Normal and reverse faults
(B) Folds and reverse faults
(C) Recumbent folds and normal faults
(D) All of these
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) Folds and reverse faults — Regional crustal shortening in the Himalayan contractional collision orogen produces predominantly folds and reverse (thrust) faults throughout the belt.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Normal and reverse faults — INCORRECT: Normal faults characterize extensional tectonic environments (rifts), whereas the Himalayas are an active compressional convergent orogen.
  • (B) Folds and reverse faults — CORRECT: The hallmark deformational structures of collisional mountain belts (MCT, MBT, MFT, and associated Siwalik and Lesser Himalayan fold-thrust nappes) are compressive folds and reverse/thrust faults.
  • (C) Recumbent folds and normal faults — INCORRECT: While recumbent folds occur locally in the crystalline nappes, normal faults are not the characteristic widespread deformation structure of the range.
  • (D) All of these — INCORRECT: Normal faults are not a primary characteristic of the general Himalayan deformation belt.
