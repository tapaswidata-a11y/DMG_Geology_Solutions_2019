Q64: Early Reptile Paleontology - Petrolacosaurus & Araeoscelis (Order Araeoscelidia)

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Michael J. Benton (Vertebrate Palaeontology, 4th ed., Ch. 5, p. 120); Carroll (1988)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. The Origin of Diapsid Reptiles

The evolution of amniotes in the Late Carboniferous witnessed a fundamental divergence based on temporal skull fenestration: Anapsida (no temporal openings; ancestral condition), Synapsida (one lower temporal opening; ancestors of mammals), and Diapsida (two temporal openings: an upper and a lower temporal fenestra; ancestors of lizards, snakes, crocodilians, dinosaurs, and birds).

For decades, paleontologists searched for the most primitive diapsid reptiles. That milestone was achieved with the discovery and description of Petrolacosaurus kansensis (Lane 1945; Peabody 1952; Reisz 1981; Michael J. Benton, Vertebrate Palaeontology, 4th ed., Chapter 5, pp. 118–122). Recovered from the Late Carboniferous (Pennsylvanian, ~302 Ma) Rock Lake Shale Member near Garnett, Kansas, Petrolacosaurus represents the earliest known and most primitive diapsid reptile in the global fossil record.

2. Phylogenetic Sister Taxon: Genus Araeoscelis

In cladistic systematic paleontology, Petrolacosaurus is placed as the basalmost representative of the Order Araeoscelidia (or Araeoscelida). Cladistic analyses universally demonstrate that the closest known relative and sister taxon of Petrolacosaurus is the Early Permian genus Araeoscelis (Williston 1910; Vaughn 1955), known from the Clear Fork Group of Texas.

Petrolacosaurus and Araeoscelis share unique derived morphological synapomorphies that define the Araeoscelidia:

  • Elongated Cervical Vertebrae: Both taxa possess exceptionally slender, elongate necks supported by 6 to 8 specialized cervical vertebrae with prominent mid-shaft lateral constrictions and mamillary processes.
  • Slender, Elongated Limbs: Highly gracile, elongated fore- and hindlimbs adapted for rapid, agile running and climbing to capture insects.
  • Diapsid Skull Architecture: Both possess true upper and lower temporal fenestrae (although in Araeoscelis, the lower temporal opening is secondarily closed by the expansion of the squamosal and jugal bones).

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q64. The closest known relative of Petrolacosaurus is —__.
(A) Sphenodon
(B) Araeoscelis
(C) Hylonomous
(D) Askeptosaurus

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "The closest known relative of Petrolacosaurus is —__."

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Sphenodon (INCORRECT): Sphenodon (the modern tuatara of New Zealand) is a living rhynchocephalian lepidosaur. While it retains the primitive diapsid condition of two complete temporal arches, it belongs to the distant crown-group Lepidosauromorpha and is not the closest familial relative of Carboniferous Petrolacosaurus.
  • (B) Araeoscelis (CORRECT): Araeoscelis is the type genus of the family Araeoscelidae and the Order Araeoscelidia. Cladistically and morphologically, Araeoscelis is universally classified as the closest known sister taxon and immediate familial relative of Petrolacosaurus.
  • (C) Hylonomous (INCORRECT): Hylonomus lyelli (from the Joggins fossil cliffs of Nova Scotia, ~315 Ma) is the oldest known amniote/reptile, but it is an anapsid eureptile (protorothyridid), not a diapsid, and is phylogenetically basal to the split of diapsids.
  • (D) Askeptosaurus (INCORRECT): Askeptosaurus is a marine thalattosaurian diapsid from the Middle Triassic of Europe, far removed from basal Carboniferous-Permian araeoscelidians.
