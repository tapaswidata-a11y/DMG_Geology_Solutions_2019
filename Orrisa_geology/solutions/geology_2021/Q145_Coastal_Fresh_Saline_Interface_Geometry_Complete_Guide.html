2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: The Ghyben-Herzberg & Glover Coastal Interface

In coastal aquifers, freshwater originating from inland recharge flows toward the sea, floating above denser seawater (freshwater density $\rho_f = 1.000\text{ g/cm}^3$; seawater density $\rho_s = 1.025\text{ g/cm}^3$):

  • The Classical Ghyben-Herzberg Relation: Under hydrostatic equilibrium: $$z = \frac{\rho_f}{\rho_s - \rho_f} h_f \approx 40 h_f$$ where $h_f$ is the freshwater water table height above sea level, and $z$ is the depth of the interface below sea level. Every 1 meter of freshwater head above sea level supports 40 meters of freshwater column below sea level.
  • Dynamic Glover (1959) Parabolic Interface:
    • In real aquifers, freshwater is discharging dynamically into the sea at an exit face on the seafloor.
    • Because freshwater flowlines converge toward the coastal discharge zone, vertical hydraulic gradients develop near the shore.
    • Mathematical solution of the flow net (Glover's equation) demonstrates that the dynamic interface profile is a parabola described by: $$z^2 = \frac{2 Q x}{K \Delta \rho / \rho_f} + \left(\frac{Q}{K \Delta \rho / \rho_f}\right)^2$$
    • This parabolic curve has a concave upward curvature (curving gently downward inland and steepening upward as it approaches the shoreline/seafloor).

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option B (Concave upward) is correct: The rigorous dynamic Glover-Hubbert parabolic profile of the fresh-saline interface.
  • Option A (Linear and horizontal): Static planar layer, impossible in dynamic discharging coastal aquifers.
  • Option C (Linear and inclined): Oversimplified hydrostatic approximation that ignores shoreline flow convergence.
  • Option D (Convex downward): Inverted curvature, physically incorrect for a lighter fluid discharging over a denser fluid wedge.
