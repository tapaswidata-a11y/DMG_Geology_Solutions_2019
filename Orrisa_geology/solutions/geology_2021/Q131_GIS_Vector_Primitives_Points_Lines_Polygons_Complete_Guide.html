2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: The Vector Data Model in GIS

In Geographic Information Systems (GIS), the vector data model represents real-world discrete geographic features on the Earth's surface using three fundamental geometric primitives based on coordinate geometry:

  1. Point Features (Zero-Dimensional, 0D): Represented by a single coordinate pair $(X, Y)$ without length or area. Used for discrete features whose spatial dimensions are negligible at the map scale (e.g., tube wells, seismic epicenters, mineral deposit locations, benchmark elevations).
  2. Line Features (One-Dimensional, 1D): An ordered series of connected coordinate vertices possessing length but zero width (e.g., fault traces, river courses, road networks, geological contacts).
  3. Polygon / Area Features (Two-Dimensional, 2D): An enclosed, closed boundary of connected lines whose starting vertex equals its ending vertex, enclosing measurable area and perimeter (e.g., geological formations, lithological outcrops, drainage basins, lakes).

Together, points, lines, and polygons collectively define and characterize every conceivable vector feature on Earth; hence, (D) "All of these" is the correct comprehensive answer.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option D (All of these) is correct: Points, lines, and polygons comprise the three cardinal vector primitives of spatial informatics.
  • Options A, B, and C: Individual geometric primitives; selecting any single one ignores the other two fundamental spatial geometries.
