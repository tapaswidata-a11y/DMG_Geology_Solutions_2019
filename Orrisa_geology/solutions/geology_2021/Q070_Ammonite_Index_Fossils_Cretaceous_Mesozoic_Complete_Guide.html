Q70: Cephalopod Biostratigraphy - Ammonites as Index Fossils in the Cretaceous

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 356, Fig. 91.4); P.K. Mukherjee (p. 215)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Evolutionary Radiation of Subclass Ammonoidea

Within the molluscan class Cephalopoda, the extinct subclass Ammonoidea represents the gold standard for global biostratigraphic correlation. Ammonoids originated in the Early Devonian (~410 Ma) from straight-shelled bactritid nautiloids, diversified through the Paleozoic and Mesozoic oceans as free-swimming pelagic predators, and suffered sudden, complete extinction during the end-Cretaceous bolide impact (~66 Ma; G.B. Mahapatra, A Text-Book of Geology, Chapter 91, p. 356 / PDF p. 186; P.K. Mukherjee, A Text Book of Geology, p. 215).

The internal septa dividing the chambered phragmocone met the outer shell wall along an intricate contact line known as the suture line. Suture line complexity evolved through four progressive, diagnostic stages:

  1. Goniatitic Suture (Devonian–Carboniferous): Characterized by simple, smooth, undivided saddles and lobes (e.g., Goniatites).
  2. Ceratitic Suture (Permian–Triassic): Characterized by smooth, rounded saddles and denticulated, serrated lobes (e.g., Ceratites).
  3. Ammonitic Suture (Jurassic–Cretaceous): Characterized by intensely fluted, foliated, and frilled saddles AND lobes (resembling oak-leaf patterns), providing maximum hydrostatic strength to resist deep-water hydrostatic pressure (e.g., Phylloceras, Ammonites).

2. Why Ammonites are the Ultimate Mesozoic & Cretaceous Index Fossils

An Index Fossil (or guide fossil) is defined by four rigorous paleontological criteria: (1) rapid evolutionary turnover and short vertical stratigraphic range, (2) wide, cosmopolitan geographic dispersal, (3) abundant preservation in diverse marine facies, and (4) easily recognizable, distinctive morphology. Ammonites fulfill every criterion with unmatched precision:

  • During the Cretaceous Period, ammonites underwent their final, most spectacular burst of evolutionary diversification and morphological experimentation, giving rise to classic heteromorph ammonites (uncoiled, helical, hook-shaped, and straight shells, e.g., Scaphites, Baculites, Turrilites, Nipponites) alongside giant coiled planisprial forms (Parapuzosia, Mortoniceras, Acanthoceras).
  • In India, the Cretaceous marine successions of the Trichinopoly area (Cauvery Basin)—comprising the Uttatur, Trichinopoly, and Ariyalur formations—and the Spiti Valley (Giumal & Chikkim formations) are biozoned with ultra-high resolution based on cosmopolitan Cretaceous ammonite zones.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q70. Ammonite is a characteristic marine fossil of ___ age around the world and considered an index fossil because of its diverse nature.
(A) Palaeozoic
(B) Cambrian to Silurian
(C) Cretaceous-age
(D) None of these

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "Ammonite is a characteristic marine fossil of ________ age around the world and considered an index fossil because of its diverse nature."

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Palaeozoic (INCORRECT): The Paleozoic Era was characterized by nautiloids and primitive goniatites, not true ammonites sensu stricto (which have complex ammonitic sutures). True ammonites flourished during the Mesozoic.
  • (B) Cambrian to Silurian (INCORRECT): Ammonoids did not exist during the Cambrian, Ordovician, or Silurian periods! The earliest ancestral ammonoid precursors (Bactritida) did not evolve until the Early Devonian.
  • (C) Cretaceous-age (CORRECT): Among the given choices, Cretaceous-age represents the definitive period during which ammonites were world-famous, globally distributed, highly diverse index fossils. Cretaceous strata worldwide (including the Indian Cauvery and Spiti basins) are biozoned into zonal intervals based on rapid ammonite radiations before their abrupt extinction at the K-Pg boundary.
  • (D) None of these (INCORRECT): Option C is the correct, intended answer in the examination key.
