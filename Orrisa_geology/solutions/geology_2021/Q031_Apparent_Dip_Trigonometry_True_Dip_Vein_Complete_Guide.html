1. Geometric Principles: True Dip vs. Apparent Dip

In structural geology and underground mining engineering, determining the geometric attitude of mineralized veins, fault planes, and sedimentary strata requires understanding the fundamental relationship between True Dip and Apparent Dip:

  • True Dip ($\delta$): The maximum vertical angle of inclination of a planar surface relative to the horizontal plane. It is measured strictly in a vertical plane oriented perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the strike line of the bed. A plane has only one unique true dip.
  • Apparent Dip ($\delta'$): The vertical angle of inclination of a planar surface measured in any vertical cross-section oriented obliquely to the strike line (at an acute angle $\alpha$ relative to the true dip direction).
  • The Invariant Rule: Because true dip is the steepest line on a plane, apparent dip is always strictly less than true dip ($\delta' < \delta$) for any non-orthogonal section. As the cross-section approaches parallelism with strike ($\alpha \to 90^\circ$), apparent dip approaches zero ($\delta' \to 0^\circ$).
The Fundamental Apparent Dip Trigonometric Equation
$$\tan\delta' = \tan\delta \cdot \cos\alpha$$

Where $\delta$ is True Dip, $\delta'$ is Apparent Dip, and $\alpha$ is the horizontal angle between the True Dip direction and the Apparent Dip direction ($\alpha = |\text{True Dip Azimuth} - \text{Apparent Dip Azimuth}|$).

2. Step-by-Step Analytical Calculation for the Sulphide Vein

Given parameters for the sulphide vein in Question 31:

  • True Dip ($\delta$): $50^\circ$ toward $\text{N}45^\circ\text{W}$ (Azimuth $315^\circ$).
  • Direction of Apparent Dip Section: $\text{N}20^\circ\text{W}$ (Azimuth $340^\circ$).
  • Angle Between Directions ($\alpha$): $\alpha = 45^\circ - 20^\circ = 25^\circ$.
Analytical Solution
$$\tan\delta' = \tan(50^\circ) \cdot \cos(25^\circ)$$ $$\tan(50^\circ) \approx 1.19175 \qquad \cos(25^\circ) \approx 0.90631$$ $$\tan\delta' = 1.19175 \times 0.90631 = 1.08009$$ $$\delta' = \arctan(1.08009) \approx \mathbf{47.20^\circ} \approx \mathbf{45^\circ}$$

Among the given multiple-choice options (55°, 35°, 25°, 45°), 45° is the closest integer option and the only mathematically viable choice (since apparent dip must be $< 50^\circ$, eliminating 55°, and cannot be as low as 35° or 25° for a small angle of $\alpha = 25^\circ$).

Figure 31.2: Apparent Dip Nomogram & Trigonometric Verification. Plotting $\delta = 50^\circ$ and $\alpha = 25^\circ$ yields an apparent dip $\delta' \approx 47^\circ$, validating option 45°.
3. Refutation of Alternative Angles

Forensic analysis of the options:

  • 55° (Option A): Physically impossible! Apparent dip can NEVER exceed true dip ($\delta' \le \delta$). A value of $55^\circ > 50^\circ$ violates basic geometry.
  • 35° (Option B): Corresponds to an angle $\alpha \approx 54^\circ$ from true dip ($\tan 35^\circ / \tan 50^\circ = 0.700 / 1.192 = 0.587 \implies \cos^{-1}(0.587) \approx 54^\circ$), far larger than the actual $25^\circ$.
  • 25° (Option C): Corresponds to an angle $\alpha \approx 67^\circ$ from true dip.

Official Question Statement — OPSC 2021 Geology Paper-II (Question 31)
Q31. A sulphide vein dips 50° toward N45°W. Its apparent dip toward of N20°W will be around _____ :
(A) 55°
(B) 35°
(C) 25°
(D) 45°  ✔
✔
Correct Answer: (D) 45°
Using the standard apparent dip equation $\tan\delta' = \tan\delta \cdot \cos\alpha$ with true dip $\delta = 50^\circ$ and $\alpha = 45^\circ - 20^\circ = 25^\circ$: $\tan\delta' = 1.19175 \times 0.90631 = 1.0801 \implies \delta' \approx 47.2^\circ$, which is approximately 45°.
(A) 55° Incorrect (Geometrically Impossible: Exceeds True Dip)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Apparent dip is measured obliquely to true dip and can never exceed true dip ($\delta' \le \delta$). Because the true dip of the vein is 50°, an apparent dip of 55° is a physical impossibility.
(B) 35° Incorrect (Severe Underestimation)
Why this distractor is incorrect: An apparent dip of 35° would require the section to be oriented at $\alpha \approx 54^\circ$ away from true dip direction (i.e., toward $\text{N}09^\circ\text{E}$), whereas $\text{N}20^\circ\text{W}$ is only $25^\circ$ away.
(C) 25° Incorrect (Confuses Angle Between Bearings with Apparent Dip)
Why this distractor is incorrect: 25° is the horizontal angle between the true dip bearing ($\text{N}45^\circ\text{W}$) and the apparent dip bearing ($\text{N}20^\circ\text{W}$), i.e., $\alpha = 45^\circ - 20^\circ = 25^\circ$. Conflating $\alpha$ with the resulting vertical apparent dip angle $\delta'$ is a classic trap.
(D) 45° Correct Verified Answer
Why this option is correct: By the fundamental formula $\tan\delta' = \tan\delta \cdot \cos\alpha$: $$\tan\delta' = \tan(50^\circ) \cdot \cos(25^\circ) = 1.19175 \times 0.90631 = 1.0801 \implies \delta' \approx 47.2^\circ$$ Rounding to the nearest multiple of 5° in standard examination formatting yields 45°.

High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Questions)
  • Practice Q1 (Numerical): A limestone bed dips $30^\circ$ due East ($090^\circ$). What is its apparent dip in a railway cutting oriented toward $\text{N}30^\circ\text{E}$ ($030^\circ$)?
    ✔ Worked Answer: $\delta' \approx 16.1^\circ$.
    Calculation:
    Angle $\alpha = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$.
    $$\tan\delta' = \tan(30^\circ) \cdot \cos(60^\circ) = (0.5774) \times (0.5000) = 0.2887$$
    $$\delta' = \arctan(0.2887) \approx 16.1^\circ$$
  • Practice Q2: Two apparent dips of a bedding plane measured in two different quarry faces are $\delta_1' = 20^\circ$ toward South ($180^\circ$) and $\delta_2' = 20^\circ$ toward East ($090^\circ$). What is the true dip direction of the bed?
    ✔ Correct Answer: $\text{S}45^\circ\text{E}$ ($135^\circ$).
    Explanation: Because the two apparent dips are identical ($20^\circ$), the true dip direction must lie exactly midway between South and East, bisecting the $90^\circ$ angle at $135^\circ$ ($\text{S}45^\circ\text{E}$).
  • Practice Q3: In civil tunneling, why must tunnel engineers compute apparent dip rather than relying on true dip?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Because tunnels rarely drive perpendicular to regional strike; the apparent dip in the tunnel's vertical alignment governs roof stability and rock bolts.
    Explanation: The apparent dip dictates how bedding planes intersect the tunnel crown and sidewalls, controlling potential block daylighting and wedge failures.
  • Practice Q4: What is the apparent dip of any planar geological surface in a vertical section drawn parallel to the strike of the surface?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Exactly $0^\circ$ (horizontal).
    Explanation: Because $\alpha = 90^\circ$, $\cos(90^\circ) = 0$, meaning $\tan\delta' = 0 \implies \delta' = 0^\circ$. Layers appear perfectly horizontal in a strike cross-section.
  • Practice Q5: How can True Dip ($\delta$) be calculated if two apparent dips ($\delta_1', \delta_2'$) and their horizontal angle separation ($\beta$) are known?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Using Cotangent Nomograms or Stereonet intersection of two apparent dip pitch lines.
    Explanation: On a stereonet, plotting the two apparent dip lines as points defines a unique great circle whose dip angle represents the true dip.
  • Practice Q6: What happens to apparent dip if a vertical borehole intersects an inclined coal seam?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The apparent dip measured on the horizontal core face equals the True Dip.
    Explanation: The core axis is vertical, so the angle between the core axis and the bedding plane normal directly reveals true dip ($\delta = 90^\circ - \theta_{\text{core}}$).
  • Practice Q7 (Numerical): A fault has a true dip $\delta = 45^\circ$. At what angle $\alpha$ from the true dip direction will the apparent dip be exactly $30^\circ$?
    ✔ Worked Answer: $\alpha \approx 54.7^\circ$.
    Calculation:
    $$\cos\alpha = \frac{\tan(30^\circ)}{\tan(45^\circ)} = \frac{0.57735}{1.0000} = 0.57735 \implies \alpha = \arccos(0.57735) \approx 54.74^\circ$$
  • Practice Q8: Why is the true dip of a planar bed always steeper than any of its apparent dips?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Because true dip follows the gradient of steepest descent on the plane ($90^\circ$ to horizontal contour/strike lines).
    Explanation: Any oblique cross-section traverses a longer horizontal distance for the same vertical elevation drop, reducing the slope gradient ($\tan\delta' = \Delta z / \Delta x_{\text{oblique}} < \Delta z / \Delta x_{\text{true}}$).
