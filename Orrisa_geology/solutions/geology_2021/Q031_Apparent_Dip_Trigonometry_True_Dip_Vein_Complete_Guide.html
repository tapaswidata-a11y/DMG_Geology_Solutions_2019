Q31: Apparent Dip vs. True Dip Trigonometric Nomography

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: M.P. Billings (Structural Geology, Appendix B); Haakon Fossen (Ch. 2, pp. 28-30)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

31.1 Geometric Derivation of Apparent Dip

In structural geology and underground mining (Billings, Structural Geology, Appendix B; Fossen, Ch. 2; Van der Pluijm & Marshak, Ch. 1), true dip ($\delta$) is the maximum angle of inclination of a planar structure measured perpendicular to the strike line. In any other vertical cross-section oriented oblique to the true dip direction, the observed inclination is the apparent dip ($\delta'$), which is always less than the true dip:

$$\delta' < \delta \quad (\text{for any angle } \alpha > 0^\circ)$$

The universal trigonometric formula relating true dip ($\delta$), apparent dip ($\delta'$), and the angular deviation between their azimuth directions ($\alpha$) is:

$$\mathbf{\tan(\delta') = \tan(\delta) \times \cos(\alpha)}$$

Where:

  • $\delta$ = True dip angle (measured in the direction of true dip).
  • $\delta'$ = Apparent dip angle (measured along an oblique vertical section).
  • $\alpha = |\text{Azimuth}_{\text{true dip}} - \text{Azimuth}_{\text{apparent dip}}|$ = Horizontal angle between the true dip bearing and the apparent dip bearing.

31.2 Critical Limiting Cases

Bearing of Oblique Section Relative to True Dip Angular Offset ($\alpha$) $\cos(\alpha)$ Resulting Apparent Dip ($\delta'$)
Parallel to True Dip Direction $\alpha = 0^\circ$ $\cos(0^\circ) = 1.0$ $\tan\delta' = \tan\delta \implies \mathbf{\delta' = \delta}$ (Maximum inclination)
Oblique to True Dip Direction ($0^\circ < \alpha < 90^\circ$) e.g. $\alpha = 25^\circ$ $\cos(25^\circ) \approx 0.906$ $\mathbf{\delta' < \delta}$ (Intermediate apparent dip)
Parallel to Strike Direction $\alpha = 90^\circ$ $\cos(90^\circ) = 0.0$ $\tan\delta' = 0 \implies \mathbf{\delta' = 0^\circ}$ (Horizontal line)

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q31. A sulphide vein dips 50° toward N45°W. Its apparent dip toward of N20°W will be around _____.
(A) 65°
(B) 60°
(C) 0°
(D) 45°
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (D)

Correct Answer: (D) 45° — Applying the apparent dip equation for a vein dipping $\delta = 50^\circ$ toward $\text{N}45^\circ\text{W}$ when viewed toward $\text{N}20^\circ\text{W}$ gives an angular offset $\alpha = 25^\circ$, yielding an apparent dip of $\delta' \approx 47.2^\circ \approx \mathbf{45^\circ}$.

Step-by-Step Mathematical Calculation

1. Identify the given parameters:

  • True dip angle: $\delta = 50^\circ$
  • True dip direction: $\text{N}45^\circ\text{W}$ ($315^\circ$ azimuth)
  • Apparent dip direction: $\text{N}20^\circ\text{W}$ ($340^\circ$ azimuth)

2. Calculate horizontal angular difference $\alpha$:

$$\alpha = |45^\circ - 20^\circ| = 25^\circ$$

3. Apply the nomographic apparent dip formula:

$$\tan(\delta') = \tan(50^\circ) \times \cos(25^\circ)$$

Using standard trigonometric tables: $\tan(50^\circ) = 1.19175$, and $\cos(25^\circ) = 0.90631$:

$$\tan(\delta') = 1.19175 \times 0.90631 = 1.08009$$ $$\delta' = \arctan(1.08009) = 47.20^\circ$$

4. Match with exam options: Among the choices ($65^\circ, 60^\circ, 0^\circ, 45^\circ$), the only physically possible and closest answer is 45° (since apparent dip must be less than true dip $50^\circ$, options A and B are impossible, while $0^\circ$ is only along strike).

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) 65° — IMPOSSIBLE: Violates the fundamental geometric law of structural geology: apparent dip can NEVER exceed true dip ($\delta' \le \delta$). A $50^\circ$ vein cannot have an apparent dip of $65^\circ$.
  • (B) 60° — IMPOSSIBLE: Exceeds true dip ($60^\circ > 50^\circ$); physically impossible.
  • (C) 0° — INCORRECT: Apparent dip is only $0^\circ$ when viewed strictly parallel to strike (which would be $\text{N}45^\circ\text{E}$ or $\text{S}45^\circ\text{W}$), not $\text{N}20^\circ\text{W}$.
  • (D) 45° — CORRECT: The only mathematically sound option consistent with $\delta' = 47.2^\circ \approx 45^\circ$ and satisfying $\delta' < 50^\circ$.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

High-Yield Mining Nomogram Problem

Practice Problem 31: Apparent Dip for Underground Cross-Cut Tunnel

A coal seam has a true dip of $35^\circ$ due South ($180^\circ$). An underground haulage drift is driven along a bearing of $\text{S}60^\circ\text{W}$ ($240^\circ$). Calculate the apparent gradient (slope angle) of the coal seam along the wall of this haulage drift.

Solution:

1. The angular difference between true dip direction ($180^\circ$) and drift bearing ($240^\circ$) is:

$$\alpha = 240^\circ - 180^\circ = 60^\circ$$

2. Calculate apparent dip using $\tan(\delta') = \tan(\delta) \cos(\alpha)$:

$$\tan(\delta') = \tan(35^\circ) \times \cos(60^\circ) = 0.7002 \times 0.5000 = 0.3501$$ $$\delta' = \arctan(0.3501) \approx 19.29^\circ$$

The seam appears to dip at approximately $19.3^\circ$ along the haulage drift.
