Q69: Invertebrate Paleontology - Major Fossil Phyla & Dominance of Mollusca

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 344); Clarkson (Invertebrate Palaeontology)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. The Invertebrate Fossil Record & Major Phyla

The macroscopic sedimentary fossil record since the Cambrian Explosion (~538 Ma) is overwhelmingly dominated by marine invertebrates possessing biomineralized hard skeletons (calcite, aragonite, chitin-protein, or silica). In standard historical geology and invertebrate paleontology (G.B. Mahapatra, A Text-Book of Geology, Chapter 91, p. 344 / PDF p. 180; Clarkson, Invertebrate Palaeontology and Evolution, 4th ed., pp. 15–28), five major animal phyla constitute more than 95% of all described fossil invertebrates:

  1. Phylum Mollusca: The second-largest animal phylum, featuring bilaterally symmetrical coelomates typically enclosed in a calcareous shell secreted by the mantle. Encompasses the most biostratigraphically and paleoecologically vital fossil groups: Bivalvia (pelecypods/clams), Gastropoda (snails), and Cephalopoda (ammonoids, nautiloids, belemnites).
  2. Phylum Brachiopoda: Solitary, sessile lophophorates enclosed in a bivalved, equilateral/inequivalve shell with pedicle attachment. Dominant throughout the Paleozoic Era.
  3. Phylum Echinodermata: Exclusively marine deuterostomes with secondary pentameral radial symmetry and a mesodermal stereom endoskeleton of single-crystal high-Mg calcite plates (crinoids, echinoids, blastoids, asteroids).
  4. Phylum Arthropoda: Segmented animals with a chitinous/calcareous jointed exoskeleton and paired jointed appendages, dominated in the fossil record by the extinct subphylum Trilobita, alongside ostracods and eurypterids.
  5. Phylum Cnidaria: Radially symmetrical diploblastic metazoans possessing nematocysts, prominently represented by skeleton-secreting corals of Class Anthozoa (Rugosa, Tabulata, Scleractinia) and stromatoporoids.

When enumerating the premier categories of invertebrate fossils alongside brachiopods, echinoderms, arthropods, and cnidarians, Phylum Mollusca is the primary, taxonomically coordinate kingdom-level division.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q69. Common kinds of invertebrate fossils include __m_., brachiopods, echinoderms, arthropods, and cnidarians.
(A) molluscs
(B) nautiloids
(C) cephalopoda
(D) All of these

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "Common kinds of invertebrate fossils include ________, brachiopods, echinoderms, arthropods, and cnidarians."

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) molluscs (CORRECT): In biological taxonomy and invertebrate paleontology, the prompt lists coordinate animal phyla: Brachiopoda, Echinodermata, Arthropoda, and Cnidaria. The missing coordinate phylum that completes this foundational list is Phylum Mollusca (molluscs). Listing "molluscs" correctly captures gastropods, bivalves, and cephalopods at the appropriate taxonomic rank.
  • (B) nautiloids (INCORRECT / SUBORDINATE): Nautiloidea is merely a subclass within Class Cephalopoda of the Phylum Mollusca. It is subordinate in taxonomic rank and far too narrow to stand alongside coordinate phyla like Arthropoda or Echinodermata.
  • (C) cephalopoda (INCORRECT / SUBORDINATE): Cephalopoda is a class within Phylum Mollusca. Like nautiloids, it is subordinate to the phylum rank. Selecting cephalopoda would arbitrarily omit bivalves and gastropods.
  • (D) All of these (INCORRECT): While nautiloids and cephalopods are indeed molluscs, the grammatical and taxonomic construction of the prompt demands the phylum-level taxon (molluscs). Coordinate taxonomic series require matching ranks.
