Q20: Topographic Remote Sensing: The Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM)

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Farr et al. (2007, Reviews of Geophysics); Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman (Remote Sensing, Ch. 7, pp. 520-525)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

20.1 Principle of Single-Pass InSAR on SRTM

In radar remote sensing and geomorphometry (Farr et al., 2007, The Shuttle Radar Topography Mission, Reviews of Geophysics; Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman, Ch. 7, pp. 520–525), the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) was flown in February 2000 aboard Space Shuttle *Endeavour* (STS-99).

SRTM revolutionized Earth observation by acquiring the first near-global high-precision three-dimensional Digital Elevation Model (DEM) using single-pass Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR):

Mission Characteristic SRTM Operational Configuration
Interferometric Baseline ($B$) $60\text{-meter}$ deployable carbon-fiber truss mast extending from shuttle cargo bay
Antenna Configuration Dual-antenna single-pass InSAR (Main antenna in payload bay; Outboard antenna at mast tip)
Radar Frequencies C-band ($\lambda = 5.6\text{ cm}$, NASA/JPL) and X-band ($\lambda = 3.1\text{ cm}$, DLR/ASI)
Global Coverage & Resolution $80\%$ of Earth's landmass ($60^\circ\text{N}$ to $56^\circ\text{S}$); 1-arc-second ($pprox 30\text{ m}$) resolution

20.2 InSAR Height Derivation Formula

Interferometric phase difference ($\Delta \phi$) between the two antennas directly determines topographic surface height ($z$):

$$z = H - R \cos \theta, \quad \text{where } \Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} B \sin(\theta - \alpha)$$

Eliminating temporal decorrelation via simultaneous single-pass acquisition enabled unprecedented 3D elevation accuracy.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q20. Name the satellite providing three-dimensional topographic data :
(A) LANDSAT
(B) SRTM
(C) IKONOS
(D) ERS
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) SRTM — The Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) is the dedicated space radar mission engineered specifically to provide high-resolution three-dimensional topographic elevation data (DEMs) globally.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) LANDSAT — INCORRECT: Landsat is a 2D optical multispectral imaging satellite providing surface reflectance, not direct 3D elevation data.
  • (B) SRTM — CORRECT: The benchmark space mission for global 3D topographic models.
  • (C) IKONOS — INCORRECT: A high-resolution 2D commercial optical imaging satellite ($0.82\text{ m}$ panchromatic).
  • (D) ERS — INCORRECT: European Remote Sensing satellite; while capable of repeat-pass InSAR, it is not a dedicated single-pass global 3D topographic mission like SRTM.

Digital Elevation Model (DEM) Practice Problem

Practice Problem 20: Digital Elevation Data Comparison

Compare SRTM DEM with ASTER GDEM and Cartosat-1 DEM in terms of remote sensing technology.

Answer:

  • SRTM: Generated via Active Radar Interferometry (InSAR) at C-band wavelengths, penetrating clouds and haze.
  • ASTER GDEM: Generated via Passive Optical Stereoscopy from nadir and backward-looking telescopes ($3N$ and $3B$ bands).
  • Cartosat-1 (ISRO): Generated via High-resolution along-track Optical Stereoscopy using Fore ($+26^\circ$) and Aft ($-5^\circ$) cameras.
