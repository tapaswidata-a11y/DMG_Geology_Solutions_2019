2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Himalayan Tectonic Architecture

The Himalayan orogen was built by the collision of India with Eurasia and is segmented from north to south by major longitudinal fault and thrust systems:

Tectonic Boundary Hanging Wall (North) Footwall (South) Kinematic & Metamorphic Character
STDS (South Tibetan Detachment System) Tethyan Sedimentary Series Higher Himalayan Crystallines North-dipping low-angle normal fault (extensional collapse)
MCT (Main Central Thrust) Higher (Great) Himalayan Crystallines (HHC) Lesser Himalayan Sequence (LHS) Major ductile shear zone; places amphibolite-granulite facies inverted metamorphic rocks over greenschist-grade LHS.
MBT (Main Boundary Thrust) Lesser Himalayan Sequence Sub-Himalayan Siwalik Group Brittle thrust faulting of pre-Tertiary LHS over Neogene molasse.
MFT / HFT (Main / Himalayan Frontal Thrust) Sub-Himalayan Siwaliks Indo-Gangetic Alluvial Plain Active southernmost blind/emergent thrust fault separating Siwaliks from the foreland basin.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A (MCT) is correct: The MCT is the definitive boundary placing the High-grade Great Himalayan Crystalline complex over the Lesser Himalayan Sequence.
  • Option B & D (MFT / HFT): The frontal thrust between the Siwalik hills and the Indo-Gangetic alluvium.
  • Option C (MBT): The boundary between the Lesser Himalaya and the Sub-Himalaya (Siwaliks).
