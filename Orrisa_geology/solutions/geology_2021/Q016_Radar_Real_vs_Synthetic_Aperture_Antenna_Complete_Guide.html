Q16: Radar Systems: Real Aperture Radar (RAR) vs. Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman (Remote Sensing, Ch. 7, pp. 488-496); Curlander & McDonough (Synthetic Aperture Radar)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

16.1 The Azimuth Resolution Paradox in Spaceborne Radar

In radar remote sensing (Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman, Remote Sensing, Ch. 7, pp. 488–496; Curlander & McDonough, Synthetic Aperture Radar), radar pulses achieve range (cross-track) resolution via pulse duration ($\tau$): $R_r = \frac{c \tau}{2 \sin \theta}$.

However, along-track (azimuth) resolution ($R_a$) for a Real Aperture Radar (RAR) depends directly on the beamwidth ($\beta$) and physical antenna length ($L$):

$$R_{a,\text{RAR}} = R \cdot \beta = \frac{\lambda \cdot R}{L}$$

Where $R$ is slant range, $\lambda$ is radar wavelength, and $L$ is the physical length of the antenna.

16.2 Why RAR Fails from Space and How SAR Solves It

For a spaceborne satellite at slant range $R = 800\text{ km}$ operating at C-band ($\lambda = 0.056\text{ m}$), achieving a modest ground azimuth resolution of $R_a = 10\text{ m}$ with RAR would require a physical antenna length of:

$$L = \frac{\lambda \cdot R}{R_a} = \frac{0.056\text{ m} \times 800,000\text{ m}}{10\text{ m}} = 4,480\text{ meters} = 4.48\text{ km!}$$

Launching a 4.5-kilometer rigid antenna into orbit is physically impossible.

Parameter Real Aperture Radar (RAR) Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)
Antenna Configuration Physical antenna only; beam determined by real aperture length $L$ Synthesizes a virtual antenna kilometers long using spacecraft forward motion and Doppler phase histories
Azimuth Resolution $R_a = \frac{\lambda R}{L}$ (degrades with distance) $R_a = \frac{L}{2}$ (completely independent of distance and wavelength!)
Spaceborne Feasibility Impractical from orbit (requires km-sized antenna) Universal orbital standard (Sentinel-1, NISAR, ALOS)

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q16. Major difference between Real Aperture Radar and Synthetic Aperture Radar is in their :
(A) Size of the Antenna
(B) Source of energy
(C) Recording of data
(D) None of these
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (A)

Correct Answer: (A) Size of the Antenna — The major fundamental difference between Real Aperture Radar (RAR) and Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) lies in the size of the antenna (physical real antenna vs. motion-synthesized long virtual aperture).

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Size of the Antenna — CORRECT: In RAR, resolution is constrained by physical antenna dimensions ($R_a \propto 1/L$), whereas SAR electronically synthesizes an antenna hundreds of times longer than its physical size.
  • (B) Source of energy — INCORRECT: Both RAR and SAR are active sensors that supply their own microwave energy transmitters (magnetrons / TWT / solid-state amplifiers).
  • (C) Recording of data — INCORRECT: Both record returning electromagnetic echoes, though SAR records coherent Doppler phase.
  • (D) None of these — INCORRECT.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

SAR Azimuth Resolution Proof

Practice Problem 16: Theoretical Azimuth Resolution of SAR

A satellite carries a Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) with a physical antenna length of $L = 10.0\text{ meters}$. What is the theoretical maximum azimuth resolution achievable by this SAR system?

Solution:

For a fully focused synthetic aperture radar, the azimuth resolution is simply half the physical antenna length:

$$R_a = \frac{L}{2} = \frac{10.0\text{ m}}{2} = 5.0\text{ meters}$$

Remarkably, this $5\text{ m}$ resolution is independent of orbital altitude ($H$) and radar wavelength ($\lambda$).
