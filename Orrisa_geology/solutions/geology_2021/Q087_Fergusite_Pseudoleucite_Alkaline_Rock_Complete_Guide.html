1. Definition, History & Petrographic Identity of Fergusite

The rock name fergusite was introduced in 1905 by the eminent petrologist Louis Valentine Pirsson (U.S. Geological Survey Bulletin 237) from its type locality in Upper Fergus County, Highwood Mountains, Montana, USA.

According to the IUGS Subcommission on the Systematics of Igneous Rocks (Le Maitre, 2002; Winter, 2014, p. 28), fergusite is formally defined as:

IUGS Definition of Fergusite:
$$\mathbf{\text{Fergusite}} = \text{A potassic foidolitic plutonic rock composed of roughly } \mathbf{70\%\ \text{Pseudoleucite}} \text{ and } \mathbf{30\%\ \text{Clinopyroxene (Aegirine-Augite / Diopside)}}$$

It occupies Field 15 (Foidolites) of the IUGS QAPF classification, representing the coarse-grained plutonic equivalent of a leucite nephelinite or leucitite, closely affiliated with the potassic shonkinite-missourite intrusive series.

Highlighting the ultra-alkaline Foidolite field where nepheline, kalsilite, and pseudoleucite rocks (such as Fergusite and Ijolite) plot.
2. The Nature of Pseudoleucite: Subsolidus Trapezohedral Breakdown

Leucite ($\text{KAlSi}_2\text{O}_6$) is an ultra-potassic, silica-deficient feldspathoid. While stable at high temperatures ($>1000^\circ\text{C}$) in subvolcanic lavas, primary sodic leucite solid solution becomes thermodynamically unstable at lower subsolidus temperatures during slow plutonic cooling, or under elevated water vapor pressures.

The Pseudoleucite Intergrowth Reaction

Under plutonic cooling, primary leucite unmixes and reacts with residual interstitial melt, decomposing into a fine-grained, multiphase microcrystalline aggregate termed pseudoleucite:

Subsolidus Breakdown of Leucite Solid Solution:
$$\mathbf{2\text{KAlSi}_2\text{O}_6\ (\text{Leucite})} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8\ (\text{Potassium Feldspar})} + \mathbf{\text{KAlSiO}_4\ (\text{Kalsilite})}$$
$$\text{When sodium is present: } \text{Leucite}_{\text{ss}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Orthoclase}} + \mathbf{\text{Nepheline}} + \mathbf{\text{Analcime}}$$

Crucially, this replacement occurs pseudomorphically: the resulting intergrowth of alkali feldspar, nepheline, kalsilite, and minor analcime faithfully preserves the iconic trapezohedral (icositetrahedral, $\{211\}$) crystal geometry of the original high-temperature leucite crystals.

3. Petrographic Demarcation: Fergusite vs. Fenite, Felsite & Ferrilite

The multiple-choice options present phonetically similar petrological terms that represent entirely different geological processes:

  • Fergusite: A primary, potassic ultra-alkalic plutonic igneous rock containing $70\%$ pseudoleucite and $30\%$ pyroxene.
  • Fenite: A metasomatic contact-metamorphic rock produced by the high-temperature alkaline fluid alteration (fenitization) of granitic country rocks bordering carbonatite and alkaline ring complexes, dominated by secondary aegirine, sodic amphibole, and albite/microperthite.
  • Felsite: A generic field term for any very fine-grained, light-colored volcanic or hypabyssal rock (e.g., devitrified rhyolite or microgranite) composed of a microcrystalline groundmass of quartz and feldspar, having an acidic silica content ($SiO_2 > 70\%$).
  • Ferrilite: A non-standard, obsolete historical term occasionally applied to iron-rich sedimentary sandstones or ironstone-cemented rocks, completely devoid of feldspathoids.

Official Examination Question
Question 87
The plutonic rock consisting of roughly 70% pseudoleucite (alkali feldspar, nepheline, kalsilite and minor analcime) and 30% pyroxene is termed as :
(A) Ferrilite
(B) Fenite
(C) Fergusite
(D) Felsite
CORRECT ANSWER: (C)
Fergusite: The definitive potassic foidolitic plutonic rock composed of approximately 70% pseudoleucite and 30% clinopyroxene, named from Fergus County, Montana.
Forensic Analysis of Answer Choices
(A) Ferrilite — INCORRECT

Ferrilite is an obsolete or informal term for iron-rich sedimentary lithologies or ferriferous rocks. It is completely unrelated to potassic alkaline foidolitic plutonism.

(B) Fenite — INCORRECT

Fenite is a metasomatically altered rock formed in the contact aureole surrounding carbonatite-ijolite complexes via alkali metasomatism (fenitization). It consists of desilicated quartz-free country rocks enriched in aegirine and alkali feldspar, but does not constitute the primary 70% pseudoleucite plutonic rock described in the question.

(C) Fergusite — CORRECT

Fergusite (Pirsson, 1905; Le Maitre, 2002, p. 80) is the exact, standard international petrographic term for a plutonic rock consisting of roughly 70% pseudoleucite (intergrowth of alkali feldspar, nepheline, kalsilite, and minor analcime) and 30% pyroxene (aegirine-augite). It is the textbook archetype of a potassic foidolite (Winter, 2014, p. 28).

(D) Felsite — INCORRECT

Felsite is a broad field term for light-colored, microcrystalline or cryptocrystalline felsic volcanic/hypabyssal rocks (such as rhyolite or dacite) consisting of quartz and feldspar, entirely lacking the potassic feldspathoids and coarse pseudoleucite crystals characteristic of fergusite.

Exam-Standard Analytical & Quantitative Practice Problems
Problem 1: Leucite Solid Solution Subsolidus Decomposition Reaction

Write the balanced chemical reaction demonstrating how a sodium-bearing leucite solid solution of composition $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Na}_{0.5}\text{AlSi}_2\text{O}_6$ unmixes into potassium feldspar and nepheline to form pseudoleucite.

Stoichiometric Reaction:
$$\mathbf{2\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Na}_{0.5}\text{AlSi}_2\text{O}_6\ (\text{Na-Leucite Solid Solution})} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8\ (\text{K-Feldspar})} + \mathbf{\text{NaAlSiO}_4\ (\text{Nepheline})}$$
Mass Balance Verification:
- Reactants: $1\text{ K} + 1\text{ Na} + 2\text{ Al} + 4\text{ Si} + 12\text{ O}$.
- Products: ($1\text{ K} + 1\text{ Al} + 3\text{ Si} + 8\text{ O}$) + ($1\text{ Na} + 1\text{ Al} + 1\text{ Si} + 4\text{ O}$) = $1\text{ K} + 1\text{ Na} + 2\text{ Al} + 4\text{ Si} + 12\text{ O}$.
The stoichiometry balances perfectly, proving that sodic leucite decomposes into equimolar orthoclase and nepheline within pseudoleucite grains.
Problem 2: Potassium/Sodium Molecular Ratio in Fergusite Petrogenesis

A bulk geochemical analysis of fergusite yields: $\text{K}_2\text{O} = 9.8\text{ wt}\%$ (molecular weight = $94.20\text{ g/mol}$) and $\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 2.4\text{ wt}\%$ (molecular weight = $61.98\text{ g/mol}$). Calculate the molecular $\text{K}_2\text{O}/\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ ratio and state whether the rock belongs to the potassic or sodic alkaline series.

Molar Ratio Calculation:
1. Moles of $\text{K}_2\text{O}$: $$n(\text{K}_2\text{O}) = \frac{9.8}{94.20} \approx \mathbf{0.1040\text{ mol}}$$
2. Moles of $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$: $$n(\text{Na}_2\text{O}) = \frac{2.4}{61.98} \approx \mathbf{0.0387\text{ mol}}$$
3. Molecular Ratio: $$\frac{\text{Molar }\text{K}_2\text{O}}{\text{Molar }\text{Na}_2\text{O}} = \frac{0.1040}{0.0387} \approx \mathbf{2.69}$$
Classification: Since the molecular ratio $\mathbf{K_2O/Na_2O = 2.69 \gg 1.0}$, the rock is classified as Ultra-Potassic, in sharp contrast to sodic foidolites like ijolite where $Na_2O/K_2O \gg 1.0$.
Problem 3: High-Pressure Instability of Leucite

In experimental petrology, leucite is never found in deep mantle xenoliths or high-pressure metamorphic rocks. Using the reaction $\text{KAlSi}_2\text{O}_6\ (\text{Leucite}) \rightleftharpoons \text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8\ (\text{Sanidine}) + \text{KAlSiO}_4\ (\text{Kalsilite})$, explain the pressure limit on leucite stability.

Thermodynamic Pressure Limit:
- Leucite has an exceptionally open, framework crystal structure with high molar volume ($V_m = 88.4\text{ cm}^3/\text{mol}$).
- Under elevated pressure ($P > 0.5\text{--}0.8\text{ GPa}$, depths $> 15\text{--}25\text{ km}$ in the presence of water), Leucite breaks down into a higher-density assemblage of Sanidine + Kalsilite, or reacts with water to form Analcime.
- Consequently, leucite can only precipitate in shallow crustal magma chambers or subaerial volcanic lavas ($P < 0.5\text{ GPa}$).
Problem 4: Microscopic Identification of Trapezohedral Pseudoleucite

Describe the optical petrographic appearance of a pseudoleucite phenocryst under plane-polarized light (PPL) and cross-polarized light (XPL).

Petrographic Description:
- Plane-Polarized Light (PPL): The grain displays a sharp, eight-sided or rounded polygonal outline representing the external crystal faces of a trapezohedron (icositetrahedron). Internally, the grain is turbid, cloudy, and filled with tiny microscopic fluid inclusions and dust-like particles.
- Cross-Polarized Light (XPL): The original isotropic character of cubic leucite is completely gone! Instead, the crystal exhibits a fine, mottled, intergrown mosaic of low-birefringence orthoclase and nepheline displaying first-order grey interference colors, forming delicate sub-radial or vermicular micro-patches within the trapezohedral boundary.
Problem 5: Geotectonic Setting of the Montana Alkaline Province

Where and in what tectonic setting did the type fergusite of the Highwood Mountains (Montana) form?

Regional Tectonics:
- Geotectonic Setting: The Central Montana Alkalic Province represents an Eocene back-arc / intraplate extensional magmatic province situated in the foreland of the North American Cordillera (~50–45 Ma).
- Petrogenesis: Magmas were generated by low-degree partial melting of ancient Archean sub-continental lithospheric mantle (SCLM) that had been heavily metasomatized by potassic fluids during earlier Proterozoic subduction events.
Problem 6: Fenite vs. Fergusite Diagnostic Criteria

State three definitive field and petrographic criteria that enable an exploration geologist to distinguish a fergusite from a fenite.

Threefold Distinction:
1. Rock Origin: Fergusite is a primary igneous pluton; Fenite is an in-situ metasomatic replacement rock formed around intrusive margins.
2. Diagnostic Texture: Fergusite exhibits panidiomorphic or hypidiomorphic igneous texture with trapezohedral pseudoleucite phenocrysts; Fenite exhibits relict gneissic/granitic foliation overprinted by radiating fibrous sheaves of aegirine and riebeckite.
3. Mineral Composition: Fergusite contains up to $70\%$ pseudoleucite (nepheline + kalsilite); Fenite is dominated by secondary albite, perthitic microcline, and aegirine, with quartz leached away but feldspathoids rarely reaching $70\%$.
Problem 7: Agpaitic vs. Miaskitic Nature of Potassic Rocks

Calculate the peralkalinity index of a fergusite containing molecular $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 0.110\text{ mol}$, $\text{K}_2\text{O} = 0.085\text{ mol}$, and $\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 0.020\text{ mol}$, and determine whether it is agpaitic or miaskitic.

Calculation:
$$\text{Alkali Sum} = \text{K}_2\text{O} + \text{Na}_2\text{O} = 0.085 + 0.020 = 0.105\text{ mol}$$
$$\text{Agpaitic Index } (AI) = \frac{\text{K}_2\text{O} + \text{Na}_2\text{O}}{\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3} = \frac{0.105}{0.110} \approx \mathbf{0.955}$$
Classification: Since $\mathbf{AI = 0.955 < 1.0}$, total alkalis do not exceed alumina; hence the rock is classified as Miaskitic (subaluminous to metaluminous alkaline), where all alkalis are consumed in forming feldspars and feldspathoids, and excess alumina is bound in pyroxene/amphibole.
Problem 8: Origin of Ultra-Potassic Primary Magmas via Phlogopite Melting

Explain why melting of a phlogopite-bearing mantle peridotite at depths $> 100\text{ km}$ generates potassic magmas (like the parent of fergusite), whereas melting of amphibole-bearing mantle generates sodic magmas.

Mantle Mineral Partitioning:
- Phlogopite ($\text{KMg}_3\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10}(\text{OH})_2$): The premier potassic hydrous phase in the deep sub-continental lithospheric mantle ($P > 3\text{ GPa}$, depths $> 100\text{ km}$). When phlogopite melts incongruently, it releases massive concentrations of potassium ($K_2O$), rubidium, and barium into the melt, yielding ultra-potassic liquids ($K_2O \gg Na_2O$) that crystallize as fergusite, leucitite, or lamproite.
- Pargasitic Amphibole: Sodic hydrous phase stable only at shallower mantle depths ($P < 2.5\text{ GPa}$, depths $< 75\text{ km}$). Melting of pargasite releases sodium ($Na_2O$), generating sodic alkali basalts, basanites, and nephelinites.
