1. Historical Discovery & Mineralogical Definition of Charnockite

The rock name Charnockite holds a historic and honored position in Indian and international petrology. It was introduced in 1893 and comprehensively established in 1900 by Sir Thomas H. Holland, Director of the Geological Survey of India (GSI).

Holland named the rock in memory of Job Charnock, the founder of modern Kolkata (Calcutta). In 1892, Holland discovered that Job Charnock's monumental tombstone in St. John's Churchyard, Kolkata (erected in 1695), was carved from a distinctive, dark, greasy, hypersthene-bearing granitic rock that had been quarried and transported from St. Thomas Mount at Pallavaram, near Chennai (Madras), Tamil Nadu.

Figure 80.1 — Essential Mineralogy & Granulitic Fabric of Charnockite: Authentic textbook scan from G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 140). Showing the essential granoblastic mineral assemblage of hypersthene orthopyroxene, perthitic potassium feldspar, and quartz, along with its characteristic greasy, waxy dark luster.

Mineralogical Definition of Charnockite (sensu stricto)

According to both Holland's classical definition and the modern IUGS Subcommission on the Systematics of Metamorphic/Igneous Rocks:

Charnockite (sensu stricto) = Hypersthene Granite
$$\mathbf{\text{Essential Minerals}} = \mathbf{\text{Hypersthene (Orthopyroxene)}} + \mathbf{\text{Potassium Feldspar (Microperthite)}} + \mathbf{\text{Quartz}} \pm \text{Oligoclase/Andesine}$$

Megascopic Diagnostic Features

  • Color & Luster: Despite containing over $70\text{ wt}\% \text{SiO}_2$ (an acidic, felsic chemistry), charnockite displays an anomalously dark greenish-grey, bluish-grey, or greasy dark brown to black coloration with a diagnostic greasy, waxy, or vitreous luster.
  • Cause of Dark Coloration: Under high-resolution transmission electron microscopy, this greasy dark appearance is produced by dense networks of submicroscopic exsolution lamellae, micro-fracture fillings of carbonic and ferruginous fluids, and submicron-scale acicular inclusions of rutile and ilmenite inside quartz and feldspar crystals.
  • Fabric: Typically exhibits a medium-to-coarse granoblastic equigranular (granulitic) texture, frequently displaying coarse metamorphic gneissose foliation.
2. Holland's Charnockite Series & Petrographic Classification

Sir Thomas Holland recognized that charnockites do not occur in isolation but form a complete, cogenetic consanguineous differentiation suite spanning from acid to ultrabasic compositions, all unified by the constant presence of orthopyroxene (hypersthene):

The Four Divisions of the Charnockite Series (Holland, 1900)

  1. Acid Division (Charnockite sensu stricto): The hypersthene granite ($SiO_2 > 70\%$). Dominated by quartz, microcline/orthoclase microperthite, and pleochroic hypersthene, with subordinate oligoclase.
  2. Intermediate Division (Enderbite / Mangerite): Quartz diorite or monzonite equivalents ($SiO_2 \approx 60\text{--}70\%$). In Enderbite (Tilley, 1936), plagioclase (antiperthitic andesine/oligoclase) vastly exceeds K-feldspar ($Plag \gg Kfs$).
  3. Basic Division (Norite / 2-Pyroxene Granulite): Basaltic/gabbroic equivalent ($SiO_2 \approx 48\text{--}55\%$). Composed of calcic plagioclase (labradorite), hypersthene, and clinopyroxene (augite/diopside), with or without garnet; quartz and K-feldspar are absent.
  4. Ultrabasic Division (Pyroxenite / Bahite): Ultramafic end-member ($SiO_2 < 45\%$). Composed entirely of hypersthene, diopside, hornblende, and magnetite, with zero feldspar.
3. Metamorphic Petrogenesis: Granulite Facies & Carbonic Fluid Influx

While Holland originally interpreted charnockites as an igneous plutonic suite, modern petrology conclusively proves that they are high-grade regional metamorphic rocks formed under Granulite Facies conditions ($T \approx 750\text{--}900^\circ\text{C}$, $P \approx 6\text{--}10\text{ kbar}$, depths of $20\text{--}35\text{ km}$ in the lower continental crust).

Dehydration Breakdown of Hydrous Minerals

At granulite facies temperatures, hydrous phyllosilicates (biotite and hornblende) become thermodynamically unstable in the presence of quartz and undergo vapor-absent dehydration melting or breakdown, forming anhydrous orthopyroxene:

Biotite Dehydration Reaction:
$$\text{K}(\text{Mg,Fe})_3\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10}(\text{OH})_2\ (\text{Biotite}) + 3\text{SiO}_2\ (\text{Quartz}) \rightleftharpoons 3(\text{Mg,Fe})\text{SiO}_3\ (\mathbf{\text{Hypersthene}}) + \text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8\ (\mathbf{\text{K-feldspar}}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

The "Incipient Charnockite" Phenomenon at Kabbaldurga (Karnataka)

In a landmark paper, Janardhan, Newton & Smith (1979) documented the genesis of "incipient charnockite" at Kabbaldurga quarry in the Dharwar Craton. Foliated, grey amphibolite-facies Peninsular Gneiss containing biotite and hornblende is transformed along fracture-controlled patches and veins into coarse, unfoliated, greasy dark charnockite.

Fluid inclusion studies confirmed that this dehydration was driven by an active influx of mantle-derived, juvenile, high-density $CO_2$-rich fluids. The massive influx of $CO_2$ flushed out water, reducing the water activity ($a_{H_2O} < 0.35$). Because $a_{H_2O}$ was depressed, the biotite dehydration curve was drawn down to lower temperatures, precipitating hypersthene + K-feldspar + quartz in situ.

Official Examination Question
Question 80
What is the composition of Charnockite ?
(A) Hypersthene + K-feldspar + Quartz
(B) Hornblende + K-feldspar + Quartz
(C) Sillimanite + K-feldspar + Quartz
(D) Epidote + Plagioclase + Quartz
CORRECT ANSWER: (A)
Hypersthene + K-feldspar + Quartz: Charnockite (sensu stricto) is canonically defined as a hypersthene granite, characterized by the essential anhydrous mineral assemblage of orthopyroxene (hypersthene), potassium feldspar (perthitic microcline), and quartz.
Forensic Analysis of Answer Choices
(A) Hypersthene + K-feldspar + Quartz — CORRECT

This is the exact, canonical definition established by Sir Thomas Holland (1900) and maintained in all international metamorphic and igneous classifications (Winter, 2014, p. 468; Mahapatra, p. 140). The defining diagnostic mineral that separates charnockite from all ordinary granites is the orthopyroxene hypersthene, coexisting with potassium feldspar (perthite) and quartz under high-grade granulite facies conditions.

(B) Hornblende + K-feldspar + Quartz — INCORRECT

An assemblage of hornblende + K-feldspar + quartz represents an ordinary hornblende granite or amphibolite-facies granitic gneiss. It lacks the defining orthopyroxene (hypersthene). In fact, charnockite forms when hornblende breaks down and dehydrates into hypersthene.

(C) Sillimanite + K-feldspar + Quartz — INCORRECT

This aluminous, pelitic granulite assemblage is diagnostic of Khondalite (a quartz-manganese-garnet-sillimanite schist/gneiss named after the Khond tribes of Odisha and Eastern Ghats), or a metapelitic restite. It represents a metamorphosed aluminous clay/shale, not the quartzofeldspathic hypersthene rock charnockite.

(D) Epidote + Plagioclase + Quartz — INCORRECT

This hydrous, low-to-medium grade assemblage is characteristic of greenschist or epidote-amphibolite facies metabasites and epidote tonalites. Epidote is completely unstable at granulite facies temperatures and pressures, breaking down at $T > 550\text{--}650^\circ\text{C}$ long before charnockite forms.

Exam-Standard Analytical & Quantitative Practice Problems
Problem 1: Stoichiometric Biotite Dehydration Reaction & Gibbs Free Energy

Write the balanced chemical reaction for the breakdown of pure phlogopite biotite in the presence of quartz to form enstatite and K-feldspar. Given standard free energies of formation ($\Delta G^\circ_f$): Phlogopite = $-5,835\text{ kJ/mol}$, Quartz = $-856.3\text{ kJ/mol}$, Enstatite = $-1,460.8\text{ kJ/mol}$, K-feldspar = $-3,742.9\text{ kJ/mol}$, and $H_2O\text{ (steam)} = -228.6\text{ kJ/mol}$. Calculate the standard Gibbs free energy of the reaction ($\Delta G^\circ_{\text{rxn}}$) at 298.15 K.

Step-by-Step Thermodynamic Derivation:
The balanced dehydration reaction is: $$\text{KMg}_3\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10}(\text{OH})_2\ (\text{Phl}) + 3\text{SiO}_2\ (\text{Qtz}) \longrightarrow 3\text{MgSiO}_3\ (\text{En}) + \text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8\ (\text{Kfs}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
1. Calculate $\sum \Delta G^\circ_f(\text{Reactants})$: $$\sum \Delta G^\circ_f(\text{reactants}) = -5,835 + 3(-856.3) = -5,835 - 2,568.9 = -8,403.9\text{ kJ}$$
2. Calculate $\sum \Delta G^\circ_f(\text{Products})$: $$\sum \Delta G^\circ_f(\text{products}) = 3(-1,460.8) + (-3,742.9) + (-228.6) = -4,382.4 - 3,742.9 - 228.6 = -8,353.9\text{ kJ}$$
3. Calculate $\Delta G^\circ_{\text{rxn}}$: $$\Delta G^\circ_{\text{rxn}} = \sum \Delta G^\circ_f(\text{products}) - \sum \Delta G^\circ_f(\text{reactants}) = -8,353.9 - (-8,403.9) = \mathbf{+50.0\text{ kJ/mol}}$$ Physical Interpretation: At 298 K, $\Delta G^\circ > 0$, meaning hydrous biotite + quartz is stable at surface conditions. Because the reaction produces steam ($\Delta S_{\text{rxn}} \gg 0$), the $-T\Delta S$ term dominates at high temperature ($T > 750^\circ\text{C}$), driving $\Delta G_{\text{rxn}}$ negative and precipitating hypersthene charnockite.
Problem 2: Thermodynamic Effect of Water Activity ($a_{H_2O}$) on Charnockitization

The equilibrium constant for the biotite dehydration reaction is given by $\ln K = -\frac{\Delta G^\circ}{RT}$. If the solid phases are pure minerals ($a_{\text{solids}} = 1$), express the equilibrium temperature ($T_{\text{eq}}$) as a function of water activity ($a_{H_2O}$). Explain why $CO_2$ flushing induces "incipient charnockite" formation at Kabbaldurga.

Thermodynamic Fluid Activity Derivation:
The equilibrium constant expression is: $$K = \frac{a_{\text{En}}^3 \cdot a_{\text{Kfs}} \cdot a_{H_2O}}{a_{\text{Phl}} \cdot a_{\text{Qtz}}^3} = a_{H_2O}$$
$$\Delta G^\circ(T) = -R T \ln(a_{H_2O}) \implies \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ = -R T \ln(a_{H_2O})$$
Solving for equilibrium temperature $T$: $$T = \frac{\Delta H^\circ}{\Delta S^\circ - R \ln(a_{H_2O})}$$
Kabbaldurga Mechanism: Influx of juvenile, mantle-derived $CO_2$ dilutes intergranular metamorphic pore fluids, drastically lowering water activity from $a_{H_2O} \approx 1.0$ down to $a_{H_2O} \approx 0.2\text{--}0.3$. Because $\ln(a_{H_2O})$ becomes a large negative number, the denominator increases, lowering the equilibrium dehydration temperature by $100\text{--}150^\circ\text{C}$. Consequently, ambient amphibolite-facies gneisses at $\sim 700^\circ\text{C}$ are instantly dehydrated into hypersthene charnockite without requiring any addition of heat!
Problem 3: Optical Mineralogy: Hypersthene Pleochroic Formula

State the diagnostic optical properties of hypersthene under the petrographic microscope, including its axial character, extinction angle, and characteristic pleochroic colors along its three vibration axes ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$).

Optical Properties of Hypersthene (Ferroan Enstatite):
- Crystal System: Orthorhombic ($P_{bca}$).
- Extinction: Parallel (straight) extinction ($0^\circ$) to prismatic $\{110\}$ cleavage in longitudinal sections.
- Cleavage: Two sets of distinct prismatic cleavages intersecting at approximately $87^\circ$ and $93^\circ$ in basal cross-sections.
- Optic Sign: Biaxial Negative ($2V \approx 50\text{--}80^\circ$).
- Diagnostic Pleochroism: The most famous pleochroism in petrology:
- $\alpha\ (X) = \mathbf{\text{Pink to brownish-red / salmon pink}}$
- $\beta\ (Y) = \mathbf{\text{Pale yellow to greenish-yellow}}$
- $\gamma\ (Z) = \mathbf{\text{Pale green to emerald green}}$
Problem 4: Petrographic Discrimination: Charnockite vs. Enderbite

A granulite facies rock contains $25\%$ quartz, $15\%$ hypersthene, $55\%$ antiperthitic plagioclase ($\text{An}_{32}$), and only $5\%$ microcline. Classify this rock into Holland's terminology and modern IUGS nomenclature.

Classification Derivation:
- Felsic Ratio: Quartz ($Q = 25\%$), Plagioclase ($P = 55\%$), Alkali Feldspar ($A = 5\%$).
- In the QAP classification, $P / (P + A) = \frac{55}{55 + 5} = \frac{55}{60} = \mathbf{91.7\%}$. This plots directly in Field 5 (Tonalite / Quartz Diorite).
- Taxonomic Designation: Because plagioclase vastly predominates over K-feldspar ($P \gg A$) and the mafic mineral is hypersthene, the rock is an Enderbite (Tilley, 1936), the plagioclase-dominant member of the Charnockitic suite, distinct from Charnockite sensu stricto where $A \ge P$.
Problem 5: Physical Cause of the Greasy Luster and Dark Color

Why do acid charnockites ($SiO_2 > 70\%$) appear dark olive-green or greasy dark brown in hand specimen, whereas common calc-alkaline granites of identical silica content appear light pink, white, or light grey?

Solid-State Physics and Optical Scattering:
1. Submicroscopic Inclusions: Charnockites formed at deep lower crustal levels under high confining pressures ($>7\text{ kbar}$). Slow cooling under anhydrous conditions caused $Fe$ and $Ti$ dissolved in the feldspar and quartz lattices at high temperature to precipitate as billions of submicron-scale needles of ilmenite ($\text{FeTiO}_3$) and rutile ($\text{TiO}_2$).
2. Micro-Fracture Hydrocarbon/Fluid Films: Intergranular grain boundaries and micro-cracks host high-density carbonic fluid inclusions and thin films of graphite/chlorite/iron-oxides.
3. Rayleigh & Mie Scattering: These submicroscopic inclusions scatter and absorb light across all visible wavelengths, completely destroying the normal white specular reflection of feldspars and imparting a dark, greasy, waxy appearance.
Problem 6: The Charnockite-Khondalite Terrain of the Eastern Ghats (EGMB)

In the Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt (EGMB) of Odisha and Andhra Pradesh, charnockites are intimately intercalated with khondalites. Contrast the protoliths and metamorphic environments of these two iconic Indian rock types.

Regional Tectonometamorphic Contrast:
- Khondalite (Metasedimentary Protolith): Formed by ultra-high temperature granulite metamorphism of highly weathered, kaolinite-rich aluminous pelitic sediments (lithomargic clays and bauxitic soils) deposited in passive margins. Mineralogy: Garnet (almandine-pyrope) + Sillimanite + Quartz + K-feldspar $\pm$ Graphite.
- Charnockite (Meta-igneous / Ortho-gneiss Protolith): Formed by deep crustal granulite metamorphism and dehydration of calc-alkaline igneous granitoids, tonalites, and diorites under high-density $CO_2$ conditions. Mineralogy: Hypersthene + K-feldspar + Quartz $\pm$ Plagioclase.
- Geodynamic Setting: Both were fused and metamorphosed together during Proterozoic collisional orogenies (Grenvillian / Kuunga orogenies, ~1000–900 Ma) when India docked against East Antarctica.
Problem 7: Geothermobarometry of the Garnet-Orthopyroxene Pair

In garnet-bearing charnockites, the exchange of iron and magnesium between garnet and hypersthene: $$\frac{1}{3}\text{Mg}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}\ (\text{Pyrope}) + \text{FeSiO}_3\ (\text{Ferrosilite}) \rightleftharpoons \frac{1}{3}\text{Fe}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}\ (\text{Almandine}) + \text{MgSiO}_3\ (\text{Enstatite})$$ is used as a geothermometer (Harley, 1984). If the distribution coefficient is $K_D = \frac{(Fe/Mg)_{\text{grt}}}{(Fe/Mg)_{\text{opx}}} = 3.25$, explain how $K_D$ responds to increasing metamorphic temperature.

Thermodynamic Response:
- The exchange reaction is endothermic in the forward direction. Consequently, as metamorphic temperature ($T$) increases, thermal agitation overcomes the energy barrier that selectively partitions $Fe^{2+}$ into the smaller 8-coordinated garnet dodecahedral site.
- Therefore, with increasing temperature, $K_D$ decreases systematically toward unity ($K_D \to 1.0$).
- A high $K_D$ value ($3.25$) reflects retrograde cooling or lower granulite facies ($T \approx 700\text{--}750^\circ\text{C}$), whereas peak ultra-high temperature (UHT) granulites ($T > 950^\circ\text{C}$) yield lower $K_D$ values ($K_D \approx 1.8\text{--}2.2$).
Problem 8: Retrograde Metamorphism: Uralitization of Hypersthene

During exhumation and hydration of a charnockite terrain, hypersthene is often partially replaced along cracks and margins by green amphibole (hornblende) and secondary biotite. Name this texture and write the general hydration reaction.

Retrograde Reaction & Nomenclature:
- Textural Name: Uralitization (when pyroxene is replaced by fibrous amphibole) or kelyphitic / corona rim.
- Hydration Reaction: Influx of aqueous fluids ($H_2O$) during cooling and crustal uplift reverses the prograde dehydration: $$\text{Hypersthene} + \text{Plagioclase} + \text{K-feldspar} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Hornblende} + \text{Biotite} + \text{Quartz}$$
- Significance: Identifies retrograde amphibolite-facies overprinting of pristine anhydrous granulite facies charnockites.
