Q80: Granulite Facies Petrography - Essential Mineral Composition of Acid Charnockite

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 140); Winter (Ch. 21)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Core Question Analysis & Verbatim Textbook Grounding

The question tests the precise petrological definition of charnockite, originally established in India by Sir Thomas Holland in 1900 at St. Thomas Mount, Chennai.

Textbook Citation (G.B. Mahapatra, A Text-Book of Geology, Chapter 7, p. 140):
"Charnockite: It is a special hypersthene-bearing granite. The essential minerals are quartz, potash feldspar (microcline perthite), and hypersthene (orthopyroxene), accompanied by accessory garnet, magnetite, and zircon. The rock is characteristically dark greenish-grey or bluish-grey with a greasy luster due to minute quartz inclusions and high-grade granulite facies anhydrous conditions."

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q80. What is the composition of Charnockite ?
(A) Hypersthene + K-feldspar + Quartz
(B) Hornblende + K-feldspar + Quartz
(C) Sillimanite + K-feldspar + Quartz
(D) Epidote + Plagioclase + Quartz

2. The Charnockite Series (Holland, 1900)

The charnockite suite ranges from acid to ultrabasic members, unified by the universal presence of orthopyroxene (hypersthene) under low $a_{\mathrm{H_2O}}$ granulite facies metamorphism:

Member of Charnockite Series Essential Mineral Assemblage Standard Igneous / Metamorphic Equivalent
Charnockite (sensu stricto) Hypersthene + K-feldspar (perthite) + Quartz (± plagioclase, garnet) Hypersthene Granite (Acid member, $SiO_2 > 70\%$)
Enderbite Hypersthene + Plagioclase (antiperthite) + Quartz Hypersthene Tonalite / Granodiorite
Mangerite Hypersthene + Clinopyroxene + Perthite + Plagioclase Hypersthene Monzonite (Intermediate member)
Norite / Basic Charnockite Hypersthene + Augite + Labradorite Basic granulite / Mafic charnockite ($SiO_2 pprox 50\%$)

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A (Hypersthene + K-feldspar + Quartz) is correct: This is the textbook definition of acid charnockite.
  • Option B (Hornblende + K-feldspar + Quartz): Standard hornblende granite; lacks the essential diagnostic orthopyroxene (hypersthene) that defines charnockite.
  • Option C (Sillimanite + K-feldspar + Quartz): Pelitic granulite / khondalite assemblage; metapelitic, not charnockitic.
  • Option D (Epidote + Plagioclase + Quartz): Epidosite / greenschist facies hydrous retrograded rock; charnockites are strictly high-temperature anhydrous granulites.
