2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Void Ratio ($e$) vs Porosity ($n$)

In rock mechanics, soil mechanics, and hydrogeology, the pore geometry of a geological material is rigorously defined by two distinct volume ratios:

Property Definition Formula Denominator Range & Usage
Void Ratio ($e$) $e = \frac{V_v}{V_s}$ Volume of Solid rock / mineral skeleton ($V_s$) Can exceed $1.0$ ($0 < e < \infty$); preferred in mechanics because solid volume $V_s$ remains constant during compaction.
Porosity ($n$) $n = \frac{V_v}{V_t} \times 100$ Total volume of the rock ($V_t = V_s + V_v$) Always $< 100\%$ (expressed as a fraction or percentage); preferred in hydrogeology and reservoir engineering.
Effective Porosity ($n_e$) $n_e = \frac{V_{\text{interconnected voids}}}{V_t}$ Total volume ($V_t$) Only measures interconnected void channels that permit fluid transmission.

The mathematical relationship linking them is: $$e = \frac{n}{1 - n} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad n = \frac{e}{1 + e}$$ Because the question explicitly specifies the ratio of the volume of pore spaces ($V_v$) to the volume of the solid rock mass ($V_s$), it is by definition the Void ratio ($e$).

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A (Void ratio) is correct: $e = V_v / V_s$ (ratio of voids to solid skeleton).
  • Option B (Porosity): The ratio of voids to total volume ($V_v / V_t$), the classic trap!
  • Option C (Pores ratio): Fabricated distractor.
  • Option D (Effective porosity): Ratio of interconnected voids to total volume.
