Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Question & Permeameter Operating Limitations

Question 144: Which of the following statements w.r.t. Constant Head Permeameter is not correct ?

  • (A) It is used to measure permeability of an aquifer sample in the laboratory.
  • (B) Undisturbed samples are collected from the field locations.
  • (C) It is suitable for measuring permeability of relatively fine-grained samples.
  • (D) The volume of water collected in a beaker after passing through the sample is measured and the time required is noted.

Correct Answer: (C) It is suitable for measuring permeability of relatively fine-grained samples.

2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Constant Head vs Falling Head Permeameters

In soil mechanics, hydrogeology, and engineering geology (IS: 2720 Part 17, ASTM D2434), laboratory measurement of hydraulic conductivity ($K$) is performed using two distinct apparatuses:

Permeameter Type Applicable Soil/Rock Texture Hydraulic Conductivity Range Operational Principle & Formula
Constant Head Permeameter Coarse-grained soils: Clean sands, gravelly sands, and permeable sandstones. $K > 10^{-4}\text{ m/s}$ ($>10^{-2}\text{ cm/s}$) Water flows through the sample under a maintained constant hydraulic head difference $h$. The discharged volume $Q$ collected in time $t$ is measured: $$K = \frac{Q \cdot L}{A \cdot h \cdot t}$$
Falling (Variable) Head Permeameter Fine-grained soils: Silts, clays, shales, and fine clayey sands. $K < 10^{-4}\text{ m/s}$ (down to $10^{-9}\text{ m/s}$) Water from a narrow standpipe of area $a$ permeates through sample area $A$ while the water level falls from $h_1$ to $h_2$ over time $t$: $$K = \frac{a L}{A t} \ln\left(\frac{h_1}{h_2}\right)$$

If a Constant Head Permeameter is applied to fine-grained clays or silts, the seepage volume discharged is infinitesimally small, evaporation losses exceed flow, and hydraulic gradients induce piping or sidewall leakage. Hence, Statement (C) is completely incorrect (making it the correct exam choice).

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option C is the incorrect statement (hence correct answer): Constant head permeameters are strictly designed for coarse-grained permeable media, NOT fine-grained soils.
  • Options A, B, and D are valid technical truths: They accurately describe standard laboratory permeability testing procedures.
