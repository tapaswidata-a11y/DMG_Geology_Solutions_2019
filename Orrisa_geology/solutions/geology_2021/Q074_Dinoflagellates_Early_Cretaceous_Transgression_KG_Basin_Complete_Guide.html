1. Tectonostratigraphy of the Krishna-Godavari (KG) Basin & the Mesozoic Transgression

The Krishna-Godavari (KG) Basin, located along the eastern passive continental margin of peninsular India (Andhra Pradesh), is a polyhistory pericratonic rift and drift basin. It initiated during the Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous breakup of Eastern Gondwana, when the Indian continental plate rifted and separated from East Antarctica and Australia (~135–120 Ma).

During the early evolution of this passive margin, a series of NE–SW trending extensional horsts and grabens (e.g., Mandapeta Graben, Chintalapudi Sub-basin, Gudivada Graben) developed. As continental separation accelerated, marine waters from the nascent Indian Ocean flooded the subsiding rift valleys, culminating in a major, regionally extensive marine transgressive event during the Early Cretaceous (Aptian–Albian stages).

The Mesozoic Lithostratigraphic Triad of the KG Basin

The classic Mesozoic sequence exposed in the Krishna-Godavari Basin comprises three conformable to paraconformable lithostratigraphic units:

  • Gollapalli Sandstone (Late Jurassic – Early Cretaceous / Neocomian): Basal syn-rift continental to deltaic sandstones, conglomerates, and minor clays deposited in fluvial channel and lacustrine systems. Dominated by terrestrial plant fossils and coniferous spore-pollen.
  • Raghavapuram Mudstone (Early Cretaceous / Aptian – Albian): The paramount marine transgressive shale unit! Consists of dark grey to carbonaceous, fissile marine shales, silty mudstones, and minor calcareous bands. It preserves a prolific assemblage of marine dinoflagellate cysts, ammonites, and marine bivalves, representing the prime syn-rift source rock of the basin.
  • Tirupati Sandstone (Late Cretaceous / Cenomanian – Campanian): Regressive, littoral to fluvio-deltaic ferruginous sandstones, grit, and conglomerates representing shoreface progradation over the drowning mudstone.
2. Dinoflagellate Cyst Biostratigraphy: Diagnostic Early Cretaceous Markers

Dinoflagellates are unicellular, biflagellated eukaryotic protists (Phylum Dinoflagellata). During their sexual life cycle, many planktic marine species form a dormant, benthic resting cyst (dinocyst) composed of dinosporin—a complex, highly resistant, lipoidal biomacromolecule functionally analogous to sporopollenin.

Because dinocysts resist non-oxidizing acids ($HCl$, $HF$), they survive severe diagenetic leaching that completely dissolves calcareous microfossils (foraminifera, nannoplankton). In the dark, dysoxic shales of the Raghavapuram Mudstone, dinoflagellate cysts provide absolute biostratigraphic resolution:

Diagnostic Early Cretaceous Marker Taxa

  • Cassiculosphaeridia magna Dave & Kumar (often cited as magma in exam papers): A proximate, spheroidal to subspheroidal dinocyst displaying an irregular reticulate/reticulocavate surface network and an apical archaeopyle. Strictly diagnostic of Aptian–Albian marine transgressive strata along the East Coast of India.
  • Hystrichodinium voigtii (Alberti) Davey: A classic chorate (spinose) dinocyst bearing long, slender, distally pointed hollow processes radiating from a fibro-reticulate central body. A cosmopolitan Early Cretaceous marker characteristic of open-marine shelf waters from the Valanginian through Albian.
  • Associated Index Taxa: Dingodinium cerviculum, Canningia colliveri, Circulodinium hirtellum, Odontochitina operculata, Cribroperidinium orthoceras, and Muderongia spp.
Transgressive Index Criterion:
$$\text{Raghavapuram Transgressive Event} \iff \text{Dinocyst Acme } (C.\text{ magna} + H.\text{ voigtii}) + \text{D/S Ratio} > 0.65$$
$$\text{Chronostratigraphic Age} = \mathbf{Early\ Cretaceous\ (Aptian\ to\ Albian,\ \sim 125\text{--}100\ Ma)}$$
3. Palynofacies, Maximum Flooding Surfaces & Hydrocarbon Source Potential

In petroleum sequence stratigraphy, dinoflagellate cysts are the definitive tool for delineating Transgressive Systems Tracts (TST) and identifying Maximum Flooding Surfaces (MFS):

  • The Dinocyst-to-Spore Ratio ($\text{D/S}$ Ratio): As the marine shoreline migrates landward during transgression, the relative abundance of terrestrial spore-pollen (derived from land plants) plummets, while marine dinocysts increase exponentially. The maximum peak in the $\text{D/S}$ ratio precisely identifies the condensed section and Maximum Flooding Surface within the Raghavapuram Mudstone.
  • Source Rock Kinetics: The transgressive marine shales of the Raghavapuram Formation contain high concentrations of hydrogen-rich Type II amorphous organic matter (AOM) derived from marine phytoplankton (dinoflagellates and prasinophytes). With Total Organic Carbon ($TOC$) values reaching $2.0\text{--}6.5\text{ wt}\%$ and Hydrogen Indices ($HI > 350\text{ mg HC/g TOC}$), this Early Cretaceous transgressive event generated the primary petroleum source beds charged into the Mandapeta and Ravva reservoirs.

Official Examination Question
Question 74
Dinoflagellate cysts like Cassiculosphaeridia magma, Hystrichodinium voigti etc. are important in establishing which age of transgressive event in the Krishna-Godavari Basin, India ?
(A) Palaeocene
(B) Jurassic
(C) Early Cretaceous
(D) Late Palaeocene
CORRECT ANSWER: (C)
Early Cretaceous: The dinocyst assemblage comprising Cassiculosphaeridia magna and Hystrichodinium voigtii is the diagnostic biostratigraphic marker for the Aptian–Albian (Early Cretaceous) marine transgression that deposited the Raghavapuram Mudstone in the Krishna-Godavari Basin.
Forensic Analysis of Answer Choices
(A) Palaeocene — INCORRECT

The Paleocene transgressive succession in the KG Basin is represented by the Palakollu Shale and basal Pasarlapudi Formation, deposited following Deccan volcanism. Its microplankton assemblage is characterized by typical Cenozoic marker dinocysts such as Damassadinium californicum, Carpatella cornuta, and Senegalinium, completely lacking the Mesozoic species Cassiculosphaeridia magna and Hystrichodinium voigtii.

(B) Jurassic — INCORRECT

In the KG Basin, Jurassic strata (represented by the lower Gollapalli Sandstone and subsurface Mandapeta Red Beds) are strictly non-marine, syn-rift continental deposits formed in fluvial-lacustrine settings. They contain terrestrial spore-pollen (e.g., Classopollis, Callialasporites, Araucariacites) but are devoid of marine dinoflagellate cysts, as marine waters had not yet breached the rift valley.

(C) Early Cretaceous — CORRECT

The regional marine transgressive event that drowned the early rift system along the eastern coast of India occurred in the Early Cretaceous (Aptian to Albian stages, ~125–100 Ma), depositing the hydrocarbon-prolific Raghavapuram Mudstone. Palynological investigations by Garg et al., Kumar, and ONGC/DGH firmly established that the acme of Cassiculosphaeridia magna, Hystrichodinium voigtii, Dingodinium cerviculum, and Canningia establishes this Early Cretaceous transgressive datum.

(D) Late Palaeocene — INCORRECT

The Late Paleocene (Thanetian stage) in the Tethyan and Indo-Pacific realms coincides with the Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum (PETM). Its diagnostic palynological signature is an extraordinary global acme of the peridinioid genus Apectodinium (e.g., Apectodinium augustum, A. homomorphum), not Cretaceous gonyaulacoids.

Exam-Standard Analytical & Quantitative Practice Problems
Problem 1: Sequence Stratigraphic Maximum Flooding Surface (MFS) Quantitative Calculation

In a deep exploration well in the KG Basin, palynological counts of 300 specimens per sample across a continuous core yielded the following terrestrial spore-pollen ($S$) and marine dinoflagellate cyst ($D$) counts:
- Depth 2,800 m: $S = 270, D = 30$
- Depth 2,850 m: $S = 180, D = 120$
- Depth 2,900 m: $S = 45, D = 255$
- Depth 2,950 m: $S = 150, D = 150$
- Depth 3,000 m: $S = 285, D = 15$
Calculate the Marine Inundation Index ($\text{MII} = \frac{D}{D+S} \times 100\%$) for each depth and identify the exact position of the Maximum Flooding Surface (MFS).

Step-by-Step Calculation:
$$\text{MII} = \frac{D}{D+S} \times 100\%$$
- Depth 2,800 m: $\text{MII} = \frac{30}{300} \times 100\% = 10.0\%$ (Regressive / Highstand Systems Tract)
- Depth 2,850 m: $\text{MII} = \frac{120}{300} \times 100\% = 40.0\%$
- Depth 2,900 m: $\text{MII} = \frac{255}{300} \times 100\% = \mathbf{85.0\%}$ (Maximum Peak!)
- Depth 2,950 m: $\text{MII} = \frac{150}{300} \times 100\% = 50.0\%$ (Transgressive Systems Tract)
- Depth 3,000 m: $\text{MII} = \frac{15}{300} \times 100\% = 5.0\%$ (Lowstand / Fluvial Sandstone)
Conclusion: The Maximum Flooding Surface (MFS) is located precisely at 2,900 meters, marked by an $85\%$ marine dinocyst peak reflecting maximum water depth and minimum clastic terrestrial dilution.
Problem 2: Morphogroup Classification: Chorate vs. Proximate Cysts

Classify Cassiculosphaeridia magna and Hystrichodinium voigtii into their respective dinoflagellate cyst morphological archetypes (proximate, cavate, or chorate) and explain their paleoenvironmental implications regarding distance from shoreline.

Morphological & Environmental Classification:
1. Cassiculosphaeridia magna: A proximate cyst, formed in close contact with the original thecal wall, bearing low-relief reticulate ridges. Proximate cysts are typically dominant in inner neritic, protected shelf, or prodeltaic marine environments near the coast.
2. Hystrichodinium voigtii: A classic chorate cyst, characterized by a small central body ($25\text{--}35\ \mu\text{m}$) bearing long, radiating, spine-like processes ($15\text{--}25\ \mu\text{m}$) that held the cyst within the motile theca. Chorate cysts are buoyant adaptations for flotation in high-energy, open-marine outer neritic and pelagic shelf waters.
Environmental Synthesis: The co-occurrence of both morphogroups indicates an open-marine shelf inundation directly receiving marine microplankton while remaining within reach of fine terrigenous sediment influx.
Problem 3: East Coast Geodynamics: Kinematics of Greater India Breakup

Relate the Early Cretaceous Raghavapuram transgressive event in the KG Basin to the plate tectonic geodynamics of the Eastern Gondwana margin. What magnetic anomaly in the Bay of Bengal dates the onset of seafloor spreading?

Plate Tectonic Derivation:
1. Rifting Phase: During the Late Jurassic (Kimmeridgian–Tithonian, ~150 Ma), lithospheric extension between Greater India and East Antarctica (Enderby Land) created the Krishna-Godavari and Cauvery rift basins along ancient Eastern Ghats structural trends.
2. Breakup & Sea-Floor Spreading: Active seafloor spreading began in the Early Cretaceous, confirmed by seafloor magnetic anomaly $M_0$ (Early Aptian, ~125–120 Ma) and $M_2$–$M_4$ in the southern Bay of Bengal.
3. Thermal Subsidence & Transgression: The transition from active crustal necking to post-rift lithospheric thermal cooling induced rapid tectonic subsidence of the margin ($dZ_{\text{thermal}} \propto \sqrt{t}$), causing the Indian Ocean to drown the coastal grabens and depositing the marine Raghavapuram Mudstone.
Problem 4: Taphonomic Preservation: Why Dinocysts Outlast Foraminifera in Dysoxic Shales

In organic-rich black shales of the Raghavapuram Formation, calcareous foraminifera are virtually absent, whereas dinoflagellate cysts are preserved in millions of specimens per gram. Provide the biogeochemical explanation for this selective preservation.

Biogeochemical Explanation:
1. Calcareous Vulnerability: In stagnant, restricted transgressive basins with high organic matter deposition, bacterial sulfate reduction ($2\text{CH}_2\text{O} + \text{SO}_4^{2-} \to \text{H}_2\text{S} + 2\text{HCO}_3^-$) and subsequent post-oxic organic decomposition produce high concentrations of carbonic and organic acids. Low porewater pH ($< 6.8$) dissolved the thin, metastable calcitic tests of planktic foraminifera.
2. Dinosporin Invariance: Dinoflagellate cysts are composed of dinosporin, an ultra-resistant, highly cross-linked, complex macromolecular network of aliphatic polycarbonates and aromatic ether bridges. Dinosporin is impervious to low pH, high $p\text{CO}_2$, and severe diagenetic leaching, allowing immaculate morphological preservation in black shales.
Problem 5: Palynofacies Analysis and Kerogen Type in the Raghavapuram Mudstone

A transmitted-light palynofacies analysis of the Raghavapuram Mudstone yields $75\%$ fluffy, orange Amorphous Organic Matter (AOM), $20\%$ marine dinocysts, and $5\%$ inert black woody phytoclasts. Determine the Kerogen Type and evaluate its petroleum yield potential.

Palynofacies Evaluation:
- AOM and Dinocyst Dominance: High concentrations of marine phytoplankton-derived amorphous organic matter ($>70\%$) combined with dinoflagellate cysts and minimal terrestrial phytoclasts ($<10\%$) is the definitive petrographic signature of Type II Marine Sapropelic Kerogen.
- Petroleum Potential: Type II kerogen has an initial Hydrogen Index ($HI$) of $400\text{--}600\text{ mg HC/g TOC}$. Upon thermal maturation in the catagenetic zone ($T = 100\text{--}130^\circ\text{C}$), it possesses world-class generative potential for light paraffinic and naphthenic crude oil and associated rich gas.
Problem 6: Well-Log Correlation of the Raghavapuram Transgression

On wireline logging suites, how is the Early Cretaceous Raghavapuram transgressive shale interval differentiated from the underlying Gollapalli Sandstone and overlying Tirupati Sandstone?

Petrophysical Log Response:
1. Gamma Ray (GR): Gollapalli and Tirupati sandstones display low gamma ray readings ($20\text{--}45\text{ API}$). In contrast, the Raghavapuram Mudstone displays a sharp, sustained "hot shale" gamma ray deflection ($90\text{--}180\text{ API}$) due to the concentration of uranium ($U$) adsorbed onto marine organic matter and thorium-potassium ($Th, K$) in detrital illite clays.
2. Resistivity ($R_t$): Low baseline in water-wet shales ($1\text{--}3\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$), with elevated resistivity ($8\text{--}25\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$) where mature hydrocarbons are actively generating within the shale matrix.
3. Sonic Transit Time ($\Delta t$): High acoustic slowness ($90\text{--}120\ \mu\text{s/ft}$) characteristic of undercompacted, high-porosity fissile marine shale.
Problem 7: Paleobathymetric Zonation using Dinocyst Associations

How do micropaleontologists use the peridinioid-to-gonyaulacoid ratio ($P/G$ ratio) in Cretaceous dinocysts to track transgressive-regressive cycles?

Paleoecological Principles:
- Peridinioid Dinocysts ($P$): Predominantly heterotrophic microzooplankton that feed on marine diatoms. They proliferate in nutrient-rich, high-turbidity, upwelling or estuarine/inner neritic coastal waters.
- Gonyaulacoid Dinocysts ($G$, e.g., Hystrichodinium): Primarily autotrophic phototrophic algae that dominate clear, oligotrophic, open-ocean neritic and oceanic waters.
- Tracking Transgressions: During a marine transgressive phase, the $P/G$ ratio decreases as clear, open-shelf marine waters flood the basin. During regressive highstands and deltaic progradation, terrestrial nutrient runoff spikes, driving a sharp increase in the $P/G$ ratio.
Problem 8: Thermal Alteration Index (TAI) of Dinocyst Palynomorphs

Palynological slides from an exploration well show Cassiculosphaeridia specimens with a dark golden-brown coloration (TAI 2.8). Correlate this value with the oil generation window and vitrinite reflectance.

Thermal Calibration:
- TAI 2.8: On Staplin's (1969) Thermal Alteration Index scale, a TAI of $2.6\text{--}3.0$ corresponds to a golden-brown to cinnamon-brown coloration of sporopollenin and dinosporin.
- Vitrinite Reflectance ($R_o$): Corresponds precisely to $R_o \approx 0.80\text{--}0.95\%$.
- Hydrocarbon Generation Window: Represents the Peak Oil Generation Window, confirming that the Raghavapuram source beds at this well location have undergone optimal thermal maturation and actively expelled liquid petroleum into adjoining sandstone reservoirs.
