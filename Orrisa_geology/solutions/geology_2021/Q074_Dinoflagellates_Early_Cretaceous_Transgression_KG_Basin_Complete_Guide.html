Q74: Basin Stratigraphy - Dinoflagellates & Early Cretaceous Transgression in KG Basin

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Sam Boggs Jr. (Principles of Sedimentology & Stratigraphy, p. 511); DGH KG Basin Biostratigraphy

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Tectonic Evolution of the Krishna-Godavari (KG) Passive Margin Basin

The Krishna-Godavari (KG) Basin, situated along the east coast of Peninsular India in Andhra Pradesh and the adjacent Bay of Bengal, is a premier petroliferous basin. The basin evolved through four major geodynamic stages:

  1. Syn-Rift Continental Phase (Late Carboniferous to Late Jurassic): Gondwana intracratonic rifting producing NE–SW trending grabens and half-grabens (Chintalapudi and Mandapeta grabens).
  2. Rift-Drift Transition & Breakup (Early Cretaceous / Neocomian–Aptian): Breakup of Eastern Gondwanaland (separation of Greater India from Antarctica-Australia), initiating the opening of the proto-Bay of Bengal.
  3. Major Marine Transgression (Early Cretaceous / Aptian–Albian): Catastrophic regional marine flooding event that drowned early fluvial valleys and deposited thick, organic-rich marine black shales across the basin.
  4. Passive Margin Post-Rift Progradation (Late Cretaceous to Cenozoic): Deltaic progradation of the proto-Krishna and Godavari rivers.

2. Dinoflagellate Cysts as Marine Transgression Markers

During the Early Cretaceous (Aptian–Albian) marine transgression, open-marine oceanic waters flooded the pericratonic KG Basin, depositing the prolific petroleum source rock known as the Raghavapuram Shale Formation (and lateral subsurface correlatives). Because macrofossils are sparse in these distal, oxygen-depleted shales, biostratigraphic dating relies on organic-walled microplankton—specifically resting cysts of marine dinoflagellates (Dinocysts; Sam Boggs Jr., Principles of Sedimentology and Stratigraphy, p. 511; DGH KG Basin Petroleum Atlas).

The transgressive Raghavapuram shales are defined by a rich, cosmopolitan marine dinoflagellate cyst assemblage that includes:

  • Cassiculosphaeridia magna: A diagnostic spherical chorate to proximate dinocyst with reticulate ornamentation, internationally calibrated to the Aptian–Albian stages of the Early Cretaceous.
  • Hystrichodinium voigti: A spinose, cavate dinoflagellate cyst characteristic of boreal and Tethyan Early Cretaceous transgressive horizons.
  • Odontochitina operculata and Cribroperidinium edwardsii: Diagnostic Early Cretaceous marker taxa.

The sudden abundance of these marine dinoflagellate cysts over non-marine terrestrial spore-pollen suites provides unequivocal micropaleontological proof of the Early Cretaceous marine transgressive event.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q74. Dinoflagellate cysts like Cassiculosphaeridia magma, Hystrichodinium voigti etc. are important in establishing which age of transgressive event in the Krishna-Godavari Basin, India ?
(A) Palaeocene
(B) Jurassic
(C) Early Cretaceous
(D) Late Palaeocene

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "Dinoflagellate cysts like Cassiculosphaeridia magma, Hystrichodinium voigti etc. are important in establishing which age of transgressive event in the Krishna-Godavari Basin, India ?"

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Palaeocene (INCORRECT): Paleocene strata in the KG Basin (e.g., the Palakollu Shale) are characterized by Cenozoic planktonic foraminifera (Morozovella, Subbotina) and Tertiary dinocysts (e.g., Apectodinium). The index species Cassiculosphaeridia magna and Hystrichodinium voigti are extinct prior to the Paleocene.
  • (B) Jurassic (INCORRECT): Jurassic strata in the KG basin (Gollapalli Sandstone) are dominantly continental syn-rift sandstones dominated by terrestrial pteridophytic spores and gymnosperm pollen (e.g., Callialasporites), lacking these marine Cretaceous dinocysts.
  • (C) Early Cretaceous (CORRECT): Cosmopolitan dinoflagellate cysts such as Cassiculosphaeridia magna and Hystrichodinium voigti are globally documented biostratigraphic index markers for the Early Cretaceous (Aptian–Albian). In the Krishna-Godavari Basin, their appearance marks the initial major marine transgressive flooding of the Raghavapuram Shale during Gondwana breakup.
  • (D) Late Palaeocene (INCORRECT): Late Paleocene strata postdate these Early Cretaceous dinoflagellate species by more than 50 million years.
