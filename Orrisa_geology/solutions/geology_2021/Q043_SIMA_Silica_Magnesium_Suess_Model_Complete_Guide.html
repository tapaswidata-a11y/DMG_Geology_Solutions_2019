1. The Classical Tripartite Geochemical Model of Eduard Suess (1885)

Before the development of modern seismological velocity models and plate tectonics, Austrian geologist Eduard Suess (1831–1914) proposed a pioneering geochemical shell model of the Earth based on bulk chemical composition and specific gravities:

  • SIAL (Silica + Aluminium): The outermost, lightest continental crustal layer. Dominated mineralogically by quartz, orthoclase, and sodic plagioclase (granitic-granodioritic composition). Characterized by low specific gravity ($\rho \approx 2.65 - 2.75\text{ g/cm}^3$) and thickness of $20-70\text{ km}$ under continents, terminating abruptly or absent under deep oceans.
  • SIMA (Silica + Magnesium): The intermediate geochemical shell underlying the SIAL and forming the bedrock floor of the global oceans. Dominated mineralogically by pyroxenes, calcic plagioclase, and olivine (basaltic to peridotitic composition). Characterized by higher specific gravity ($\rho \approx 2.90 - 3.40\text{ g/cm}^3$).
  • NIFE (Nickel + Ferrum): The innermost metallic core of the Earth. Dominated by dense iron-nickel alloy ($\text{Fe} \sim 85-90\%, \text{Ni} \sim 5-10\%$) with high specific gravity ($\rho \approx 10.0 - 13.0\text{ g/cm}^3$), responsible for the Earth's geomagnetic dynamo.
Suess Chemical Etymology
$$\mathbf{SIAL} = \mathbf{Si} \text{licon} + \mathbf{Al} \text{uminium} \quad (\text{Granitic Continental Crust})$$ $$\mathbf{SIMA} = \mathbf{Si} \text{licon} + \mathbf{Ma} \text{gnesium} \quad (\text{Basaltic Oceanic Crust \& Mantle})$$ $$\mathbf{NIFE} = \mathbf{Ni} \text{ckel} + \mathbf{Fe} \text{rrum (Iron)} \quad (\text{Metallic Core})$$

2. Modern Petrochemical Reinterpretation of SIAL and SIMA

Modern geochemistry and petrology correlate Suess's historical nomenclature with verified geochemical subdivisions:

  • SIAL $\leftrightarrow$ Continental Crust: Enriched in Large Ion Lithophile Elements (LILE: $\text{K, Rb, Ba, Th, U}$) and High Field Strength Elements ($\text{Si, Al}$). Felsic bulk composition ($>60\text{ wt}\% \text{ SiO}_2$).
  • SIMA $\leftrightarrow$ Oceanic Crust & Upper Mantle: Depleted in incompatible elements; strongly enriched in ferromagnesian components ($\text{MgO} \approx 8-40\text{ wt}\%, \text{FeO} \approx 8-12\text{ wt}\%$). Composed of mid-ocean ridge basalts (MORB), sheeted dikes, gabbros, and mantle peridotites.
  • Conrad Discontinuity: The mid-crustal seismic boundary separating upper granitic SIAL ($v_p \approx 6.0\text{ km/s}$) from lower basaltic SIMA ($v_p \approx 6.7\text{ km/s}$).

Official Examination Question
Question 43
SIMA stands for:
(A) Silicon and Manganese
(B) Strontium and Magnesium
(C) Sulphur and Manganese
(D) Silicon and Magnesium
Verified Correct Answer: (D) Silicon and Magnesium [Classical geochemical portmanteau coined by Eduard Suess: SI (Silicon/Silica) + MA (Magnesium).]
Correct Option: (D) Silicon and Magnesium

Detailed Geochemical Forensic Analysis:

  • In historical physical geology and geochemistry (pioneered by Eduard Suess in 1885 and documented in all standard Indian geology textbooks, including K.M. Bangar, Ch. 1, p. 12), the Earth was divided into three compositional layers: SIAL, SIMA, and NIFE.
  • The acronym SIMA is derived directly from the first two letters of its two dominant characteristic chemical constituents: $$\mathbf{SI} \text{licon (or Silica, } \text{SiO}_2) + \mathbf{MA} \text{gnesium (or Magnesia, } \text{MgO})$$
  • The SIMA layer forms the continuous mafic-ultramafic shell underlying the continental granitic crust (SIAL) and floor of the ocean basins.
  • Thus, Option (D) Silicon and Magnesium is the unique, definitive correct answer.
Incorrect Distractor: (A) Silicon and Manganese

Detailed Refutation:

  • Manganese ($\text{Mn}$) is a minor trace element in the Earth's crust (average abundance $\sim 0.1\text{ wt}\% \text{ MnO}$). It was never a major rock-forming component used to designate planetary shells.
  • The letters 'MA' stand for Magnesium, not Manganese. Hence, option (A) is chemically and historically false.
Incorrect Distractor: (B) Strontium and Magnesium

Detailed Refutation:

  • Strontium ($\text{Sr}$) is an alkaline earth trace element occurring at trace levels (a few hundred parts per million) in plagioclase. It does not begin with 'SI' nor does it constitute a major Earth layer.
  • 'SI' stands for Silicon. Therefore, option (B) is completely erroneous.
Incorrect Distractor: (C) Sulphur and Manganese

Detailed Refutation:

  • Neither Sulphur ($\text{S}$) nor Manganese ($\text{Mn}$) forms the silicate-magnesium rich oceanic and lower crustal rock mass.
  • This option is a deceptive distractor substituting random chemical elements for Silicon and Magnesium.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Geochemistry & Crustal Architecture
Problem 1: Calculation of Clarke Values for Crustal Elements

In the Earth's continental crust (SIAL), oxygen and silicon account for approximately $46.6\%$ and $27.7\%$ by weight, respectively. Calculate the total combined percentage of Silica ($\text{SiO}_2$) in an average granodioritic continental crust containing $28.0\text{ wt}\%$ elemental Silicon ($M_{\text{Si}} = 28.085\text{ g/mol}$, $M_{\text{O}} = 16.000\text{ g/mol}$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

Molecular weight of $\text{SiO}_2 = 28.085 + 2(16.000) = 60.085\text{ g/mol}$.

$$\text{Weight fraction of Si in } \text{SiO}_2 = \frac{28.085}{60.085} \approx 0.4674$$ $$\text{Total } \text{SiO}_2 \text{ wt}\% = \frac{\text{Elemental Si wt}\%}{0.4674} = \frac{28.0\%}{0.4674} \approx 59.9\% \approx 60.0\text{ wt}\%$$

Final Answer: Total $\text{SiO}_2$ is approximately $60.0\text{ wt}\%$, representing intermediate-to-felsic crustal composition.

Problem 2: Goldschmidt's Geochemical Affinity Classification

Classify the characteristic elements of SIAL, SIMA, and NIFE into V.M. Goldschmidt's four geochemical categories: Lithophile, Siderophile, Chalcophile, and Atmophile.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Lithophile (Rock-loving / Silicate phase): $\text{Si, Al, Mg, Ca, Na, K}$ — Concentrate overwhelmingly in the SIAL and SIMA layers as silicates and oxides.
  • Siderophile (Iron-loving / Metallic phase): $\text{Fe, Ni, Co, Au, Pt}$ — Partitioned preferentially into the metallic NIFE core during planetary core segregation.
  • Chalcophile (Sulfur-loving / Sulfide phase): $\text{Cu, Pb, Zn, S}$ — Form ore deposits and accessory sulfides in crust and mantle.
  • Atmophile (Gas-loving): $\text{N, noble gases}$ — Concentrate in the atmosphere and hydrosphere.
Problem 3: Density Inversion and Floating SIAL

Applying Archimedes' principle, if a granitic SIAL block of thickness $H = 35\text{ km}$ and density $\rho_{\text{sial}} = 2700\text{ kg/m}^3$ floats in isostatic equilibrium on a denser SIMA substratum ($\rho_{\text{sima}} = 3000\text{ kg/m}^3$), calculate the depth of the submerged root ($r$) below the SIMA surface.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$\rho_{\text{sial}} \cdot H = \rho_{\text{sima}} \cdot r$$ $$r = H \left(\frac{\rho_{\text{sial}}}{\rho_{\text{sima}}}\right) = 35\text{ km} \times \left(\frac{2700}{3000}\right) = 35 \times 0.90 = 31.5\text{ km}$$

The freeboard elevation above the SIMA is $h = H - r = 35 - 31.5 = 3.5\text{ km}$.

Final Answer: The submerged root depth is $31.5\text{ km}$.

Problem 4: Wegener's Continental Drift Hypothesis and SIAL

How did Alfred Wegener utilize Suess's SIAL and SIMA concept in his 1912 Continental Drift hypothesis?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Wegener envisioned the continents as rigid, buoyant blocks of SIAL that plowed directly through the weaker, yieldable basaltic SIMA ocean floor like icebergs through water. While correct in identifying continental displacement, his mechanical concept of SIAL plowing through rigid oceanic SIMA was geophysically flawed; modern plate tectonics proved that oceanic SIMA and continental SIAL are both frozen into rigid lithospheric plates that move together over the asthenosphere.

Problem 5: Conrad Discontinuity Seismic Velocity Jump

What is the typical seismic compressional velocity ($v_p$) jump across the Conrad Discontinuity between SIAL and SIMA?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Across the Conrad Discontinuity (located at depths of $15-25\text{ km}$ under stable continental cratons), $v_p$ steps up from $\sim 6.0-6.2\text{ km/s}$ in the upper granitic/felsic SIAL to $\sim 6.6-6.9\text{ km/s}$ in the lower amphibolitic-to-granulitic mafic SIMA.

Problem 6: Core Mass Fraction of NIFE

If Earth's total mass is $M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$ and the core (NIFE) mass is $M_C = 1.94 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$, calculate the mass percentage of the metallic core relative to the whole planet.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$\% \text{ Mass}_{\text{Core}} = \left(\frac{1.94 \times 10^{24}}{5.97 \times 10^{24}}\right) \times 100\% = 32.5\%$$

Final Answer: The metallic NIFE core comprises approximately $32.5\%$ (nearly one-third) of Earth's total mass.

Problem 7: Mg# (Magnesium Number) of the SIMA Layer

Define the Magnesium Number ($\text{Mg}\#$) used in mantle petrology and calculate it for a peridotitic mantle SIMA sample with $\text{MgO} = 40.3\text{ wt}\%$ (molar mass $40.3\text{ g/mol}$) and $\text{FeO} = 7.2\text{ wt}\%$ (molar mass $71.85\text{ g/mol}$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$\text{Moles of MgO} = \frac{40.3}{40.3} = 1.000\text{ mol}$$ $$\text{Moles of FeO} = \frac{7.2}{71.85} = 0.1002\text{ mol}$$ $$\text{Mg}\# = \frac{\text{Mg}^{2+}}{\text{Mg}^{2+} + \text{Fe}^{2+}} \times 100 = \frac{1.000}{1.000 + 0.1002} \times 100 = \frac{1.000}{1.1002} \times 100 \approx 90.9$$

Final Answer: $\text{Mg}\# \approx 91$, typical of primitive mantle peridotite.

Problem 8: Whole Earth Elemental Composition vs. Crustal Composition

Why is Iron ($\text{Fe}$) the most abundant element in the Whole Earth by mass ($\sim 32\%$), whereas Oxygen and Silicon dominate the crust?

Step-by-Step Solution:

During the early Hadean accretion and magma ocean differentiation of the proto-Earth ($\sim 4.5\text{ Ga}$), planetary melting allowed high-density immiscible liquid iron-nickel metal to sink to the gravitational center (the "iron catastrophe"), forming the massive NIFE core. Lighter silicate melts rose to form the mantle and crust, concentrating oxygen, silicon, and aluminum in the outermost shells.
