1. Question & Electrode Array Geometry

Question 130: In a resistivity survey, the current and potential electrodes are such arranged that the distances between the potential electrodes and the centre of the array are less than those between the current and potential electrodes. What type of configuration is this ?

  • (A) Wenner
  • (B) Schlumberger
  • (C) Dipole-dipole
  • (D) Pole-dipole

Correct Answer: (B) Schlumberger

2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Electrical Resistivity Electrode Configurations

In direct current (DC) electrical resistivity surveying, four collinear electrodes are placed on the ground surface: two outer current electrodes ($A$ and $B$) to inject current $I$, and two inner potential electrodes ($M$ and $N$) to measure potential difference $\Delta V$:

Configuration Electrode Geometry & Spacing Rules Geometric Factor ($K$)
Schlumberger Array The potential electrodes ($M, N$) are closely spaced at the center, such that distance from the center ($s = MN/2$) is substantially less than distance to the outer current electrodes ($L = AB/2$), with condition $MN \le \frac{AB}{5}$. $K = \frac{\pi (L^2 - s^2)}{2 s} \approx \frac{\pi L^2}{MN}$
Wenner Array All four electrodes are equally spaced: $AM = MN = NB = a$. $K = 2\pi a$
Dipole-Dipole Array Current dipole ($AB$) and potential dipole ($MN$) are separated by a multiple $n$ of the dipole length $a$: distance between dipoles $R = n a$. $K = \pi n (n+1)(n+2) a$
Pole-Dipole Array One current electrode is placed at "infinity" ($>10\times$ survey spread), while $A, M, N$ remain on the survey line. $K = \frac{2\pi}{\frac{1}{AM} - \frac{1}{AN}}$

Because the question explicitly specifies that the distance between potential electrodes and the center is smaller than the distance between current and potential electrodes, this precisely describes the Schlumberger configuration.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option B (Schlumberger) is correct: Closely spaced potential electrodes centered between widely spaced current electrodes.
  • Option A (Wenner): All 4 electrodes are strictly equidistant ($a = a = a$).
  • Option C (Dipole-dipole): Electrodes grouped in pairs (dipoles) separated by distance $na$.
  • Option D (Pole-dipole): One current electrode at infinity.
