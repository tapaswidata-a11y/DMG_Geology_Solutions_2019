1. The IUGS QAPF Plutonic Classification & Field 8* (Quartz Monzonite)

The standardized international system for classifying plutonic igneous rocks containing less than $90\%$ mafic minerals ($M' < 90\%$) was established by the IUGS Subcommission on the Systematics of Igneous Rocks (Streckeisen, 1976; Le Maitre, 2002).

Classification is performed on the basis of the modal proportions of three light-colored felsic mineral groups, normalized to $100\%$:

  • Q: Quartz ($\text{SiO}_2$) and its polymorphs (tridymite, cristobalite).
  • A: Alkali Feldspar (potassium feldspar: orthoclase, microcline, sanidine, perthite; and sodic plagioclase albite with $\text{An}_{00\text{--}05}$).
  • P: Plagioclase Feldspar (all plagioclase with compositions from oligoclase to anorthite, $\text{An}_{05\text{--}100}$).

Highlighting Field 8*, defined by $Q = 5\text{--}20\%$ and roughly equal proportions of alkali feldspar and plagioclase ($P/(A+P) = 35\text{--}65\%$).

Strict Mathematical Definition of Field 8* (Quartz Monzonite)

IUGS Criteria for Quartz Monzonite (Field 8*):
$$\mathbf{\text{Quartz Parameter:}} \quad \mathbf{5\% \le \frac{Q}{Q + A + P} \le 20\%}$$
$$\mathbf{\text{Plagioclase Ratio:}} \quad \mathbf{35\% \le \frac{P}{A + P} \le 65\%} \quad (\mathbf{\text{Approximately equal amounts of A and P!}})$$

If quartz exceeds $20\%$ ($Q > 20\%$), the rock enters the true granite realm (Field 3b: Monzogranite). If quartz falls below $5\%$ ($Q < 5\%$), it is a true Monzonite (Field 8).

2. Historical Terminology: The Adamellite Problem & Petrography

In classical 19th- and 20th-century petrology, this rock was widely designated as Adamellite (named by A. Cathrein in 1890 from Mount Adamello in the Italian Alps).

  • The British Tradition: British and Commonwealth geologists historically used "adamellite" for any granitic rock containing approximately equal proportions of plagioclase and orthoclase with significant quartz ($>10\%$).
  • The IUGS Recommendation: Because the term "adamellite" had been applied ambiguously to both quartz monzonites ($Q = 5\text{--}20\%$) and monzogranites ($Q = 20\text{--}60\%$), the IUGS officially discarded the name adamellite, replacing it with the unambiguous, systematic term Quartz Monzonite.

Essential Mineralogy and Microscopic Fabric

  • Alkali Feldspar ($35\text{--}65\%$ of total feldspar): Typically pink to buff perthitic orthoclase or microcline displaying Carlsbad or tartan twinning.
  • Plagioclase ($35\text{--}65\%$ of total feldspar): Sodic plagioclase (oligoclase to andesine, $\text{An}_{20\text{--}40}$), frequently showing well-developed polysynthetic albite twinning and normal compositional zoning.
  • Quartz ($5\text{--}20\%$ of felsics): Anhedral, clear grains occupying interstitial spaces between earlier-crystallized subhedral feldspar crystals.
  • Mafic Minerals ($M' = 10\text{--}25\%$): Biotite mica and calcic amphibole (hornblende), with accessory titanite (sphene), apatite, zircon, and magnetite.
3. Diagnostic Demarcation from Adjacent QAPF Plutonic Fields

Understanding the boundary coordinates of Field 8* is critical for solving classification questions:

  • Field 8* vs. Field 3b (Monzogranite): Both have co-equal alkali feldspar and plagioclase ($P/(A+P) = 35\text{--}65\%$), but Monzogranite contains $> 20\%$ quartz ($Q = 20\text{--}60\%$), making it a true granite.
  • Field 8* vs. Field 9* (Quartz Monzodiorite / Quartz Monzogabbro): In Field 9*, plagioclase clearly dominates over alkali feldspar ($P/(A+P) = 65\text{--}90\%$). If plagioclase is sodic ($\text{An} < 50$), it is Quartz Monzodiorite; if calcic ($\text{An} > 50$), it is Quartz Monzogabbro.
  • Field 8* vs. Field 10* (Quartz Norite / Quartz Gabbro): In Field 10*, plagioclase comprises $>90\%$ of total feldspar ($P/(A+P) > 90\%$). A Quartz Norite is a gabbroic rock where orthopyroxene (hypersthene) is the dominant mafic phase, and alkali feldspar is virtually absent ($< 10\%$).

Official Examination Question
Question 88
A plutonic rock consisting of approximately equal amounts of alkali feldspar and plagioclase and with essential quartz (5%-20% of felsic minerals) but not enough to make the rock a granite is termed as :
(A) Quartz Monzonite
(B) Quartz Monzodiorite
(C) Quartz Norite
(D) Quartz Monzogabbro
CORRECT ANSWER: (A)
Quartz Monzonite: Occupies Field 8* of the IUGS QAPF classification, characterized by roughly equal proportions of alkali feldspar and plagioclase ($P/(A+P) = 35\text{--}65\%$) and 5% to 20% modal quartz.
Forensic Analysis of Answer Choices
(A) Quartz Monzonite — CORRECT

Quartz Monzonite is the exact, canonical definition established by the IUGS Subcommission (Streckeisen, 1976; Le Maitre, 2002; Winter, 2014, p. 28) for a plutonic rock containing approximately equal amounts of alkali feldspar and plagioclase ($P/(A+P) = 35\text{--}65\%$) and essential quartz between 5% and 20% of total felsic minerals. Because quartz is $<20\%$, it lacks sufficient silica to be classified as a true granite.

(B) Quartz Monzodiorite — INCORRECT

In Quartz Monzodiorite (Field 9*), plagioclase is heavily dominant over alkali feldspar, accounting for 65% to 90% of total feldspars ($P/(A+P) = 65\text{--}90\%$). The feldspars are NOT in approximately equal amounts.

(C) Quartz Norite — INCORRECT

A Quartz Norite plots in Field 10* of the QAPF diagram where plagioclase constitutes greater than 90% of all feldspars ($P/(A+P) > 90\%$), and alkali feldspar is virtually absent ($<10\%$). Furthermore, it is a basic rock dominated by calcic plagioclase ($\text{An} > 50$) and orthopyroxene (hypersthene).

(D) Quartz Monzogabbro — INCORRECT

Like Quartz Monzodiorite, Quartz Monzogabbro occupies Field 9* with plagioclase comprising 65% to 90% of total feldspars, with the specific distinction that the plagioclase is calcic ($\text{An} > 50$). It does not possess equal amounts of alkali feldspar and plagioclase.

Exam-Standard Analytical & Quantitative Practice Problems
Problem 1: Step-by-Step QAPF Modal Normalization Calculation

A point-count modal analysis of an unknown plutonic rock yields the following raw volume percentages: $$\text{Quartz} = 12.0\%, \quad \text{Microcline} = 34.0\%, \quad \text{Plagioclase (An}_{30}) = 36.0\%, \quad \text{Biotite} = 12.0\%, \quad \text{Hornblende} = 4.0\%, \quad \text{Apatite+Magnetite} = 2.0\%$$ Normalize the felsic minerals to $100\%$ ($Q + A + P$) and determine the exact IUGS classification field.

Step-by-Step Normalization:
1. Sum of Felsic Minerals ($Q + A + P$): $$\sum_{\text{felsic}} = 12.0 + 34.0 + 36.0 = 82.0\%$$
2. Calculate Normalized Felsic Percentages: $$Q' = \frac{12.0}{82.0} \times 100\% = \mathbf{14.63\%}$$
$$A' = \frac{34.0}{82.0} \times 100\% = \mathbf{41.46\%}$$
$$P' = \frac{36.0}{82.0} \times 100\% = \mathbf{43.90\%}$$
Verification: $14.63 + 41.46 + 43.90 = 99.99\% \approx 100\%$.

3. Calculate Plagioclase Ratio ($P^*$): $$P^* = \frac{P'}{A' + P'} \times 100\% = \frac{43.90}{41.46 + 43.90} \times 100\% = \frac{43.90}{85.36} \times 100\% \approx \mathbf{51.43\%}$$
4. Evaluate Against IUGS Criteria:
- Quartz $Q' = 14.63\%$, which falls strictly within $5\% \le Q \le 20\%$.
- Plagioclase ratio $P^* = 51.43\%$, which falls strictly within $35\% \le P^* \le 65\%$.
Conclusion: The rock plots squarely in Field 8* and is definitively classified as a Quartz Monzonite.
Problem 2: The Critical Granite Boundary at 20% Quartz

Explain why the IUGS established the boundary between quartz-bearing intermediate rocks (Field 8* Quartz Monzonite) and true granites (Field 3b Monzogranite) at exactly $20\%$ modal quartz, rather than at $10\%$ or $30\%$.

Eutectic Phase Equilibrium Justification:
In experimental petrology of the "granite system" ($SiO_2\text{--}NaAlSi_3O_8\text{--}KAlSi_3O_8\text{--}H_2O$ at $P_{H_2O} = 1\text{ to } 5\text{ kbar}$, Tuttle & Bowen, 1958), the ternary eutectic / minimum melt composition contains between $25\text{ wt}\%$ and $35\text{ wt}\%$ quartz.
- True granites ($Q > 20\%$) represent near-eutectic liquids produced by advanced crustal melting or extensive fractional crystallization.
- Rocks containing $5\text{--}20\%$ quartz are transitional sub-eutectic rocks whose parental melts were not saturated with quartz until very late in their crystallization history. Setting the master boundary at $Q = 20\%$ cleanly separates true eutectic granites from quartz-bearing intermediate plutons.
Problem 3: Distinguishing Quartz Monzodiorite vs. Quartz Monzogabbro

A plutonic rock has $Q = 10\%$, $P = 75\%$, and $A = 15\%$, placing it in Field 9*. State the specific mineralogical test required to determine whether it should be named Quartz Monzodiorite or Quartz Monzogabbro.

Plagioclase Composition Rule ($\text{An}_{50}$):
According to IUGS rules (Le Maitre, 2002, p. 25):
- Measure the average anorthite content ($\text{An}\%$) of the plagioclase feldspar using optical extinction methods (Michel-Lévy method) or electron microprobe.
- If the plagioclase is sodic ($\text{An} < 50$, oligoclase or andesine), the rock is named Quartz Monzodiorite.
- If the plagioclase is calcic ($\text{An} > 50$, labradorite or bytownite), the rock is named Quartz Monzogabbro.
Problem 4: Volcanic Equivalent of Quartz Monzonite

Name the volcanic (extrusive) equivalent of a Quartz Monzonite in the IUGS volcanic classification system, and state its position on the Total Alkali-Silica (TAS) diagram.

Extrusive Equivalent:
- The volcanic equivalent of Quartz Monzonite is Quartz Latite (or Trachyandesite / Trachydacite on the TAS diagram).
- On the TAS diagram ($\text{Na}_2\text{O} + \text{K}_2\text{O}$ vs $\text{SiO}_2$), it plots in the intermediate-to-high alkali sector at $\text{SiO}_2 \approx 57\text{--}63\text{ wt}\%$ and total alkalis between $7\%$ and $10\%$.
Problem 5: Tectonic Setting of Quartz Monzonite Plutonism

In what plate tectonic settings do quartz monzonites typically form, and how does their geochemistry differ from island arc tonalites?

Tectonic & Geochemical Environment:
- Tectonic Setting: Quartz monzonites are typical of mature continental magmatic arcs (e.g., Sierra Nevada Batholith, USA; Gangdese Arc, Tibet) and post-collisional extensional collapse regimes.
- Geochemical Contrast: Primitive island arc magmas (tonalites) are depleted in potassium and enriched in sodium and calcium ($P \gg A, Na_2O \gg K_2O$). In contrast, quartz monzonites involve thick continental crust where assimilation and partial melting of ancient K-rich crustal gneisses enriches the magma in potassium, producing co-equal alkali feldspar and plagioclase ($K_2O \approx Na_2O$).
Problem 6: CIPW Normative Calculation of Quartz Monzonite

A rock analysis yields CIPW normative minerals: $\text{Q} = 8.5\%, \text{Or} = 26.0\%, \text{Ab} = 28.5\%, \text{An} = 22.0\%, \text{Di} = 8.0\%, \text{Hy} = 5.0\%$. Calculate its normative QAP coordinates and verify its classification.

Normative Calculation:
1. $Q = 8.5\%$.
2. $A = \text{Or} = 26.0\%$.
3. $P = \text{Ab} + \text{An} = 28.5 + 22.0 = 50.5\%$.
4. Total Felsic Norm: $\sum = 8.5 + 26.0 + 50.5 = 85.0\%$.
5. Normalized Quartz: $Q' = \frac{8.5}{85.0} \times 100\% = \mathbf{10.0\%}$ (within $5\text{--}20\%$).
6. Plagioclase Ratio: $P^* = \frac{50.5}{26.0 + 50.5} \times 100\% = \frac{50.5}{76.5} \times 100\% \approx \mathbf{66.0\%}$ (at the Field 8*/9* boundary).
This confirms the rock is a transitional Quartz Monzonite to Quartz Monzodiorite.
Problem 7: Distinction from Norite and Gabbro

Why is "Quartz Norite" (Option C) completely incompatible with the description "approximately equal amounts of alkali feldspar and plagioclase"?

Fundamental Mineralogical Contradiction:
- In the IUGS system, any rock bearing the root name Gabbro, Norite, or Anorthosite must plot in Field 10, which requires that plagioclase constitutes at least $90\%$ of total feldspar ($P/(A+P) \ge 90\%$).
- Alkali feldspar can never exceed $10\%$. Therefore, a norite can NEVER have "approximately equal amounts of alkali feldspar and plagioclase" ($35\text{--}65\%$).
Problem 8: Optical Identification of Quartz Monzonite in Hand Specimen

How can a geologist distinguish a Quartz Monzonite from a Granite in a field hand specimen without a microscope?

Field Megascopic Diagnostic Criteria:
1. Feldspar Color Contrast: In hand specimen, two distinctly different feldspars are visible in roughly co-equal quantities: pink/salmon-colored potassium feldspar (orthoclase) and white/grey plagioclase displaying visible striations (twinning) on cleavage faces.
2. Quartz Abundance: Quartz is present as vitreous, smoky grey grains, but comprises only about 1 out of every 10 crystals ($10\text{--}15\%$), appearing distinctly subordinate to the overwhelming mass of interlocking feldspars—unlike true granite where vitreous quartz forms prominent, abundant puddles comprising $25\text{--}35\%$ of the rock surface.
