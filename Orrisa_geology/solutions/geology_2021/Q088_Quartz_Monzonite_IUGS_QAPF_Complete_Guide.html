1. Question & IUGS QAPF Classification

Question 88: A plutonic rock consisting of approximately equal amounts of alkali feldspar and plagioclase and with essential quartz (5%-20% of felsic minerals) but not enough to make the rock a granite is termed as :

  • (A) Quartz Monzonite
  • (B) Quartz Monzodiorite
  • (C) Quartz Norite
  • (D) Quartz Monzogabbro

Correct Answer: (A) Quartz Monzonite

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: The IUGS QAPF Plutonic Classification

The International Union of Geological Sciences (IUGS) classifies plutonic rocks using modal proportions of Quartz (Q), Alkali Feldspar (A), and Plagioclase (P):

  • Field 8 (Monzonite): $P/(A+P) = 35 ext{--}65\%$ (equal feldspars), with $Q < 5\%$ and $F < 10\%$.
  • Field 8* (Quartz Monzonite): $P/(A+P) = 35 ext{--}65\%$ (approximately equal amounts of alkali feldspar and plagioclase), with quartz content between $5\%$ and $20\%$ of total felsic minerals.
  • Field 3 (Granite): Requires quartz content $Q > 20\%$ (20–60% Q). Hence, 5–20% Q is explicitly "not enough to make the rock a granite".
  • Quartz Monzodiorite / Quartz Monzogabbro (Field 9*): Dominated by plagioclase where $P/(A+P) = 65 ext{--}90\%$.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A (Quartz Monzonite) is correct: Equal alkali feldspar and plagioclase ($35 ext{--}65\%$) with 5–20% quartz is the exact definition of Field 8*.
  • Option B & D (Quartz Monzodiorite / Monzogabbro): Plagioclase heavily dominates over alkali feldspar ($P/(A+P) > 65\%$).
  • Option C (Quartz Norite): Hypersthene-bearing gabbroic rock dominated almost entirely by calcic plagioclase ($P/(A+P) > 90\%$).
