2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Index Minerals vs Typomorphic Minerals

In metamorphic petrology, minerals whose presence is diagnostic of a specific, narrow range of temperature and pressure conditions are designated by two formal terms:

  • Index Minerals: First introduced by George Barrow (1912), an index mineral marks the first appearance of a new mineral phase (isograd) in pelitic rocks as metamorphic grade increases (e.g., chlorite, biotite, garnet, staurolite, kyanite, sillimanite).
  • Typomorphic Minerals: Introduced by Friedrich Becke and formalized by Paul Niggli and V.M. Goldschmidt, typomorphic minerals are minerals that are chemically stable only within a restricted range of physical conditions (specific metamorphic facies or temperature-pressure sub-zones) and therefore serve as diagnostic "type" minerals of that metamorphic regime (e.g., glaucophane for blueschist facies; staurolite for medium-pressure amphibolite facies).
  • Because both terms ("Typomorphic minerals" and "Index minerals") denote minerals characteristic of a specific metamorphic grade, option (D) is the strictly accurate answer.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option D (Both A and C are correct) is correct: Typomorphic minerals (Becke/Eskola) and Index minerals (Barrow/Tilley) are standard accepted terms for metamorphic grade indicators.
