2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: The Brazilian Indirect Tensile Test (ISRM / ASTM D3967)

Rocks are brittle materials that are exceptionally weak in tension compared to compression (tensile strength is typically only $5\% - 10\%$ of unconfined compressive strength, $\sigma_t \approx 0.1\,\sigma_c$).

  • The Brazilian Test Principle: Direct uniaxial tensile testing of rock cores is notoriously difficult because gripping the rock core induces stress concentrations that cause premature failure at the clamps. To circumvent this, the Brazilian Test (diametral compression test) was developed:
    • A circular disc of intact rock (thickness-to-diameter ratio $t/D \approx 0.5$) is placed between curved steel loading jaws and loaded compressively across its diameter.
    • According to the theory of elasticity (Hertz / Timoshenko), diametral line compression generates a uniform, biaxial tensile stress perpendicular to the loaded diameter across approximately $75\%$ of the disc diameter.
    • Failure initiates at the center as a tensile split running along the loaded diametral axis.
  • Mathematical Formula: $$\sigma_t = \frac{2 P}{\pi D t}$$ where $P$ is the failure load, $D$ is the disc diameter, and $t$ is the disc thickness.
  • Thus, the Brazilian test is the universally recognized standard test for determining the indirect tensile strength of rock materials.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A (Tensile strength) is correct: The Brazilian test is universally defined as the indirect tensile strength test for rock and concrete discs.
  • Option B (Compressive strength): Measured via Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS) test on cylindrical cores ($L/D = 2.0 - 2.5$).
  • Option C (Shear strength): Measured via direct shear box or triaxial shear testing.
  • Option D (Modulus of elasticity): Derived from stress-strain curves using axial strain extensometers.
