Q71: Cnidarian Paleontology - Subclass Rugosa: Tetracoralla or Horn Corals

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 358); P.K. Mukherjee (A Text Book of Geology, p. 170)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Subclass Rugosa (Order Rugosa / Tetracorallia)

Corals belong to the Phylum Cnidaria, Class Anthozoa. In the geological past, three major subclasses of skeleton-secreting corals dominated marine carbonate reef and shelf environments: Rugosa (Paleozoic), Tabulata (Paleozoic), and Scleractinia (Hexacorallia; Mesozoic to Recent). Among these, the extinct solitary and colonial corals of the Subclass Rugosa (Milne-Edwards & Haime, 1850; G.B. Mahapatra, A Text-Book of Geology, Chapter 91, p. 358 / PDF p. 187; P.K. Mukherjee, A Text Book of Geology, Chapter 14, p. 170) are famous for their unique morphological and structural features.

2. Why Rugose Corals are Called "Horn Corals" and "Tetracoralla"

The name Rugosa derives from Latin rugosus ("wrinkled" or "creased"), referring to the distinctive, coarse, wrinkled concentric growth lines visible on the outer protective wall (the epitheca). They are universally known by two iconic alternate designations:

  1. Horn Corals: Solitary rugose corallites possess an arched, expanding, conical skeleton that closely resembles a cow's horn (calceoloid or ceratoid shape). The animal (polyp) sat in the cup-shaped depression at the top, called the calyx, secreting a robust calcitic corallum as it grew upwards.
  2. Tetracoralla: Unlike modern reef-building corals (Scleractinia or Hexacorallia) whose septa are inserted in multiples of six (hexameral symmetry), rugose corals insert their radial vertical skeletal partitions (septa) in multiples of four (tetrameral symmetry) into four distinct quadrants!

3. Septal Insertion Mechanics in Tetracorals

During the ontogeny of a rugose coral, six primary (protosepta) septa appear initially: the cardinal septum, the counter septum, two alar septa, and two counter-lateral septa. Subsequent major and minor septa (metasepta) are inserted exclusively in four locations: on either side of the cardinal septum and on the counter side of the two alar septa, creating a pronounced bilateral symmetry and leaving a visible gap or depression called the cardinal fossula.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q71. The Rugose corals have unique horn-shaped chamber with a wrinkled or rugose wall are called ___.

(B) Tetracoralla or Horn corals
(C) Octocoralla
(D) All of these

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "The Rugose corals have unique horn-shaped chamber with a wrinkled or rugose wall are called ________"

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Scleractinia or Hexacorallia (INCORRECT): Scleractinian corals are the modern, living stony reef-building corals (Triassic to Recent). They possess an aragonitic skeleton with hexameral (six-fold) septal symmetry and lack the wrinkled calcitic horn-shaped morphology of the Paleozoic rugosans.
  • (B) Tetracoralla or Horn corals (CORRECT): The definitive, universal synonym for Subclass Rugosa is Tetracoralla (or Horn corals). The term "Horn corals" reflects their solitary horn-like conical corallum, while "Tetracoralla" reflects their diagnostic four-fold septal insertion and bilateral symmetry. Verbatim authority: G.B. Mahapatra, p. 358 classifies Rugosa explicitly as "Tetracoralla or Horn corals."
  • (C) Octocoralla (INCORRECT): Octocorallia (Alcyonaria) includes sea pens, sea fans, and soft corals possessing eight pinnate tentacles and eight internal mesenteries. They do not form the massive, horn-shaped wrinkled calcitic corallites of the rugose corals.
  • (D) All of these (INCORRECT): Rugosa, Scleractinia, and Octocorallia are mutually exclusive subclasses of Class Anthozoa.
