Q150. Water with electrical conductivity value of 2500 micro siemens/cm (at 25°C) is considered suitable for drinking purpose. When expressed this drinking water standard in terms of TDS, the corresponding TDS value (in mg/l) would become :

(B) 1550 mg/l
(C) 1600 mg/l
(D) 1650 mg/l

2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Hydrochemical Correlation between EC and TDS

Electrical Conductivity (EC) measures the ability of water to conduct an electric current, directly proportional to the concentration of dissolved ionized mineral salts. Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) represents the total mass of dissolved inorganic minerals expressed in milligrams per liter ($ ext{mg/l}$):

  • The Empirical Conversion Equation: $$\text{TDS (mg/l)} = A \times \text{EC (}\mu\text{S/cm at } 25^\circ\text{C)}$$ where $A$ is an empirical conversion factor determined by the dominant dissolved ionic species.
  • Standard Hydrochemical Values of Factor $A$:
    • For fresh waters dominated by bicarbonate, $A \approx 0.55 - 0.60$.
    • For mixed and moderately mineralized waters, $A \approx 0.64 - 0.65$ (the USGS standard factor established by Hem, 1985; Water-Supply Paper 2254).
    • For saline waters rich in sulfate and chloride, $A \approx 0.70 - 0.80$.
  • Calculation for the Exam Parameter: $$\text{Given: } \text{EC} = 2500\,\mu\text{S/cm}$$ Using the standard empirical multiplier $A = 0.64$: $$\text{TDS} = 2500 \times 0.64 = \mathbf{1600\text{ mg/l}}$$ This matches Option (C) exactly.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option C (1600 mg/l) is correct: $2500\,\mu\text{S/cm} \times 0.64 = 1600\text{ mg/l}$, matching the standard hydrochemical conversion coefficient.
  • Option A (1500 mg/l): Corresponds to $A = 0.60$, which understates mineralization for a high $2500\,\mu\text{S/cm}$ water.
  • Option B & D: Off-target multipliers ($0.62$ and $0.66$).
