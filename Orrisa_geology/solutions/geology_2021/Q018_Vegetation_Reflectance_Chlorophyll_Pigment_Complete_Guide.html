Q18: Vegetation Spectral Signature & Chlorophyll Pigment Control

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Gates et al. (1965); Knipling (1970); Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman (Remote Sensing, Ch. 1, pp. 17-19)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

18.1 The Spectral Reflectance Curve of Healthy Green Vegetation

In bio-optical remote sensing (Gates et al., 1965; Knipling, 1970; Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman, Remote Sensing, Ch. 1, pp. 17–19), the spectral reflectance of a healthy plant leaf is divided into three distinct optical domains:

Spectral Domain Wavelength Band Primary Governing Biological Factor Optical Phenomenon
Visible Domain $0.40 - 0.70\ \mu\text{m}$ Leaf Pigments (Chlorophyll a & b, Carotenoids) Intense absorption bands at $0.45\ \mu\text{m}$ (blue) and $0.65\ \mu\text{m}$ (red); intermediate green reflection peak at $0.55\ \mu\text{m}$ ($10 - 20\%$) gives foliage its green appearance.
Near-Infrared (NIR) $0.70 - 1.30\ \mu\text{m}$ Internal Spongy Mesophyll Cell Structure Refractive index mismatches between hydrated cell walls and air cavities cause multiple internal scattering ($40 - 60\%$ reflectance).
Shortwave-Infrared (SWIR) $1.30 - 2.50\ \mu\text{m}$ Leaf Moisture & Water Content Strong liquid water absorption valleys at $1.45\ \mu\text{m}$ and $1.94\ \mu\text{m}$.

18.2 Chlorophyll Pigment Absorption Peaks

Chlorophyll 'a' exhibits peak absorption at $0.43\ \mu\text{m}$ and $0.66\ \mu\text{m}$; chlorophyll 'b' absorbs at $0.45\ \mu\text{m}$ and $0.65\ \mu\text{m}$. The unabsorbed green photons between $0.50$ and $0.58\ \mu\text{m}$ are reflected, directly creating the green peak.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q18. In the visible region (green band), leaf reflectance is governed by :
(A) Leaf moisture
(B) Leaf pigment
(C) Leaf cell structure
(D) None of these
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) Leaf pigment — In the visible region of the spectrum (including the green band), leaf reflectance is governed almost exclusively by leaf pigments (primarily chlorophyll).

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Leaf moisture — INCORRECT: Leaf moisture governs reflectance in the Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR: $1.4 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$), not the visible green band.
  • (B) Leaf pigment — CORRECT: Chlorophyll and accessory carotenoid pigments completely dominate optical absorption and reflection throughout the $0.4 - 0.7\ \mu\text{m}$ visible spectrum.
  • (C) Leaf cell structure — INCORRECT: Spongy mesophyll cell structure controls the high reflectance plateau in the Near-Infrared (NIR: $0.7 - 1.3\ \mu\text{m}$).
  • (D) None of these — INCORRECT.

Vegetation Remote Sensing Application

Practice Problem 18: The "Red Edge" Phenomenon

What is the "Red Edge" in vegetative remote sensing, and what biological transition causes it?

Answer:

The Red Edge is the extremely steep rise in reflectance between $0.68\ \mu\text{m}$ and $0.75\ \mu\text{m}$. It marks the abrupt physical transition from intense chlorophyll pigment absorption in the visible red band to massive internal mesophyll cellular scattering in the near-infrared band.
