2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Continental Red Beds & Arid Paleoclimates

Red beds are sequence of continental siliciclastic sedimentary rocks (sandstones, siltstones, conglomerates, and mudstones) that possess a distinctive red, reddish-brown, or purple coloration due to microscopic coatings and pigments of ferric oxide (principally hematite, $ ext{Fe}_2 ext{O}_3$):

  • Arid / Semi-Arid Depositional Setting: Red beds form in oxidizing continental environments such as alluvial fans, desert dunes (aeolian ergs), playa lake margins, and arid fluvial floodplains. The dry, oxidizing conditions prevent the reduction of iron into soluble ferrous ($Fe^{2+}$) forms or preservation of organic plant carbon.
  • Diagenetic Pigmentation: In warm, oxidizing, arid groundwaters, detrital iron-bearing silicates (hornblende, pyroxene, biotite) break down to release iron, which crystallizes as ultra-fine hematite pigments coating quartz and feldspar grains.
  • Ironstones: Typically marine sedimentary iron deposits formed under fluctuating redox conditions, not diagnostic of continental aridity.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option B (Red beds) is correct: Classical sedimentary lithofacies used globally as an unequivocal indicator of continental arid to semi-arid paleoclimates (e.g., Old Red Sandstone, New Red Sandstone).
  • Option A (Ironstone): Marine chemical/biochemical iron deposit.
  • Option C (Cherty sandstone): Siliceous sandstone formed in varied environments, not an arid paleoclimate indicator.
