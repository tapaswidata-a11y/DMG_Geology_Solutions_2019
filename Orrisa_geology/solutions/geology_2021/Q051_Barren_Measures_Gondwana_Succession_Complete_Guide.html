1. The Damuda Group: The Permian Coal Measures of Peninsular India

The Damuda Group (Permian) of the Lower Gondwana Supergroup contains the world-famous commercial bituminous coal measures of Peninsular India. Across the Damodar, Son-Mahanadi, and Pranhita-Godavari valleys, the Damuda succession is classically subdivided into three distinct lithostratigraphic formations in ascending order:

  • 1. Barakar Formation (Early Permian / Artinskian-Kungurian): The primary lower coal-bearing formation ($250-700\text{ m}$ thick). Characterized by fining-upward channel cycles of coarse, white feldspathic (arkosic) sandstones, grey shales, fireclays, and multiple thick, workable bituminous coal seams (e.g., Seams I through XVI in Jharia and Raniganj).
  • 2. Barren Measures Formation (Middle Permian / Roadian-Wordian): The non-coaliferous stratigraphic interval sandwiched conformably between the two major coal-bearing units. It attains a thickness of $300-600\text{ m}$ in the Jharia and Bokaro coalfields. In the Raniganj Coalfield, this identical lithostratigraphic horizon is historically termed the Ironstone Shale Formation due to the abundant siderite and limonite nodules embedded in its dark carbonaceous shales.
  • 3. Raniganj Formation (Late Permian / Capitanian-Changhsingian): The upper coal-bearing formation ($500-1000\text{ m}$ thick). Dominated by fine to medium-grained calcareous and feldspathic sandstones, siltstones, and numerous high-volatile, long-flame superior coal seams accompanied by an advanced Glossopteris flora.

2. Depositional Mechanics and Climatic Hiatus of Coalification

The total absence of workable coal in the Barren Measures reflects a profound paleoclimatic and paleoenvironmental shift across eastern Gondwanaland during the Mid-Permian:

The Classic Tripartite Damuda Chronostratigraphic Sandwich
$$\mathbf{Barakar \text{ Formation}} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{Barren \text{ Measures (Ironstone Shales)}} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{Raniganj \text{ Formation}}$$

Warm Humid Peat Swamps $\to$ Warm Semi-Arid Stagnant Lakes $\to$ Restored Humid River-Floodplain Peat Swamps.

  • Semi-Arid Climatic Pulsing: During the Middle Permian, an interval of seasonal aridity and intense seasonal drying curtailed the continuous, lush growth of swamp vegetation required for thick peat accumulation.
  • Stagnant Lacustrine Ironstone Deposition: Sedimentation shifted from high-energy braided fluvial channels to extensive, shallow, stratified lacustrine bodies. High dissolved ferrous iron in anoxic, acidic bottom waters reacted with bicarbonate to precipitate bedded and nodular siderite ($\text{FeCO}_3$), which weathered subaerially to limonite and goethite ironstones.
  • Basin Equivalents: In the Godavari and Satpura basins, coeval non-coal strata are termed the Motur Formation and Lower Kamthi beds.

Official Examination Question
Question 51
The Mid-Permian non-coaliferous lithounit sandwitched between two major coal-bearing formations in the Indian Lower Gondwana succession is known as.________ Formation.
(A) Barakar
(B) Lower Kamthi
(C) Barren Measures
(D) Raniganj
Verified Correct Answer: (C) Barren Measures [The Barren Measures Formation (known as Ironstone Shales in Raniganj) is the non-coal-bearing Mid-Permian unit sandwiched between the coal-bearing Barakar and Raniganj Formations.]
Correct Option: (C) Barren Measures

Detailed Stratigraphic Forensic Analysis:

  • Within the Permian Damuda Group of the Indian Gondwana Supergroup, coal deposition was famously punctuated during the Middle Permian by an interval devoid of economic coal seams.
  • This non-coal-bearing formation is formally designated the Barren Measures Formation (so named by Fox in 1931 because it is completely barren of workable coal seams). In the Raniganj basin, it is known as the Ironstone Shale Formation.
  • It lies directly and conformably above the richly coal-bearing Barakar Formation and directly below the major coal-bearing Raniganj Formation.
  • Therefore, the unit sandwiched between the two major coal-bearing formations is unequivocally the Barren Measures Formation.
Incorrect Distractor: (A) Barakar

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Barakar Formation is the premier coal-bearing formation of India, hosting the thickest and most valuable coking coal seams in the Damodar, Mahanadi, and Son basins.
  • It represents the underlying coaliferous member, not the non-coaliferous unit sandwiched in the middle.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) Raniganj

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Raniganj Formation is the upper major coal-bearing formation of the Damuda Group, celebrated for superior non-coking and long-flame thermal coals.
  • It overlies the Barren Measures; it is not the sandwiched non-coal unit.
Incorrect Distractor: (B) Lower Kamthi

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Kamthi Formation is a lithostratigraphic unit of the Pranhita-Godavari and Wardha-Nagpur valleys, extending from the Late Permian into the Early Triassic.
  • In standard Pan-Indian Gondwana nomenclature, the canonical Damuda sandwich sequence is Barakar $\to$ Barren Measures $\to$ Raniganj. Lower Kamthi is a lateral provincial correlate in central India, not the standard textbook sandwich unit.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Gondwana Stratigraphy & Coal Petrology
Problem 1: Siderite to Limonite Diagenetic Oxidation

In the Ironstone Shales of Raniganj, bedded siderite ($\text{FeCO}_3$, density $\rho = 3.96\text{ g/cm}^3$) oxidizes upon exposure to goethite ($\text{FeO(OH)}$, density $\rho = 4.28\text{ g/cm}^3$). Write the balanced geochemical oxidation-hydration reaction.

Step-by-Step Solution:

In the presence of atmospheric oxygen and surface groundwater:

$$4\text{FeCO}_3 + \text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 4\text{FeO(OH)} + 4\text{CO}_2 \uparrow$$

This reaction dissolves soluble bicarbonate, precipitating insoluble hydrous iron oxyhydroxides (limonite and goethite) that cement the shales into dense, reddish-brown ironstone bands historically smelted at Kulti (Bengal Iron Works).

Problem 2: Coal Seam Peat Compaction Ratio

If the peat-to-bituminous coal compaction ratio in the Barakar Formation is $10:1$, calculate the original peat swamp thickness required to generate a $12\text{ m}$ thick coal seam.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$T_{\text{peat}} = 10 \times T_{\text{coal}} = 10 \times 12\text{ m} = 120\text{ m}$$

Final Answer: A massive $120\text{ m}$ of waterlogged peat was accumulated to yield a $12\text{ m}$ thick bituminous coal seam.

Problem 3: Vitrinite Reflectance and Coal Rank

Barakar coking coals typically exhibit mean random vitrinite reflectance $R_o = 1.15\%-1.35\%$, whereas Raniganj non-coking coals have $R_o = 0.70\%-0.85\%$. Explain the geological reason for this rank disparity.

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Barakar Formation was buried beneath more than $2000-3000\text{ m}$ of overlying Barren Measures, Raniganj, and Panchet strata. Due to deeper burial depth ($z$) and higher paleo-temperatures ($T \propto z \cdot dT/dz$), the Barakar coals underwent more intense thermal catagenesis, aromatization, and volatile loss, advancing to high-rank medium-volatile coking coal.

Problem 4: Botanical Identity of Vertebraria

What anatomical part of the Gondwana seed-fern plant does the common fossil Vertebraria represent?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Vertebraria indica represents the specialized root system (rhizome) of the Glossopteris tree. Its unique transverse rectangular partitions (resembling vertebrae) provided internal air cavities (aerenchyma) allowing the plant to breathe in waterlogged, anoxic peat bogs.

Problem 5: Proximate Analysis Calculation

A coal sample from the Barakar Formation yields: Moisture $M = 2.0\%$, Ash $A = 18.0\%$, Volatile Matter $VM = 26.0\%$. Calculate the Fixed Carbon ($FC$) content on an air-dried basis.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$FC = 100\% - (M + A + VM)$$ $$FC = 100 - (2.0 + 18.0 + 26.0) = 100 - 46.0 = 54.0\%$$

Final Answer: Fixed Carbon is $54.0\%$.

Problem 6: Differentiation of Gondwanidium vs. Glossopteris

How does the basal Gondwana index plant fossil Gondwanidium differ from Glossopteris?

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Gondwanidium validum: Possesses pinnate, lobed fronds with neuropteroid venation without a true midrib or reticulate anastomosing mesh. Diagnostic index fossil of the early Sakmarian Karharbari Formation.
  • Glossopteris: Possesses simple, tongue-shaped (lingulate) leaves with a distinct prominent central midrib and complex polygonal reticulate anastomosing secondary veins.
Problem 7: Palynological Biozone of the Barren Measures

Which miospore assemblage characterizes the Barren Measures Formation in Indian palynostratigraphy?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Barren Measures is defined by the Densipollenites indicus Assemblage Zone (monosaccate envelope pollen), with sub-dominant Striatopodocarpites and Faunipollenites, reflecting drought-tolerant gymnosperm floral elements surrounding shrinking lakes.

Problem 8: Coalfield Boundary Fault Reactivation

Why are Indian Gondwana coalfields characterized by half-graben geometry with a single master boundary fault on the southern margin?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Extensional intraplate stress during the Permo-Triassic reactivated pre-existing Proterozoic basement faults as asymmetric listric normal faults. Continued fault displacement rotated the sedimentary floor into a hanging-wall syncline, tilting strata southward towards the master normal boundary fault where maximum sediment thickness is preserved.
