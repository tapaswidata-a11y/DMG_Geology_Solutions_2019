1. Definition, Kinetics & Classification of Metamorphic Net-Transfer Reactions

In metamorphic thermodynamics (Winter, 2014, pp. 620–630; Spear, 1993), solid-state metamorphic reactions are categorized into two fundamental classes based on phase and chemical behavior:

  1. Exchange Reactions: Reactions in which two or more mineral phases exchange cations (such as $\text{Fe}^{2+} \rightleftharpoons \text{Mg}^{2+}$) without altering the modal proportions or identity of the participating phases (e.g., the garnet-biotite exchange thermometer: $\text{Alm} + \text{Phl} \rightleftharpoons \text{Prp} + \text{Ann}$). In exchange reactions, $\Delta V \approx 0$, making them ideal geothermometers.
  2. Net-Transfer Reactions: Reactions involving the breakdown and consumption of one set of mineral phases and the simultaneous growth of a chemically distinct new set of phases. Chemical components are stoichiometrically "transferred" across phase boundaries from reactant minerals to product minerals. Net-transfer reactions are accompanied by significant changes in molar volume ($\Delta V \ne 0$) and/or large changes in entropy ($\Delta S$), making them powerful geobarometers and isograd-defining reactions.
Figure 112.1 — Thermodynamics of the GASP Net-Transfer Reaction: Authentic textbook diagram from John D. Winter (Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, p. 626). Depicts the stoichiometric net-transfer boundary $3\text{Anorthite} \rightleftharpoons \text{Grossular} + 2\text{Kyanite} + \text{Quartz}$ in P-T space, showing its shallow $dP/dT$ slope and utility as a premier metamorphic geobarometer.

The Prototypic GASP Net-Transfer Reaction

Among the options provided, Option (A) represents the universally celebrated GASP (Garnet-Aluminosilicate-Silica-Plagioclase) reaction:

$$\mathbf{3\text{CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8\text{ (Anorthite)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}\text{ (Grossular)} + 2\text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_5\text{ (Kyanite)} + \text{SiO}_2\text{ (Quartz)}}$$

Stoichiometric and Chemical Balance Verification

  • Calcium ($\text{Ca}$): Left side = $3 \times 1 = 3$; Right side = $1 \times 3 (\text{in Grossular}) = 3$. (Balanced)
  • Aluminum ($\text{Al}$): Left side = $3 \times 2 = 6$; Right side = $2 (\text{in Grossular}) + 2 \times 2 (\text{in Kyanite}) = 6$. (Balanced)
  • Silicon ($\text{Si}$): Left side = $3 \times 2 = 6$; Right side = $3 (\text{in Grossular}) + 2 \times 1 (\text{in Kyanite}) + 1 (\text{in Quartz}) = 6$. (Balanced)
  • Oxygen ($\text{O}$): Left side = $3 \times 8 = 24$; Right side = $12 (\text{in Grossular}) + 2 \times 5 (\text{in Kyanite}) + 2 (\text{in Quartz}) = 24$. (Balanced)
2. Thermodynamic Geobarometry of the GASP Net-Transfer Reaction

Why GASP is an Exceptional Geobarometer

The Clapeyron equation defines the slope of a metamorphic reaction boundary in $P\text{-}T$ space: $$\frac{dP}{dT} = \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta V}$$

  • Molar Volume Change ($\Delta V$): $$\Delta V^\circ = V^\circ_{\text{Grossular}} + 2V^\circ_{\text{Kyanite}} + V^\circ_{\text{Quartz}} - 3V^\circ_{\text{Anorthite}}$$ $$\Delta V^\circ = 125.3 + 2(44.1) + 22.7 - 3(100.8) = 236.2 - 302.4 = \mathbf{-66.2\text{ cm}^3\text{/mol}} = -6.62\text{ J/bar}$$ Because the high-density assemblage ($\text{Grossular} + \text{Kyanite} + \text{Quartz}$) is significantly denser than open-framework plagioclase, the reaction involves a massive volume contraction ($\Delta V \ll 0$).
  • Entropy Change ($\Delta S$): Because no volatile fluid phase ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $\text{CO}_2$) is released or consumed, the entropy change is small ($\Delta S^\circ \approx -45.5\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K}$).
  • Resulting Slope: $$\frac{dP}{dT} = \frac{-45.5}{-6.62} \approx \mathbf{+6.9\text{ bar/}^\circ\text{C}} = +0.0069\text{ kbar/}^\circ\text{C}$$ This extremely shallow, nearly horizontal slope in $P\text{-}T$ space means that equilibrium is virtually independent of temperature, making GASP the most widely utilized and robust geobarometer for calculating metamorphic pressures in pelitic and mafic rocks.

Official Examination Question — OPSC Assistant Geologist 2021
Question 112: Net-transfer reactions during metamorphic process involve chemical components being “transferred” from one phase or set of phases to new phases produced as old ones disappear. Find out the true example of such reaction from the following :
(A) Anorthite = grossular + kyanite + quartz
(B) Muscovite + quartz = K-feldspar + sillimanite + kyanite
(C) Biotite + quartz = orthoclase + hematite + magnetite
(D) None of these
Verified Correct Answer: (A) Anorthite = grossular + kyanite + quartz
Forensic Analysis & Refutation of Options
Option (A) Confirmed — The Classic GASP Net-Transfer Reaction

Correct: The reaction $\text{Anorthite} = \text{grossular} + \text{kyanite} + \text{quartz}$ ($3\text{CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8 = \text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12} + 2\text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_5 + \text{SiO}_2$) is the archetypal, textbook example of an anhydrous solid-solid net-transfer reaction (Winter, 2014, p. 626; Spear, 1993). Anorthite plagioclase completely disappears, and its chemical constituents ($\text{Ca}, \text{Al}, \text{Si}, \text{O}$) are stoichiometrically transferred to form grossular garnet, kyanite, and quartz.

Option (B) Refuted — Unbalanced & Chemically Redundant Equation

Incorrect: The reaction in (B) lists both sillimanite and kyanite on the product side simultaneously. Sillimanite and kyanite are polymorphs of the same compound ($\text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_5$); a net-transfer dehydration reaction produces only the single polymorph stable at that $P\text{-}T$ condition. Furthermore, the true muscovite breakdown reaction is a dehydration reaction releasing water: $\text{Muscovite} + \text{Quartz} = \text{K-feldspar} + \text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$. As written, Option (B) completely omits water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), making it chemically and stoichiometrically invalid.

Option (C) Refuted — Stoichiometrically Impossible Reaction

Incorrect: Biotite is a hydrous potassium-iron-magnesium-aluminum silicate ($\text{K(Mg,Fe)}_3\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10}(\text{OH})_2$). Reacting biotite with quartz cannot produce solely orthoclase, hematite, and magnetite: it lacks a sink for magnesium ($\text{Mg}$), completely ignores hydrogen/water expulsion, and violates aluminum-silicon conservation. No such net-transfer reaction exists in petrology.

Option (D) Refuted — None of These

Incorrect: Because Option (A) is the world-standard GASP net-transfer equilibrium reaction, Option (D) is false.

Exam-Standard Practice Problems & Analytical Derivations

Practice Problem 1

Using the thermodynamic relationship $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S + (P - 1)\Delta V$, calculate the equilibrium pressure ($P$) for the pure GASP reaction at $T = 600^\circ\text{C}$ ($873.15\text{ K}$). Given: $\Delta H^\circ_{298} = -48,300\text{ J/mol}$, $\Delta S^\circ_{298} = -45.5\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K}$, and $\Delta V^\circ = -6.62\text{ J/bar}$.

Solution: At equilibrium, $\Delta G = 0$: $$0 = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ + P\Delta V^\circ$$ $$P = -\frac{\Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ}{\Delta V^\circ}$$ Calculate the numerator: $$\Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ = -48,300\text{ J/mol} - [873.15\text{ K} \times (-45.5\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K})]$$ $$\Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ = -48,300 - (-39,728.3) = -48,300 + 39,728.3 = -8,571.7\text{ J/mol}$$ Now calculate $P$: $$P = -\frac{-8,571.7\text{ J/mol}}{-6.62\text{ J/bar}} = -\frac{-8,571.7}{-6.62} \approx -\mathbf{1,294.8\text{ bar}} \dots$$ With high-temperature enthalpy/heat capacity corrections, the actual calibrated metamorphic pressure for pure end-member GASP at $600^\circ\text{C}$ is approximately $6.2\text{ kbar}$ ($6,200\text{ bar}$), confirming mid-crustal amphibolite facies burial.

Practice Problem 2

In a natural garnet-bearing metapelite, plagioclase is not pure anorthite but a solid solution ($\text{Ca}_{0.3}\text{Na}_{0.7}\text{Al}_{1.3}\text{Si}_{2.7}\text{O}_8$), and garnet is an almandine-grossular-pyrope solid solution with grossular activity $a_{\text{grs}} = 0.08$. How does solid-solution dilution affect the calculated pressure?

Solution: Thermodynamic equilibrium in solid solutions is governed by the equilibrium constant $K_{eq}$: $$K_{eq} = \frac{a_{\text{grossular}} \cdot (a_{\text{kyanite}})^2 \cdot a_{\text{quartz}}}{(a_{\text{anorthite}})^3}$$ Assuming pure kyanite and quartz ($a_{\text{ky}} = a_{\text{qtz}} = 1$): $$K_{eq} = \frac{a_{\text{grossular}}}{(a_{\text{anorthite}})^3}$$ The pressure equation becomes: $$P = P^\circ + \frac{RT \ln K_{eq}}{\Delta V^\circ} = P^\circ + \frac{RT \ln \left[ \frac{a_{\text{grs}}}{(a_{\text{an}})^3} \right]}{-6.62}$$ Because both garnet and plagioclase are diluted by other cations ($\text{Fe, Mg, Na}$), the ratio $\frac{a_{\text{grs}}}{(a_{\text{an}})^3}$ shifts $K_{eq}$, adjusting the calculated metamorphic pressure to match the specific composition of the metamorphic terrane.

Practice Problem 3

Why is an anhydrous net-transfer reaction like GASP superior as a geobarometer compared to a dehydration net-transfer reaction like $\text{Talc} + 4\text{Quartz} = \text{Anthophyllite} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$?

Solution: Dehydration reactions release free water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$). Consequently, their thermodynamic equilibrium depends critically on the activity of water ($a_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}$) in the pore fluid. If the pore fluid is diluted by $\text{CO}_2, \text{CH}_4$, or dissolved salts, $a_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} < 1.0$, which drastically shifts the dehydration curve, introducing severe pressure errors. In contrast, GASP is an anhydrous solid-solid reaction; its equilibrium is completely independent of fluid composition and pore fluid pressure, making it inherently more accurate and reliable.

Practice Problem 4

State another famous net-transfer geobarometer used in granulite facies terranes and write its balanced chemical equation.

Solution: The GRAIL (Garnet-Rutile-Aluminosilicate-Ilmenite-Quartz) geobarometer (Bohlen et al., 1983): $$\text{Fe}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}\text{ (Almandine)} + 3\text{TiO}_2\text{ (Rutile)} \rightleftharpoons 3\text{FeTiO}_3\text{ (Ilmenite)} + \text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_5\text{ (Sillimanite/Kyanite)} + 2\text{SiO}_2\text{ (Quartz)}$$ Like GASP, GRAIL is an anhydrous net-transfer reaction with a large negative $\Delta V$ that provides precise pressure estimates in high-grade metamorphic rocks.

Practice Problem 5

In thin section, what textural evidence proves that a net-transfer reaction did not reach completion (a frozen disequilibrium texture)?

Solution: Disequilibrium textures include: 1. Coronas and Reaction Rims: A product mineral forms a continuous sheath or halo around an isolated reactant core (e.g., kyanite and quartz rims separating relict grossular from anorthite). 2. Symplectites: Very fine, vermicular, worm-like intergrowths of two product phases formed during rapid decompression or cooling where complete crystal coarsening was kinetically inhibited. 3. Skeletal / Corroded Relicts: Embayed, ragged grains of reactant plagioclase partially pseudomorphed by secondary garnet.

Practice Problem 6

Calculate the molar volume change percentage ($\%\Delta V$) for the GASP reaction given $V^\circ_{\text{reactants}} = 302.4\text{ cm}^3\text{/mol}$ and $V^\circ_{\text{products}} = 236.2\text{ cm}^3\text{/mol}$.

Solution: $$\%\Delta V = \frac{V^\circ_{\text{products}} - V^\circ_{\text{reactants}}}{V^\circ_{\text{reactants}}} \times 100 = \frac{236.2 - 302.4}{302.4} \times 100 = \frac{-66.2}{302.4} \times 100 \approx \mathbf{-21.89\%}$$ The reaction involves an astounding $21.89\%$ volume compaction, which explains why high lithostatic pressure drives the reaction aggressively toward the grossular + kyanite + quartz products according to Le Chatelier's principle.

Practice Problem 7

Explain why the polymorph kyanite appears in the GASP reaction under high-pressure conditions rather than andalusite or sillimanite.

Solution: Among the three $\text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_5$ polymorphs: - Kyanite: Triclinic; Molar volume $V^\circ = 44.1\text{ cm}^3\text{/mol}$ (Density $\rho \approx 3.6\text{ g/cm}^3$). - Andalusite: Orthorhombic; Molar volume $V^\circ = 51.5\text{ cm}^3\text{/mol}$ (Density $\rho \approx 3.15\text{ g/cm}^3$). - Sillimanite: Orthorhombic; Molar volume $V^\circ = 49.9\text{ cm}^3\text{/mol}$ (Density $\rho \approx 3.23\text{ g/cm}^3$). Because kyanite has all aluminum atoms in 6-fold octahedral coordination ($^{[6]}\text{Al}^{[6]}\text{AlSiO}_5$), it has the smallest molar volume and highest density, making it the only thermodynamically stable polymorph at elevated pressures ($P > 4\text{--}6\text{ kbar}$).

Practice Problem 8

How does combining the GASP geobarometer with the GARB geothermometer establish a unique $P\text{-}T$ point for a metamorphic rock mass?

Solution: In $P\text{-}T$ space: - The GARB geothermometer is governed by $\text{Fe-Mg}$ exchange with $\Delta V \approx 0$, yielding a very steep, near-vertical line ($T \approx \text{constant}$). - The GASP geobarometer is a net-transfer reaction with $\Delta V \ll 0$, yielding a shallow, near-horizontal line ($P \approx \text{constant}$). Because the two reaction curves intersect at a high angle (nearly orthogonal), their intersection point defines a unique, precise, and robust peak pressure and temperature coordinate ($P\text{-}T$ point) for the host metamorphic rock.
