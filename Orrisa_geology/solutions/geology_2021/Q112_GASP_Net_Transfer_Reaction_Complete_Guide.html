1. Question & Reaction Stoichiometry

Question 112: Net-transfer reactions during metamorphic process involve chemical components being “transferred” from one phase or set of phases to new phases produced as old ones disappear. Find out the true example of such reaction from the following :

  • (A) Anorthite = grossular + kyanite + quartz
  • (B) Muscovite + quartz = K-feldspar + sillimanite + kyanite
  • (C) Biotite + quartz = orthoclase + hematite + magnetite
  • (D) None of these

Correct Answer: (A) Anorthite = grossular + kyanite + quartz

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q112. Net-transfer reactions during metamorphic process involve chemical components being “transferred” from one phase or set of phases to new phases produced as old ones disappear. Find out the true example of such reaction from the following :
(A) Anorthite = grossular + kyanite + quartz
(B) Muscovite + quartz = K-feldspar + sillimanite + kyanite
(C) Biotite + quartz = orthoclase + hematite + magnetite
(D) None of these

2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Net-Transfer Reactions and the GASP Barometer

Metamorphic reactions are categorized into continuous solid-solution exchange reactions and discontinuous net-transfer reactions. In net-transfer reactions, modal proportions of reactant phases decrease to zero as entirely new product phases nucleate and grow through mass transfer across phase boundaries:

  • The GASP Barometer Reaction: $$3\,\text{CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8\text{ (Anorthite in plagioclase)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}\text{ (Grossular in garnet)} + 2\,\text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_5\text{ (Kyanite)} + \text{SiO}_2\text{ (Quartz)}$$
    • This is the celebrated GASP (Garnet - Aluminosilicate - Silica - Plagioclase) reaction, the premier geobarometer for metapelites.
    • Because grossular garnet and kyanite are much denser than calcic plagioclase, the reaction involves a large negative volume change ($\Delta V_r < 0$). Consequently, the equilibrium boundary has a very gentle $dP/dT$ slope, making it extraordinarily sensitive to pressure.
  • Why Options B and C are Stoichiometrically False:
    • Option B: The second-sillimanite breakdown is $\text{Muscovite} + \text{Quartz} = \text{K-feldspar} + \text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$. Having both sillimanite AND kyanite simultaneously as reaction products with no $H_2O$ is chemically and phase-rule invalid.
    • Option C: Biotite breakdown requires oxygen and water balancing and yields pyroxenes/garnet + orthoclase, not simultaneous hematite and magnetite without stoichiometry.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A is correct: The classic, stoichiometrically balanced GASP net-transfer reaction: $3\,\text{Anorthite} = \text{Grossular} + 2\,\text{Kyanite} + \text{Quartz}$.
  • Option B: Erroneously includes two polymorphs on product side and omits liberated water.
  • Option C: Chemically unbalanced fiction.
