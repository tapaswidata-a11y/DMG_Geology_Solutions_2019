Q11: Atmospheric Scattering Mechanisms (Rayleigh, Mie, Non-Selective) & Sky Coloration

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman (Remote Sensing & Image Interpretation, 7th Ed., Ch. 1, pp. 9-14); Sabins (Ch. 1)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

11.1 Physics of Atmospheric Scattering in Remote Sensing

In atmospheric physics and remote sensing (Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman, Remote Sensing and Image Interpretation, 7th Ed., Ch. 1, pp. 9–14; Sabins, Ch. 1), solar radiation interacts with atmospheric particles through three distinct scattering mechanisms, classified by the ratio of particle diameter ($d$) to incident wavelength ($\lambda$):

Scattering Type Particle Diameter vs. Wavelength Primary Atmospheric Agents Wavelength Dependence & Spectral Behavior
Rayleigh Scattering $d \ll \lambda$ (typically $d < 0.1\lambda$) Gas molecules ($N_2, O_2, CO_2$) Extremely strong inverse 4th power dependence: $I \propto \lambda^{-4}$. Scatters blue light $\sim 9.4$ times more than red light.
Mie Scattering $d \approx \lambda$ ($0.1\lambda < d < 10\lambda$) Smoke, dust, pollen, fine aerosols Weak wavelength dependence ($I \propto \lambda^{-\alpha}$, where $\alpha \approx 0.5 - 2$). Creates overcast haze.
Non-Selective Scattering $d \gg \lambda$ ($d > 10\lambda$) Water droplets, ice crystals, cloud particles ($> 5-100\ \mu\text{m}$) No wavelength dependence; scatters all visible wavelengths equally ($I \approx \text{const}$), making clouds and fog appear white.

11.2 Mathematical Derivation of Sky Coloration

According to Lord Rayleigh's scattering formula, the intensity of scattered light ($I_s$) at distance $R$ and scattering angle $\theta$ is:

$$I_s = I_0 \frac{8\pi^4 \alpha^2}{\lambda^4 R^2} (1 + \cos^2 \theta)$$

Comparing blue light ($\lambda_b = 0.45\ \mu\text{m}$) and red light ($\lambda_r = 0.70\ \mu\text{m}$):

$$\frac{I_{s,\text{blue}}}{I_{s,\text{red}}} = \left(\frac{\lambda_r}{\lambda_b}\right)^4 = \left(\frac{0.70}{0.45}\right)^4 = (1.555)^4 \approx 5.86$$

Because nitrogen and oxygen molecules scatter shorter blue wavelengths almost 6 to 10 times more effectively throughout the daytime sky, the diffuse scattered skylight arriving at the Earth's surface is predominantly blue.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q11. What is responsible for the blue colour of the sky ?
(A) Mie Scattering
(B) Non-Selective Scattering
(C) Rayleigh Scattering
(D) Water droplets present in the sky
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (C)

Correct Answer: (C) Rayleigh Scattering — Rayleigh scattering by atmospheric gas molecules ($N_2, O_2$) with an intensity proportional to $\lambda^{-4}$ preferentially scatters short blue wavelengths, causing the blue color of the daytime sky.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Mie Scattering — INCORRECT: Mie scattering is caused by dust, pollen, and aerosols of sizes comparable to wavelength ($d \approx \lambda$). It creates whitish-gray atmospheric haze, not the clear blue sky.
  • (B) Non-Selective Scattering — INCORRECT: Caused by large cloud droplets ($d \gg \lambda$) that scatter all visible wavelengths equally, resulting in white clouds and fog.
  • (C) Rayleigh Scattering — CORRECT: Classical, universal atmospheric mechanism where $d \ll \lambda$, generating the $\lambda^{-4}$ selective scattering of blue light.
  • (D) Water droplets present in the sky — INCORRECT: Water droplets produce non-selective scattering (white clouds) or rain, not molecular Rayleigh scattering.

Worked Scattering & Optical Remote Sensing Problem

Practice Problem 11: Atmospheric Path Radiance & Band Selection

Why do Landsat and Sentinel-2 optical geological sensors avoid using blue-band data for rock-type classification, preferring SWIR bands?

Answer:

Due to Rayleigh scattering ($I \propto \lambda^{-4}$), atmospheric path radiance (haze) is at its maximum in the blue band ($0.45 - 0.52\ \mu\text{m}$), severely degrading surface image contrast. In contrast, SWIR bands ($1.55 - 2.35\ \mu\text{m}$) have $\lambda$ values $4-5$ times larger, experiencing virtually zero Rayleigh scattering ($(\frac{0.45}{2.20})^4 \approx 0.0017$), providing razor-sharp surface diagnostic mineral absorption spectra.
