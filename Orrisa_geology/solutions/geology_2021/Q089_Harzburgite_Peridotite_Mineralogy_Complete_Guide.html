1. Definition, History & Mineralogical Formula of Harzburgite

The rock name harzburgite was introduced into petrological literature in 1880 by K.A. Lossen, named after its type locality near Bad Harzburg in the Harz Mountains of Lower Saxony, Germany.

In the standard IUGS classification of ultramafic rocks (Le Maitre, 2002; Winter, 2014, p. 29), harzburgite is formally defined as an ultramafic plutonic rock ($M' \ge 90\%$) belonging to the Peridotite group ($\text{Olivine} \ge 40\%$), composed essentially of:

IUGS Mineralogical Definition of Harzburgite:
$$\mathbf{\text{Harzburgite}} = \mathbf{\text{Olivine (40--90\%)}} + \mathbf{\text{Orthopyroxene (10--60\%)}} \quad [\mathbf{\text{Clinopyroxene} < 10\%}]$$

It is universally recognized as the two-mineral ultramafic rock composed of olivine and orthopyroxene (enstatite or bronzite), commonly accompanied by accessory chromite or spinel.

Highlighting the Harzburgite field defined by $\text{Olivine} = 40\text{--}90\%$, $\text{Opx} > 10\%$, and $\text{Cpx} < 10\%$.
2. The IUGS Peridotite-Pyroxenite Ternary System ($Ol\text{--}Opx\text{--}Cpx$)

Ultramafic rocks ($M' \ge 90\%$) are classified on a triangular ternary diagram whose vertices represent the three dominant ferromagnesian phases normalized to $100\%$: Olivine ($Ol$), Orthopyroxene ($Opx$), and Clinopyroxene ($Cpx$):

The Peridotite Division ($\text{Olivine} \ge 40\%$)

  • Dunite: $\text{Olivine} > 90\%$ (essentially pure olivine rock).
  • Harzburgite: $\text{Olivine} = 40\text{--}90\%$, $\mathbf{Opx > 10\%}$, and $\mathbf{Cpx < 10\%}$ ($\mathbf{\text{Olivine} + \text{Orthopyroxene}}$).
  • Wehrlite: $\text{Olivine} = 40\text{--}90\%$, $\mathbf{Cpx > 10\%}$, and $\mathbf{Opx < 10\%}$ ($\mathbf{\text{Olivine} + \text{Clinopyroxene}}$).
  • Lherzolite: $\text{Olivine} = 40\text{--}90\%$, with both $Opx > 10\%$ and $Cpx > 10\%$ ($\text{Olivine} + \text{Opx} + \text{Cpx}$). Represents fertile, un-depleted mantle.

The Pyroxenite Division ($\text{Olivine} < 40\%$)

  • Orthopyroxenite: $Opx > 90\%$ of total pyroxenes.
  • Clinopyroxenite: $Cpx > 90\%$ of total pyroxenes.
  • Websterite: Two-pyroxene rock with both $Opx > 10\%$ and $Cpx > 10\%$.
3. Geodynamic Significance: Depleted Mantle Restite & Ophiolite Tectonites

Harzburgite is of immense geodynamic significance as the primary constituent of the depleted sub-oceanic and sub-continental lithospheric mantle:

  • Mantle Melting Residue (Restite): When fertile mantle lherzolite undergoes partial melting beneath mid-ocean ridges or island arcs, the low-melting mineral clinopyroxene (diopside) melts out first into the basaltic liquid. The solid refractory residue left behind after $15\text{--}25\%$ basalt extraction is depleted harzburgite: $$\text{Fertile Lherzolite } (Ol + Opx + Cpx + Sp) \xrightarrow{\text{Partial Melting}} \text{Basaltic Melt} + \mathbf{\text{Depleted Harzburgite Restite } (Ol + Opx + Chromite)}$$
  • Ophiolite Basal Tectonites: In complete ophiolite sequences (e.g., Semail Ophiolite in Oman; Andaman Ophiolite; Sukinda in Odisha), the lower several kilometers beneath the Moho consist of foliated, sheared harzburgite tectonite, recording high-temperature mantle plastic flow.
  • Economic Podiform Chromite: Interactions between ascending melts and harzburgite dissolve orthopyroxene to precipitate replacive dunite envelopes hosting world-class podiform chromite ore bodies (e.g., Sukinda valley, Odisha).

Official Examination Question
Question 89
Harzburgite is an ultramafic plutonic rock composed essentially of :
(A) Olivine + orthopyroxene
(B) Olivine + clinopyroxene
(C) Olivine + plagioclase
(D) Clinopyroxene + plagioclase
CORRECT ANSWER: (A)
Olivine + orthopyroxene: Harzburgite is canonically defined as a peridotite composed essentially of olivine (40–90%) and orthopyroxene (10–60%), with less than 10% clinopyroxene.
Forensic Analysis of Answer Choices
(A) Olivine + orthopyroxene — CORRECT

Harzburgite is strictly defined in the IUGS classification as an ultramafic peridotite composed essentially of olivine (40–90%) and orthopyroxene (enstatite/bronzite) with $<10\%$ clinopyroxene (Winter, 2014, p. 29; Le Maitre, 2002; Bangar, p. 165). It forms the dominant refractory mantle rock of the oceanic and cratonic lithosphere.

(B) Olivine + clinopyroxene — INCORRECT

An ultramafic peridotite composed essentially of olivine + clinopyroxene (diopside/augite) with $<10\%$ orthopyroxene is named Wehrlite (named after Alois Wehrle).

(C) Olivine + plagioclase — INCORRECT

A plutonic rock composed of olivine + calcic plagioclase is named Troctolite (or "troutstone"). Because plagioclase is a felsic mineral, troctolite is a basic/mafic gabbroic rock ($M' < 90\%$), NOT an ultramafic rock.

(D) Clinopyroxene + plagioclase — INCORRECT

A plutonic rock composed essentially of clinopyroxene + calcic plagioclase is an ordinary Gabbro (or its volcanic equivalent, Basalt). It is a basic/mafic rock, completely excluded from the ultramafic peridotite group.

Problem 1: Modal Point-Count Plotting on the Ultramafic Ternary

Point-count modal analysis of an ultramafic rock from an ophiolite mantle section yields: $65\%$ Olivine, $28\%$ Orthopyroxene, $4\%$ Clinopyroxene, and $3\%$ Chromite. Normalize the silicate phases to $100\%$ on the $Ol\text{--}Opx\text{--}Cpx$ triangle and determine the precise rock name.

Step-by-Step Ternary Normalization:
1. Sum of Essential Silicates: $$\sum = 65\% + 28\% + 4\% = 97.0\%$$
2. Normalized Percentages: $$Ol' = \frac{65}{97} \times 100\% = \mathbf{67.01\%}$$
$$Opx' = \frac{28}{97} \times 100\% = \mathbf{28.87\%}$$
$$Cpx' = \frac{4}{97} \times 100\% = \mathbf{4.12\%}$$
3. Classification Criteria:
- Normalized Olivine ($67.0\%$) falls between $40\%$ and $90\%$ $\implies$ Peridotite.
- Normalized Orthopyroxene ($28.9\%$) exceeds $10\%$.
- Normalized Clinopyroxene ($4.1\%$) is strictly $< 10\%$.
Conclusion: The rock plots unambiguously in the Harzburgite field.
Problem 2: Mantle Partial Melting Stoichiometry

Write a representative mass-balance melting equation demonstrating how partial melting of a primitive mantle lherzolite produces a basaltic melt and leaves behind a refractory harzburgitic residue.

Petrogenetic Mass Balance:
At $P = 1.5\text{ GPa}$ (depth $\sim 50\text{ km}$), decompression melting of spinel lherzolite follows the approximate reaction (in wt% coefficients): $$\mathbf{1.0\ \text{Lherzolite }} (0.55\ Ol + 0.25\ Opx + 0.15\ Cpx + 0.05\ Sp) \longrightarrow \mathbf{0.20\ \text{Basaltic Liquid}} + \mathbf{0.80\ \text{Harzburgite Residue }} (0.62\ Ol + 0.35\ Opx + 0.03\ Cr\text{-}Sp)$$
Because clinopyroxene contributes disproportionately to the melt ($\sim 70\%$ of the liquid components), $Cpx$ is completely consumed at $F \approx 20\%$, abruptly transforming the residual mantle from lherzolite to harzburgite.
Problem 3: Troctolite vs. Harzburgite Petrologic Demarcation

Explain why Troctolite (Option C) is classified under the Gabbroic clan while Harzburgite (Option A) is classified under the Peridotite clan, citing the Color Index boundary ($M' = 90\%$).

Color Index Demarcation:
- Harzburgite: Composed of $>90\%$ mafic minerals ($Ol + Opx$, $M' \ge 90\%$). It contains no plagioclase. Therefore, it belongs strictly to the Ultramafic Clan (Peridotite group).
- Troctolite: Composed of olivine plus calcic plagioclase (typically $35\text{--}65\%$ plagioclase). Because plagioclase is a felsic mineral, the Color Index of troctolite is $M' = 35\text{--}65\%$, which is far below the $90\%$ ultramafic threshold. Therefore, troctolite is an olivine gabbroid (basic/mafic rock), not an ultramafic rock.
Problem 4: Sukinda Ultramafic Belt, Odisha

Describe the geological significance of the Sukinda ultramafic complex in Odisha. What is the spatial relationship between chromitite seams, dunite, and harzburgite?

Odisha Regional Geology:
- Economic Significance: The Sukinda Valley in Jajpur district, Odisha, contains over $95\%$ of India's total chromite reserves.
- Stratigraphic Architecture: Sukinda represents a Precambrian dismembered layered intrusion/ophiolitic complex. The stratigraphy consists of basal serpentinized harzburgite and dunite, passing upward into pyroxenite and gabbro.
- Chromite Hosting: Thick stratiform/podiform seams of metallurgical-grade chromitite are enclosed within lenses of pure serpentinized dunite that form envelopes within the larger harzburgite mantle country rock.
Problem 5: Bastitization of Orthopyroxene during Low-Temperature Hydration

When harzburgite undergoes hydrothermal serpentinization at $T < 300^\circ\text{C}$, olivine and orthopyroxene transform into distinct serpentine pseudomorphs. Name the pseudomorph after orthopyroxene and state its optical appearance.

Serpentinization Microstructure:
- Olivine: Hydrates to lizardite/chrysotile forming a classic mesh texture with magnetite strings along grain boundaries.
- Orthopyroxene: Hydrates to a fibrous, silvery-bronze pseudomorph called bastite (composed of oriented lizardite or antigorite fibers). Bastite preserves the exact rectangular prismatic shape and $\{110\}$ cleavage planes of the original enstatite, extinguishing uniformly under cross-polarized light.
Problem 6: Spinel Harzburgite vs. Garnet Harzburgite Depth Zonation

State the pressure-depth transition separating spinel harzburgite from garnet harzburgite in the upper mantle, and write the mineralogical reaction.

Mantle Facies Transition:
- At depths shallower than $\sim 70\text{--}80\text{ km}$ ($P < 2.5\text{--}2.8\text{ GPa}$), aluminium in the mantle is accommodated in chromian spinel ($\text{Spinel Harzburgite Facies}$).
- At greater depths ($P > 2.8\text{ GPa}$, depths $> 80\text{--}90\text{ km}$), spinel reacts with orthopyroxene to form pyrope garnet: $$\text{MgAl}_2\text{O}_4\ (\text{Spinel}) + 4\text{MgSiO}_3\ (\text{Enstatite}) \rightleftharpoons \text{Mg}_3\text{Al}_2\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}\ (\text{Pyrope Garnet}) + \text{Mg}_2\text{SiO}_4\ (\text{Forsterite})$$
- Garnet harzburgites form the deep sub-continental lithospheric keels of ancient Archean cratons (e.g., Dharwar, Kaapvaal, Siberian cratons), serving as the primary source rock for diamond-bearing kimberlitic xenoliths.
Problem 7: Optical Distinction: Enstatite vs. Diopside in Peridotites

How does a petrologist optically differentiate the orthopyroxene (enstatite) of harzburgite from the clinopyroxene (diopside) of wehrlite under cross-polarized light?

Optical Mineralogy Comparison:
- Orthopyroxene (Enstatite in Harzburgite): Orthorhombic. Displays straight (parallel) extinction ($0^\circ$) relative to prismatic cleavage lines in all longitudinal sections; low birefringence ($\delta = 0.008\text{--}0.010$, showing first-order grey to yellow interference colors); pale pink to green pleochroism in hypersthene.
- Clinopyroxene (Diopside in Wehrlite): Monoclinic. Displays inclined (oblique) extinction ($Z \wedge c = 38\text{--}44^\circ$); moderate-to-high birefringence ($\delta = 0.024\text{--}0.030$, showing vibrant second-order blue, red, and green interference colors); non-pleochroic or pale green.
Problem 8: Suprasubduction Zone (SSZ) vs. Mid-Ocean Ridge (MOR) Ophiolites

Why do Suprasubduction Zone (SSZ) ophiolites contain highly depleted harzburgite with ultra-refractory Cr-rich chromite ($Cr\# > 0.60$), whereas abyssal mid-ocean ridge peridotites are dominated by fertile lherzolite with Al-rich chromite ($Cr\# < 0.40$)?

Geodynamic Fluid-Fluxed Melting:
- Mid-Ocean Ridges (Dry Melting): Adiabatic decompression melting is limited to moderate melt fractions ($F \approx 10\text{--}18\%$), leaving a relatively fertile, clinopyroxene-bearing residual mantle (lherzolite to transitional harzburgite).
- Suprasubduction Zones (Forearcs / Back-arcs): The mantle wedge undergoes a second stage of melting driven by the influx of water and hydrous fluids from the subducting slab. High water activity depresses the solidus, driving extreme melt fractions ($F > 25\text{--}30\%$). This completely melts out all clinopyroxene and strips aluminium, producing an ultra-depleted harzburgite characterized by magnesium-rich olivine ($\text{Fo}_{92\text{--}94}$) and high-chromium chromite ($\text{Cr}\# = \text{Cr}/(\text{Cr}+\text{Al}) > 0.60$).
