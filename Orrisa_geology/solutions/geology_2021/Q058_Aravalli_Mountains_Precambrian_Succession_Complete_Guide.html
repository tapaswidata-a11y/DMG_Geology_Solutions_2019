1. The Precambrian Framework of Rajasthan: Basement vs. Supracrustal Belts

The Aravalli Mountain Belt in Rajasthan and northern Gujarat represents one of the oldest and most spectacular orogenic belts on Earth, extending over $700\text{ km}$ from Delhi south-southwest to Champaner. Its geological history records multiple Precambrian cycles of continental rifting, shelf sedimentation, island arc accretion, and continent-continent collision:

  • 1. Archean Basement (Oldest):
    • Banded Gneissic Complex (BGC) / Mewar Gneiss (Archean, $>2.5\text{ Ga}$ to $3.3\text{ Ga}$): Composed of tonalite-trondhjemite-granodiorite (TTG) gneisses, amphibolites, pelitic schists, and intrusive multi-phase granitoids (e.g., Berach Granite $\sim 2.5\text{ Ga}$). This ancient basement forms unit 1(d).
  • 2. Aravalli Supergroup (Paleoproterozoic, $\sim 2.5\text{ Ga}$ to $1.8\text{ Ga}$): Separated from the underlying BGC by a profound angular unconformity marked by basal polymictic conglomerates. The supergroup developed through continental rifting into a passive margin shelf-slope basin, classically subdivided in ascending chronological order from older to younger into:
    1. (a) Delwara Group (Basal): Represents the initial syn-rift stage; amygdaloidal tholeiitic metabasalts (Delwara volcanics), quartzites, and conglomerates.
    2. (b) Debari Group (Middle): Platform carbonate and arenite shelf deposits; arkosic quartzites, dolomites, and carbonaceous phyllites. Hosts the world-renowned Jhamarkotra stromatolitic phosphorite deposit.
    3. (c) Jharol Group (Upper): Deep-marine turbidites, phyllites, mica schists, and thin-bedded quartzites with serpentinized ultramafic bodies (ophiolitic suture fragments).

Official Examination Question
Question 58
General geological formation of the Aravalli Mountains : [Group/Supergroup] Formation. Match the correct sequence from older to younger formations. The sequence is :
(A) 1(d), 2(a, b, c)
(B) 1(d), 2(c, b, a)
(C) 1(a), 2(b, c, d)
(D) None of these
Verified Correct Answer: (A) 1(d), 2(a, b, c) [Matching Archean Basement (1) with BGC (d), and Paleoproterozoic Aravalli Supergroup (2) in ascending chronological order: Delwara (a) $\to$ Debari (b) $\to$ Jharol (c).]
Correct Option: (A) 1(d), 2(a, b, c)

Detailed Stratigraphic Forensic Analysis:

  • In standard regional stratigraphy of Rajasthan (Heron 1953; Roy 1988; Mahapatra p. 164 & 312; Mukherjee p. 200), the Precambrian formations are ordered from older to younger:
    1. Unit 1 (Archean Basement): Matched with (d) Banded Gneissic Complex (BGC), representing the $\sim 3.3-2.5\text{ Ga}$ granite-gneiss floor.
    2. Unit 2 (Aravalli Supergroup): Subdivided in strict ascending chronological order (older to younger) into:
      • (a) Delwara Group: Basal rift volcanics and quartzites.
      • (b) Debari Group: Middle platform carbonates, quartzites, and Jhamarkotra phosphorites.
      • (c) Jharol Group: Upper deep-water turbidites and schists.
  • Therefore, the correct chronological sequence is represented by the formula 1(d), 2(a, b, c).
Incorrect Distractor: (B) 1(d), 2(c, b, a)

Detailed Refutation:

  • This option lists the Aravalli Supergroup subdivisions in reverse chronological order (from youngest Jharol down to oldest Delwara).
  • Because the examination prompt explicitly demands the sequence from older to younger formations, this inverted sequence is incorrect.
Incorrect Distractor: (C) 1(a), 2(b, c, d)

Detailed Refutation:

  • This incorrectly pairs Unit 1 (Basement) with (a) Delwara Group, which is an overlying supracrustal unit, and misplaces (d) BGC as the youngest unit of the Aravalli.
  • This violates fundamental superposition and basement-cover relationships.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) None of these

Detailed Refutation:

  • Because Option (A) is the exact, canonical sequence matching the established literature, Option (D) is false.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Precambrian Geodynamics & Ore Geology
Problem 1: Phosphorite Biomineralization at Jhamarkotra

What biogenic sedimentary structure is responsible for the concentration of high-grade phosphorite ($\sim 30-35\% \text{ P}_2\text{O}_5$) in the Debari Group at Jhamarkotra?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Jhamarkotra deposit is hosted within columnar stromatolites (specifically Collenia columnaris, Baicalia). Ancient cyanobacterial mats trapped and precipitated fine colloidal carbonate-fluorapatite (francolite) from nutrient-rich coastal upwelling waters, alternating with pure dolomite laminae to form thick, economic stratabound phosphorite reefs.

Problem 2: Geochronology of the Berach Granite

The Berach Granite intrudes the BGC but is unconformably overlain by the basal Aravalli conglomerate. If its U-Pb zircon age is $2550 \pm 10\text{ Ma}$, what does this establish about the Aravalli Supergroup?

Step-by-Step Solution:

By the Principle of Inclusions and Unconformities, the Aravalli Supergroup must be strictly younger than $2550\text{ Ma}$. This places the initiation of Aravalli rifting in the earliest Paleoproterozoic, establishing the Berach Granite as part of the Archean basement stabilization.

Problem 3: Zawar Lead-Zinc Metallogenesis

What is the ore mineralogy and genetic model of the world-class lead-zinc deposits at Zawar?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Hosted in dolomites of the Mochia, Balaria, and Baroi formations (Aravalli Supergroup), the ore mineralogy consists of sphalerite ($\text{ZnS}$) and galena ($\text{PbS}$) with associated native silver and pyrite. It represents a Sedimentary Exhalative (SEDEX) to Mississippi Valley-Type (MVT) stratabound hydrothermal deposit.

Problem 4: Phulad Ophiolite Suite Significance

What tectonic feature is represented by the Phulad Ophiolite along the western margin of the Delhi fold belt?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Phulad Ophiolite Suite (comprising pillowed metabasalts, sheeted dikes, gabbros, and serpentinized peridotites) represents obducted remnants of Mesoproterozoic oceanic crust, marking the paleo-suture zone of continental collision between the Marwar Craton and the Bundelkhand/BGC Craton.

Problem 5: Great Boundary Fault (GBF) Displacement

What is the nature of displacement along the Great Boundary Fault of Rajasthan, and which blocks are juxtaposed?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The GBF is a major longitudinal reverse / thrust fault dipping northwestward, along which the deformed Precambrian rocks of the Aravalli-Bhilwara belt are thrust southeastward over the undeformed Upper Vindhyan strata (Bhander and Kaimur groups).

Problem 6: Metamorphic Inversion across the Aravalli Belt

Describe the regional metamorphic grade variation across the Aravalli Mountain Range from east to west.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Metamorphic grade increases systematically from east to west: from low-grade sub-greenschist/greenschist facies (slates and phyllites) in the eastern Hindoli and Delwara belts to amphibolite and granulite facies (garnet-sillimanite schists, calc-granulites, and charnockites) in the western Delhi and BGC sectors.

Problem 7: Debari Basal Conglomerate Clast Composition

What clast lithologies dominate the polymictic Debari Conglomerate at the base of the Aravalli Supergroup?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Debari Conglomerate contains rounded to subangular clasts of quartzite, granite, vein quartz, and BGC tourmaline-bearing gneisses, proving that the underlying crystalline Archean crust was uplifted and subaerially eroded prior to Aravalli basin subsidence.

Problem 8: Tectonic Origin of the Aravalli-Delhi Belt

Explain the Wilson Cycle model for the evolution of the Aravalli and Delhi orogenic belts.

Step-by-Step Solution:

The region underwent two successive Wilson Cycles: (1) Aravalli Cycle ($\sim 2.5-1.8\text{ Ga}$): Continental rifting of Archean BGC, ocean basin opening, passive margin deposition, and subsequent compressional closure and collision; followed by (2) Delhi Cycle ($\sim 1.8-1.0\text{ Ga}$): Intracratonic rifting, development of the South Delhi oceanic tract, subduction-related arc magmatism, and terminal collision during the assembly of the Rodinia supercontinent.
