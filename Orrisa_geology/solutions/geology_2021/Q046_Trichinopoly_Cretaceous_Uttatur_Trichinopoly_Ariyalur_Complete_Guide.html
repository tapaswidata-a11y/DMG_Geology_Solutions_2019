Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

46.1 Cretaceous Marine Transgression in the Cauvery Basin

In Indian stratigraphy and paleontology (M.S. Krishnan, Geology of India and Burma, Ch. 15; Ramakrishnan & Vaidyanadhan, Geology of India, Vol. 2, Ch. 24; Blanford, 1862), the Cretaceous succession of the Tiruchirappalli (Trichinopoly) district in Tamil Nadu constitutes the most complete, world-renowned marine Cretaceous reference section in peninsular India. Deposited during global Cenomanian-Campanian marine transgressions across the newly rifted eastern margin of Gondwana, it rests unconformably upon Archean charnockites and gneisses:

46.2 Historical Classification by H.F. Blanford (1862)

H.F. Blanford of the Geological Survey of India established the canonical tripartite division of the Trichinopoly Cretaceous basin into:

  1. Uttatur Formation (lower Cenomanian)
  2. Trichinopoly Formation (middle Turonian)
  3. Ariyalur Formation (upper Senonian / Campanian-Maastrichtian)

Later workers (Rama Rao, 1956) recognized the topmost Danian (Paleocene) beds as the Niniyur Formation, but the classical Cretaceous sequence consists strictly of Uttatur, Trichinapoli, and Ariyalur.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q46. The Upper Cretaceous rocks of the Tiruchchirappalli area has been classified into three Formations viz., Uttatur, Trichinapoli and _____.
(A) Ariyalur Formation
(B) Haimanta Formation
(C) Lilang Formation
(D) Chikkim Formation
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (A)

Correct Answer: (A) Ariyalur Formation — The Upper Cretaceous rocks of the Tiruchirappalli area are classically classified into three major Formations: Uttatur, Trichinapoli, and Ariyalur Formation.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Ariyalur Formation — CORRECT: Forms the uppermost division of the tripartite Cretaceous of Trichinopoly, famous for its rich Campanian-Maastrichtian invertebrate fauna.
  • (B) Haimanta Formation — INCORRECT: The Haimanta Group is an early Paleozoic (Cambrian) unit of the Tethyan Himalayan succession in the Spiti Valley, over $2500\text{ km}$ away in northern India.
  • (C) Lilang Formation — INCORRECT: The Lilang Group is the monumental Triassic marine limestone succession of the Spiti Tethyan belt.
  • (D) Chikkim Formation — INCORRECT: The Chikkim Formation is the Cretaceous limestone-shale formation of the Spiti basin in the Himalayas, not Tamil Nadu.
