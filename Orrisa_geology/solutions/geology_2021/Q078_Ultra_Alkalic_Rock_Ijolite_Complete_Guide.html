1. Definition of Ultra-Alkalic Rocks & the IUGS Foidolite Suite

In igneous petrology, alkaline rocks are defined as magmas whose molecular alkali-to-alumina ratio $\frac{\text{Na}_2\text{O} + \text{K}_2\text{O}}{\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3}$ or alkali-to-silica ratio is unusually high, such that minerals deficient in silica—most notably feldspathoids (foids: nepheline, leucite, kalsilite, sodalite)—appear in the norm or mode.

The designation ultra-alkalic (or hyper-alkaline) is reserved for extraordinary plutonic rocks where silica deficiency is so profound that true feldspars (plagioclase and alkali feldspar) are completely excluded from the mineral assemblage. In the standard IUGS QAPF classification (Le Maitre, 2002; Winter, 2014, p. 28), these rocks plot in Field 15 (Foidolites), where feldspathoids comprise greater than $60\%$ (and frequently $100\%$) of the light felsic minerals.

Figure 78.1 — IUGS QAPF Classification & Field 15 (Foidolites): Authentic textbook diagram from J.D. Winter (Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, Ch. 2). Highlighting the ultra-alkalic, feldspar-free Foidolite field containing the Urtite-Ijolite-Melteigite series.
2. Petrography of the Urtite-Ijolite-Melteigite Series

The classic plutonic suite of feldspar-free, nepheline-clinopyroxene rocks is known as the Ijolite Series (named after the type locality at Iivaara / Ijola, Kuusamo, Finland). The rocks are classified strictly on the basis of their Color Index ($M'$), representing the modal percentage of dark mafic minerals:

The Three Members of the Ijolite Series

  • Urtite ($M' = 0\text{--}30\%$): Leucocratic end-member, consisting predominantly of nepheline ($>70\text{--}90\%$) with minor green aegirine-augite. (Named from Lujaur-Urt, Kola Peninsula).
  • Ijolite ($M' = 30\text{--}70\%$): The quintessential mesocratic ultra-alkalic rock, consisting of approximately equal proportions of nepheline ($30\text{--}70\%$) and sodic clinopyroxene (aegirine or aegirine-augite, $30\text{--}70\%$). Feldspars are completely absent! Common accessory minerals include titanium-rich black andradite garnet (melanite), fluorapatite, titanite (sphene), perovskite, and primary magmatic calcite.
  • Melteigite ($M' = 70\text{--}90\%$): Melanocratic end-member, dominated by aegirine-augite ($>70\%$) with subordinate interstitial nepheline ($10\text{--}30\%$). (Named from Melteig, Fen complex, Norway).
Mineralogical Archetype of Ijolite:
$$\mathbf{\text{Ijolite}} = \mathbf{\text{Nepheline (30--70\%)}} + \mathbf{\text{Aegirine-Augite (30--70\%)}} + \text{Melanite Garnet} + \text{Apatite} \quad [\mathbf{\text{Feldspar = 0\%}}]$$
3. Geodynamic Setting, Carbonatite Association & Fenitization

Ijolites never occur as isolated regional batholiths; instead, they are diagnostic components of alkaline-carbonatite ring complexes emplaced within intra-continental rift valleys (e.g., East African Rift, Kola Alkaline Province in Russia, Fen Complex in Norway, and the Amba Dongar complex in Gujarat, India).

Petrogenesis and Liquid Immiscibility

  • Primary Carbonated Mantle Melting: Primary carbonated nephelinite magmas are generated by extremely low degrees of partial melting ($F < 2\text{--}5\%$) of deep, volatile-metasomatized sub-continental lithospheric mantle (SCLM) at depths $> 100\text{ km}$ ($P > 3.0\text{ GPa}$) in the presence of high $CO_2$ and $H_2O$.
  • Silicate-Carbonatite Unmixing: As the primary nephelinitic melt ascends and decompresses into crustal chambers, it intersects a two-liquid miscibility gap, unmixing into an ultra-alkaline silicate melt (crystallizing as ijolite) and a conjugate carbonate-rich liquid (crystallizing as carbonatite: sovite/alvikite).
  • Fenitization (Alkali Metasomatism): The extreme alkali enrichment of ijolitic and carbonatitic intrusions generates intense hydrothermal exsolution of sodium-potassium-rich fluids. These reactive fluids invade surrounding granitic and gneissic country rocks, leaching silica and quartz while metasomatically replacing host minerals with aegirine, sodic amphibole (riebeckite/arfvedsonite), and alkali feldspar—a process termed fenitization.

Official Examination Question
Question 78
Which of the following is an ultra-alkalic rock ?
(A) Adamellite
(B) Rhyodacite
(C) Trondhjemite
(D) Ijolite
CORRECT ANSWER: (D)
Ijolite: An ultra-alkalic, feldspar-free plutonic rock composed essentially of nepheline (30–70%) and sodic pyroxene (aegirine-augite), plotting in the Foidolite field of the IUGS QAPF classification.
Forensic Analysis of Answer Choices
(A) Adamellite — INCORRECT

Adamellite is the classic European petrographic synonym for Quartz Monzonite (IUGS Field 8). It is a subalkaline, quartz-bearing granitoid consisting of roughly equal proportions of alkali feldspar and plagioclase with 5–20% quartz. It is completely subalkaline to calc-alkaline and contains abundant silica, making it the diametric opposite of an ultra-alkalic rock.

(B) Rhyodacite — INCORRECT

Rhyodacite is a subalkaline, volcanic intermediate-to-felsic rock compositional equivalent to granodiorite. It is characterized by phenocrysts of plagioclase, quartz, and biotite/hornblende in a fine-grained, silica-oversaturated groundmass ($SiO_2 \approx 66\text{--}72\%$). It belongs to typical calc-alkaline subduction arc suites.

(C) Trondhjemite — INCORRECT

Trondhjemite is a leucocratic variety of tonalite belonging to the famous Archean TTG suite (Tonalite-Trondhjemite-Granodiorite). It consists almost entirely of sodic plagioclase (oligoclase) and quartz, with $<10\%$ K-feldspar and minor biotite. It is strongly subalkaline, metaluminous to peraluminous, and completely devoid of feldspathoids.

(D) Ijolite — CORRECT

Ijolite is the classic, textbook archetype of an ultra-alkalic plutonic rock (Winter, 2014, p. 28, 412). It is a foidolite composed exclusively of nepheline and aegirine-augite with total exclusion of feldspars. Its agpaitic index, extreme silica-undersaturation, and association with carbonatites firmly establish it as ultra-alkalic.

Exam-Standard Analytical & Quantitative Practice Problems
Problem 1: Agpaitic Index ($AI$) and Peralkalinity Quantitative Calculation

A geochemical analysis of an ultra-alkalic plutonic rock yields the following molar concentrations: $\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 0.142\text{ mol}$, $\text{K}_2\text{O} = 0.038\text{ mol}$, and $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 0.125\text{ mol}$. Calculate the Agpaitic Index ($AI$) and classify the rock as peralkaline, metaluminous, or peraluminous.

Step-by-Step Calculation:
The Agpaitic Index ($AI$, or Peralkalinity Index) is defined as the molar ratio of total alkalis to alumina: $$AI = \frac{\text{Molar }(\text{Na}_2\text{O} + \text{K}_2\text{O})}{\text{Molar }\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3}$$
Substituting the molar values: $$AI = \frac{0.142 + 0.038}{0.125} = \frac{0.180}{0.125} = \mathbf{1.44}$$
Classification:
- Since $\mathbf{AI = 1.44 > 1.0}$, the rock is strictly Peralkaline (Agpaitic).
- Surplus sodium beyond that needed to form feldspars and feldspathoids ($(\text{Na}+\text{K}) > \text{Al}$) forces the crystallization of sodic mafic minerals, namely aegirine ($\text{NaFe}^{3+}\text{Si}_2\text{O}_6$) and arfvedsonite, characteristic of ijolites.
Problem 2: Color Index ($M'$) Classification in the Ijolite Series

Point-counting modal analysis of three foidolitic plutonic thin sections from a carbonatite complex yielded the following modal percentages:
- Sample A: $82\%$ Nepheline, $14\%$ Aegirine-augite, $4\%$ Melanite garnet.
- Sample B: $48\%$ Nepheline, $42\%$ Aegirine-augite, $6\%$ Melanite, $4\%$ Apatite.
- Sample C: $18\%$ Nepheline, $74\%$ Aegirine-augite, $8\%$ Titanite + Magnetite.
Classify Samples A, B, and C into their specific members of the Ijolite Series.

Color Index ($M'$) Derivation:
$M'$ is the sum of all dark, mafic, and accessory minerals ($100 - \text{Felsic Foids}$):
- Sample A: $M' = 14\% + 4\% = 18\%$. Since $M' < 30\%$, it is classified as Urtite (leucocratic foidolite).
- Sample B: $M' = 42\% + 6\% + 4\% = 52\%$. Since $M'$ is between $30\%$ and $70\%$, it is classified as Ijolite sensu stricto.
- Sample C: $M' = 74\% + 8\% = 82\%$. Since $M' > 70\%$, it is classified as Melteigite (melanocratic foidolite).
Problem 3: Liquid Immiscibility: Silicate-Carbonatite Unmixing

Explain why ijolite and carbonatite rocks are intimately associated in space and time within alkaline complexes, citing experimental phase equilibria in the system $\text{Na}_2\text{O}\text{--}\text{CaO}\text{--}\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\text{--}\text{SiO}_2\text{--}\text{CO}_2$.

Experimental Petrologic Mechanism:
1. Miscibility Gap: Freestone & Hamilton (1980) and Kjarsgaard & Hamilton (1988) demonstrated that at crustal pressures ($0.2\text{--}0.5\text{ GPa}$) and temperatures of $850\text{--}1050^\circ\text{C}$, a primary $CO_2$-saturated nephelinitic melt enters a wide two-liquid immiscibility field.
2. Phase Separation: The homogeneous melt splits into two conjugate, immiscible liquids: - An alkali-aluminosilicate liquid enriched in $SiO_2, Al_2O_3, Na_2O$, which crystallizes as ijolite.
- A conjugate alkaline-carbonatitic liquid enriched in $CaO, CO_2, P_2O_5$, and LREE, which crystallizes as carbonatite (calciocarbonatite/sovite).
3. This explains why ijolites form concentric ring dikes and plugs directly enclosing central carbonatite cores.
Problem 4: Chemical Impossibility of Quartz and Nepheline Coexistence

Demonstrate thermodynamically why quartz and nepheline can never coexist in thermodynamic equilibrium in any primary igneous rock.

Thermodynamic Reaction:
Nepheline is a silica-undersaturated feldspathoid. In the presence of free silica (quartz), it undergoes instantaneous, spontaneous reaction to form albite feldspar: $$\text{NaAlSiO}_4\ (\text{Nepheline}) + \text{SiO}_2\ (\text{Quartz}) \rightleftharpoons \text{NaAlSi}_3\text{O}_8\ (\text{Albite})$$
The standard Gibbs free energy of this reaction is strongly negative ($\Delta G^\circ_{298} \approx -26.5\text{ kJ/mol}$) across all magmatic temperatures and pressures. Therefore, free quartz consumes nepheline to form albite until one of the reactants is completely exhausted. Rocks containing nepheline (like ijolite) have zero modal or normative quartz.
Problem 5: Fenitization Halo Metasomatic Mass Balance

Describe the mineralogical transformations that occur when a granitic country rock containing quartz ($30\%$), microcline ($40\%$), and oligoclase ($30\%$) undergoes intense fenitization by fluids exsolved from an ijolitic intrusion.

Metasomatic Progression:
1. Desilication: High-pH alkaline hydrothermal fluids dissolve quartz entirely: $$\text{SiO}_{2\text{ (solid)}} + 2\text{OH}^- \longrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{SiO}_4^{2-\text{ (aqueous)}}$$
2. Aegirine Formation: Sodium and ferric iron metasomatically replace host mafic minerals and quartz: $$\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{Na}^+ + 2\text{SiO}_2 \longrightarrow \text{NaFe}^{3+}\text{Si}_2\text{O}_6\ (\text{Aegirine})$$
3. Alkali Feldspar Recrystallization: Oligoclase is albitized, and potassium feldspar recrystallizes into perthitic orthoclase/microcline. The resulting rock is a fenite: a quartz-free rock composed of perthite, albite, and radiating needles of aegirine.
Problem 6: Melanite Garnet: The Diagnostic Accessory Phase of Ijolite

In thin section, ijolite typically contains dark brown to opaque dodecahedral garnet crystals. Identify this garnet variety, write its chemical formula, and explain why titanium enters its crystal lattice.

Mineralogical Characterization:
- Mineral Variety: Melanite (or Schorlomite when $TiO_2 > 10\text{ wt}\%$), a titanium-rich variety of the ugrandite garnet Andradite.
- Chemical Formula: $\text{Ca}_3(\text{Fe}^{3+}, \text{Ti})_2(\text{SiO}_4, \text{Fe}^{3+}\text{O}_4)_3$, ideally $\text{Ca}_3\text{Fe}_2^{3+}\text{Si}_3\text{O}_{12}$ with up to $5\text{--}15\text{ wt}\% TiO_2$.
- Titanium Incorporation: In silica-undersaturated, alkaline ijolitic magmas, the low activity of silica ($a_{\text{SiO}_2}$) impedes the formation of sphene/titanite or ilmenite. Instead, $Ti^{4+}$ substitutes into the octahedral $Y$-site and tetrahedral $Z$-site of andradite via coupled substitutions ($\text{Ti}^{4+} + \text{Fe}^{3+} \rightleftharpoons \text{Si}^{4+} + \text{Al}^{3+}$), yielding the deep black/brown melanite garnet.
Problem 7: Amba Dongar Alkaline-Carbonatite Complex, Gujarat

The Amba Dongar complex in the Chhota Udaipur alkaline sub-province of Gujarat represents India's most famous carbonatite-alkaline ring complex. Identify the spatial distribution of ijolites and carbonatites within this ring structure and its relation to Deccan volcanism.

Indian Regional Geology:
- Tectonic & Temporal Context: Emplaced at $\sim 65\text{ Ma}$, Amba Dongar is intimately associated with the terminal rifting stage of the Deccan Traps flood basalt province (Reunion hotspot interaction with the Narmada-Tapi rift zone).
- Ring Architecture: The complex forms a prominent ring dike system cutting Cretaceous Bagh Sandstones. The central volcanic diatreme depression consists of carbonatite breccia and large ring dikes of calciocarbonatite (sovite) and ankeritic carbonatite.
- Ijolite Occurrence: Ijolitic and nephelinitic plutonic bodies occur as peripheral intrusions and ring segments intimately associated with extensive fenitization of the surrounding Bagh Sandstones. It hosts world-class hydrothermal fluorite mineralization.
Problem 8: CIPW Norm Calculation: Differentiating Silica Saturation

Given the following CIPW normative mineral percentages for two igneous rocks, determine which one is an ultra-alkalic rock and explain why:
- Rock X: $Q = 14.5\%, Or = 28.0\%, Ab = 32.5\%, An = 15.0\%, Hy = 6.0\%, Mt = 2.5\%$.
- Rock Y: $Ne = 42.0\%, Di = 44.0\%, Ol = 8.0\%, Ap = 3.5\%, Mt = 2.5\%$.

Normative Analysis:
- Rock X: Contains $14.5\%$ normative Quartz ($Q$) and abundant normative feldspars ($Or + Ab + An = 75.5\%$). It is a silica-oversaturated, calc-alkaline rock (Quartz Monzonite / Adamellite).
- Rock Y: Contains $42.0\%$ normative Nepheline ($Ne$) and zero normative quartz or feldspars ($Or = Ab = An = 0\%$). The entire felsic norm is nepheline, accompanied by dominant normative Diopside ($Di = 44\%$). This normative composition corresponds precisely to an ultra-alkalic foidolite (Ijolite).
