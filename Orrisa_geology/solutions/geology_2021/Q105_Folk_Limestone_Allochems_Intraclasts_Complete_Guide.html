2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Folk's Carbonate Classification

Robert L. Folk (1959, 1962) classified limestones based on the volumetric proportions of three basic components: (1) Allochems (framework grains), (2) Micrite (microcrystalline carbonate mud matrix, $<4\,\mu\text{m}$), and (3) Sparite (sparry calcite pore-filling cement, $>10\,\mu\text{m}$).

Folk recognized exactly four types of allochems:

  1. Intraclasts: Fragments of penecontemporaneous, semi-consolidated carbonate sediment torn up by wave or storm agitation from within the same depositional basin and redeposited (intrabasinal clasts).
  2. Oolites (Ooids): Spherical, concentrically laminated grains formed by chemical or biological accretion of calcium carbonate around a nucleus in agitated shallow warm waters.
  3. Fossils (Bioclasts): Whole or broken skeletal remains of calcareous organisms (shells, foraminifera, crinoids, algae).
  4. Pellets (Peloids): Structureless, ovoid grains ($0.03 - 0.2\text{ mm}$) composed of microcrystalline calcite, commonly of fecal origin.

Micrite and sparry calcite are interstitial binding media (matrix and cement), NOT allochems!

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A (intraclasts) is correct: The fourth cardinal allochem defined by Folk.
  • Option B (micrite): Microcrystalline matrix, strictly distinct from allochems.
  • Option C (sparry calcite): Secondary cement, not an allochem.
  • Option D (All of these): A fatal trap for candidates who confuse carbonate matrix/cement with framework grains.
