Q27: Plunge of Linear Structural Features & Coordinate Geometry

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Marland P. Billings (Structural Geology, 3rd Ed., Ch. 3); Haakon Fossen (Ch. 2, pp. 24-28)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

27.1 Spatial Definition of Linear Structural Elements

In structural geology (Billings, Structural Geology, 3rd Ed., Ch. 3; Haakon Fossen, Structural Geology, Ch. 2; Van der Pluijm & Marshak, Ch. 1), the spatial orientation of any linear geological feature (fold hinge lines, mineral elongation lineations, intersection lineations, slickenlines) is defined in 3D Euclidean space by two fundamental angles:

Coordinate Element Angular Definition Measurement Plane & Reference Angular Bounds
Plunge ($\alpha$) The vertical angle between the horizontal plane and the line of maximum elongation of the feature. Measured strictly downward in the vertical plane passing through the lineation. $0^\circ \le \alpha \le 90^\circ$
Trend ($\beta$) The geographic compass bearing / azimuth of the horizontal projection of the plunging lineation. Measured clockwise from True North in the horizontal plane. $000^\circ \le \beta < 360^\circ$
Pitch / Rake ($\phi$) The angle between the linear feature and the strike line of the inclined plane on which it lies. Measured within the inclined plane containing the feature. $0^\circ \le \phi \le 90^\circ$
Dip Angle ($\delta$) The maximum angle of inclination of a planar surface measured relative to the horizontal. Measured in a vertical plane perpendicular to strike. $0^\circ \le \delta \le 90^\circ$

27.2 Stereographic Projection & Lineation Notation

A plunging lineation is recorded in standard structural notation as Plunge → Trend (e.g., $34^\circ \to 210^\circ$). The fundamental spherical trigonometry equation linking plunge ($\alpha$), pitch ($\phi$), and plane dip ($\delta$) is:

$$\sin(\alpha) = \sin(\delta) \times \sin(\phi)$$

Note on Exam Paper Nuance: In the official OPSC 2021 Question Paper, Question 27 repeats verbatim the question statement of Question 22. In both instances, the vertical angle between the horizontal plane and the axis/line of maximum elongation is strictly plunge.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q27. The vertical angle between the horizontal plane and the axis or line of maximum elongation of a feature is called ____.
(A) dip angle
(B) strike
(C) right-lateral strike slip
(D) plunge
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (D)

Correct Answer: (D) plunge — The vertical angle between the horizontal plane and the axis or line of maximum elongation of a structural feature is defined as plunge.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) dip angle — INCORRECT: Dip angle applies exclusively to planar surfaces (e.g., bedding planes, fault surfaces, foliation), representing the angle of inclination of the plane relative to the horizontal.
  • (B) strike — INCORRECT: Strike is the compass direction of a horizontal line formed by the intersection of an inclined planar feature with the horizontal plane ($000^\circ$–$360^\circ$).
  • (C) right-lateral strike slip — INCORRECT: A kinematic sense of fault motion (dextral) where the opposite block moves to the observer's right, having no angular coordinate meaning.
  • (D) plunge — CORRECT: Precisely defines the vertical inclination of a linear element measured downward from the horizontal plane.

High-Yield Coordinate Conversion & Numerical Problem

Practice Problem 27: Pitch to Plunge Trigonometric Conversion

A mineral stretching lineation lies on a fault plane dipping at $\delta = 60^\circ$. If the pitch (rake) of the lineation measured on the fault plane is $\phi = 30^\circ$, calculate the true plunge ($\alpha$) of the lineation.

Solution:

Using the spherical kinematic relation:

$$\sin(\alpha) = \sin(\delta) \times \sin(\phi)$$ $$\sin(\alpha) = \sin(60^\circ) \times \sin(30^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1.732}{4} = 0.433$$ $$\alpha = \arcsin(0.433) \approx 25.66^\circ$$

The true plunge of the mineral lineation is approximately $25.7^\circ$.
