1. Geometric Foundations: Plunge as the Vertical Inclination of Lineations

In structural geology and finite strain analysis, linear geological features—including the axis of a fold, mineral lineations, stretched pebble axes, and the line of maximum elongation ($X$-axis of the strain ellipsoid)—require exact spatial orientation in three dimensions. The primary quantitative measure of a line's inclination is its Plunge:

  • Plunge ($\beta$): The vertical angle between the horizontal plane and the inclined linear feature, measured strictly downward in a vertical plane ($0^\circ \le \beta \le 90^\circ$).
  • Line of Maximum Elongation: During finite strain, deformed spherical markers (oolites, pebbles, reduction spots) are transformed into triaxial ellipsoids with principal axes $X \ge Y \ge Z$. The long axis ($X$) represents the line of maximum elongation, and its vertical angle of inclination to the horizontal plane is its plunge.

2. Geometric Interrelationships: Plunge, Trend, and Rake

When a lineation lies upon an inclined geological plane (e.g., mineral alignment on foliation, or slickenside striae on a fault surface), structural geologists relate its true vertical plunge to its rake within the plane:

Spherical Trigonometry of Lineations on Planes
$$\sin(\text{Plunge}) = \sin(\text{Dip of Plane}) \cdot \sin(\text{Rake})$$ $$\cos(\text{Rake}) = \cos(\text{Plunge}) \cdot \cos(\alpha - \text{Strike})$$

Where $\alpha$ is the trend of the lineation. If the plane is vertical ($\text{Dip} = 90^\circ$), Plunge equals Rake. If the plane is horizontal ($\text{Dip} = 0^\circ$), Plunge is identically zero.

Figure 27.2 (Haakon Fossen, Structural Geology): Detailed geometric block diagram distinguishing Plunge (angle in vertical plane $V$) from Rake/Pitch (angle on the dipping plane $S$).
3. Refutation of Strike, Dip, and Rake

Re-affirming fundamental structural taxonomy:

  • Strike: Azimuth of a horizontal line within an inclined plane; has zero vertical angle.
  • Dip: Vertical angle of an inclined plane, not an individual linear axis.
  • Rake: Angle measured in an inclined plane, not relative to the horizontal plane.

Official Question Statement — OPSC 2021 Geology Paper-II (Question 27)
Q27. The vertical angle between the horizontal plane and the axis or line of maximum elongation of a... (linear feature) is known as :
(A) strike
(B) dip
(C) rake
(D) plunge  ✔
✔
Correct Answer: (D) plunge
The vertical angle measured in a vertical plane between the horizontal plane and any linear structural feature (such as a fold axis, line of maximum elongation, or lineation) is universally defined as the plunge.
(A) strike Incorrect (Horizontal Azimuth of a Planar Feature)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Strike is the horizontal compass direction of a planar surface (bedding, fault, or joint). It is not a vertical angle and does not apply to lines.
(B) dip Incorrect (Vertical Inclination of a Plane)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Dip is the maximum vertical angle of an inclined geological plane. While both dip and plunge are vertical angles, dip is strictly planar, whereas plunge is the universal term for linear features.
(C) rake Incorrect (Angle Measured Inside an Inclined Plane)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Rake is measured within the dipping surface itself relative to its strike line, not in the true vertical plane.
(D) plunge Correct Verified Answer
Why this option is correct: By standard international definition in structural geology (Bangar, p. 192; Fossen, p. 25), the acute vertical angle between an inclined linear feature and the horizontal reference plane is its plunge.

High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Questions)
  • Practice Q1: If a fault plane dips at $45^\circ$ and slickenlines display a rake of $90^\circ$ (pointing directly down-dip), what is the plunge of the slickenlines?
    ✔ Worked Answer: Plunge is exactly $45^\circ$.
    Calculation:
    $$\sin(\text{Plunge}) = \sin(45^\circ) \cdot \sin(90^\circ) = \sin(45^\circ) \times 1 = \sin(45^\circ) \implies \text{Plunge} = 45^\circ$$
  • Practice Q2: What geological instrument is used in the field to measure both the plunge and trend of a lineation?
    ✔ Correct Answer: A geological compass-clinometer (Brunton compass or Freiberg compass).
    Explanation: Aligning the compass edge along the line and leveling the clinometer bubble measures plunge; setting the compass horizontal along the vertical sighting line measures trend.
  • Practice Q3: What is a mullion structure, and what is its orientation relative to regional fold plunge?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Linear fluted columns formed along competent/incompetent rock interfaces; parallel to fold hinges and regional plunge.
    Explanation: Mullions develop by cuspate buckling along interfaces with high viscosity contrast (e.g., sandstone-shale), running parallel to the fold axis.
  • Practice Q4: In a shear zone, if mineral lineations plunge at $0^\circ$ (horizontal) on a vertical fault plane, what is the kinematic sense of the fault?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Pure strike-slip faulting.
    Explanation: Horizontal slip lines on a vertical plane indicate lateral displacement with zero dip-slip component.
  • Practice Q5: How do pencil structures (pencil cleavage) form, and what does their plunge indicate?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Formed by the intersection of flat bedding ($S_0$) with weakly developed tectonic cleavage ($S_1$) in shales; plunge indicates the fold axis plunge.
    Explanation: Weathering causes the rock to splinter into elongated pencil-like polygonal rods parallel to the intersection lineation.
  • Practice Q6: What is the difference between a stretching lineation and an intersection lineation?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Stretching lineations are physical markers of maximum finite strain ($X$ direction); intersection lineations are geometric intersections of two planar fabrics.
    Explanation: Stretching lineations record ductile flow; intersection lineations are non-kinematic intersection traces.
  • Practice Q7: On an equal-area stereonet, what geometric feature represents the locus of all possible linear features that can lie within a single plane?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The great circle arc representing that plane.
    Explanation: Any line lying on a plane must plot as a point along the plane's great circle trace.
  • Practice Q8: If an anticline plunges at $80^\circ$, what term is used to describe such an extreme orientation?
    ✔ Correct Answer: A steeply plunging fold (or vertically plunging fold if plunge $> 80^\circ$).
    Explanation: Plunge classification: Horizontal ($0^\circ - 10^\circ$), Gently plunging ($10^\circ - 30^\circ$), Moderately plunging ($30^\circ - 60^\circ$), Steeply plunging ($60^\circ - 80^\circ$), and Vertical ($80^\circ - 90^\circ$).
