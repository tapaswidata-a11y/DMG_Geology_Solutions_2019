Q49: Tethyan Himalayan Paleozoic: Muth Quartzite & Arthropod Trace Fossils

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: O.N. Bhargava (The Indian Subcontinent and Tethys, 2011); Adolf Seilacher (Cruziana Stratigraphy, 1970)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

49.1 Stratigraphic Setting of the Muth Quartzite in Spiti

In Himalayan Paleozoic stratigraphy (O.N. Bhargava, 2011, The Indian Subcontinent and Tethys; Gansser, 1964; Srikantia, 1981; Ramakrishnan & Vaidyanadhan, Vol. 2, Ch. 27), the Muth Quartzite is one of the most prominent, snow-white regional marker horizons running continuously along the Tethyan Himalayan belt from Kashmir, Zanskar, and Spiti through Kumaon into Nepal:

Lithostratigraphic Formation Chronostratigraphic Age Diagnostic Lithological Facies Paleoenvironmental & Paleontological Characteristics
Lipak Formation (Overlying) Early Carboniferous (Tournaisian-Visean) Fossiliferous marine limestones, gypsum, and dark shales. Syringothyris fauna, productive crinoidal biostromes.
Muth Quartzite (Marker Horizon) Middle to Late Devonian Super-mature, massive, pure white orthoquartzite (> 95-98% quartz) with silica cement and cross-bedding. High-energy coastal barrier, shoreface, and beach sands. Body fossils are exceedingly rare due to wave winnowing and quartz recrystallization. Dominated by Arthropod Trace Fossils (Ichnofossils).
Takche Formation (Underlying) Late Ordovician to Silurian Coralline limestones, siltstones, and dolomites. Halysites, Favosites coral-stromatoporoid bioherms.

49.2 Ichnology of the Muth Quartzite

Because body fossils are virtually absent, the environmental and biological dynamics of the Devonian Muth Quartzite are deciphered exclusively from trace fossils (ichnofossils) belonging to the shallow-marine Cruziana and Skolithos ichnofacies:

  • Cruziana: Bilobed furrowing and feeding traces produced by primitive benthic arthropods (trilobites).
  • Rusophycus: Bilobed resting and nesting traces of arthropods.
  • Diplichnites: Parallel trackways of walking arthropod appendages.

Hence, the trace fossils famously described and identified from the Lower/Middle Devonian Muth Quartzite in Spiti are unequivocally arthropod trace fossils.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q49. Which trace fossils are described from the Lower Devonian Muth Quartzite in Spiti, India ?
(A) Nautilus
(B) Unio
(C) Pelecypod
(D) Arthropod
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (D)

Correct Answer: (D) Arthropod — The trace fossils described from the Lower-Middle Devonian Muth Quartzite in Spiti represent the Cruziana and Rusophycus ichnofacies, which are trackways and burrows created by Arthropods (trilobites and benthic arthropod ancestors).

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Nautilus — INCORRECT: Nautilus is a pelagic shelled cephalopod mollusc whose fossil forms leave body fossils (shells), not typical sandstone trace fossil trackways in the Muth Quartzite.
  • (B) Unio — INCORRECT: Unio is a modern freshwater bivalve mollusc (unionid) found in Cenozoic fresh waters; it did not exist in the Devonian marine Tethys.
  • (C) Pelecypod — INCORRECT: While bivalves (pelecypods) produce Lockeia bivalve resting traces, the predominant, diagnostic trace fossil assemblage described from the Muth Quartzite is the arthropod suite.
  • (D) Arthropod — CORRECT: The diagnostic bilobed tracks and walking traces (*Cruziana*, *Rusophycus*, *Diplichnites*) recorded from the Muth Quartzite are universally attributed to marine arthropods.
