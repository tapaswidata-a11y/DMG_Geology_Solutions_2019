1. The Talchir Formation: Basal Hallmark of the Gondwana Supergroup

The Gondwana Supergroup of Peninsular India represents a massive continental sedimentary sequence ranging in age from the Late Carboniferous (Asselian) to Early Cretaceous. In all Indian Gondwana basins—and specifically in its type locality, the Talcher Coalfield of the Mahanadi-Brahmani Valley in Odisha—the base of the Gondwana succession is invariably marked by the Talchir Formation:

  • Discovery in Odisha (Blanford Brothers, 1856): The formation takes its name from the former princely state of Talcher (now Angul district, Odisha), where W.T. Blanford, H.F. Blanford, and William Theobald first recognized that the basal boulder-bearing beds were deposited under ancient glacial conditions.
  • The Basal Talchir Boulder Bed (Glacial Tillite / Diamictite): The lowermost member is an unsorted, unstratified, polymictic diamictite (tillite) resting with profound unconformity upon striated and polished Precambrian basement gneisses:
    • Clast Characteristics: Contains angular to subrounded pebbles, cobbles, and giant boulders (up to several meters across) embedded in a matrix of fine-grained greenish-grey rock flour (silt-clay matrix).
    • Glacial Signatures: Many clasts exhibit characteristic faceted surfaces, flat soles, chatter marks, and glacial striations. Dropstones punctuate thinly laminated glacio-lacustrine shales.
  • Periglacial & Glacio-Lacustrine Facies: Overlying the basal tillite are periglacial rhythmically laminated greenish silts known as needle shales (which weather into delicate pencil-like fragments), turbidite sandstones, and varved glacio-lacustrine clays.

2. The Permo-Carboniferous Gondwana Ice Age

The Talchir deposits document the climax and retreat of the great Late Paleozoic Ice Age across the supercontinent Gondwanaland:

Stratigraphic Triad of the Basal Gondwana Succession
$$\text{Striated Precambrian Basement} \xrightarrow{\text{Glacial Abrasion}} \mathbf{Tillite \text{ (Boulder Bed)}} \xrightarrow{\text{Meltwater}} \mathbf{Needle \text{ Shales}} \xrightarrow{\text{Post-Glacial Warmth}} \mathbf{Coal \text{ Measures (Barakar)}}$$
  • Paleogeographic Location: During the Late Carboniferous ($\sim 300\text{ Ma}$), Greater India was located at high southern latitudes ($60^\circ-75^\circ\text{S}$) near the South Pole as part of Gondwanaland. Vast continental ice sheets covered the craton, radiating glacial debris into proto-rift grabens.
  • Climatic Amelioration: As Gondwanaland drifted equatorward during the Early Permian, the ice sheets melted rapidly. Meltwater rivers flooded the rift valleys, depositing thick braided-stream sandstones and extensive peat swamps that gave rise to the overlying coal-bearing Barakar Formation and rich Glossopteris flora.

Official Examination Question
Question 48
The base of Gondwana sediments in Talcher coalfield, is marked by a pile of :
(A) Glacial and Periglacial deposits
(B) Fossiliferous shale
(C) Glossopteris flora
(D) Talcher sandstone
Verified Correct Answer: (A) Glacial and Periglacial deposits [The basal Gondwana sequence in Talcher is universally characterized by the Talchir Boulder Bed (tillite) and associated glacio-lacustrine needle shales reflecting the Permo-Carboniferous Gondwana glaciation.]
Correct Option: (A) Glacial and Periglacial deposits

Detailed Stratigraphic Forensic Analysis:

  • In all Indian Gondwana basins—and precisely in the Talcher Coalfield of Odisha (the global type area established by W.T. Blanford in 1856)—the absolute base of the Gondwana Supergroup is represented by the Talchir Formation.
  • The defining, unmistakable lithological feature of the basal Talchir Formation is a massive pile of glacial and periglacial deposits:
    1. The Talchir Boulder Bed: A true subglacial tillite (diamictite) containing faceted, polished, and striated boulders embedded in a fine rock-flour matrix resting directly upon polished Precambrian basement.
    2. Periglacial rhythmite shales ("needle shales") and varved clays carrying ice-rafted dropstones deposited in proglacial lakes.
  • These deposits serve as the prime stratigraphic marker of the widespread Late Carboniferous to Early Permian glaciation of Gondwanaland. Thus, (A) is the definitive correct answer.
Incorrect Distractor: (B) Fossiliferous shale

Detailed Refutation:

  • The basal Talchir glacial tillites are almost entirely devoid of macrofossils due to the hostile polar/glacial environment, containing only rare reworked cold-climate monosaccate miospores (e.g., Plicatipollenites).
  • Abundant fossiliferous shales (carbonaceous and coaly shales) only appear higher up in the succession within the overlying Barakar and Raniganj formations.
Incorrect Distractor: (C) Glossopteris flora

Detailed Refutation:

  • The characteristic Glossopteris seed-fern flora (comprising Glossopteris, Gangamopteris) flourished during the post-glacial warm, humid climate of the Artinskian-Kungurian stages.
  • It reaches its peak abundance in the coal-bearing Barakar and Raniganj Formations. While very rare, primitive Gangamopteris appears in the uppermost transitional Talchir beds, the base of the Talchir sediments is physically marked by sterile glacial diamictite, not by plant flora.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) Talcher sandstone

Detailed Refutation:

  • Sandstones occur as subordinate beds within the middle-to-upper Talchir Formation, but they do not form the basal marker.
  • The base of the Gondwana sequence is physically and genetically defined by the Talchir Boulder Bed (glacial tillite), not sandstone.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Gondwana Stratigraphy & Glaciology
Problem 1: Identification of Glacial Pavement Striations

In 1872, Thomas Oldham discovered the famous Irai striated pavement on the banks of the Penganga River in Maharashtra. What does the orientation of glacial striae on the underlying Precambrian limestone pavement reveal?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The fine parallel scratches, grooves, and chattermarks etched onto the hard Neoproterozoic limestone pavement were carved by angular quartz pebbles dragged beneath the sliding basal sole of a grounded continental ice sheet. The azimuthal orientation of the striae ($010^\circ-190^\circ$, nearly NNE-SSW) demonstrates the exact paleo-ice flow direction of the Gondwana ice sheet moving north-northeastward across Peninsular India.

Problem 2: Clast-to-Matrix Ratio in Subglacial Tillites

A core from the basal Talchir Boulder Bed reveals an unsorted diamictite where pebble and boulder clasts occupy $25\%$ of the total volume and do not touch each other. What sedimentological term describes this fabric, and what is its genetic origin?

Step-by-Step Solution:

This is a matrix-supported diamictite (paraconglomerate). In subglacial lodgement till and glaciomarine dropstone muds, sediment is transported en masse by ice without fluid sorting, depositing clasts suspended directly within an abundant, fine-grained rock-flour matrix without grain-to-grain contact.

Problem 3: Glaciomarine Marine Transgression in the Talchir

Name the famous cold-water marine bivalve and gastropod fauna discovered within the upper Talchir beds in the Umaria and Manendragarh marine beds of central India.

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Eurydesma fauna (dominated by thick-shelled marine bivalves Eurydesma mytiloides, Eurydesma hobartense, and gastropod Peruvispira). This diagnostic fauna proves that during the melting of the ice sheet in the earliest Permian (Asselian), a brief marine transgression penetrated deep into the interior of the Indian craton via the Narmada and Damodar rift corridors.

Problem 4: Mechanical Origin of "Needle Shales"

Why do the green silty shales of the Talchir Formation weather characteristically into slender, pencil-like prismatic needles?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Talchir needle shales possess extremely delicate, rhythmic, paper-thin primary horizontal laminations intersected at right angles by two closely spaced, mutually perpendicular conjugate sets of micro-tectonic joints. Upon subaerial desiccation and weathering, the rock fractures simultaneously along the horizontal bedding and vertical joint planes, disaggregating into characteristic polygonal needles.

Problem 5: Palynological Dating of the Talchir Formation

Which distinctive fossil pollen group dominates the palynological assemblage of the Talchir Formation?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The assemblage is dominated by monosaccate pollen grains (e.g., Plicatipollenites, Parasaccites, Potonieisporites). These saccate pollen grains were produced by primitive gymnosperms (cordaitaleans and primitive conifers) adapted to survive cold, arid periglacial tundra winds.

Problem 6: Coalification Lag Following Glaciation

Why are economic coal seams completely absent in the Talchir Formation, only appearing in the overlying Barakar Formation?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Coal formation requires luxuriant, high-density terrestrial forest growth and rapid biomass accumulation in subsiding anoxic wetlands. The polar, permafrost climate of the Talchir stage was too frigid to support dense arboreal forests. Only when the climate shifted to warm, humid temperate conditions during Barakar times could vast swamp forests of Glossopteris trees flourish and generate thick peat seams.

Problem 7: Dropstone Impact Deformation in Varves

How does a sedimentologist conclusively distinguish an ice-rafted glacial dropstone from a normal river gravel pebble in a finely laminated mudstone?

Step-by-Step Solution:

A true glacial dropstone displays distinct penetration and drape structures: as the rock falls from melting floating icebergs or lake ice, its kinetic impact violently pierces and downwarps the underlying soft laminations beneath it, while subsequent sediment laminations continuously drape over the top of the protruding boulder.

Problem 8: Tectonic Mechanism of Gondwana Basin Formation

Explain the structural graben geometry that preserved the Talchir and overlying Gondwana sediments from subsequent erosion.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Gondwana sediments were deposited in actively subsiding, linear, fault-bounded half-grabens and grabens (e.g., Damodar, Son-Mahanadi, and Pranhita-Godavari troughs) controlled by intraplate extensional rifting along Precambrian sutures. Downfaulting continuously lowered the sedimentary pile below the regional base level, shielding the soft coal measures and basal Talchir tillites from Post-Mesozoic subaerial erosion.
