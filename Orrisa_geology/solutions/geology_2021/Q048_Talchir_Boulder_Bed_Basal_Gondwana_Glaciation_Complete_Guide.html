Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

48.1 Gondwana Basin Inception in Peninsular India

In Indian fuel geology and stratigraphy (W.T. Blanford, 1856; Ramakrishnan & Vaidyanadhan, Geology of India, Vol. 2, Ch. 18; M.S. Krishnan, Ch. 10), the continental Gondwana basins of Peninsular India (Damodar, Mahanadi, Son, Pranhita-Godavari, and Satpura basins) originated in fault-bounded down-faulted rift grabens during the Late Carboniferous to Early Permian (Asselian, $\sim 300\text{ Ma}$):

Lithostratigraphic Unit Chronostratigraphic Age Lithological Association Paleoenvironmental & Paleoclimatic Signatures
Talchir Formation (Basal Unit) Late Carboniferous to Early Permian (Asselian) Basal Tillite / Diamictite (Boulder Bed), Rikba Plant Bed, Khaki-green "Needle Shales", and turbiditic varvites. Widespread continental glaciation across Gondwanaland (glaciomarine and periglacial outwash deposits). NO COAL SEAMS.
Karharbari Formation Early Permian (Sakmarian) Coarse, pebbly arkosic sandstones, reworked tillites, and thin coal seams. Transition from glacial retreat to cool-temperate humid climate.
Barakar Formation Early Permian (Artinskian-Kungurian) Coarse white sandstones, shales, and thick, high-grade workable coal seams. Warm-temperate, humid, luxuriant Glossopteris swamp forest. Major coal measures of India.

48.2 The Talchir Boulder Bed (Diamictite)

The Talcher (Talchir) coalfield in Odisha is the historical type locality where W.T. Blanford and H.F. Blanford (1856) first discovered the Talchir Boulder Bed. It consists of unassorted, faceted, polished, and striated boulders and pebbles of Archean granite, quartzite, and amphibolite embedded in a dense fine-grained matrix of silt and clay. This tillite is equivalent to the Dwyka tillite of South Africa and the Bacchus Marsh tillite of Australia, providing irrefutable proof of the Permo-Carboniferous Southern Hemisphere Gondwana glaciation.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q48. The base of Gondwana sediments in Talcher coalfield, is marked by a pile of :
(A) Glacial and Periglacial deposits
(B) Fossiliferous shale
(C) Glossopteris flora
(D) Talcher sandstone
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (A)

Correct Answer: (A) Glacial and Periglacial deposits — The base of the Gondwana succession in the Talcher coalfield (and across peninsular India) is the Talchir Formation, marked by a basal pile of glacial tillites (Boulder Bed) and periglacial outwash deposits.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Glacial and Periglacial deposits — CORRECT: The hallmark of the basal Talchir Formation: tillites with striated erratics and periglacial needle shales/varves deposited by retreating ice sheets.
  • (B) Fossiliferous shale — INCORRECT: Fossiliferous shales become common only in younger post-glacial units; the basal Talchir tillite is devoid of animal fossils.
  • (C) Glossopteris flora — INCORRECT: The peak of Glossopteris flora characterizes the post-glacial coal-forming Barakar and Raniganj Formations; Talchir yields only sparse pioneer forms (Gangamopteris cyclopteroides).
  • (D) Talcher sandstone — INCORRECT: The defining basal lithology is the glacial boulder tillite bed, not ordinary sandstone.
