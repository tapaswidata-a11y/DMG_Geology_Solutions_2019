1. The Lameta Formation: The Continental Infratrappean Benchmark

The Lameta Formation (named after the type locality at Lameta Ghat on the Narmada River near Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh, by J.G. Medlicott in 1860) is an internationally celebrated Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian, $\sim 70-66\text{ Ma}$) sedimentary formation found extensively across Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, and Maharashtra:

  • The Concept of Infratrappean Beds: In Indian stratigraphy, the Lameta Formation is classically and universally designated as the Infratrappean Beds (from Latin infra, meaning "below" or "beneath").
    The Fundamental Trappean Stratigraphic Relationship
    $$\mathbf{Infratrappean \text{ Beds (Lameta)}} \quad \xrightarrow{\text{Subjacent To}} \quad \mathbf{Deccan \text{ Basalt Traps}} \quad \xrightarrow{\text{Intercalated With}} \quad \mathbf{Intertrappean \text{ Beds}}$$
  • Structural & Stratigraphic Position: The Lameta Beds rest unconformably upon Precambrian crystalline basement (Jabalpur granite/schists) or Gondwana sandstones, and lie directly beneath the lowermost basaltic lava flows of the Deccan Traps.
  • Contrast with Intertrappeans:
    • Infratrappeans (Lameta): Sedimentary strata deposited prior to the onset of Deccan volcanism, immediately underlying the first lava flow.
    • Intertrappeans: Thin, localized, highly fossiliferous lacustrine/fluvial sedimentary lenses deposited during quiescent volcanic intervals between two successive basalt flows (e.g., at Rajahmundry, Nagpur, Worli).
Figure 59.1 (G.B. Mahapatra, p. 175): Stratigraphic position of the Lameta Formation as the classic Infratrappean horizon underlying the Deccan volcanic trap pile.
2. World-Class Vertebrate Paleontology: Dinosaurs of the Lameta Beds

The Lameta Formation is globally renowned as one of the most prolific Cretaceous vertebrate fossil localities in the world, documenting terrestrial life directly preceding the catastrophic K-Pg mass extinction:

  • Sauropod & Theropod Dinosaurs: Yielded holotypes of titanosaurs (Titanosaurus indicus, Isisaurus colberti, Jainosaurus septentrionalis) and abelisaurid predatory theropods (Rajasaurus narmadensis, Indosuchus, Carnotaurus relatives).
  • Dinosaur Nesting Grounds & Megaloolithid Eggs: Extensive nesting sites at Balasinor (Rahioli, Gujarat) and Jabalpur containing clutches of spherical sauropod eggs (Megaloolithus) and embryonic bone remains.
  • Depositional Environment: Represents semi-arid to sub-humid fluvio-lacustrine, palustrine, and caliche-rich paleosol environments bordering the shallow epicontinental marine seaway of the Narmada rift.
Figure 59.2 (G.B. Mahapatra, p. 176): Textbook cross-section illustrating the clear physical distinction between Infratrappean Lameta beds (under the basal flow) and Intertrappean sedimentary interbeds (between basalt flows).
3. Comparative Matrix: Infratrappean vs. Intertrappean Beds

Official Examination Question
Question 59
The Lameta Formation is also known as the ________, is a sedimentary geological formation found in Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, and Maharashtra, India, associated with the Deccan Traps.
(A) Bagh Beds
(B) Intertrappean Beds
(C) Infratrappean Beds
(D) All of these
Verified Correct Answer: (C) Infratrappean Beds [The Lameta Formation directly underlies the Deccan Traps and is universally designated as the Infratrappean Beds in Indian stratigraphy.]
Correct Option: (C) Infratrappean Beds

Detailed Stratigraphic Forensic Analysis:

  • In Indian geology and stratigraphy (Krishnan 1982; Wadia 1975; Mahapatra 2019, p. 175; Mukherjee 2013, p. 220), the Lameta Formation is universally synonymized with and formally termed the Infratrappean Beds.
  • The term *infratrappean* explicitly designates sedimentary strata that were deposited on the pre-volcanic land surface immediately prior to the massive outpourings of the Deccan volcanic eruptions, therefore lying directly *below* (infra) the basal trap flows.
  • These beds are famous for their cherty limestones, mottled clays, and world-class dinosaur nesting sites and skeletons (e.g., Rajasaurus, Titanosaurus).
  • Hence, Option (C) Infratrappean Beds is the precise, unambiguous correct answer.
Incorrect Distractor: (B) Intertrappean Beds

Detailed Refutation:

  • Intertrappean Beds are thin ($1-5\text{ m}$), localized sedimentary lenses that formed in freshwater lakes and rivers during dormant periods *between* two successive Deccan basalt lava flows.
  • They contain abundant freshwater gastropods such as Physa prinsepii and Lymnaea. Because the Lameta Formation lies *beneath* the entire volcanic pile rather than between flows, calling it "Intertrappean" is an egregious stratigraphic error.
Incorrect Distractor: (A) Bagh Beds

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Bagh Beds represent a marine Cretaceous transgressive succession (Nodular Limestone, Deola-Chirakhan Marl, Coralline Limestone) deposited along the Narmada rift valley.
  • While partly coeval with the Lameta Formation, the Bagh Beds represent marine facies, whereas the Lameta Formation represents continental fluvio-lacustrine infratrappean facies. They are two distinct stratigraphic formations.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) All of these

Detailed Refutation:

  • Because only Option (C) Infratrappean Beds correctly describes the Lameta Formation, Option (D) is incorrect.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Mesozoic Paleontology & Deccan Volcanism
Problem 1: Paleosol Calcretes and Pedogenesis in Lameta Beds

The Mottled Nodular Beds of the Lameta Formation exhibit extensive pedogenic calcrete (caliche) nodules. What paleoclimatic conditions do calcretes signify?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Pedogenic calcretes precipitate in the B-horizon of soils where annual evapotranspiration significantly exceeds annual precipitation. In the modern world, mature calcretes form in semi-arid climates with seasonal rainfall between $100\text{ mm}$ and $500\text{ mm/year}$, proving that central India experienced a pronounced seasonal, semi-arid monsoon climate during the terminal Cretaceous.

Problem 2: Titanosaur Sauropod Bone Histology

What unique histological structure in Isisaurus long bones demonstrates rapid juvenile growth rates in Indian titanosaurs?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Cross-sections of titanosaur limb bones from the Lameta beds reveal fibrolamellar bone tissue with dense, reticulate and plexiform vascular canals, punctuated by annual Lines of Arrested Growth (LAGs). Fibrolamellar tissue is characteristic of warm-blooded mammals and birds, proving that sauropods maintained high metabolic rates and explosive growth.

Problem 3: Discovery of Rajasaurus narmadensis

Where was the famous predatory horned abelisaurid dinosaur Rajasaurus narmadensis discovered, and what is its anatomical hallmark?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Rajasaurus was discovered in the Lameta limestone quarry at Rahioli, Kheda district, Gujarat, by GSI paleontologists Suresh Srivastava and Ashok Sahni. Its anatomical hallmark is a prominent, rounded median frontal horn on its skull roof, short robust jaws, and vestigial forelimbs characteristic of Gondwanan Abelisauridae.

Problem 4: K-Pg Boundary Ash in Intertrappean Beds

High-precision $^{40}\text{Ar}/^{39}\text{Ar}$ dating of Deccan basalt flows bounding the Jhilmili intertrappean bed in MP yields an age of $66.05 \pm 0.05\text{ Ma}$. What is the significance of this date?

Step-by-Step Solution:

This date is identical within analytical error to the globally defined Cretaceous-Paleogene (K-Pg) boundary ($66.043\text{ Ma}$). The presence of both Danian planktonic foraminifera and iridium spikes within the Jhilmili intertrappeans proves that Deccan flood basalt volcanism erupted across the K-Pg boundary, acting as a major contributor to the global mass extinction.

Problem 5: Thermal Metamorphism of Infratrappean Rocks

What metamorphic contact feature is commonly observed on the top surface of the Lameta limestone where it contacts the overlying basalt?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The intense thermal heat ($1150-1200^\circ\text{C}$) of the overriding basaltic lava flow thermally bakes the upper $0.5-2\text{ m}$ of the underlying Lameta limestone and shale, creating a hardened, red-burned baked zone (hornfels / jasperoid porcelainization), providing unequivocal proof that the limestone was deposited *prior* to the lava flow.

Problem 6: Megaloolithus Eggshell Microstructure

What structural type characterizes the dinosaur eggshells found in the Lameta nesting grounds?

Step-by-Step Solution:

They belong to the oofamily Megaloolithidae, characterized by spherulitic radiating calcitic prisms (dinosauroid-spherulitic type) with high-density tubocanaliculate pore systems, designed for gas exchange in moist, buried colonial sand nests.

Problem 7: Physa prinsepii as an Intertrappean Index Fossil

Why is the sinistral gastropod Physa prinsepii universally found in Intertrappean beds but absent from Infratrappean Lameta beds?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Physa prinsepii is a specialized freshwater pulmonary snail that colonized warm, silica-rich volcanic crater lakes and shallow ponds that formed on the basalt flow surfaces between eruptions. It evolved during the peak volcanic phases and is thus diagnostic of the Intertrappeans.

Problem 8: Volume of Deccan Flood Basalts

If the Deccan volcanic province originally covered $1.5 \times 10^6\text{ km}^2$ with an average cumulative lava thickness of $1.0\text{ km}$, calculate the total erupted basalt volume in cubic kilometers ($\text{km}^3$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$V = \text{Area} \times \text{Thickness} = (1.5 \times 10^6\text{ km}^2) \times 1.0\text{ km} = 1.5 \times 10^6\text{ km}^3$$

Final Answer: Total erupted volume was approximately $1.5\text{ million cubic kilometers}$, representing one of the largest Large Igneous Provinces (LIPs) in Phanerozoic Earth history.
