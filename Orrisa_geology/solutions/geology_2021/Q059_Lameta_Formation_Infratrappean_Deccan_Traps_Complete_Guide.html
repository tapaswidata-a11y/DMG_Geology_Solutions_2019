Q59: Upper Cretaceous Stratigraphy - Lameta Formation & Infratrappean Beds

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, pp. 334–336); P.K. Mukherjee (A Text Book of Geology, p. 220)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. The Mesozoic-Tertiary Transition & Deccan Volcanism

Towards the close of the Cretaceous Period (Maastrichtian, ~70–66 Ma), the Indian continental plate migrated over the Marion and Réunion mantle plumes, triggering the catastrophic eruption of the Deccan Continental Flood Basalt Province. Across central and western India (Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh), sedimentary strata associated with these volcanic outbursts provide an invaluable window into terminal Cretaceous paleoenvironments, dinosaur faunas, and mass extinction dynamics.

2. The Lameta Formation: Definition & Geographic Occurrence

The Lameta Formation (named after the type locality at Lameta Ghat on the Narmada River near Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh) is a distinctive, thin (10 to 45 meters thick) sedimentary succession found outcropping extensively across the margins of the Deccan volcanic plateau in Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, and Maharashtra. Deposited immediately prior to the eruption of the earliest Deccan lava flows, the formation is universally known in historical and regional Indian geology as the Infratrappean Beds (from Latin infra, "below", indicating strata that lie directly beneath the basaltic "traps"; G.B. Mahapatra, A Text-Book of Geology, Chapter 88, pp. 334–336 / PDF pp. 175–176; P.K. Mukherjee, A Text Book of Geology, p. 220).

The Lameta Formation rests with an erosional disconformity upon Precambrian basement rocks or Jurassic Gondwana strata (Jabalpur Formation) and is directly capped and baked by the basaltic lava flows of the Deccan Traps. The lithology comprises green and purple mottled clays, sandy limestones, calcareous sandstones, and cherty nodules, deposited in shallow freshwater lacustrine, estuarine, and semi-arid palustrine calcrete floodplain environments.

3. Infratrappean vs. Intertrappean Beds: Fundamental Distinction

  • Infratrappean Beds (Lameta Formation): Sediments deposited below the base of the lowest lava flow of the Deccan Traps. They record pre-volcanic Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian) continental sedimentation. World-famous for rich sauropod and abelisaurid dinosaur remains (Titanosaurus indicus, Isisaurus colberti, Rajasaurus narmadensis) and extensive fossilized titanosaur egg clutches.
  • Intertrappean Beds: Sedimentary layers (typically 1 to 5 meters thick) deposited in temporary lakes and ponds in between successive Deccan basalt flows during quiescent intervals between volcanic eruptions. They yield Paleocene freshwater gastropods (Physa prinsepii), charophyte algae, frog fossils (Indobatrachus), and early mammals.
  • Bagh Beds: Coeval marine Cretaceous strata developed in the lower Narmada Valley (Gujarat and western MP), deposited during an arm of the Tethyan marine transgression.

(B) Intertrappean Beds
(C) Infratrappean Beds

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "The Lameta Formation is also known as the ________, is a sedimentary geological formation found in Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, and Maharashtra, India, associated with the Deccan Traps."

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Bagh Beds (INCORRECT): The Bagh Beds represent marine Upper Cretaceous strata (nodular limestone, Coralline limestone, Deola/Chirakhan marls) deposited during a marine embayment in the lower Narmada Valley. While coeval with the Lametas, the Bagh Beds are distinct marine clastic-carbonate units and are not called the "Lameta Formation."
  • (B) Intertrappean Beds (INCORRECT): Intertrappean beds are sedimentary units sandwiched between successive lava flows of the Deccan Traps. The Lameta Formation lies completely beneath the base of the volcanic pile, making it "infratrappean" by definition.
  • (C) Infratrappean Beds (CORRECT): In standard Indian stratigraphy, the Lameta Formation is explicitly and synonymously referred to as the Infratrappean Beds because of its universal structural and stratigraphic position directly underlying the basaltic sheets of the Deccan Traps. Verbatim text in G.B. Mahapatra, p. 336 confirms: "the age of the underlying beds (called the infratraps)... Lameta beds lie beneath the Deccan traps."
  • (D) All of these (INCORRECT): The terms are mutually exclusive: Infratrappean is beneath the traps, Intertrappean is between the traps, and Bagh Beds are marine facies.
