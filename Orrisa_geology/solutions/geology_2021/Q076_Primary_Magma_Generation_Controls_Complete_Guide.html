Q76: Primary Magma Composition - Parameters and Mantle Melting Controls

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: J.D. Winter (Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, Ch. 10, p. 205)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Core Question Analysis & Verbatim Textbook Grounding

The question tests the fundamental thermodynamic and petrogenetic parameters that determine the chemical composition of a primary mantle magma (melt derived directly from mantle peridotite without subsequent differentiation).

Textbook Citation (John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology, 2nd Ed., Chapter 10, Section 10.2, p. 204):
"The chemical composition of a primary melt generated by partial melting of mantle peridotite is fundamentally controlled by: (1) the bulk mineralogical and chemical composition of the mantle source (fertile lherzolite vs. depleted harzburgite), (2) the confining pressure (depth of melting, which shifts the eutectic/peritectic liquid compositions from silica-oversaturated tholeiites at shallow depths to silica-undersaturated alkalic picrites at high pressures), (3) the activity and relative concentrations of volatile species (especially H2O and CO2, where dissolved H2O depresses the solidus and expands the olivine field toward silica-rich melts, while CO2 promotes silica-poor, carbonatitic/nephelinitic melts), and (4) the degree of partial melting (F), where low degrees of melting (F < 5%) concentrate incompatible elements into alkaline melts, whereas higher degrees of melting (F > 15-25%) dilute incompatible elements to yield MORB tholeiitic melts."

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q76. The composition of a primary magma depends on the following parameters : (i) Composition of the source material being partially melted, (ii) Relative concentrations of volatiles, (iii) Confining pressure, (iv) Degree of partial melting. Choose the correct set of answer from the following options :
(A) (i), (ii), (iii)
(B) (ii), (iii), (iv)
(C) (i), (iii), (iv)
(D) (i), (ii), (iii), (iv)

2. Multi-Dimensional Petrogenetic Masterclass

Primary magmas represent pristine liquids formed at high pressure in the upper mantle. The four governing parameters operate synergistically:

Parameter Physical / Chemical Mechanism Petrogenetic Impact on Primary Melt Composition
(i) Source Composition Fertile vs. refractory mantle residues (primitive mantle vs. depleted MORB mantle DMM vs. enriched mantle EM1/EM2/HIMU). Dictates starting fertility, fertility of Al-phase (plagioclase < 30 km, spinel 30–85 km, garnet > 85 km), and isotopic signatures.
(ii) Volatile Abundances (H2O, CO2) Dissolution of volatile species into silicate melt network; polymerization changes ($Si-O-Si$ network modifier vs. former). Hydrous melting ($H_2O$) shifts eutectic toward quartz ($SiO_2$-oversaturation, calc-alkaline series). Carbonated melting ($CO_2$) shifts liquid away from $SiO_2$, yielding melilitites, kimberlites, and nephelinites.
(iii) Confining Pressure ($P$) Shifts liquidus phase boundaries and eutectic minimum in pseudo-ternary systems (Fo-Di-Qtz). Increasing pressure contracts the primary phase field of enstatite/orthopyroxene, forcing primary liquids toward silica undersaturation (alkali olivine basalts at $P > 1.5$–$2.5 ext{ GPa}$).
(iv) Degree of Melting ($F$) Batch melting equation: $C_L = C_0 / [D_0 + F(1 - D_0)]$. Low $F$ (1–5%) extracts highly incompatible elements ($K, Ba, LREE$) to form alkaline basalts/basanites; high $F$ (15–30%) yields depleted sub-alkaline tholeiitic basalt.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A ((i), (ii), (iii)) is incomplete: Neglects the degree of partial melting ($F$), which is universally recognized as the master variable determining whether a tholeiite or alkali basalt is produced.
  • Option B ((ii), (iii), (iv)) is incomplete: Ignores the source composition. Melting of a pyroxenite vs. harzburgite vs. lherzolite yields completely disparate primary magmas.
  • Option C ((i), (iii), (iv)) is incomplete: Ignores volatile content ($H_2O, CO_2$), which can depress the peridotite solidus by >400°C and completely invert silica activity.
  • Option D ((i), (ii), (iii), (iv)) is correct: All 4 thermodynamic variables collectively define the Gibbs free energy minimization and resulting primary melt stoichiometry.
