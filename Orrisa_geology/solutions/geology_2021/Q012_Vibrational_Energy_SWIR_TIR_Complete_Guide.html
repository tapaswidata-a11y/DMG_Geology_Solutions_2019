Q12: Spectroscopy & Molecular Vibrational Energy in SWIR and TIR Bands

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Lillesand et al. (Remote Sensing, Ch. 1 & 8); Roger N. Clark (USGS Spectroscopy of Rocks & Minerals); White (Geochemistry)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

12.1 Electronic vs. Vibrational Transitions Across the Electromagnetic Spectrum

In optical remote sensing and mineral spectroscopy (Clark, 1999, USGS Spectroscopy of Rocks and Minerals; Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman, Ch. 1 & 8; White, Geochemistry, Ch. 13), electromagnetic radiation interacts with matter via two fundamental physical mechanisms:

Energy Transition Type Electromagnetic Spectral Domain Physical Mechanism & Diagnostic Geological Absorption
Electronic Transitions UV, Visible & Near-Infrared (VNIR: $0.2 - 1.0\ \mu\text{m}$) Valence electrons jump between orbital states. Crystal field effects ($d-d$ transitions in $Fe^{2+}, Fe^{3+}, Cr^{3+}$), charge transfer (iron-oxygen charge transfer in hematite, goethite), and conduction bands.
Molecular Vibrational Energy (Fundamental & Overtones) Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR: $1.4 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$) & Thermal Infrared (TIR: $8.0 - 14.0\ \mu\text{m}$) Stretching and bending vibrations of interatomic chemical bonds:
• SWIR: Overtones and combination vibrational modes of $Al-OH$ ($2.20\ \mu\text{m}$), $Mg-OH$ ($2.31\ \mu\text{m}$), and $CO_3^{2-}$ ($2.35\ \mu\text{m}$).
• TIR: Fundamental $Si-O-Si$ asymmetric stretching (Reststrahlen bands, $8.5 - 12\ \mu\text{m}$) and kinetic molecular thermal emission.
Rotational Transitions Microwave & Far-Infrared ($> 100\ \mu\text{m}$) Pure rotational energy level transitions in free gas molecules.

12.2 Quantum Harmonic Oscillator Mechanics

The vibrational frequency $(\nu)$ of a diatomic chemical bond is governed by Hooke's spring law and reduced mass $(\mu)$:

$$\nu = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}}, \quad \text{where } \mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$$

The energy associated with vibrational quantum transitions $(\Delta E = h\nu)$ corresponds precisely to photon wavelengths in the SWIR and TIR regions of the spectrum.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q12. The vibrational energy of an object is associated with the _____ of the electromagnetic spectrum.
(A) UV and Photo UV region
(B) SWIR and TIR region
(C) Visible and NIR region
(D) Microwave and Radio wave region
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) SWIR and TIR region — Molecular vibrational modes (fundamental stretching/bending and combination overtones of $Si-O$, $O-H$, $C-O$ bonds) are uniquely associated with the SWIR and TIR regions of the electromagnetic spectrum.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) UV and Photo UV region — INCORRECT: Governed by high-energy electronic transitions and photo-ionization, not vibrational modes.
  • (B) SWIR and TIR region — CORRECT: SWIR ($1.4 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$) features overtone/combination vibrational bands, and TIR ($8 - 14\ \mu\text{m}$) features fundamental bond-stretching vibrations and thermal emissions.
  • (C) Visible and NIR region — INCORRECT: Dominated by crystal field electronic transitions ($Fe^{2+}/Fe^{3+}$) and chlorophyll $\pi$-electron orbital excitation.
  • (D) Microwave and Radio wave region — INCORRECT: Associated with rotational states and magnetic dipole spin flips (e.g. 21-cm hydrogen line), possessing far too low photon energy for vibrational bond excitations.

Mineral Spectroscopy Practice Problem

Practice Problem 12: Hydrothermal Alteration Mapping in SWIR

Which diagnostic vibrational absorption feature in the SWIR spectrum ($2.20\ \mu\text{m}$) allows exploration geologists to identify clay minerals like kaolinite and alunite in copper-gold porphyry systems?

Answer:

The combination overtone of the $Al-OH$ bond stretching + $Al-OH$ bending vibration occurs at precisely $2.20\ \mu\text{m}$. Kaolinite produces a diagnostic doublet ($2.16\ \mu\text{m}$ and $2.20\ \mu\text{m}$), whereas alunite exhibits absorption at $2.17\ \mu\text{m}$.
