1. Quantum Energy States & Spectral Transitions Across the EMS

When electromagnetic radiation interacts with matter, photons are selectively absorbed at resonant frequencies corresponding to discrete quantum energy transitions within atoms, crystal lattices, and molecules. In optical remote sensing and mineral spectroscopy (Hunt, 1977; Clark, 1999), radiation is partitioned into fundamental physical interaction domains:

  • Electronic Transitions (UV, Visible, & NIR: $0.1 - 1.0\ \mu\text{m}$): Transitions of valence electrons between discrete atomic orbitals (crystal field splitting, charge transfer, conduction band transitions). Examples: $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ and $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ absorption bands in iron oxides (hematite, goethite) at $0.85 - 0.95\ \mu\text{m}$.
  • Vibrational Transitions (SWIR & TIR: $1.4 - 14.0\ \mu\text{m}$): Mechanical stretching and bending vibrations of covalent chemical bonds within molecular radicals (e.g., $\text{O-H}, \text{Al-OH}, \text{Mg-OH}, \text{CO}_3^{2-}, \text{Si-O}$). Fundamental vibrations occur in the Thermal Infrared (TIR, $8 - 14\ \mu\text{m}$), while their overtone and combination modes occur in the Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR, $1.4 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$).
  • Rotational Transitions (Far-IR & Microwave: $> 100\ \mu\text{m} - 1\text{ m}$): End-over-end rotational motions of free dipolar gaseous molecules (e.g., water vapor rotations in the microwave band).
Quantum Harmonic Oscillator Energy Equation
$$E_v = \left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right) h \nu_0 = \left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right) \frac{h}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}}$$

Where $v$ is vibrational quantum number, $h$ is Planck's constant, $k$ is interatomic chemical bond force constant, and $\mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$ is reduced mass. Bond vibrations require infrared photon energies.

2. SWIR Vibrational Overtones ($1.4 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$) in Mineral Exploration

The Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR, $1.4 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$) region is the most critical window in geological remote sensing for hydrothermal alteration mapping:

  • Al-OH Minerals (Phyllic Alteration): Illite, muscovite, kaolinite, and pyrophyllite display diagnostic vibrational absorption doublets at $2.20\ \mu\text{m}$ due to combination stretching and bending of aluminum-hydroxyl bonds.
  • Mg-OH Minerals (Propylitic Alteration): Chlorite, epidote, and talc display sharp vibrational absorption features at $2.30 - 2.35\ \mu\text{m}$ due to magnesium-hydroxyl vibrations.
  • Carbonates ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$): Calcite and dolomite show intense asymmetric stretching overtones at $2.33\ \mu\text{m}$ and $2.50\ \mu\text{m}$.
"Vibrational processes produce absorption bands in the short-wave infrared (SWIR, 1.3 to 2.5 $\mu$m) and thermal infrared (TIR, 8 to 14 $\mu$m). In the SWIR, overtones and combination tones of OH, H2O, CO3, and Al-OH or Mg-OH bonds allow precise identification of clay and carbonate minerals." — Floyd F. Sabins, Remote Sensing: Principles and Interpretation (3rd Ed., 1997, p. 89)
3. TIR Fundamental Vibrations ($8 - 14\ \mu\text{m}$): The Reststrahlen Band

In the Thermal Infrared (TIR, $8 - 14\ \mu\text{m}$), fundamental stretching vibrations of silicon-oxygen ($\text{Si-O-Si}$) tetrahedral bonds produce intense reflectance peaks accompanied by emissivity minima known as Reststrahlen bands:

  • Silica Polymerization Shift: In quartz and felsic rocks (framework silicates), the reststrahlen minimum occurs around $8.5 - 9.0\ \mu\text{m}$. In mafic and ultramafic rocks (isolated tetrahedral olivine, pyroxene), it shifts systematically to longer wavelengths ($10.5 - 11.5\ \mu\text{m}$), allowing direct mapping of rock lithology using ASTER thermal bands.

Official Question Statement — OPSC 2021 Geology Paper-II (Question 12)
Q12. The vibrational energy of an object is associated with the _____ of the electromagnetic spectrum.
(A) UV and Photo UV region
(B) SWIR and TIR region  ✔
(C) Visible and NIR region
(D) Microwave and Radio wave region
✔
Correct Answer: (B) SWIR and TIR region
In molecular spectroscopy and remote sensing, vibrational energy transitions (bond stretching and bending of $\text{O-H}, \text{Al-OH}, \text{Mg-OH}, \text{CO}_3$, and $\text{Si-O}$) are strictly associated with the Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR: $1.4 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$) and Thermal Infrared (TIR: $8 - 14\ \mu\text{m}$) regions.
(A) UV and Photo UV region Incorrect (High-Energy Electronic Ionization Transitions)
Why this distractor is incorrect: The ultraviolet ($< 0.40\ \mu\text{m}$) region contains exceptionally high photon energies ($E = h\nu = 3.1 - 100\text{ eV}$) that drive deep valence electron excitation, photo-ionization, and molecular dissociation—not mechanical vibrational bond oscillations.
(B) SWIR and TIR region Correct Verified Answer
Why this option is correct: Molecular vibrational bonds have resonant frequencies whose energy quanta precisely match infrared photons. The fundamental bending and stretching vibrational modes occur directly in the Thermal Infrared (TIR, $8 - 14\ \mu\text{m}$), while their overtone and combination vibrational modes dominate the Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR, $1.4 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$).
(C) Visible and NIR region Incorrect (Electronic Orbital Transitions)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Spectral features in the Visible ($0.4 - 0.7\ \mu\text{m}$) and Near-Infrared ($0.7 - 1.1\ \mu\text{m}$) regions are driven by electronic processes, such as crystal-field transitions in transition metal cations ($\text{Fe}^{2+}, \text{Fe}^{3+}$ in iron oxides) and chlorophyll pigment absorption in green leaves.
(D) Microwave and Radio wave region Incorrect (Low-Energy Rotational & Macroscopic Interactions)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Microwave and radio waves have very low photon energies ($< 10^{-4}\text{ eV}$), which correspond to end-over-end molecular rotation in free gases or macroscopic surface scattering and dielectric interactions, lacking sufficient energy to excite interatomic vibrational modes.

High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Questions)
  • Practice Q1: Which specific molecular bond combination causes the diagnostic $2.20\ \mu\text{m}$ absorption band in illite, kaolinite, and muscovite?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The combination of the $\text{Al-O-H}$ bending fundamental with the $\text{O-H}$ stretching fundamental.
    Explanation: The fundamental $\text{O-H}$ stretch occurs at $\sim 2.7\ \mu\text{m}$ and the $\text{Al-OH}$ bend at $\sim 11\ \mu\text{m}$; their quantum mechanical combination produces the diagnostic $2.20\ \mu\text{m}$ overtone used globally to map phyllic alteration.
  • Practice Q2: What is a Reststrahlen band in thermal infrared spectroscopy?
    ✔ Correct Answer: A sharp fundamental reflectance peak (emissivity trough) caused by resonant mechanical vibration of $\text{Si-O}$ crystal lattices.
    Explanation: Coined from the German for "residual rays", Reststrahlen bands occur between $8.5 - 12\ \mu\text{m}$ where resonant silicate vibrations prevent photon transmission, maximizing specular reflectance and producing an emissivity minimum.
  • Practice Q3: How does the position of the Reststrahlen minimum shift from felsic (granitic) to ultramafic (peridotitic) rocks?
    ✔ Correct Answer: It shifts systematically from shorter wavelengths ($\sim 8.5\ \mu\text{m}$ in quartz) to longer wavelengths ($\sim 11.0\ \mu\text{m}$ in olivine).
    Explanation: Highly polymerized framework silicates (quartz, feldspar) have stronger, stiffer bond networks than isolated $\text{SiO}_4$ tetrahedra, shifting resonant vibrational frequencies to higher energy (shorter wavelengths).
  • Practice Q4: Why do liquid water and water vapor produce broad absorption bands at $1.4\ \mu\text{m}$ and $1.9\ \mu\text{m}$?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Overtones and combination modes of the asymmetric stretch ($\nu_3$) and bending ($\nu_2$) of the water molecule ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
    Explanation: Free water molecules have fundamental vibrations at $2.66\ \mu\text{m}$ ($\nu_1$), $6.27\ \mu\text{m}$ ($\nu_2$), and $2.74\ \mu\text{m}$ ($\nu_3$). The $1.9\ \mu\text{m}$ band is the combination $\nu_2 + \nu_3$, while $1.4\ \mu\text{m}$ is the first overtone of $\text{O-H}$ stretching.
  • Practice Q5: In satellite multispectral imaging, which ASTER sensor subsystems cover the vibrational absorption bands?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The SWIR subsystem (Bands 4–9: $1.60 - 2.43\ \mu\text{m}$) and TIR subsystem (Bands 10–14: $8.125 - 11.65\ \mu\text{m}$).
    Explanation: ASTER was specifically engineered with 6 contiguous SWIR bands to resolve clay/carbonate overtones and 5 TIR bands to resolve silicate Reststrahlen features.
  • Practice Q6: What quantum transition explains why hematite ($\alpha\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$) appears red in the visible spectrum and displays a broad reflectance trough around $0.86\ \mu\text{m}$?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Electronic crystal field splitting of the $3d$ orbital energy levels of the ferric ion ($\text{Fe}^{3+}$).
    Explanation: Electric fields from surrounding oxygen ligands split the $3d$ electron orbitals, absorbing green and blue photons ($^6A_1 \to\ ^4T_1, ^4T_2$), while reflecting red photons.
  • Practice Q7: Why can remote sensors not observe ground vibrational features in the $5.5 - 7.5\ \mu\text{m}$ spectral region?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Severe atmospheric opacity caused by the fundamental bending vibration ($\nu_2$) of atmospheric water vapor centered at $6.27\ \mu\text{m}$.
    Explanation: This region is a complete atmospheric absorption window closure, where solar and terrestrial radiation cannot penetrate to or from the ground.
  • Practice Q8: How does the spectral signature of calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) differ from dolomite ($\text{CaMg}(\text{CO}_3)_2$) in the SWIR vibrational band?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Calcite absorbs at $2.335\ \mu\text{m}$, while dolomite absorbs at a slightly shorter wavelength of $2.315\ \mu\text{m}$ due to magnesium cation substitution.
    Explanation: Smaller $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions increase the interatomic bond force constant, shifting the carbonate vibrational combination band toward shorter wavelengths.
