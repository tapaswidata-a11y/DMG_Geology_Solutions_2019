Q4: Quantitative Morphotectonic Indices for Drainage Basin Analysis

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Keller & Pinter (Active Tectonics: Earthquakes, Uplift, and Landscape, 2nd Ed., Ch. 5, pp. 121-148); Burbank & Anderson (2011)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

4.1 The Keller-Pinter Framework for Morphotectonic Analysis

In tectonic geomorphology and active fault studies (Keller & Pinter, 2002, Active Tectonics: Earthquakes, Uplift, and Landscape, Ch. 5; Burbank & Anderson, 2011), four quantitative indices are systematically computed to assess relative tectonic activity ($I_{at}$):

Index Name & Symbol Mathematical Formula Tectonic Significance & Diagnostic Signatures
1. Drainage Basin Asymmetry ($AF$) $AF = 100 \left(\frac{A_r}{A_t}\right)$ Detects tectonic tilt perpendicular to trunk stream. $AF = 50$ indicates symmetric equilibrium; significant deviation from 50 reveals active structural tilting.
2. Stream Length-Gradient ($SL$) $SL = \left(\frac{\Delta H}{\Delta L}\right) L$ Sensitive to changes in river gradient caused by active fault scarps, tectonic knickpoints, or contrasting rock resistance. High $SL$ values highlight active uplift.
3. Mountain-Front Sinuosity ($S_{mf}$) $S_{mf} = \frac{L_{mf}}{L_s}$ Reflects balance between active uplift and stream embayment along front faults. $S_{mf} < 1.4$ indicates high tectonic activity (straight front); $S_{mf} > 2.0$ indicates quiescence.
4. Valley Floor Width to Height Ratio ($V_f$) $V_f = \frac{2 V_{fw}}{(E_{ld} - E_{sc}) + (E_{rd} - E_{sc})}$ Distinguishes actively uplifting deep V-shaped canyons ($V_f \ll 1.0$) from flat, tectonically inactive wide-bottomed valleys ($V_f \gg 1.0$).

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q4. Which of the following indices belong to 'Tectonic Activity Indices' used for morphotectonic analysis of a drainage basin? (i) Drainage basin asymmetry, (ii) Stream Length-Gradient Index (SL Index), (iii) Mountain-front sinuosity, (iv) Ratio of valley floor width to valley height. Choose the correct set of answer from the following options :
(A) (i), (ii) and (iv)
(B) (ii) and (iv)
(C) (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv)
(D) (ii), (iii) and (iv)
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (C)

Correct Answer: (C) (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) — All four listed indices ($AF$, $SL$, $S_{mf}$, $V_f$) are standard, recognized Tectonic Activity Indices used in morphotectonic drainage basin analysis.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) (i), (ii) and (iv) — INCORRECT: Omission of Mountain-front sinuosity ($S_{mf}$), which is one of the most critical indices used to distinguish active Range-bounding fault fronts.
  • (B) (ii) and (iv) — INCORRECT: Omits basin asymmetry and mountain front sinuosity.
  • (C) (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) — CORRECT: Captures the complete, canonical four-index morphotectonic suite formalized by Keller & Pinter (2002).
  • (D) (ii), (iii) and (iv) — INCORRECT: Omits basin asymmetry ($AF$).

Worked Morphotectonic Problem

Practice Problem 4: Calculation of Mountain Front Sinuosity ($S_{mf}$)

Along an active mountain front fault in the Eastern Ghats, the measured length along the topographic break-in-slope of the mountain front is $L_{mf} = 14.8\text{ km}$, while the straight-line distance along the fault front is $L_s = 12.0\text{ km}$. Calculate $S_{mf}$ and interpret the tectonic activity class.

Solution:

$$S_{mf} = \frac{L_{mf}}{L_s} = \frac{14.8}{12.0} = 1.23$$

According to Keller & Pinter classification: Class 1 (Highly Active): $S_{mf} < 1.4$. Since $S_{mf} = 1.23 < 1.4$, the mountain front is classified as Tectonically Active (Class 1) with rapid uplift rates outcompeting stream erosion and embayment.
