2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS)

In engineering geology and rock mechanics (IS: 9143, ISRM Suggested Methods, Bieniawski's RMR, and Hoek-Brown failure criterion), the Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS, $\sigma_c$)—also termed uniaxial compressive strength—is unanimously recognized as the single most critical index property of intact rock:

  • Testing Protocol: A prepared cylindrical rock core (length-to-diameter ratio $L/D = 2.0 - 2.5$) is placed between steel platens and axially loaded to failure without lateral confinement ($\sigma_2 = \sigma_3 = 0$): $$\sigma_c = \frac{P_{\text{failure}}}{A}$$ where $P_{\text{failure}}$ is the peak axial load at catastrophic failure, and $A$ is the cross-sectional area.
  • Why UCS is the Cardinal Index:
    • It governs rock mass classification systems (RMR, Q-system, GSI).
    • It dictates bearing capacity for dam foundations, bridge piers, and high-rise footings.
    • It controls tunnel drillability, excavatability, and stability of underground openings.
  • Confined, drained, and undrained strengths are specialized parameters measured in triaxial cells primarily for soft cohesive soils, weak clays, or deep reservoir conditions, whereas UCS is the universal standard index for building stones.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option D (Unconfined) is correct: UCS is the universal index parameter used across engineering specifications (Bangar p. 186; IS 9143).
  • Option A (Confined): Requires specialized triaxial cells ($\sigma_3 > 0$); used for deep rock mass modeling, not routine stone index characterization.
  • Option B & C (Drained / Undrained): Soil mechanics terms governing pore water drainage states.
