2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: The Penman-Monteith Framework for Evapotranspiration

Evapotranspiration ($ET$) is the combined process of direct water evaporation from soil and water surfaces plus biological transpiration through plant stomata. In hydrogeology and watershed hydrology (governed by the FAO-56 Penman-Monteith equation), $ET$ is controlled by four interdependent spheres:

  1. Thermal Energy & Temperature (Factor i): Solar radiation and temperature of soil, air, and water provide the latent heat of vaporization ($\approx 2.45\text{ MJ/kg}$) required to convert liquid water into vapor.
  2. Moisture & Vapor Pressure Gradient (Factor ii): Evaporation rate is directly proportional to the saturation vapor deficit ($e_s - e_a$). High soil moisture provides available water, while low relative humidity (air moisture deficit) accelerates vapor diffusion.
  3. Aerodynamic Transport & Pressure (Factor iii): Wind velocity sweeps away saturated boundary layer air, maintaining steep vapor gradients; lower atmospheric pressure slightly facilitates molecular diffusion.
  4. Vegetation Characteristics (Factor iv): Canopy architecture, stomatal resistance, leaf area index (LAI), root depth, and phenological stage govern biological transpiration rates.

Because all four groups are fundamental determinants of evapotranspiration, option (D) is the only comprehensive, correct answer.
