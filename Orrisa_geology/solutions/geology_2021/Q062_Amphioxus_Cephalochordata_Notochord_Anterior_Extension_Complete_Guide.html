Q62: Chordate Morphology - Notochord Extension in Amphioxus (Cephalochordata)

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Michael J. Benton (Vertebrate Palaeontology, Ch. 1); Romer & Parsons (The Vertebrate Body)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Phylogenetic Position of Amphioxus (Subphylum Cephalochordata)

Amphioxus (commonly represented by the genus Branchiostoma, formerly Amphioxus lanceolatus) is a small (5 to 7 cm long), translucent, fish-like marine organism inhabiting shallow, clean sand substrates in coastal waters worldwide. In evolutionary biology and comparative vertebrate paleontology (Michael J. Benton, Vertebrate Palaeontology, 4th ed., Chapter 1; Romer & Parsons, The Vertebrate Body), amphioxus occupies a celebrated role as the archetype model for the ancestral chordate body plan.

Phylum Chordata is defined by five diagnostic morphological synapomorphies present at some stage of development:

  1. A dorsal, flexible, rod-like skeletal axis termed the notochord.
  2. A single, hollow dorsal nerve cord.
  3. Pharyngeal gill slits or pouches.
  4. An endostyle (or its derivative, the thyroid gland).
  5. A muscular, post-anal tail.

2. The Diagnostic Cephalochordate Distinction: The Anterior Notochord

While amphioxus shares all primary chordate traits, its subphylum name—Cephalochordata (from Greek kephale, "head", and chorda, "cord")—highlights its single most defining anatomical divergence from Subphylum Vertebrata (Craniata):

In true vertebrates, the notochord terminates beneath the midbrain, immediately posterior to the infundibulum and pituitary gland, leaving the anterior cerebral lobes unbraced by chordal tissue. In contrast, in amphioxus, the notochord extends persistently along the entire length of the dorsal body axis to the extreme ANTERIOR tip of the rostrum (ahead of the cerebral vesicle and anterior pigment cup!).

Functional morphology explains this unique adaptation: Amphioxus is an active, rapid burrower that darts backwards and forwards into marine sand. The extension of the rigid, incompressible, muscularized notochord beyond the brain to the extreme anterior tip transforms the snout into a stiffened hydrodynamic shovel or drill, allowing the lancelet to penetrate compacted sand sediments without collapsing its delicate cephalic neural tissues!

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q62. The notochord of amphioxus differs from that of vertebrates by extending to the extreme _______ end of the body.
(A) posterior
(B) anterior
(C) open
(D) None of these

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "The notochord of amphioxus differs from that of vertebrates by extending to the extreme ________ end of the body."

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) posterior (INCORRECT): In vertebrates, urochordates, and cephalochordates alike, the notochord extends posteriorly into the caudal tail region. Extending posteriorly does not differentiate amphioxus from vertebrates; both possess a posterior notochordal axis.
  • (B) anterior (CORRECT): The defining morphological hallmark that separates Cephalochordata (Amphioxus) from Vertebrata (Craniata) is that the notochord extends forward beyond the brain to the extreme anterior end of the body (the tip of the rostrum). In vertebrates, the anterior end of the notochord stops behind the brain.
  • (C) open (INCORRECT): "Open" is an anatomical non sequitur; the notochord is a sealed, fluid-filled, turgid cylindrical organ, not an open aperture.
  • (D) None of these (INCORRECT): Option B is the universally documented anatomical diagnostic feature.
