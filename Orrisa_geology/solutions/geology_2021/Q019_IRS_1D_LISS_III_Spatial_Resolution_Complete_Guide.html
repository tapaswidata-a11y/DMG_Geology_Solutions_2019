1. The Indian Remote Sensing (IRS-1D) Mission & Sensor Suite

Launched on 29 September 1997 aboard India's Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV-C1) from Sriharikota, IRS-1D (along with its identical predecessor IRS-1C, launched in 1995) was an internationally acclaimed second-generation earth observation satellite developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). Operating in a Sun-synchronous circular orbit at an altitude of $817\text{ km}$ ($i = 98.69^\circ$, 24-day repeat cycle), IRS-1D carried three cutting-edge imaging payloads:

  • 1. LISS-III (Linear Imaging Self-Scanning Sensor III): The primary multispectral sensor (MSS) designed for regional natural resources management, agricultural monitoring, and geological mapping. LISS-III delivers a benchmark spatial resolution of $23.5\text{ meters}$ in the Visible and Near-Infrared (VNIR) bands.
  • 2. PAN (Panchromatic Camera): A high-resolution imaging camera operating across $0.50 - 0.75\ \mu\text{m}$ with a spatial resolution of $5.8\text{ meters}$ and a $70\text{ km}$ swath, equipped with off-nadir steering ($\pm 26^\circ$) for stereoscopic 3D terrain mapping. In 1995, it was the highest-resolution civilian earth observation sensor in the world!
  • 3. WiFS (Wide Field Sensor): A dual-band sensor (Red: $0.62 - 0.68\ \mu\text{m}$, NIR: $0.77 - 0.86\ \mu\text{m}$) with a spatial resolution of $188\text{ meters}$ and an ultra-wide swath of $810\text{ km}$ (5-day revisit) for national-scale vegetation and drought dynamics.

2. Technical Specifications of LISS-III: The 23.5m Multispectral Benchmark

LISS-III utilized linear charge-coupled device (CCD) pushbroom line arrays (6000 elements per array) to image terrain without moving optical scan mirrors. Its four spectral bands and spatial resolutions are structured as follows:

LISS-III Spectral Band Wavelength Range ($\mu\text{m}$) Spatial Resolution (GSD) Swath Width Primary Geological & Land Use Application
Band 2 (Green) $0.52 - 0.59\ \mu\text{m}$ $23.5\text{ meters}$ $141\text{ km}$ Vegetation green reflectance peak; water turbidity
Band 3 (Red) $0.62 - 0.68\ \mu\text{m}$ $23.5\text{ meters}$ $141\text{ km}$ Chlorophyll absorption; soil/vegetation contrast; cultural features
Band 4 (Near-Infrared) $0.77 - 0.86\ \mu\text{m}$ $23.5\text{ meters}$ $141\text{ km}$ Biomass mapping; water-land boundary delimitation
Band 5 (Short-Wave IR) $1.55 - 1.70\ \mu\text{m}$ $70.5\text{ meters}$ $148\text{ km}$ Soil moisture; snow vs. cloud discrimination; hydrothermal alteration
"The LISS-III camera operates in four spectral bands: three in the VNIR (0.52-0.59, 0.62-0.68, 0.77-0.86 $\mu$m) with a spatial resolution of 23.5 m at nadir, and one in the SWIR (1.55-1.70 $\mu$m) with a resolution of 70.5 m." — K. Kasturirangan et al., IRS-1C - The Indian Space Programme's Advanced Remote Sensing Satellite (Current Science, 1996, Vol. 70, No. 7, p. 494)
3. Forensic Dissection of Distractor Resolutions

Candidates must clearly associate other famous remote sensing resolutions:

  • (A) 1m: Characteristic of modern sub-meter commercial high-resolution imaging satellites such as IKONOS-2 ($1\text{ m}$ PAN in 1999) or ISRO's Cartosat-2 series ($0.8\text{ m}$). It is far too fine for IRS-1D LISS-III.
  • (B) 30m: The classic spatial resolution of NASA's Landsat Thematic Mapper (TM/ETM+/OLI Bands 1–5, 7), which defined multispectral resolution from 1982 onwards.
  • (C) 79m (or 80m): The historical spatial resolution of the original Multispectral Scanner (MSS) onboard early Landsat-1, 2, and 3 (1972–1978).

Official Question Statement — OPSC 2021 Geology Paper-II (Question 19)
Q19. In IRS 1D satellite, the spatial resolution of MSS sensor is :
(A) 1m.
(B) 30m
(C) 79m
(D) 23.5m  ✔
✔
Correct Answer: (D) 23.5m
In the Indian Remote Sensing satellite IRS-1D, the primary multispectral sensor (MSS)—the LISS-III camera—operates with a spatial resolution of 23.5 meters in its three visible and near-infrared bands (Bands 2, 3, and 4).
(A) 1m. Incorrect (High-Resolution Satellite Domain like IKONOS / Cartosat-2)
Why this distractor is incorrect: A 1-meter spatial resolution belongs to very high-resolution military and commercial mapping satellites (e.g., IKONOS-2 launched in 1999, OrbView, or Cartosat-2). The highest optical resolution on IRS-1D was the Panchromatic (PAN) camera at $5.8\text{ m}$.
(B) 30m Incorrect (Thematic Mapper / Landsat Benchmark)
Why this distractor is incorrect: $30\text{ meters}$ is the global benchmark spatial resolution of NASA's Landsat Thematic Mapper (Landsat-4, 5, 7, 8, 9). ISRO purposely engineered LISS-III with an enhanced $23.5\text{ m}$ resolution to surpass Landsat TM in ground detail.
(C) 79m Incorrect (Landsat-1/2/3 First-Generation MSS Resolution)
Why this distractor is incorrect: $79\text{ meters}$ (nominally $80\text{ m}$) was the resolution of the historical Multispectral Scanner (MSS) flown on Landsat-1, 2, and 3 in the 1970s, as well as ISRO's earliest first-generation LISS-I sensor on IRS-1A/1B ($72.5\text{ m}$).
(D) 23.5m Correct Verified Answer
Why this option is correct: On IRS-1C and IRS-1D, the flagship multispectral sensor LISS-III provided $23.5\text{ m}$ ground spatial resolution across its VNIR bands (Green: $0.52 - 0.59\ \mu\text{m}$, Red: $0.62 - 0.68\ \mu\text{m}$, and NIR: $0.77 - 0.86\ \mu\text{m}$) over a $141\text{ km}$ swath, establishing India as a world leader in civilian remote sensing.

High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Questions)
  • Practice Q1: What was the spatial resolution of the SWIR band (Band 5: $1.55 - 1.70\ \mu\text{m}$) on the IRS-1D LISS-III sensor?
    ✔ Correct Answer: $70.5\text{ meters}$ (with a swath of $148\text{ km}$).
    Explanation: To maintain adequate signal-to-noise ratio with uncooled InGaAs linear array detectors in the lower solar flux SWIR region, pixel size was set at $70.5\text{ m}$ (exactly three times the $23.5\text{ m}$ VNIR pixel size).
  • Practice Q2: What scanning technology did LISS-III utilize instead of the oscillating scan mirrors used by Landsat TM?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Pushbroom scanning (Linear Imaging Self-Scanning) using solid-state Charge-Coupled Device (CCD) arrays.
    Explanation: Pushbroom linear arrays image an entire cross-track line simultaneously as the satellite moves, achieving much longer dwell times per pixel and superior radiometric sensitivity.
  • Practice Q3: What upgrade did ISRO introduce in the LISS-IV sensor flown on Resourcesat-1 (2003) compared to LISS-III?
    ✔ Correct Answer: It delivered $5.8\text{ m}$ spatial resolution in three multispectral bands (Green, Red, NIR), whereas IRS-1D PAN was only panchromatic (monochrome) at $5.8\text{ m}$.
    Explanation: Resourcesat-1 was among the first satellites worldwide to achieve $< 6\text{ m}$ multi-band color imagery across a $23\text{ km}$ or $70\text{ km}$ swath.
  • Practice Q4: What was the off-nadir steering capability of the IRS-1D PAN camera, and what capability did it enable?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Up to $\pm 26^\circ$ across-track steering; enabled stereoscopic 3D terrain viewing and rapid revisit within 5 days.
    Explanation: An optical mirror rotated to view the same ground target from two consecutive orbits, generating stereo pairs for topographic elevation extraction.
  • Practice Q5: What is the revisit capability of the Wide Field Sensor (WiFS) on IRS-1D, and why was it chosen for national crop forecasting?
    ✔ Correct Answer: 5-day revisit period with an $810\text{ km}$ swath.
    Explanation: Frequent 5-day coverage ensured cloud-free composite imagery during critical phenological growth stages of India's kharif and rabi crops.
  • Practice Q6: What Indian ground station network receives, processes, and disseminates IRS-1D and Resourcesat satellite data?
    ✔ Correct Answer: NRSC (National Remote Sensing Centre), Shadnagar Earth Station near Hyderabad.
    Explanation: NRSC is the nodal agency under ISRO responsible for acquisition, processing, and archiving of all Indian Earth observation missions.
  • Practice Q7: How does the radiometric quantization of IRS-1D LISS-III (7-bit / 8-bit) compare with Landsat-8 OLI (12-bit)?
    ✔ Correct Answer: LISS-III quantizes signal into 128 (7-bit) or 256 (8-bit) gray levels; Landsat-8 OLI records 4,096 levels (12-bit scaled to 16-bit).
    Explanation: Higher bit depth prevents saturation over highly reflective targets and extracts subtle geological detail in steep shadow zones.
  • Practice Q8: What was the operational life of IRS-1D, exceeding its designed mission life of 3 years?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Over 10 years of active operational service (1997 to 2007+).
    Explanation: Excellent orbital insertion and onboard fuel management allowed IRS-1D to deliver valuable high-resolution imagery for more than a decade.
