Q17: Optical Remote Sensing: Soil Moisture Absorption & NIR Image Tonal Response

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman (Remote Sensing, Ch. 1, pp. 16-20); Sabins (Ch. 2)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

17.1 Spectral Reflectance Behavior of Water & Soil Moisture

In optical remote sensing and soil physics (Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman, Remote Sensing, Ch. 1, pp. 16–20; Sabins, Ch. 2), the spectral reflectance curve of bare soil is dramatically altered by the introduction of moisture:

Soil Moisture State Optical Mechanism & Water Absorption Tonal Appearance on NIR Imagery
Dry Bare Soil High surface scattering, minimal absorption; reflectance steadily increases from visible into NIR ($30 - 45\%$) Light / Bright / White tone
Moist / Wet Soil Water films coat soil particles, lowering refractive index contrast and causing multiple internal refractions; water strongly absorbs NIR radiation Dark / Grey to Black tone
Standing Open Water Near-total absorption of NIR wavelengths ($> 0.8\ \mu\text{m}$, reflectance $< 2-3\%$) Jet Black tone

17.2 The Physics of Water Film Refraction

When soil pores fill with water, the relative refractive index between mineral grains ($n_{\text{quartz}} \approx 1.54$) and the surrounding medium ($n_{\text{water}} \approx 1.33$) is much lower than grain-to-air ($n_{\text{air}} = 1.00$):

$$\frac{n_{\text{quartz}}}{n_{\text{water}}} = 1.16 \quad \text{vs.} \quad \frac{n_{\text{quartz}}}{n_{\text{air}}} = 1.54$$

This index matching increases the path length of light within the absorbing liquid, drastically reducing reflectance and darkening the image tone to grey.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q17. The moisture present in the ground surface is shown as _______ tone in the NIR region of imagery.
(A) white
(B) grey
(C) red
(D) blue
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) grey — Ground moisture strongly absorbs near-infrared (NIR) radiation, causing moist surfaces to appear in a dark grey tone on black-and-white NIR imagery (as opposed to the bright white tone of dry soil).

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) white — INCORRECT: White tone represents dry bare soil, salt flats, or snow with high NIR reflectance.
  • (B) grey — CORRECT: Water absorption lowers reflectance, producing medium-to-dark grey tones on NIR imagery.
  • (C) red — INCORRECT: On a standard False Color Composite (FCC), healthy vigorous vegetation appears red, not ground moisture.
  • (D) blue — INCORRECT: "Tone" in photographic interpretation strictly refers to the relative brightness or colour on a black-and-white photographic gray scale (white, light grey, grey, dark grey, black).

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

Remote Sensing Image Interpretation Practice

Practice Problem 17: Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI)

Write the mathematical formula for McFeeters' (1996) Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI) and explain how it delineates water-saturated zones from surrounding dry land.

Answer:

$$\text{NDWI} = \frac{\text{Green} - \text{NIR}}{\text{Green} + \text{NIR}}$$

Because water features have high green reflectance and near-zero NIR reflectance, water bodies and saturated soils yield positive NDWI values ($> 0$), while terrestrial dry soil and vegetation (with high NIR reflectance) yield negative values, creating sharp binary contrast.
