Q40: Landslide Morphology: Kinematics of Displaced Mass vs. Subjacent Bedrock

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: D.J. Varnes (1978, Landslides: Analysis and Control); Cruden & Varnes (1996); K.M. Bangar (Ch. 12)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

40.1 Varnes (1978) Morphological Anatomy of a Landslide

In geotechnical engineering and engineering geology (Cruden & Varnes, 1996; Varnes, 1978; Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology, Ch. 12), a landslide is defined as the downslope movement of a mass of rock, debris, or earth under the direct influence of gravity. The slope is partitioned by a distinct surface of rupture (shear plane) into two mechanically distinct masses:

Zonal Component Physical Description & Terminology Mechanical State & Factor of Safety ($FS$) Geotechnical Stability
Displaced Mass (Mass Above Slip Surface) The volume of rock or soil that has broken away from the parent slope and moved downslope along or beyond the surface of rupture. Shear stress exceeded shear strength ($\tau > \tau_f$); $FS < 1.0$. Highly fractured, remolded, dilation occurred; lost cohesive bond. UNSTABLE / ACTIVELY DEFORMING
Subjacent Mass (Mass Below Slip Surface) The intact, undisturbed rock or soil mass situated beneath the surface of rupture and forming the stable foundation/bedrock. Resisting shear strength intact; $\tau < \tau_f$; $FS > 1.0$. Preserves native confinement and structural integrity. STABLE / INTACT

40.2 Mohr-Coulomb Rupture Localization

The failure occurs along the surface of rupture when driving stresses overcome shear resistance:

$$\tau_f = c' + (\sigma_n - u) \tan \phi'$$

The mass above the rupture surface experiences loss of shear strength, catastrophic displacement, and extensive disaggregation, rendering it unstable. The mass below the surface of rupture remains fully anchored to the autochthonous geological formation, remaining completely stable.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q40. What is the distinguishing feature of the mass above and below a landslide ?
(A) Both masses are stable
(B) Mass above is stable, whereas mass below is unstable
(C) Mass above is unstable, whereas mass below is stable
(D) Both masses are unstable
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (C)

Correct Answer: (C) Mass above is unstable, whereas mass below is stable — In landslide kinematics, the displaced mass situated above the shear failure plane is mechanically unstable, while the underlying intact bedrock mass situated below the slip plane is stable.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Both masses are stable — INCORRECT: If both were stable, no landslide failure or downslope movement could ever take place.
  • (B) Mass above is stable, whereas mass below is unstable — INCORRECT: Complete inversion of physical reality; the displaced material that slides downslope is above the slip surface, not below it.
  • (C) Mass above is unstable, whereas mass below is stable — CORRECT: Precisely captures the mechanical demarcation defined by the basal shear rupture surface (Varnes 1978).
  • (D) Both masses are unstable — INCORRECT: The underlying deep bedrock below the shear plane is not in a state of sliding failure; it forms the stable substrate across which the slide moves.
