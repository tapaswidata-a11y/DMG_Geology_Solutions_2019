Q55: Proterozoic Stratigraphy - Vindhyan Supergroup Four-Fold Classification

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 314); P.K. Mukherjee (A Text Book of Geology, p. 202)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Regional Framework of the Vindhyan Basin

The Vindhyan Supergroup occupies an immense sickle-shaped intracratonic purana basin (covering over 104,000 $\mathrm{km^2}$) in central India, exposed across Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Bihar. Resting with profound unconformity upon the Archean-Paleoproterozoic basement (Bundelkhand Granite, Gwalior/Bijawar metasediments, and Mahakoshal greenstones), the Vindhyan represents one of the thickest (~4,000 to 5,500 m) and best-preserved Mesoproterozoic to Neoproterozoic sedimentary successions on Earth.

The basin is divided into two major geographic domains: the Son Valley sector in the east and the Rajasthan (Chittorgarh-Kota) sector in the west, separated by the Great Boundary Fault (GBF) of Rajasthan. Sedimentation spans from ~1,700 Ma (Semri basal beds) to ~650–600 Ma (Upper Bhander sandstones), recording repeated marine transgressions, deltaic progradations, and arid coastal-aeolian cycles.

2. The Four-Fold Stratigraphic Classification

In standard Indian stratigraphy (G.B. Mahapatra, A Text-Book of Geology, Chapter 83, p. 314 / PDF p. 165; P.K. Mukherjee, A Text Book of Geology, p. 202), the Vindhyan Supergroup is classically partitioned by a basin-wide regional unconformity into two major divisions and four distinct groups:

  1. Lower Vindhyan:
    • Semri Group (~1,700–1,600 Ma): Dominantly marine carbonates, porcellanites, shales, and glauconitic sandstones. Subdivided into the Mirzapur (Basal) subgroup, Porcellanite Formation (felsic volcaniclastic tuffs), Kheinjua Formation (olive shales and Fawn Limestone with exquisite columnar stromatolites), and Rohtas Formation (dense, fine-grained cement-grade limestones).
  2. Upper Vindhyan: Separated from the Semri Group by a regional erosional unconformity, characterized by siliciclastic dominance:
    • Kaimur Group: Massive, ridge-forming orthoquartzites, sandstones, and subordinate shales (Sasaram Sandstone, Bijaigarh Shale, Dhandraul Quartzite). Famous for ripple marks, mud cracks, and early multicellular carbonaceous metaphytes (Chuaria circularis).
    • Rewa Group: Alternating shales and sandstones (Panna Shale, Asan Sandstone, Jhiri Shale, Govindgarh Sandstone). It is world-renowned for hosting the diamondiferous diamond-bearing conglomerates at Panna, Madhya Pradesh, derived from kimberlitic/lamproitic diatremes (e.g., Majhgawan pipe).
    • Bhander Group: The uppermost and final group of the Vindhyan Supergroup. It represents a complete shallowing-upward marine-to-fluvial/aeolian red-bed megacycle, comprising the Ganurgarh Shale, Bhander Limestone (extensively quarried for Portland cement), Lower Bhander Sandstone, Sirbu Shale, and the uppermost Upper Bhander Sandstone (a celebrated dark-red building stone utilized in historical Indian monuments including the Red Fort and Sanchi Stupa).

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q55. The Vindhyan Supergroup is divisible into four groups viz., Semri Group, Kaimur Group, Rewa Group and
(A) Bijawar group
(B) Bhander group
(C) Gwalior group
(D) None of these

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "The Vindhyan Supergroup is divisible into four groups viz., Semri Group, Kaimur Group, Rewa Group and ________"

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Bijawar group (INCORRECT): The Bijawar Group represents a Paleoproterozoic (pre-Vindhyan) sequence of low-grade metasediments, ferruginous quartzites, dolomites, and basic lavas (~2.4–1.8 Ga) that forms part of the folded basement upon which the Vindhyan Supergroup was unconformably deposited.
  • (B) Bhander group (CORRECT): The Bhander Group is universally the fourth, uppermost group of the Vindhyan Supergroup. The established succession from base to top is: (1) Semri Group, (2) Kaimur Group, (3) Rewa Group, and (4) Bhander Group.
  • (C) Gwalior group (INCORRECT): The Gwalior Group (Par and Morar formations) is another Paleoproterozoic platform sequence occurring north of the Bundelkhand craton, unconformably overlain by the Kaimur sandstones of the Vindhyan Supergroup.
  • (D) None of these (INCORRECT): Because the Bhander Group is the exact, standard stratigraphic fourth group, selecting "None of these" is incorrect.
