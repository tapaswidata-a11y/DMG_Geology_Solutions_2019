1. The Quadripartite Architecture of the Vindhyan Supergroup

The Vindhyan Supergroup represents India's largest and most magnificent Mesoproterozoic to Neoproterozoic intracratonic sedimentary basin (spanning over $100,000\text{ km}^2$ across Central India). Established by J.B. Auden (1933) and reaffirmed by the Geological Survey of India, the Supergroup is universally divided into two primary sub-divisions and four canonical groups (in ascending chronological order):

  • Lower Vindhyan:
    • 1. Semri Group: Basal marine carbonate-volcaniclastic sequence ($\sim 1700-1600\text{ Ma}$). Comprises the Kajrahat Limestone, Porcellanite Formation (acid tuffs), Olive Shales, Fawn Limestone, and Rohtas Limestone. Rich in columnar stromatolites (Collenia, Kussiella) and microfossils.
  • Upper Vindhyan: Dominated by mature clastics, separated from the Semri Group by a regional disconformity:
    • 2. Kaimur Group: Orthoquartzites, quartz arenites, and conglomerates (Dhandraul Quartzite, Scarp Sandstone, Bijaigarh Shale) recording high-energy littoral barrier regimes ($\sim 1150-1000\text{ Ma}$).
    • 3. Rewa Group: Alternating shales and sandstones (Panna Shale, Asan Sandstone, Jhiri Shale, Govindgarh Sandstone). Famous for diamondiferous basal conglomerates derived from the Majhgawan pipe.
    • 4. Bhander Group: The uppermost, thickest, and most widely exposed group ($\sim 900-550\text{ Ma}$). Marks the return of extensive marine carbonate and tidal flat-sabkha sedimentation.

2. Detailed Lithostratigraphy of the Uppermost Bhander Group

The Bhander Group terminates the Proterozoic sedimentary history of the Great Vindhyan Basin. It is divided into four constituent formations (in ascending order):

The Ascending Formations of the Bhander Group
$$\mathbf{Ganurgarh \text{ Shale}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Bhander \text{ Limestone}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Sirbu \text{ Shale}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Upper \text{ Bhander Sandstone}}$$
  • Ganurgarh Shale: Reddish-brown, thinly bedded calcareous shales with interbedded siltstones, desiccation cracks, and halite casts indicating tidal flat muds.
  • Bhander Limestone: High-grade calcitic to dolomitic limestone ($50-100\text{ m}$ thick) exhibiting spectacular domal and columnar stromatolites (Baicalia, Maiharia). Forms the premier raw material base for Central India's portland cement industry.
  • Sirbu Shale: Olive-green to purple gypsiferous shales and fine sandstones with abundant ripple marks, mudcracks, and hopper crystal pseudomorphs.
  • Upper Bhander Sandstone: Dark red to pinkish-buff, fine-to-medium grained quartz arenite displaying large-scale tabular cross-bedding and planar lamination, quarried across Rajasthan and MP for world-famous architectural monument stone.

Official Examination Question
Question 55
The Vindhyan Supergroup is divisible into four groups viz., Semri Group, Kaimur Group, Rewa Group and
(A) Bijawar group
(B) Bhander group
(C) Gwalior group
(D) None of these
Verified Correct Answer: (B) Bhander group [The four constituent groups of the Vindhyan Supergroup in ascending chronological order are Semri Group, Kaimur Group, Rewa Group, and Bhander Group.]
Correct Option: (B) Bhander group

Detailed Stratigraphic Forensic Analysis:

  • The Vindhyan Supergroup of Central India is universally subdivided across all Indian geological treatises (Auden 1933; Krishnan 1982; Ramakrishnan & Vaidyanadhan 2008; Mahapatra 2019; Mukherjee 2013) into exactly four major lithostratigraphic groups:
    1. Semri Group (Lower Vindhyan)
    2. Kaimur Group (Upper Vindhyan)
    3. Rewa Group (Upper Vindhyan)
    4. Bhander Group (Upper Vindhyan)
  • The Bhander Group represents the fourth, uppermost, and youngest member of this classic quadripartite succession, celebrated for its high-grade cement limestones (Bhander Limestone) and building stones (Upper Bhander Sandstone).
  • Thus, Option (B) Bhander group is the indisputably correct answer.
Incorrect Distractor: (A) Bijawar group

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Bijawar Group is a deformed Paleoproterozoic sequence of quartzites, dolomites, ferruginous beds, and basic lavas that forms the unconformable floor (basement) beneath the Vindhyan Supergroup.
  • The Semri Group rests with angular unconformity upon the Bijawar rocks; hence, Bijawar is an older basement unit, not a constituent of the Vindhyan Supergroup.
Incorrect Distractor: (C) Gwalior group

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Gwalior Group (Par and Morar Formations) is another Paleoproterozoic sedimentary-volcanic succession exposed around Gwalior in northern Madhya Pradesh.
  • It unconformably underlies the Kaimur Group of the Vindhyan Supergroup, making it an older basement series rather than a member of the Vindhyan Supergroup.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) None of these

Detailed Refutation:

  • Because Option (B) Bhander group is the exact canonical fourth group, Option (D) is incorrect.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Vindhyan Stratigraphy & Sedimentary Petrology
Problem 1: Stromatolite Biostratigraphy of Bhander Limestone

Which branching columnar stromatolite taxon characterizes the Neoproterozoic Bhander Limestone and correlates it with the Riphean of Russia?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Bhander Limestone is characterized by the branching columnar stromatolite Baicalia baicalica and Maiharia maiharensis. In Riphean biostratigraphy, Baicalia is the diagnostic index form for the Middle to Upper Riphean ($\sim 1000-700\text{ Ma}$), establishing global biostratigraphic correlation for the Bhander Group.

Problem 2: Halite Pseudomorphs as Evaporite Indicators

Explain the step-by-step mechanism of hopper-crystal halite pseudomorph formation in the Sirbu Shale.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Under hyper-saline, arid conditions on supratidal sabkha mudflats, brine evaporates, precipitating skeletal cubic "hopper" crystals of halite ($\text{NaCl}$) within the soft mud.
  2. During episodic marine flooding or rainstorms, less saline water dissolves the soluble halite crystal, leaving a hollow cubic mold in the mud.
  3. Inflowing silt or mud deposits immediately fill the cavity, casting a perfect cubic pseudomorph preserved on shale bedding planes.
Problem 3: Architectural Durability of the Upper Bhander Sandstone

Why has the Upper Bhander Sandstone survived centuries of environmental exposure in Mughal monuments (e.g., Red Fort, Fatehpur Sikri) without crumbling?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Upper Bhander Sandstone is a super-mature quartz arenite consisting of $>95\%$ monocrystalline quartz grains bonded by syntaxial silica cement in optical continuity with the host grains (secondary quartz overgrowths), with a microscopic coating of hematite providing the warm red color. The absence of unstable feldspars and soluble carbonate cements renders it virtually impervious to chemical weathering and acid rain.

Problem 4: The Great Eparchean Hiatus at the Base of the Semri

The basal conglomerate of the Semri Group rests upon the Bundelkhand Granite ($\sim 2500\text{ Ma}$). What is the duration of the missing geological time interval represented by this unconformity if the Semri initiated at $\sim 1700\text{ Ma}$?

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$\text{Hiatus} = 2500\text{ Ma} - 1700\text{ Ma} = 800\text{ Ma}$$

Final Answer: The basal nonconformity represents an enormous erosional hiatus of approximately $800\text{ million years}$.

Problem 5: Carbon Isotope Excursion ($\delta^{13}\text{C}$) in the Bhander Limestone

Modern chemostratigraphy shows that the Bhander Limestone exhibits strongly positive carbon isotope ratios ($\delta^{13}\text{C} \approx +4.0\text{ to } +6.0\text{ \textperthousand}$). What global biogeochemical condition does this indicate?

Step-by-Step Solution:

High positive $\delta^{13}\text{C}_{\text{carb}}$ values indicate high global rates of organic carbon burial in anoxic sediments (preferentially sequestering light $^{12}\text{C}$ into buried biomass, leaving the ocean reservoir enriched in heavy $^{13}\text{C}$). This massive burial of organic carbon corresponds to the Neoproterozoic oxygenation event preceding the radiation of multicellular life.

Problem 6: Origin of Porcellanite in the Semri Group

What is the petrogenetic origin of the banded "porcellanite" beds in the Semri Group?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Porcellanites are dense, chert-like, microcrystalline silicified rocks that formed by the devitrification and diagenetic alteration of airborne felsic volcanic ash and rhyolitic tuffs deposited in a shallow marine basin during syn-sedimentary Paleoproterozoic volcanism.

Problem 7: Great Boundary Fault Juxtaposition

Along the northwestern margin of the Vindhyan basin in Rajasthan, which specific Vindhyan group is brought into direct thrust contact against pre-Vindhyan schists by the Great Boundary Fault?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Bhander Group (specifically the Upper Bhander Sandstone and Sirbu Shale) is folded into steep marginal synclines and faulted directly against the Aravalli-Bhilwara metamorphic basement by the Great Boundary Fault.

Problem 8: Modern Terminal Age of the Vindhyans

Recent discoveries of Ediacaran trace fossils and discs in the Bhander Group place the terminal sedimentation close to which global chronostratigraphic boundary?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The uppermost Bhander Group extends to approximately $540-550\text{ Ma}$, placing the cessation of Vindhyan sedimentation immediately at the Ediacaran-Cambrian boundary (the Precambrian-Cambrian transition).
