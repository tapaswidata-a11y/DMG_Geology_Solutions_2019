Q52: Petroleum Geology of Assam - Digboi Oilfield & Tipam Group Reservoir

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: K.M. Bangar (Principles of Engineering Geology, p. 340, Fig. 9.17); G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 340)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Tectonic Architecture of the Assam-Arakan Basin & Belt of Schuppen

The Assam-Arakan Basin in Northeastern India represents a complex polyhistory petroleum province situated at the tectonic junction of the active Himalayan collision zone to the north and the Indo-Burman subduction orogen to the east. Petroleum exploration in India was born in this region with the discovery of the historic Digboi oilfield in 1889 (Well No. 1 completed in 1890), making it the oldest continuously producing commercial oilfield in Asia.

Structurally, Digboi is situated within the Belt of Schuppen—a classic imbricate thrust belt consisting of multiple southeast-dipping, northwest-directed low-to-moderate angle thrust sheets. The most prominent regional discontinuity is the Naga Thrust, along which Upper Assam Shelf Tertiary sedimentary wedges were overridden by geosynclinal clastics of the Schuppen belt during Neogene-Quaternary Indo-Eurasian plate convergence.

2. Structural Style: The Digboi Asymmetric Anticline

The Digboi oil accumulation is classic structural trap formed by a sharply folded, elongate, east-northeast to west-southwest (ENE–WSW) trending asymmetric, steeply dipping anticline (K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology, Chapter 9, Fig. 9.17 & p. 340). Key structural parameters include:

  • Axial Length & Trend: Extends approximately 13 to 15 km in length with an average width of 1 to 1.5 km, trending parallel to the structural grain of the Naga Hills.
  • Asymmetric Flank Dips: The southern limb dips gently (around 20° to 35° SE), whereas the northern limb is steeply tilted to near-vertical (60° to 85° NW) and locally overturned.
  • Fault Disruption: The steep northern limb is severely disrupted and truncated by the regional Digboi Thrust (a frontal branch of the Naga Thrust system), forming a combined fault-propagation anticline and structural seal.

3. Stratigraphy & Reservoir Horizons: The Tipam Group

In the standard lithostratigraphic classification of Upper Assam (G.B. Mahapatra, A Text-Book of Geology, Chapter 90, p. 340 / PDF p. 178), the Cenozoic succession is partitioned into three major groups:

  1. Jaintia Group (Paleocene–Eocene): Basal marine/shelf carbonaceous shales (Disang), nummulitic limestones (Sylhet Limestone), and splintery shales (Kopili Formation).
  2. Barail Group (Oligocene): Deltaic to shallow marine coal-shale-sandstone sequence (Naogaon, Baragolai, and Tikak Parbat Formations). The Barails act as the primary petroleum source rocks for the entire basin and provide the prolific reservoirs for the deeper alluvial plains fields (Nahorkatiya, Moran, Lakwa).
  3. Tipam Group (Early to Middle Miocene): Deposited in massive, high-energy fluvial, braided-river regimes during rapid Himalayan-Burman orogenic uplift. It is divided into two distinct formations:
    • Tipam Sandstone Formation: Composed of coarse-to-medium grained, ferruginous, salt-and-pepper sandstones interbedded with subordinate clays. This formation hosts all major oil-producing reservoir sands (Bapu Sand, Digboi Main Sand, Lower Tipam Sands) of the Digboi oilfield!
    • Girujan Clay Formation: Composed of thick, mottled, impervious blue-gray lacustrine/floodplain clays that provide the regional, impermeable cap rock (top seal) preventing hydrocarbons from escaping to the surface.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q52. The Digboi oil field of Assam is on a faulted elongate anticline in rocks of the.
(A) Tipam Group
(B) Barail Group
(C) Jaintia Group
(D) All of these

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "The Digboi oil field of Assam is on a faulted elongate anticline in rocks of the ________"

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Tipam Group (CORRECT): The oil reservoirs of the historic Digboi field occur almost exclusively within the Miocene Tipam Sandstone Formation of the Tipam Group. The oil-bearing multi-layered sands are trapped within the crest and gentle flank of the faulted Digboi anticline beneath the sealing Girujan Clays of the same Tipam Group. Verbatim textbook authority: G.B. Mahapatra, p. 340 states: "the lower Tipam sandstones are oil-bearing and the oil fields at Digboi are of Tipam sandstone age."
  • (B) Barail Group (INCORRECT): Although the Oligocene Barail Group (specifically the Tikak Parbat Formation) is the dominant petroleum reservoir in the Brahmaputra Valley shelf fields (such as Nahorkatiya, Moran, and Rudrasagar), at the surface-breached Digboi structure, production is overwhelmingly from the younger, overlying Tipam Sandstones.
  • (C) Jaintia Group (INCORRECT): The Jaintia Group consists of Eocene marine limestones and shales (Kopili and Sylhet Formations). They lie thousands of meters stratigraphically below the Tipam Group in Upper Assam and do not form the reservoir rock of the Digboi anticline.
  • (D) All of these (INCORRECT): Because the reservoir and outcropping anticlinal fold at Digboi are specifically confined to the Tipam Group, selecting "All of these" is a classic exam trap that confuses regional basin source rocks with the specific reservoir unit of Digboi.

2. Structural Mechanics of the Digboi Trap

  • Anticlinal Fold Mechanism: Fault-propagation folding induced by horizontal compressive stress generated during the collision of the Indian Plate with the Myanmar (Burma) Microplate.
  • Petroleum Migration: Hydrocarbons generated within mature, deeply buried Oligocene Barail coal-shale source facies migrated vertically up fracture networks and fault conduits into the porous, permeable Tipam Sandstone reservoir beds.
  • Sealing Efficiency: Lateral structural closure is provided by the sealing gouge of the Naga/Digboi thrust fault to the north, while vertical retention is guaranteed by the thick, ductile Girujan Clays.
