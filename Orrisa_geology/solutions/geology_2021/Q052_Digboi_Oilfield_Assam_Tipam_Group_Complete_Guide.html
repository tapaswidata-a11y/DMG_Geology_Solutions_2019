1. The Historic Digboi Oilfield: Birthplace of the Indian Petroleum Industry

The Digboi Oilfield in the Tinsukia district of Upper Assam is the oldest operating oil field in India and Asia, discovered in 1889 following the drilling of Well No. 1 by the Assam Railways and Trading Company (later Assam Oil Company):

  • Structural Geology of the Trap: The oilfield is situated on a prominent, narrow, sharply folded, asymmetric anticlinal structure termed the Digboi Anticline:
    • Trend and Symmetry: Trends approximately ENE–WSW ($060^\circ-240^\circ$) for over $14\text{ km}$, plunging towards the west-southwest. Its northern flank is extremely steep ($70^\circ-80^\circ$ to near vertical) and truncated by a regional low-angle reverse fault known as the Naga Thrust. The southern flank dips much more gently ($30^\circ-40^\circ$).
    • Trap Type: A classic faulted asymmetric anticlinal trap (combination structural trap).
  • Reservoir Lithology: The commercial hydrocarbon reservoirs occur in multi-layered, porous, deltaic-to-fluvial sandstone beds belonging to the Tipam Group (specifically the Middle Miocene Tipam Sandstone Formation).
  • Petroleum System:
    • Source Rock: Deeply buried carbonaceous shales and coals of the Oligocene Barail Group (Tikak Parbat Formation) that reached thermal maturity in the adjacent foreland trough.
    • Reservoir Rock: Porous, permeable sandstones of the Miocene Tipam Group (Bappapung Sandstone / Digboi Oil Sands).
    • Cap Rock (Seal): Overlying impermeable silts and shales of the Girujan Clay Formation (upper Tipam Group).

2. Structural Geology of Anticlinal Petroleum Traps

The anticlinal theory of petroleum accumulation, first formulated by T. Sterry Hunt and I.C. White, is classically exemplified by the Digboi structure:

Hydrocarbon Buoyancy Secondary Migration in an Anticline
$$F_{\text{buoyancy}} = (\rho_{\text{water}} - \rho_{\text{oil}}) g \cdot V > P_{\text{capillary entry}} = \frac{2\gamma \cos\theta}{r_{\text{pore}}}$$

Because oil ($\rho \sim 850\text{ kg/m}^3$) and gas ($\rho \sim 150\text{ kg/m}^3$) are lighter than formation connate brine ($\rho \sim 1050\text{ kg/m}^3$), buoyancy drives hydrocarbons upward into the anticlinal crest until trapped beneath the impermeable Girujan Clay roof.

Figure 52.2 (K.M. Bangar, Fig. 9.17): Cross-section of an anticlinal oil trap demonstrating gravitational segregation: Gas at crest, Oil in intermediate flanks, and Formation Water at the base.

Official Examination Question
Question 52
The Digboi oil field of Assam is on a faulted elongate anticline in rocks of the.
(A) Tipam Group
(B) Barail Group
(C) Jaintia Group
(D) All of these
Verified Correct Answer: (A) Tipam Group [The producing structural trap of the Digboi oilfield is developed in porous sandstones of the Miocene Tipam Group within the faulted Digboi anticline.]
Correct Option: (A) Tipam Group

Detailed Petroleum Geological Forensic Analysis:

  • The Digboi oilfield (Upper Assam) is developed along an elongated, sharply folded, asymmetric anticlinal structure whose northern flank is truncated by the regional Naga Thrust.
  • The hydrocarbon reservoirs at Digboi are hosted in the porous and permeable deltaic sandstones of the Tipam Group (specifically the Middle Miocene Tipam Sandstone Formation, comprising the Bappapung and Digboi oil sands), capped by the impervious Girujan Clays.
  • Over 24 distinct oil-bearing sand horizons are recognized within the Tipam Group in the Digboi field.
  • Therefore, Option (A) Tipam Group is the precise and correct answer.
Incorrect Distractor: (B) Barail Group

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Barail Group (Oligocene) is famous as the principal source rock for the entire Assam-Arakan petroleum province (via the coal-shale beds of the Tikak Parbat Formation) and serves as the main reservoir in deeper plains fields such as Nahorkatiya, Moran, and Rudrasagar.
  • However, at the Digboi field itself, commercial production has historically been won from the shallower Miocene Tipam Group sandstones.
Incorrect Distractor: (C) Jaintia Group

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Jaintia Group (Eocene) comprises the Sylhet Limestone and Kopili Formation. It forms the deep carbonate reservoir sequence in the Borholla-Champang fields and south of the Shillong Plateau.
  • It is buried thousands of meters beneath the Digboi anticline and does not constitute the producing reservoir formation of the Digboi oilfield.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) All of these

Detailed Refutation:

  • Because the specific faulted anticlinal reservoir of the Digboi field is specifically and classically defined in the Tipam Group, selecting "All of these" is incorrect.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Petroleum Systems & Basin Analysis
Problem 1: Oil-Water Contact and Capillary Entry Pressure

In an anticlinal reservoir, the water density is $\rho_w = 1050\text{ kg/m}^3$ and oil density is $\rho_o = 850\text{ kg/m}^3$. Calculate the buoyancy overpressure gradient ($\Delta \nabla P$) in $\text{kPa/m}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$\Delta \nabla P = (\rho_w - \rho_o) g = (1050 - 850)\text{ kg/m}^3 \times 9.81\text{ m/s}^2 = 200 \times 9.81 = 1962\text{ Pa/m} = 1.962\text{ kPa/m}$$

Final Answer: The buoyancy drive is $1.96\text{ kPa}$ per meter of oil column height.

Problem 2: API Gravity Calculation for Digboi Crude

Digboi crude oil has a measured specific gravity $SG = 0.840$ at $60^\circ\text{F}$. Calculate its API Gravity ($^\circ\text{API}$) and classify its crude weight.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$^\circ\text{API} = \frac{141.5}{SG} - 131.5 = \frac{141.5}{0.840} - 131.5 = 168.45 - 131.5 = 36.95^\circ\text{API}$$

Final Answer: The crude oil has an API gravity of $37.0^\circ\text{API}$, classifying it as high-value light sweet crude (rich in paraffin and lubricating oils).

Problem 3: Hydrocarbon Column Capacity of an Anticlinal Spill Point

The crest of the Tipam reservoir in an anticline is at a subsea depth of $-1200\text{ m}$ and the structural spill point is at $-1350\text{ m}$. Calculate the maximum vertical column height of oil ($h_{\max}$) that this structure can retain before spilling.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$h_{\max} = \text{Depth}_{\text{spill}} - \text{Depth}_{\text{crest}} = 1350\text{ m} - 1200\text{ m} = 150\text{ m}$$

Final Answer: The maximum structural oil column height is $150\text{ m}$.

Problem 4: Role of the Naga Thrust in Digboi Trapping

Explain how the regional Naga Thrust functions both as a trapping fault and as a potential leakage hazard at Digboi.

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Naga Thrust juxtaposes impermeable clayey Tipam and Barail rocks across fault planes, providing a lateral structural barrier (fault seal) that traps oil in the northern limb. However, where the fault breaches the surface, active seepage of oil occurs (the famous Digboi oil seepages discovered by elephants in 1867), demonstrating that the fault also acts as a dynamic migration conduit.

Problem 5: Kerogen Type in the Barail Group

Which type of kerogen dominates the coal-shale sequences of the Oligocene Tikak Parbat Formation (Barail Group)?

Step-by-Step Solution:

It is dominated by Type-III Kerogen (derived from terrestrial higher plants, rich in vitrinite) with subordinate Type-II Kerogen (resinite, cutinite, and exinite). This unique hydrogen-rich coaly kerogen generates both waxy paraffinic crude oil and rich natural gas upon thermal maturation.

Problem 6: Porosity and Permeability of Tipam Sandstone

A core plug of Tipam Sandstone has bulk volume $V_b = 50\text{ cm}^3$ and dry grain volume $V_g = 39\text{ cm}^3$. Calculate its effective porosity ($\phi$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$\phi = \frac{V_b - V_g}{V_b} \times 100\% = \frac{50 - 39}{50} \times 100\% = \frac{11}{50} \times 100\% = 22.0\%$$

Final Answer: The porosity is $22\%$, representing an excellent quality commercial petroleum reservoir.

Problem 7: Structural Belt of Schuppen

In which regional tectonic zone of the Assam-Arakan Basin is the Digboi oilfield located?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Digboi is situated in the Belt of Schuppen—a narrow, $20-40\text{ km}$ wide imbricate thrust belt located along the southeastern margin of the Upper Assam Valley where multiple southeast-dipping reverse thrust faults (Naga, Disang, Haflong thrusts) telescope Tertiary strata over the Brahmaputra foreland.

Problem 8: Darcy's Law for Flow in a Tipam Reservoir Sand

Oil of viscosity $\mu = 2.5\text{ cP}$ flows through a Tipam sand block of length $L = 100\text{ m}$, area $A = 20\text{ m}^2$, permeability $k = 150\text{ mD}$ under a pressure differential $\Delta P = 5.0\text{ atm}$. Calculate the flow rate $Q$ in $\text{cm}^3/\text{s}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Using Darcy's Law in practical oilfield units ($Q = \frac{k A \Delta P}{\mu L}$ with $k$ in Darcies, $A$ in $\text{cm}^2$, $P$ in atm, $\mu$ in cP, $L$ in cm):

$$k = 0.150\text{ D}, \quad A = 20\text{ m}^2 = 2.0 \times 10^5\text{ cm}^2, \quad L = 100\text{ m} = 1.0 \times 10^4\text{ cm}$$ $$Q = \frac{0.150 \times (2.0 \times 10^5) \times 5.0}{2.5 \times (1.0 \times 10^4)} = \frac{1.5 \times 10^5}{2.5 \times 10^4} = 6.0\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$$

Final Answer: The flow rate is $6.0\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$.
