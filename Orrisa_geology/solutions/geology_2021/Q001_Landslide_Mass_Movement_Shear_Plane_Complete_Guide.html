Q001: Slope Stability, Mass Movement vs. Landslide & Shear Plane Mechanics

Official Exam: OPSC Assistant Geologist Paper-II (2021), Question 1
Authoritative Reference: K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Ch. 10 & 12, pp. 377–380); D.J. Varnes (1978, TRB Special Report 176)
PART I
Theoretical Foundation & Engineering Geology Principles
Downslope mass displacement, shear plane localization, Mohr-Coulomb failure criteria, and kinematic slope stability
1. The Critical Demarcation: Mass Movement vs. True Landslides

In physical geology and geotechnical engineering, the downslope displacement of rock, regolith, and soil under gravitational body forces is collectively designated as mass movement (or mass wasting). However, engineering geology and slope mechanics establish a fundamental physical demarcation based on whether movement occurs by diffuse internal deformation or is localized along a discrete, well-defined shear failure plane (Varnes, 1978; Cruden & Varnes, 1996; Bangar, Chapter 10).

"Where a mass of earth or rock slides down the slope along a definite zone or surface, the movement is called a landslide... A slope fails primarily by shear and the slip surface is approximately cylindrical or spoon shaped. The movement of the mass starts by cracking along a shearing surface and then the separated mass slides down rapidly." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Standard University Edition), Chapter 10, Section 10.9.2, p. 378

In contrast, when earth materials deform without a discrete basal shear surface, the movement falls into other kinematic categories such as earthflows or soil creep:

"In earthflows the movements are distributed through the mass and the well-defined slip surface which is characteristic of landslides, does not occur." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology, Section 10.9.1, p. 377
2. Quantitative Mohr-Coulomb Failure Criteria & Factor of Safety ($FS$)

A landslide is mechanically triggered when the gravitational driving shear stress along a potential failure surface exceeds the shear strength of the geomaterial. The available shear strength ($\tau_f$) is governed by the Terzaghi Effective Stress Mohr-Coulomb criterion:

Mohr-Coulomb Shear Strength Equation
$$\tau_f = c' + \sigma_n' \tan \phi' = c' + (\sigma_n - u) \tan \phi'$$

Where:

  • $c'$ = Effective cohesive intercept of the rock or soil mass ($\text{kPa}$ or $\text{MPa}$).
  • $\sigma_n$ = Total normal stress acting perpendicular to the failure plane ($\sigma_n = \gamma \cdot z \cdot \cos^2 heta$).
  • $u$ = Pore water pressure ($\text{kPa}$), which reduces effective confining stress ($\sigma_n' = \sigma_n - u$).
  • $\phi'$ = Effective angle of internal friction ($\text{degrees}$).

The stability of any slope is quantitatively evaluated via the Factor of Safety ($FS$):

$$FS = \frac{\text{Resisting Shear Strength } (\tau_f)}{\text{Driving Shear Stress } (\tau_d)} = \frac{c' + (\sigma_n - u) \tan \phi'}{\tau_d}$$

When $FS > 1.0$, the slope is stable; when $FS = 1.0$, limit equilibrium exists; and when $FS < 1.0$, catastrophic shear failure initiates along the rupture surface.

Official Question Statement — OPSC 2021 Geology Paper-II (Question 1)
Q1. The generic term describing those downward movements of slope-forming material as a result of shear failure occurring along a well-defined shear plane is :
(A) Mass movement
(B) Landslide  ✔
(C) Creep
(D) Avalanche
✔
Correct Answer: (B) Landslide
In international engineering geology (Varnes 1978, IAEG, Cruden & Varnes 1996) and standard university syllabi (K.M. Bangar, p. 378), downward movement of slope-forming material resulting from shear failure localized along a well-defined shear plane is strictly and generically termed a Landslide.
(A) Mass movement Incorrect (Broad Umbrella Term)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Mass movement (or mass wasting) is an all-inclusive super-category covering all gravity-driven downslope movements—including rockfalls, topples, debris flows, mudflows, solifluction, and soil creep. Many of these movements (specifically flows, creep, and falls) do not possess a well-defined shear plane. In mudflows and earthflows, shear strain is continuously distributed throughout the fluid-saturated mass; in falls, the material moves through free-fall in the air. Thus, mass movement lacks the diagnostic criterion of failure along a discrete shear plane.
(B) Landslide Correct Verified Answer
Why this option is correct: The presence of a discrete, recognizable shear surface (rupture surface) is the singular defining criterion that demarcates a landslide from all other forms of mass wasting. Whether the failure surface is circular/cylindrical (rotational slump in homogeneous clays) or planar (translational slide along bedding/fault surfaces), displacement is accommodated by shear displacement across a distinct boundary separating the moving block from the stationary parent rock.
(C) Creep Incorrect (Diffuse Viscous Strain)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Creep is defined as the exceptionally slow, steady, imperceptible continuous downslope movement of soil, regolith, or weathered mantle (typically millimeters to centimeters per year). Mechanically, creep operates via cyclic freeze-thaw, thermal expansion-contraction, and distributed viscous shear strain ($\dot{\gamma} = \frac{\tau}{\eta}$). It never possesses a distinct shear rupture plane; fence lines, tree trunks, and telephone poles tilt gradually because the entire upper column suffers distributed deformation.
(D) Avalanche Incorrect (Airborne Turbulent Flow)
Why this distractor is incorrect: An avalanche (whether snow, rock, or debris avalanche) is a catastrophic, high-velocity turbulent flow where particles are pulverized and supported by trapped air cushions or dynamic granular dispersion. The kinematic mechanics represent turbulent fluid flow rather than rigid or semi-rigid sliding along a stationary shear plane.

High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Questions)
  • Practice Q1 (Numerical): A planar rockslide is threatened along a joint dipping at $\alpha = 30^\circ$ into a valley. The rock block has a total weight $W = 500\text{ kN}$ and basal contact area $A = 20\text{ m}^2$. The joint properties are $c' = 10\text{ kPa}$ and $\phi' = 28^\circ$. If water pressure is negligible, calculate the Factor of Safety ($FS$) against sliding.
    ✔ Worked Answer: $FS = 1.01$ (Limit Equilibrium / Imminent Failure)
    Detailed Proof:
    Normal force $N = W \cos \alpha = 500 \cos 30^\circ = 433.01\text{ kN}$.
    Normal stress $\sigma_n = N / A = 433.01 / 20 = 21.65\text{ kPa}$.
    Driving shear force $T = W \sin \alpha = 500 \sin 30^\circ = 250\text{ kN}$.
    Driving shear stress $\tau_d = T / A = 250 / 20 = 12.50\text{ kPa}$.
    Resisting shear strength $\tau_f = c' + \sigma_n \tan \phi' = 10 + 21.65 \tan 28^\circ = 10 + 21.65(0.5317) = 10 + 11.51 = 21.51\text{ kPa}$.
    Resisting force $R = \tau_f \cdot A = 21.51 \times 20 = 430.2\text{ kN}$.
    $$FS = \frac{R}{T} = \frac{c' A + W \cos \alpha \tan \phi'}{W \sin \alpha} = \frac{10(20) + 433.01(0.5317)}{250} = \frac{200 + 230.23}{250} = \frac{430.23}{250} \approx 1.72$$ Note: If cohesive bond is destroyed ($c'=0$), $FS = \frac{\tan \phi'}{\tan \alpha} = \frac{\tan 28^\circ}{\tan 30^\circ} = \frac{0.5317}{0.5774} = 0.92$ (Catastrophic collapse occurs).
  • Practice Q2: What is the primary kinematic difference between a rotational landslide (slump) and a translational landslide?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Geometry of the failure plane (Curved concave-upward vs. planar structural discontinuity).
    Explanation: In a rotational slump, the slip surface is spoon-shaped (concave upward), producing a backward rotation of the displaced block toward the scarp. In a translational slide, the slip surface is planar or undulating, often guided by structural weaknesses (bedding, joints, foliation), and the block moves parallel to the plane without rotational tilting.
  • Practice Q3: How does an increase in pore water pressure ($u$) trigger slope failure according to Terzaghi's effective stress principle?
    ✔ Correct Answer: By reducing effective normal stress ($\sigma_n' = \sigma_n - u$), which directly decreases frictional resistance ($\tau_f$).
    Explanation: Water pressure exerts an outward buoyant pore pressure that pushes geomaterial grains apart. Since frictional resistance is proportional to grain-to-grain contact force ($\sigma_n' \tan \phi'$), increasing $u$ reduces $\sigma_n'$ without changing the gravitational driving stress $\tau_d$, thereby driving $FS < 1.0$.
  • Practice Q4: In a dry, cohesionless granular material (e.g., dry clean sand), what governs the maximum stable slope angle?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The angle of internal friction of the material ($\alpha_{max} = \phi'$), universally termed the angle of repose.
    Explanation: For $c'=0$ and $u=0$, $FS = \frac{\tan \phi'}{\tan \alpha}$. The slope remains stable ($FS \ge 1.0$) as long as the slope inclination $\alpha \le \phi'$. The angle $\phi'$ defines the natural angle of repose.
  • Practice Q5: In the Varnes (1978) classification of slope movements, what two primary parameters form the orthogonal classification axes?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Type of movement (fall, topple, slide, spread, flow) and Type of material (bedrock vs. engineering soil/debris/earth).
    Explanation: The Varnes matrix establishes 5 kinematic movement mechanisms along the horizontal axis and 2 primary geomaterial types (Rock and Soil, subdivided into coarse Debris and fine Earth) along the vertical axis.
  • Practice Q6: What engineering mitigation technique addresses the root cause of rainfall-induced translational landslides along planar joints?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Horizontal sub-surface drainage holes and toe drainage to dissipate pore water pressure ($u$).
    Explanation: Drainage lowers the phreatic surface, dissipating $u$ and restoring effective normal stress ($\sigma_n'$). Dewatering is universally recognized in geotechnical engineering as the most cost-effective stabilization method.
  • Practice Q7: Curved, pistol-butted tree trunks (J-shaped trunks) and tilted fence posts on a grassy hillside are classic field diagnostics of which process?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Soil creep (continuous creep).
    Explanation: Because soil creep is fastest at the ground surface and decreases with depth, the root base moves slower than the upper trunk. Trees continually attempt to grow phototropically vertically, creating curved trunks.
  • Practice Q8: Why is day-lighting of bedding planes in a road-cut slope considered the most dangerous structural condition in civil excavations?
    ✔ Correct Answer: It removes kinematic lateral support at the toe, allowing the block to slide unhindered along the bedding plane into the cut.
    Explanation: When bedding planes dip in the same direction as the hill slope at an angle less than the cut slope ($\alpha_{bed} < \alpha_{cut}$), the plane "daylights" into the open cut. Frictional resistance alone must hold the entire weight; if $\alpha_{bed} > \phi'$, instantaneous planar block sliding occurs.
