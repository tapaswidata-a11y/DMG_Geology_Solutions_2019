1. Cartographic Scale & The Representative Fraction (RF)

In geological cartography, civil engineering, and surveying, the Map Scale is defined as the fixed ratio between the distance measured between any two points on a map ($D_{\text{map}}$) and the corresponding true horizontal distance between those same two points on the ground ($D_{\text{ground}}$):

Fundamental Map Scale Equation
$$\text{Map Scale} = \frac{\text{Distance on Map } (D_{\text{map}})}{\text{True Distance on Ground } (D_{\text{ground}})}$$

Scale is expressed in three standard formats:

  • 1. Representative Fraction (RF): A unitless ratio where the numerator is always unity ($1$). In a $1 : 25,000$ map, $1\text{ unit on the map}$ represents $25,000\text{ identical units on the ground}$ (e.g., $1\text{ cm} = 25,000\text{ cm}$, or $1\text{ inch} = 25,000\text{ inches}$).
  • 2. Verbal Scale: An engineering statement equating map units to convenient ground units (e.g., "$1\text{ cm to } 250\text{ meters}$").
  • 3. Graphical (Bar) Scale: A subdivided ruler printed on the map margin that remains mathematically accurate even if the map is photographically enlarged or reduced.

2. Step-by-Step Derivation for 1:25,000 Scale

Applying the Representative Fraction $1 : 25,000$ to a measured map distance of $1.5\text{ cm}$:

Analytical Ground Distance Calculation
$$D_{\text{ground}} = D_{\text{map}} \times \text{Scale Factor} = 1.5\text{ cm} \times 25,000 = 37,500\text{ cm}$$

Converting centimeters to standard metric meters ($1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm}$):

$$D_{\text{ground}} = \frac{37,500\text{ cm}}{100\text{ cm/m}} = \mathbf{375\text{ meters}}$$
"Scale of Maps: The scale is the ratio between a distance measured on the map and the corresponding distance on the ground. When expressed as a fraction, such as 1:25,000 or 1:50,000, it is called the Representative Fraction (R.F.). On a 1:25,000 map, 1 centimetre represents 25,000 centimetres, which equals 250 metres on the ground." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Ch. 12: Geological Maps, p. 386)

3. Survey of India (SOI) Topographic Map Scale Hierarchies

In Indian geology and mining, the Survey of India (SOI) toposheet series follows strict geometric numbering hierarchies:

  • Million Sheet Series ($1 : 1,000,000$): Covers $4^\circ \times 4^\circ$ quadrants (e.g., Sheet No. 73 covers Odisha/Jharkhand).
  • Degree Sheet Series ($1 : 250,000$): Covers $1^\circ \times 1^\circ$ quadrangles (designated by letters A to P, e.g., 73H). Scale: $1\text{ cm} = 2.5\text{ km}$.
  • Quadrant Sheet Series ($1 : 50,000$): Covers $15' \times 15'$ quadrangles (numbered 1 to 16, e.g., 73H/15). Scale: $1\text{ cm} = 500\text{ meters}$.
  • Special Planning Series ($1 : 25,000$): Covers $7.5' \times 7.5'$ quadrangles (designated NW, NE, SW, SE). Scale: $1\text{ cm} = 250\text{ meters}$.

Official Question Statement — OPSC 2021 Geology Paper-II (Question 30)
Q30. The distance between two points on a 1: 25,000 map is 1.5 cm than the true distance between them is:
(A) 375 m  ✔
(B) 37.5 m
(C) 3750 m
(D) 3.75 m
✔
Correct Answer: (A) 375 m
At a Representative Fraction of $1 : 25,000$, $1\text{ cm}$ on the map equals $25,000\text{ cm} = 250\text{ m}$ on the ground. A measured map distance of $1.5\text{ cm}$ corresponds to $1.5 \times 250\text{ m} = \mathbf{375\text{ meters}}$.
(A) 375 m Correct Verified Answer
Why this option is correct: By direct metric conversion: $$D_{\text{ground}} = 1.5\text{ cm} \times 25,000 = 37,500\text{ cm}$$ Since $1\text{ meter} = 100\text{ centimeters}$: $$D_{\text{ground}} = \frac{37,500}{100} = \mathbf{375\text{ meters}}$$ Thus, option (A) is mathematically exact.
(B) 37.5 m Incorrect (Factor of 10 Decimal Error)
Why this distractor is incorrect: $37.5\text{ m}$ results from an arithmetic error dividing by 1000 instead of 100 ($37,500 / 1000 = 37.5\text{ m}$). It corresponds to a scale of $1 : 2,500$, not $1 : 25,000$.
(C) 3750 m Incorrect (Factor of 10 Overestimation)
Why this distractor is incorrect: $3,750\text{ meters}$ ($3.75\text{ km}$) corresponds to a map scale of $1 : 250,000$ (an SOI degree sheet), where $1.5\text{ cm} \times 250,000 = 375,000\text{ cm} = 3750\text{ m}$.
(D) 3.75 m Incorrect (Factor of 100 Error)
Why this distractor is incorrect: $3.75\text{ meters}$ corresponds to an engineering architectural drafting scale of $1 : 250$.

High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Questions)
  • Practice Q1 (Numerical): On a $1 : 50,000$ Survey of India geological map, an iron ore body has an outcrop strike length of $4.8\text{ cm}$. Calculate the actual ground strike length of the ore body in kilometers.
    ✔ Worked Answer: $2.40\text{ km}$.
    Calculation:
    $$D_{\text{ground}} = 4.8\text{ cm} \times 50,000 = 240,000\text{ cm} = 2,400\text{ m} = 2.40\text{ km}$$
  • Practice Q2: What is the difference between a large-scale map and a small-scale map?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Large-scale maps have larger numerical RF fractions ($> 1 : 25,000$) showing small areas in great detail; small-scale maps have small fractions ($< 1 : 500,000$) covering vast regional areas with low detail.
    Explanation: Because $1/25,000 = 0.00004$ is mathematically larger than $1/250,000 = 0.000004$, a $1 : 25,000$ map is a large-scale map.
  • Practice Q3 (Area Scale Conversion): A mining lease covers a rectangular area measuring $4.0\text{ cm} \times 5.0\text{ cm}$ on a $1 : 25,000$ toposheet. Calculate the true ground area of the lease in square kilometers ($\text{km}^2$) and hectares ($1\text{ ha} = 10,000\text{ m}^2$).
    ✔ Worked Answer: $\text{Area} = 1.25\text{ km}^2 = 125\text{ hectares}$.
    Calculation:
    $$\text{Ground Width } W = 4.0\text{ cm} \times 250\text{ m/cm} = 1000\text{ m} = 1.0\text{ km}$$
    $$\text{Ground Length } L = 5.0\text{ cm} \times 250\text{ m/cm} = 1250\text{ m} = 1.25\text{ km}$$
    $$\text{Ground Area } A = 1.0\text{ km} \times 1.25\text{ km} = 1.25\text{ km}^2 = 1,250,000\text{ m}^2 = 125\text{ hectares}$$
  • Practice Q4: If a map with a scale of $1 : 50,000$ is photocopied and enlarged by $200\%$ ($2\times$ linear enlargement), what is the new scale of the enlarged copy?
    ✔ Correct Answer: $1 : 25,000$.
    Explanation: Linear features are twice as long on the copy ($2\text{ cm}$ represents what $1\text{ cm}$ previously represented). The new scale is $2 / 50,000 = 1 / 25,000$.
  • Practice Q5: Why does a graphical bar scale remain accurate after photographic map resizing, whereas a written Representative Fraction (e.g., $1 : 50,000$) becomes false?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The bar scale enlarges or shrinks in exact proportion with the map graphics.
    Explanation: Resizing alters physical distances on paper; the printed RF numbers do not change, but the graphical bar expands/contracts synchronously with the image.
  • Practice Q6: What is the standard contour interval on Survey of India $1 : 50,000$ toposheets in plains vs. mountainous terrain?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Typically $20\text{ meters}$ (or $10\text{ meters}$ on $1 : 25,000$ sheets).
    Explanation: A $20\text{ m}$ contour interval provides clear topographic visualization without overcrowding steep slopes with contour lines.
  • Practice Q7: On an aerial photograph of an unmapped region, a runway known to be $1800\text{ meters}$ long measures $7.2\text{ cm}$ on the print. What is the approximate photo scale?
    ✔ Worked Answer: $1 : 25,000$.
    Calculation:
    $$\text{Scale} = \frac{D_{\text{photo}}}{D_{\text{ground}}} = \frac{7.2\text{ cm}}{1800\text{ m}} = \frac{7.2\text{ cm}}{180,000\text{ cm}} = \frac{1}{25,000}$$
  • Practice Q8: What is the geographic coverage area in latitude and longitude of a standard Survey of India $1 : 50,000$ quadrangle sheet (e.g., 73H/15)?
    ✔ Correct Answer: $15\text{ minutes of latitude} \times 15\text{ minutes of longitude}$ ($15' \times 15'$).
    Explanation: A degree sheet ($1^\circ \times 1^\circ$) is divided into 16 quadrants, each measuring $15' \times 15'$ (approximately $28\text{ km} \times 28\text{ km} \approx 780\text{ km}^2$).
