Q38: Seismology: Hypocenter (Focus), Epicenter & Isoseismal Attenuation

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Peter M. Shearer (Introduction to Seismology, Ch. 1, pp. 1-8); K.M. Bangar (Ch. 10, pp. 142-148)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

38.1 Geometric & Physical Definitions of Earthquake Origins

In observational seismology and geophysics (Peter M. Shearer, Introduction to Seismology, 2nd Ed., Ch. 1; Lowrie, Fundamentals of Geophysics, Ch. 3; Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology, Ch. 10), an earthquake involves the rapid release of accumulated elastic strain energy along a fault rupture surface:

Seismological Term Spatial & Geometric Definition Physical Role in Wave Dynamics Damage & Intensity Relationship
Focus (Hypocenter) The exact point inside the Earth's interior where the fault rupture nucleates and initial slip begins. Origin point of radiating spherical $P$ and $S$ body wavefronts. Subsurface point; depth ($h$) ranges from shallow ($0-70\text{ km}$) to deep ($> 300\text{ km}$).
Epicenter The point on the Earth's surface directly vertically above the focus (hypocenter). Point of initial arrival of seismic waves at the surface. Location of maximum seismic ground shaking intensity ($I_{\text{max}}$) and catastrophic structural damage for shallow crustal earthquakes.
Focal Depth ($h$) The vertical distance between the epicenter on the surface and the subsurface focus. Directly governs geometrical attenuation: $I \propto 1/\sqrt{\Delta^2 + h^2}$. Shallower focal depth = greater concentration of energy at epicenter.
Isoseismal Lines Closed contour curves connecting points on the Earth's surface experiencing equal earthquake intensity. Reflects directional radiation pattern and local geology. Form concentric rings centered around the epicenter.

38.2 Why the Epicenter Suffers Maximum Damage

Seismic energy density ($E_d$) attenuates with hypocentral distance ($R = \sqrt{\Delta^2 + h^2}$) due to geometrical spreading ($1/R^2$) and anelastic intrinsic attenuation ($e^{-\alpha R}$):

$$E_d(\Delta) \propto \frac{E_0}{\Delta^2 + h^2} \exp\left(-\frac{\omega R}{Q v}\right)$$

Where $\Delta$ is the epicentral distance. At the epicenter, epicentral distance is zero ($\Delta = 0$), so $R = h$ is at its absolute minimum possible value. Consequently, energy density, peak ground acceleration (PGA), and destructive surface wave amplitude are concentrated at the epicenter.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q38. What will be the location where an earthquake does the maximum damage ?
(A) Epicentre
(B) Focus
(C) 100 km from the epicenter
(D) Isocentre
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (A)

Correct Answer: (A) Epicentre — The point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus is the Epicenter, which experiences the earliest wave arrival, highest peak ground acceleration, and maximum structural damage.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Epicentre — CORRECT: The point on the Earth's surface vertically above the focus where seismic intensity (Modified Mercalli / MSK scale) reaches its maximum ($I_{\text{max}}$).
  • (B) Focus — INCORRECT: The focus (hypocenter) is located deep underground inside the Earth's crust or mantle; damage to human civil infrastructure occurs at the Earth's surface.
  • (C) 100 km from the epicenter — INCORRECT: Seismic intensity attenuates rapidly with distance; shaking at $100\text{ km}$ is orders of magnitude weaker than at the epicenter.
  • (D) Isocentre — INCORRECT: In photogrammetry, an isocenter is the point on an aerial photograph where the bisector of the tilt angle meets the image; it is completely meaningless in seismology.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

Seismological Intensity Attenuation Problem

Practice Problem 38: Hypocentral Distance & Geometric Spreading

A shallow crustal earthquake has a focal depth of $h = 10\text{ km}$. Compare the geometrical wave amplitude at the epicenter ($\Delta_1 = 0\text{ km}$) with that at a seismic station located at an epicentral distance of $\Delta_2 = 30\text{ km}$, assuming simple spherical body wave geometrical spreading ($A \propto 1/R$).

Solution:

1. Hypocentral distance to epicenter ($R_1$):

$$R_1 = \sqrt{\Delta_1^2 + h^2} = \sqrt{0^2 + 10^2} = 10\text{ km}$$

2. Hypocentral distance to distant station ($R_2$):

$$R_2 = \sqrt{\Delta_2^2 + h^2} = \sqrt{30^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{900 + 100} = \sqrt{1000} \approx 31.62\text{ km}$$

3. Ratio of seismic wave amplitudes ($A_1 / A_2$):

$$\frac{A_{\text{epicenter}}}{A_{\text{station}}} = \frac{R_2}{R_1} = \frac{31.62}{10.0} \approx 3.16$$

The ground shaking amplitude at the epicenter is more than 3 times greater than at $30\text{ km}$ away (and energy density is $\sim 10$ times higher), explaining why maximum devastation is localized at the epicenter.
