Q77: Igneous Textures Matching - Phaneritic, Ophitic, Holocrystalline, and Pillow Structures

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: K.M. Bangar (Principles of Engineering Geology, Ch. 5, p. 166); Winter (Ch. 3)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Core Question Analysis & Verbatim Textbook Grounding

This match-the-following item connects classic igneous textures and macroscopic structures to their diagnostic rock types.

Textbook Citation (K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology, Chapter 5, p. 76–78):
"Phaneritic texture: In this texture, individual mineral grains are sufficiently large to be recognized by the unaided eye. It is typical of plutonic rocks like granite... Ophitic texture: Laths of plagioclase feldspar are completely enclosed by large plates of pyroxene (augite). This texture is characteristic of dolerite... Pillow structure: Formed during submarine extrusion of basaltic lavas, forming interconnected sack-like bodies, characteristic of spilites and ocean floor basalts."

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q77. Match the following in List — I and List — II and choose the correct answer : List — I: (a) Phaneritic texture, (b) Ophitic texture, (c) Holocrystalline texture, (d) Pillow structure. List — II: (i) Dolerite, (ii) Gabbro, (iii) Spillite, (iv) Granite.
(A) (a) - (ii), (b) - (i), (c) - (iv), (d) - (iii)
(B) (a) - (iv), (b) - (i), (c) - (ii), (d) - (iii)
(C) (a) - (iv), (b) - (ii), (c) - (i), (d) - (iii)
(D) (a) - (i), (b) - (ii), (c) - (iii), (d) - (iv)

2. Detailed Petrographic Texture Matrix

Texture / Structure (List I) Diagnostic Petrographic Characteristics Matched Rock (List II) Cooling & Crystallization Setting
(a) Phaneritic texture Coarse-grained (> 1 mm) interlocking crystals visible without magnification; slow plutonic cooling. (iv) Granite Deep-seated plutonic batholiths/stocks.
(b) Ophitic texture Subhedral/euhedral laths of plagioclase partially or completely embedded in a larger single optically continuous clinopyroxene crystal. (i) Dolerite (Diabase) Hypabyssal shallow subvolcanic dykes and sills.
(c) Holocrystalline texture Rock composed 100% of crystalline minerals with zero volcanic glass ($0\% ext{ glass}$). (ii) Gabbro Plutonic basic intrusions / layered mafic complexes.
(d) Pillow structure Convex-topped, tapering-bottom ellipsoidal lobes with glassy chilled margins formed by rapid quenching under water. (iii) Spilite Submarine volcanism / mid-ocean ridge hydrothermal spilitization.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Why (b) - (i) is indisputable: Ophitic texture is universally the defining textural hallmark of dolerite. This immediately eliminates Option D.
  • Why (d) - (iii) is fixed: Pillow structures are synonymous with submarine basaltic / spilitic eruptions. This eliminates Option D.
  • Distinguishing Option B vs. Option A: In Indian geological university curricula (Bangar, p. 76; Mukherjee, p. 115), Granite is universally introduced as the archetype for phaneritic coarse texture, while Gabbro is the classic coarse-grained basic rock designated as holocrystalline. Therefore, Option B ((a)-(iv), (b)-(i), (c)-(ii), (d)-(iii)) is the intended key.
