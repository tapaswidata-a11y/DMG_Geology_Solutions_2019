Q10: Hogback vs. Cuesta vs. Mesa Morphometrics in Dipping Strata

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: K.M. Bangar (Engineering Geology, Ch. 8, pp. 162-165); Fossen (Structural Geology); Mahapatra (Ch. 4)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

10.1 Structural Control on Ridge Topography

In structural geomorphology (K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology, Ch. 8; Fossen, Structural Geology; G.B. Mahapatra, Ch. 4), differential erosion of sedimentary strata with varying dip angles produces three diagnostic landform types:

Landform Strata Dip Angle ($\theta$) Slope Morphology & Symmetry
Hogback Steeply dipping: $\theta > 30^\circ - 45^\circ$ (up to vertical) Symmetrical or near-symmetrical: Narrow, sharp crest with steep slopes of nearly equal inclination on both flanks (dip slope $\approx$ scarp slope).
Cuesta Gently dipping: $\theta < 20^\circ$ (typically $5^\circ - 15^\circ$) Asymmetrical: Gentle dip slope (concordant with strata) and an abrupt, steep escarpment/scarp slope on the opposite flank.
Mesa Horizontal / Flat-lying: $\theta \approx 0^\circ$ Flat tableland capped by a horizontal resistant stratum with steep perimeter cliffs.
Butte Horizontal / Flat-lying: $\theta \approx 0^\circ$ Isolated steep-sided pinnacled hill, narrower than a mesa (width is less than height).

10.2 Geometric Proof of Slope Symmetry

When strata dip gently (Cuesta, $\theta \approx 10^\circ$), the dip slope is bounded by the dip angle ($10^\circ$), while the opposite scarp slope erodes along joint-controlled near-vertical cliff faces ($> 60^\circ$).

When strata dip steeply (Hogback, $\theta \approx 45^\circ - 60^\circ$), the dip slope angle becomes equal to or steeper than the natural angle of repose of the talus, causing both the dip flank and the scarp flank to exhibit steep, nearly equal slope inclinations.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q10. A long, narrow ridge or a series of hills with a narrow crest and steep slopes of nearly equal inclination on both flanks created by the differential erosion of outcropping sedimentary strata is termed as :
(A) Cuesta
(B) Hogback
(C) Mesa
(D) Butte
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) Hogback — A narrow, steep ridge with slopes of nearly equal inclination on both flanks created by differential erosion of steeply dipping strata is a Hogback.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Cuesta — INCORRECT: A cuesta is distinctly asymmetrical, possessing a gentle dip slope on one side and a steep escarpment on the other.
  • (B) Hogback — CORRECT: Precisely matches the morphological definition: steep inclination of nearly equal angles on both flanks due to steep structural dip.
  • (C) Mesa — INCORRECT: A mesa is a broad, flat-topped tableland formed on horizontal beds.
  • (D) Butte — INCORRECT: A butte is a smaller, pillar-like remnant of a mesa formed on horizontal beds.

Practice Problem 10: Structural Transition Cuesta to Hogback

Explain how the landscape changes as one traverses from the gentle foreland flank towards the core of an asymmetrical mountain fold where bed dips increase from $8^\circ$ to $55^\circ$.

Answer:

At $8^\circ$ dip, the landscape displays broad, sweeping Cuestas with extensive gentle dip slopes. As the beds steepen towards $55^\circ$ near the fold hinge, the cuestas compress into sharp, knife-edge, symmetrical Hogbacks with near-equal steep slopes on both flanks.
