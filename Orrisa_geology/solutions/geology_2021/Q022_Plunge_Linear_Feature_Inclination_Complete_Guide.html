1. Geometric Definitions: Plunge & Trend of Linear Elements

In structural geology, geological structures are geometric entities classified as either planar features (e.g., bedding planes, fault planes, foliation surfaces, joint sets) or linear features (e.g., fold hinge lines, stretching lineations, intersection lineations, slickenlines, elongate mineral needles). While planar features are completely defined by Strike and Dip, linear features are completely defined in space by Plunge and Trend:

  • Plunge ($\beta$): The vertical angle between the horizontal plane and the inclined linear feature, measured downward in a vertical plane from the horizontal to the line ($0^\circ \le \beta \le 90^\circ$). If a line lies horizontally, its plunge is $0^\circ$; if it is vertical, its plunge is $90^\circ$.
  • Trend ($\alpha$): The geographic azimuth (compass bearing, $000^\circ - 360^\circ$) of the vertical plane containing the linear feature, pointing in the direction of downward plunge.
Structural Notation for Linear Orientation
$$\text{Notation: } \beta \to \alpha \quad \text{(e.g., } 35^\circ \to 140^\circ\text{)}$$

Read as: "Plunging 35 degrees toward an azimuth of 140 degrees (South-East)". The plunge angle is always written first as a two-digit number, followed by the trend as a three-digit azimuth.

2. The Crucial Distinction: Plunge vs. Rake (Pitch)

Candidates must strictly master the geometric difference between Plunge and Rake (Pitch):

  • Plunge: Measured strictly within a true vertical plane intersecting the line. It requires no reference to an inclined plane ($0^\circ \le \text{Plunge} \le 90^\circ$).
  • Rake (or Pitch): The acute angle measured within an inclined structural plane between the linear feature and the horizontal strike line of that plane ($0^\circ \le \text{Rake} \le 90^\circ$). For example, slickenlines (fault scratch marks) on a fault plane dipping $60^\circ$ are measured directly on the rock face as a rake angle relative to the fault's strike line.
Mathematical Relation Connecting Plunge, Dip, and Rake
$$\sin(\text{Plunge}) = \sin(\text{Dip}) \cdot \sin(\text{Rake})$$ $$\tan(\text{Apparent Dip}) = \tan(\text{True Dip}) \cdot \cos(\text{Angle to Strike})$$
"Plunge: The angle of inclination of the fold axis (or any linear feature) with the horizontal, measured in a vertical plane, is called the plunge of the fold. When the axis is horizontal, the plunge is zero." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Ch. 7: Folds, pp. 192–193, Fig. 7.5)
Figure 22.2 (K.M. Bangar, Fig. 7.5 & 7.8): Plunging fold geometry, illustrating the vertical inclination angle between the horizontal plane and the fold axis (plunge).
3. Refutation of Strike and Dip (Planar Parameters)

Understanding geometric dimensionality eliminates distractors:

  • Strike: The compass bearing of a horizontal line formed by the intersection of an inclined geological plane with the horizontal plane. It applies to planes, not lines.
  • Dip: The maximum vertical inclination angle of an inclined geological plane relative to the horizontal. Dip is measured perpendicular to the strike line of a plane.

Official Question Statement — OPSC 2021 Geology Paper-II (Question 22)
Q22. The vertical angle between the horizontal plane and the axis or line of maximum elongation of a... (linear feature) is known as :
(A) strike
(B) dip
(C) rake
(D) plunge  ✔
✔
Correct Answer: (D) plunge
The vertical angle measured in a vertical plane between the horizontal plane and an inclined linear feature (such as a fold axis, line of maximum elongation, or mineral lineation) is universally defined as the plunge of that line (Bangar, p. 192; Fossen, p. 25).
(A) strike Incorrect (Compass Azimuth of a Planar Feature)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Strike is the horizontal compass direction of a planar surface (bedding, joint, fault). It is a horizontal azimuth, not a vertical angle of inclination, and applies exclusively to planes, not linear axes.
(B) dip Incorrect (Vertical Angle of an Inclined Plane)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Dip is the angle of inclination of a planar surface measured perpendicular to strike. While both dip and plunge are vertical angles, "dip" is reserved strictly for planar surfaces, whereas "plunge" is the precise scientific term for linear features.
(C) rake Incorrect (Angle Measured in an Inclined Plane, Not Vertical Plane)
Why this distractor is incorrect: Rake (also termed pitch) is the angle between a linear feature and the strike line measured within an inclined plane. Plunge is measured strictly in a true vertical plane.
(D) plunge Correct Verified Answer
Why this option is correct: By standard definition in structural geology, the vertical angle between the horizontal reference plane and an inclined line (fold axis, mineral stretching lineation, or line of maximum elongation) is the plunge. It ranges from $0^\circ$ (horizontal line) to $90^\circ$ (vertical line).

High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Questions)
  • Practice Q1 (Numerical Trigonometry): Slickenlines on a fault plane dipping $60^\circ$ display a rake of $30^\circ$. Calculate the true plunge of the slickenlines.
    ✔ Worked Answer: $\text{Plunge } \beta \approx 25.66^\circ$.
    Calculation:
    Using the fundamental geometric formula: $$\sin(\text{Plunge}) = \sin(\text{Dip}) \cdot \sin(\text{Rake}) = \sin(60^\circ) \cdot \sin(30^\circ) = (0.8660) \times (0.5000) = 0.4330$$
    $$\text{Plunge } \beta = \arcsin(0.4330) \approx 25.66^\circ$$
  • Practice Q2: If a mineral lineation lies directly along the true dip direction of an inclined foliation plane, what is the mathematical relationship between its plunge and the plane's dip?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Plunge equals True Dip ($\beta = \text{Dip}$); Rake is $90^\circ$.
    Explanation: Because the line points down the steepest path of the plane, $\sin\beta = \sin(\text{Dip}) \sin(90^\circ) = \sin(\text{Dip})$, meaning plunge is identical to the plane's dip angle.
  • Practice Q3: If a linear feature lies parallel to the strike line of an inclined plane, what are its plunge and rake?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Plunge is $0^\circ$ (horizontal line); Rake is $0^\circ$.
    Explanation: Any line parallel to the strike is horizontal by definition, hence its vertical plunge angle is zero.
  • Practice Q4: What type of lineation is formed by the intersection of sedimentary bedding ($S_0$) with tectonic cleavage foliation ($S_1$)?
    ✔ Correct Answer: An intersection lineation ($L_{0-1}$), which is parallel to the fold hinge line.
    Explanation: In cylindrical folds, axial planar cleavage intersects bedding along lines parallel to the regional fold axis, allowing fold plunge to be determined from hand samples.
  • Practice Q5: On a geological map, how is a plunging anticline recognized from the outcrop pattern of its beds?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The outcrop pattern forms a V-shaped or U-shaped nose that points (closes) in the direction of plunge.
    Explanation: For an anticline, the outcrop closes in the direction of plunge, with older rocks inside the V; for a syncline, the outcrop opens in the direction of plunge (closes away from plunge).
  • Practice Q6: What is a stretching lineation, and what does its plunge and trend indicate in ductile shear zones?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Elongate mineral aggregates (stretched pebbles, quartz ribbons) parallel to the maximum finite elongation direction ($\lambda_1$ or $X$-axis of the strain ellipsoid).
    Explanation: Stretching lineations record the flow trajectory of ductile tectonic transport within mylonites and orogenic shear belts.
  • Practice Q7: On an equal-area stereonet (Schmidt net), how is a linear structural feature represented?
    ✔ Correct Answer: As a single point (pole), plotted at an angular distance from the primitive circle equal to $90^\circ - \text{plunge}$ along its trend radius.
    Explanation: Planes plot as great circle arcs; lines plot as unique discrete points on the lower hemisphere projection.
  • Practice Q8: If a fold has an axis that is horizontal, what is its plunge, and what is the term for such a fold?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Plunge is $0^\circ$; termed a Non-Plunging (or Horizontal) Fold.
    Explanation: A horizontal fold produces perfectly parallel ribbon-like outcrop stripes on a planar horizontal geologic map that never close into V-shaped noses.
