Q15: Photogrammetry: Fiducial Marks & Principal Point Determination

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Wolf, Dewitt & Wilkinson (Elements of Photogrammetry with Applications in GIS, 4th Ed., Ch. 4); Lillesand (Ch. 3)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

15.1 Anatomy of a Metric Aerial Photograph

In aerial photogrammetry (Paul R. Wolf, Bon A. Dewitt, & Benjamin E. Wilkinson, Elements of Photogrammetry with Applications in GIS, 4th Ed., Ch. 4; Lillesand et al., Ch. 3), metric aerial cameras are equipped with precisely calibrated index markers rigidly affixed to the focal plane frame, known as fiducial marks.

Photogrammetric Point Geometric Definition Diagnostic Property & Significance
Principal Point ($PP$ or $o$) The intersection point of straight lines connecting opposite fiducial marks on an aerial photograph Origin of the photographic coordinate system $(x, y)$; point where optical axis intersects focal plane at $90^\circ$.
Nadir Point ($n$) The point where the true plumb line (vertical) through the camera exposure station intersects the photograph Origin of relief displacement ($d = \frac{r \cdot h}{H}$). On a truly vertical photo, $n = PP$.
Isocenter ($i$) The point on the photograph halfway between the principal point and nadir on the principal line Origin of tilt displacement ($d_t$). Scale is exactly true along the isometric parallel passing through $i$.

15.2 Photographic Coordinate System Calibration

The calibration certificate of a metric aerial camera specifies the exact $(x, y)$ coordinates of each fiducial mark relative to the calibrated principal point to within $\pm 1 - 2\ \mu\text{m}$, eliminating film shrinkage and scanner distortion.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q15. Fiducial marks recorded on Aerial photographs are used to determine the :
(A) Flight path
(B) Scale of Aerial photographs
(C) Principal points
(D) Flight height
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (C)

Correct Answer: (C) Principal points — Fiducial marks imaged on the edges or corners of an aerial photograph are used to locate the geometric center or Principal Point of the photograph.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Flight path — INCORRECT: The flight path is determined by connecting conjugate principal points (flight strip track), not by fiducial marks alone.
  • (B) Scale of Aerial photographs — INCORRECT: Photo scale is determined by the ratio of focal length to flying height ($S = f/H$) or ground control distance.
  • (C) Principal points — CORRECT: The defining function of fiducial marks is to define the photo coordinate axes and locate the principal point ($PP$).
  • (D) Flight height — INCORRECT: Flight height is measured by altimeters or stereoscopic parallax equations ($H = B \cdot f / p$).

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

Photogrammetric Relief Displacement Problem

Practice Problem 15: Relief Displacement from the Principal Point

On a vertical aerial photograph taken with focal length $f = 152.4\text{ mm}$ from flying height $H = 2,500\text{ m}$ above terrain, the top of a communication tower has radial distance $r = 82.5\text{ mm}$ from the principal point, and relief displacement $d = 3.3\text{ mm}$. Calculate the height of the tower.

Solution:

The relief displacement formula on a vertical photograph is:

$$d = \frac{r \cdot h}{H} \implies h = \frac{d \cdot H}{r}$$ $$h = \frac{3.3\text{ mm} \times 2500\text{ m}}{82.5\text{ mm}} = \frac{8250}{82.5} = 100\text{ meters}$$

The height of the tower is exactly $100\text{ meters}$.
