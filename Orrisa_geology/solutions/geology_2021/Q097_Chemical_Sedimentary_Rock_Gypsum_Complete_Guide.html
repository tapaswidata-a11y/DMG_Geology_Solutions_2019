2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Evaporites and Chemical Sedimentary Rocks

Sedimentary rocks are categorized by origin into clastic, chemical, biogenic, and residual types:

  • Chemical Sedimentary Rocks: Formed by inorganic chemical precipitation of dissolved mineral salts from water bodies (marine or lacustrine) due to supersaturation and evaporation:
    • Gypsum ($ ext{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2 ext{H}_2 ext{O}$): A primary chemical evaporite that precipitates when seawater is concentrated to roughly $19\%$ of its original volume (after calcite and before halite).
    • Other chemical rocks include halite (rock salt), anhydrite, and chemical chert.
  • Breccia: A coarse-grained clastic sedimentary rock composed of angular, broken fragments ($>2 ext{ mm}$) cemented in a fine-grained matrix.
  • Laterite & Bauxite: Residual soils / weathering crusts (duricrusts) formed by intense in-situ subaerial leaching of silica in humid tropical climates, not precipitated chemical sedimentary rocks.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option D (Gypsum) is correct: True chemical evaporite sedimentary rock precipitated from saline brines.
  • Option A (Breccia): Clastic / mechanical sedimentary rock with angular fragments.
  • Option B & C (Laterite / Bauxite): Residual chemical weathering profiles (paleosols), not sedimentary chemical strata.
