Q33: Recumbent Fold Geometry & Axial Surface Inclination

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: M.J. Fleuty (The Description of Folds, 1964); Haakon Fossen (Ch. 11, pp. 240-244)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

33.1 Fleuty's (1964) Classification of Folds by Axial Surface Dip

In structural geology (M.J. Fleuty, 1964, The Description of Folds; Haakon Fossen, Structural Geology, Ch. 11; Billings, Ch. 4; Bangar, Ch. 7), folds are rigorously categorized according to the inclination (dip angle $\delta_{\text{ap}}$) of their axial surface (axial plane):

Fold Category Dip of Axial Surface ($\delta_{\text{ap}}$) Limb Attitude & Structural Symmetry Geotectonic Significance
Upright Fold $\delta_{\text{ap}} = 80^\circ - 90^\circ$ (Vertical to Sub-vertical) Limbs dip symmetrically in opposite directions at equal angles. Mild to moderate horizontal compression.
Steeply Inclined Fold $\delta_{\text{ap}} = 60^\circ - 80^\circ$ Asymmetrical limbs dipping in opposite directions. Progressive asymmetric shear.
Moderately Inclined Fold $\delta_{\text{ap}} = 30^\circ - 60^\circ$ Asymmetrical limbs. Oblique contractional shortening.
Gently Inclined Fold $\delta_{\text{ap}} = 10^\circ - 30^\circ$ One limb normal, one limb overturned. Intense overthrust shear strain.
Recumbent Fold $\mathbf{\delta_{\text{ap}} = 0^\circ - 10^\circ}$ (Sub-horizontal to Horizontal, $\approx 0^\circ$) Axial plane is horizontal; the lower limb is completely overturned and inverted. Extreme ductile shear nappe transport in metamorphic cores.

33.2 Anatomy of a Recumbent Fold

In a true recumbent fold, the fold lies essentially on its side. It consists of:

  • Upper Normal Limb (Digit): Strata are in normal stratigraphic superposition (younger beds over older beds).
  • Hinge Zone (Closure): Point of maximum curvature where the axial surface trace is sub-horizontal.
  • Lower Inverted Limb (Flank): Strata are completely overturned (inverted, upside down; older beds over younger beds), often subjected to extreme ductile attenuation and shearing, eventually detaching along thrusts to generate nappes.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q33. In a recumbent fold, the axial plane dips about _____.
(A) 45°
(B) 90°
(C) 60°
(D) 0°
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (D)

Correct Answer: (D) 0° — In a recumbent fold, the defining structural criterion is that the axial plane dips sub-horizontally, essentially at 0° ($\le 10^\circ$).

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) 45° — INCORRECT: An axial plane dipping at $45^\circ$ defines an inclined fold, not a recumbent fold.
  • (B) 90° — INCORRECT: An axial plane dipping at $90^\circ$ defines an upright (vertical) fold.
  • (C) 60° — INCORRECT: An axial plane dipping at $60^\circ$ defines a steeply inclined fold.
  • (D) 0° — CORRECT: By universal academic definition (Fleuty 1964; Fossen; Billings), a recumbent fold is a fold whose axial surface is horizontal to sub-horizontal (dip $\approx 0^\circ$).

High-Yield Fold Anatomy Problem

Practice Problem 33: Identifying Facing and Vergence in Recumbent Nappes

A geologist in the Lesser Himalayan Almora Nappe observes graded turbidite sandstone beds on the lower limb of a large recumbent fold whose axial surface dips at $4^\circ\text{ North}$. The coarse pebbles are found at the stratigraphic top of each bed while fine silts are at the bottom. What is the facing direction and structural status of this limb?

Solution:

1. In graded bedding, normal younging direction (facing) requires coarse grains at the bottom grading upward to fine grains at the top. Here, coarse pebbles are at the top, indicating that the strata are stratigraphically inverted (overturned).

2. In a recumbent fold with an axial surface dip of $4^\circ \approx 0^\circ$, the lower limb is the inverted / overturned limb. Facing is directed downward, confirming classic recumbent nappe kinematics.
