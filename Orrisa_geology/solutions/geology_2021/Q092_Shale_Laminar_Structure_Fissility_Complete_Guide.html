2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Shale Petrology and Lamination

Shale is a fine-grained siliciclastic sedimentary rock composed of silt- and clay-sized particles ($<0.0625 ext{ mm}$).

  • Laminar Structure & Fissility: The defining physical characteristic of shale is its finely laminated depositional bedding (laminae $<10 ext{ mm}$ thick) and its tendency to split easily along these parallel bedding planes (fissility). This laminar structure arises from the parallel sedimentation and subsequent compaction of platy phyllosilicate clay minerals (illite, kaolinite, smectite).
  • Cleavage: A tectonic deformational feature produced by metamorphic recrystallization under directed stress (e.g., slaty cleavage in slate), not an inherent primary sedimentary structure of unmetamorphosed shales.
  • Parting & Joints: Secondary structural planes that occur in all rock types, not the diagnostic feature of shale.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option B (laminar structure) is correct: Shales are textbook-defined by their distinct laminar structure and fissility.
  • Option A (cleavage): Metamorphic rock property (slate/phyllite).
  • Option C & D (joints / parting): Universal rock mechanical fracture properties, not diagnostic of shale petrogenesis.
