Q34: Crustal Rheology & Brittle vs. Ductile Deformation Regimes

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Van der Pluijm & Marshak (Ch. 7, pp. 148-156); J. Byerlee (Friction of Rocks, 1978); R.H. Sibson (1977)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

34.1 Byerlee's Law & Frictional Failure in the Upper Crust

In structural geology, rock mechanics, and lithospheric dynamics (Byerlee, 1978; Sibson, 1977; Van der Pluijm & Marshak, Ch. 7; Fossen, Ch. 6), the deformation behavior of lithospheric rocks is governed by confining pressure ($P$), temperature ($T$), fluid pressure ($P_f$), and strain rate ($\dot{\varepsilon}$):

Crustal Domain P-T Conditions & Typical Depth Dominant Deformation Mechanism Characteristic Microstructures & Structures
Upper Crust Low Temperature ($T < 300^\circ\text{C}$) & Low Confining Pressure ($P < 300\text{ MPa}$); Depth: $0 - 10\text{–}15\text{ km}$ Brittle Process (Frictional Cataclasis & Fracture) Joints, Faults, Microcracking, Frictional Sliding (Byerlee's Law), Cataclasites, Fault Gouge, Pseudotachylytes.
Brittle-Ductile Transition (BDT) $T \approx 300^\circ - 350^\circ\text{C}$, Depth $\sim 10 - 15\text{ km}$ in quartz-bearing continental crust Semi-brittle / Transitional Greenschist facies transition; onset of quartz crystal plasticity; peak crustal shear strength.
Middle to Lower Crust High Temperature ($T > 350^\circ - 500^\circ\text{C}$) & High Pressure; Depth $> 15\text{ km}$ Ductile / Plastic Process (Viscous Crystal Plasticity) Dislocation creep, dynamic recrystallization, mylonites, solid-state flow, ductile folds.

34.2 Byerlee's Frictional Law

In the brittle upper crust, rock shear strength ($\tau$) is pressure-dependent but almost entirely independent of rock type and temperature (Byerlee's Law):

$$\tau = 0.85 \sigma_n \quad (\text{for } \sigma_n < 200\text{ MPa})$$ $$\tau = 50\text{ MPa} + 0.6 \sigma_n \quad (\text{for } \sigma_n > 200\text{ MPa})$$

Because rocks fail along discrete fractures and slip planes under these low P-T conditions before any significant crystal-plastic flow can occur, deformation is overwhelmingly brittle.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q34. In the upper crust where The P and T condition is relatively low, the deformational process dominates is :
(A) Brittle process
(B) Ductile-brittle process
(C) Plastic deformation
(D) Both (A) and (B) are true
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (A)

Correct Answer: (A) Brittle process — In the upper crust where confining pressure and temperature are relatively low, deformation is dominated by brittle processes (fracturing, jointing, cataclasis, and faulting).

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Brittle process — CORRECT: Low temperature ($< 300^\circ\text{C}$) and low confining pressure in the upper $10\text{–}15\text{ km}$ prevent crystal-plastic flow, restricting deformation to elastic strain followed by brittle frictional failure.
  • (B) Ductile-brittle process — INCORRECT: Ductile-brittle behavior is restricted to the narrow transitional zone (BDT) at mid-crustal depths ($10\text{–}15\text{ km}$), not the general low P-T upper crust.
  • (C) Plastic deformation — INCORRECT: True plastic deformation (dislocation creep, diffusion flow) requires elevated temperatures ($T > 300^\circ\text{–}500^\circ\text{C}$) occurring exclusively in the middle and lower crust or mantle.
  • (D) Both (A) and (B) are true — INCORRECT: Brittle deformation is the singular dominant mechanism governing the upper crust.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

Crustal Rheology & Strength Envelope Problem

Practice Problem 34: Calculating Brittle Failure Stress at 5 km Depth

Assuming an average upper continental crust density of $\rho = 2700\text{ kg/m}^3$ and hydrostatic pore water pressure with rock internal friction coefficient $\mu = 0.85$, calculate the vertical lithostatic stress ($\sigma_v$) at a depth of $z = 5.0\text{ km}$ ($g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).

Solution:

1. The vertical overburden lithostatic stress is:

$$\sigma_v = \rho g z = 2700\text{ kg/m}^3 \times 9.8\text{ m/s}^2 \times 5000\text{ m} = 132,300,000\text{ Pa} = 132.3\text{ MPa}$$

2. Hydrostatic pore fluid pressure at $5\text{ km}$ ($\rho_w = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$):

$$P_f = \rho_w g z = 1000 \times 9.8 \times 5000 = 49.0\text{ MPa}$$

3. Effective normal stress:

$$\sigma_v' = \sigma_v - P_f = 132.3 - 49.0 = 83.3\text{ MPa}$$

Under Byerlee's law, shear stress required to cause fault slip is $\tau = 0.85 \times 83.3 \approx 70.8\text{ MPa}$. This frictional stress threshold dictates earthquake faulting throughout the brittle seismogenic zone.
