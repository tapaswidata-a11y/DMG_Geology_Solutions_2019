2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: The Q-F-L Sandstone Ternary

In the standard classification schemes of Pettijohn (1975) and Folk (1980), terrigenous sandstones with $<15\%$ matrix (arenites) are classified on a triangular diagram whose poles represent the three essential detrital framework components:

Sandstone Family Framework Composition Tectonic & Climatic Significance
1. Quartz Sandstone (Quartz arenite) $\ge 95\%$ Quartz Stable cratonic platform or passive margin; prolonged transport / multi-cycle recycling; supermature.
2. Arkose (Feldspathic arenite) $\ge 25\%$ Feldspar (K-feldspar > Plagioclase) Rapid erosion of uplifted granitic basement / fault blocks in arid or high-relief tectonic settings (immature/submature).
3. Lithic Sandstone (Litharenite) $\ge 25\%$ Unstable rock fragments (clasts) Orogenic foreland basins, trench wedges, or volcanic arcs; rapid deposition close to source.

Greywacke (Wacke): Defined by its high matrix content ($>15\%$ fine detrital silt/clay matrix), representing a separate textural class (wackes vs arenites).
Molasse: A post-orogenic regional stratigraphic facies complex, not a petrographic compositional class.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A (Arkose) is correct: The third cardinal member of the classic compositional arenite trinity (Quartz Arenite, Arkose, Litharenite).
  • Option B (Greywacke): Defined by abundant matrix ($>15\%$ matrix), not an arenite end-member.
  • Option C (Molasse): Stratigraphic basin facies term (foreland basin continental/shallow marine molasse).
