Q28: Fault Classification & Slip Kinematics (Dip-Slip vs. Strike-Slip)

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Van der Pluijm & Marshak (Ch. 16, pp. 402-408); Haakon Fossen (Ch. 8, pp. 154-160); Bangar (Ch. 8)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

28.1 Kinematic Classification of Faults Based on Net Slip Vector

In structural geology and geomechanics (Van der Pluijm & Marshak, Ch. 16; Fossen, Ch. 8; Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology, Ch. 8), faults are fractures along which measurable relative displacement has occurred parallel to the fracture surface. The classification depends on the orientation of the net slip vector relative to the strike and dip of the fault plane:

Fault Class Slip Vector Orientation Rake / Pitch on Fault Kinematic Sub-types
Dip-Slip Fault Movement is parallel to the dip direction of the fault surface (strictly up or down the dip). $\text{Rake } \approx 90^\circ$ Normal Fault (hanging wall moves down; extension)
Reverse / Thrust Fault (hanging wall moves up; contraction)
Strike-Slip Fault (Transcurrent / Wrench) Movement is parallel to the horizontal strike line of the fault plane. $\text{Rake } \approx 0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$ Sinistral (left-lateral)
Dextral (right-lateral)
Oblique-Slip Fault Movement has significant components of both strike-slip and dip-slip. Intermediate rake ($10^\circ < \phi < 80^\circ$) Transtensional (normal + strike-slip)
Transpressional (reverse + strike-slip)
Step Fault A geometric series of parallel normal faults stepping down in the same direction. Dip-slip arrangement Structural terrace / staircase graben boundary

28.2 Components of Fault Displacement Vector

In 3D Cartesian coordinates on the fault surface, net slip ($S_{\text{net}}$) is resolved into:

$$S_{\text{net}}^2 = S_{\text{strike}}^2 + S_{\text{dip}}^2$$

Where pure dip-slip occurs, $S_{\text{strike}} = 0$ and $S_{\text{net}} = S_{\text{dip}}$. The vertical component of dip slip is termed the throw ($T = S_{\text{dip}} \sin\delta$), and the horizontal component perpendicular to strike is termed the heave ($H = S_{\text{dip}} \cos\delta$).

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q28. A fault in which the movement is parallel to the dip of the fault is called :
(A) Step fault
(B) Dip-slip fault
(C) Strike-slip fault
(D) None of these
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) Dip-slip fault — A fault in which the relative movement of the blocks is parallel to the dip of the fault surface is unequivocally designated as a Dip-slip fault.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Step fault — INCORRECT: Step faulting refers to a structural arrangement or pattern of multiple parallel faults downthrown in steps in the same direction, not the kinematic definition of motion along the dip.
  • (B) Dip-slip fault — CORRECT: Standard textbook definition: relative movement parallel to the true dip direction of the fault surface. Includes both normal and reverse/thrust faults.
  • (C) Strike-slip fault — INCORRECT: Motion is parallel to the horizontal strike line of the fault plane, with zero or negligible vertical dip-slip displacement.
  • (D) None of these — INCORRECT: Option (B) is the universally recognized correct term.

High-Yield Fault Kinematics & Practice Problem

Practice Problem 28: Resolving Net Slip and Rake Angle

A slickensided fault plane has an observed strike-slip component of $S_s = 60\text{ m}$ and a dip-slip component of $S_d = 80\text{ m}$. Calculate the net slip ($S_{\text{net}}$) and the rake angle ($\phi$) of the movement vector on the fault surface.

Solution:

1. The net slip magnitude is given by the Pythagorean theorem on the fault plane:

$$S_{\text{net}} = \sqrt{S_s^2 + S_d^2} = \sqrt{60^2 + 80^2} = \sqrt{3600 + 6400} = \sqrt{10000} = 100\text{ m}$$

2. The rake angle ($\phi$) measured from the strike line is:

$$\tan(\phi) = \frac{S_d}{S_s} = \frac{80}{60} = 1.3333$$ $$\phi = \arctan(1.3333) \approx 53.13^\circ$$

Because the rake angle is $53.1^\circ$, this is classified as an oblique-slip fault (transpressional or transtensional depending on whether $S_d$ is reverse or normal).
