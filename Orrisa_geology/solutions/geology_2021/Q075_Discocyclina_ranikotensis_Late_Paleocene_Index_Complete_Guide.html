Q75: Larger Foraminiferal Biostratigraphy - Discocyclina ranikotensis in the Late Paleocene

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 340); P.K. Mukherjee (p. 170); Davies (1927)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. Larger Benthic Foraminifera (LBF) in Paleogene Biostratigraphy

The early Cenozoic (Paleogene Period) witnessed a spectacular evolutionary radiation of giant, complex, multichambered Larger Benthic Foraminifera (LBF) across the warm, shallow carbonate platforms of the Tethyan Seaway. Among the most biostratigraphically crucial superfamilies is Discocyclinoidea (represented primarily by the genus Discocyclina Gümbel 1870; G.B. Mahapatra, A Text-Book of Geology, Chapter 90, p. 340 / PDF p. 178; P.K. Mukherjee, A Text Book of Geology, Chapter 14, p. 170).

The test of Discocyclina is discoidal, lenticular, or saddle-shaped, ranging in diameter from a few millimeters to over 5 centimeters. In equatorial thin-section, it is easily recognized by its distinctive architecture:

  • An inner embryonic apparatus (proloculus and deuteroloculus).
  • A prominent equatorial layer composed of concentric annuli of rectangular or square chamberlets.
  • Flanking lateral chamber layers connected by fine stolons and pillars that form exterior surface pustules.

2. Stratigraphic Range of Genus Discocyclina vs. Species Discocyclina ranikotensis

While the genus Discocyclina has a broader stratigraphic range spanning from the Early Paleocene (Danian) through the Early and Middle Eocene to the terminal Eocene extinction (Priabonian, ~56–34 Ma), individual species have exceptionally narrow vertical stratigraphic ranges:

The celebrated species Discocyclina ranikotensis (Davies 1927) is universally recognized in Indo-Pakistan and Tethyan biostratigraphy as strictly confined to the LATE PALEOCENE (Thanetian Stage, ~59 to 56 Ma):

  1. Type Occurrence: Described from the Upper Ranikot Series of Sindh and the Salt Range (type locality Ranikot), where it occurs abundantly in shallow-marine limestones immediately underlying the basal Eocene Laki Series.
  2. Indian Sedimentary Basins: In India, D. ranikotensis occurs in the Late Paleocene horizons of the Meghalaya shelf (Sylhet Limestone / Lakadong Member), the Cambay Basin, and the Jaisalmer Basin of Rajasthan.
  3. Shallow Benthic Zonation: In the standard Tethyan biozonation (Serra-Kiel et al., 1998), Discocyclina ranikotensis is the primary index fossil characterizing Shallow Benthic Zones SBZ 3 and SBZ 4, defining the latest Paleocene immediately prior to the Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum (PETM).

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q75. While genous Discocyclina is found in sediments of Danian to Early Eocene but its species Discoclina ranikotensis is only confined to
(A) Late Paleocene
(B) Early Paleocene
(C) Miocene
(D) Late Miocene

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "While genous Discocyclina is found in sediments of Danian to Early Eocene but its species Discoclina ranikotensis is only confined to ________"

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) Late Paleocene (CORRECT): Discocyclina ranikotensis is the premier index fossil of the Late Paleocene (Thanetian stage) throughout the Indo-Tethyan biogeographic province. Named after the Upper Ranikot Series (~59–56 Ma), its vertical range terminates abruptly at the Paleocene-Eocene boundary, making it an infallible zonal marker for the Late Paleocene.
  • (B) Early Paleocene (INCORRECT): The Early Paleocene (Danian stage) is characterized by primitive, uniserial and biserial foram lineages and early unspecialized larger foraminifera; D. ranikotensis had not yet evolved in the Danian.
  • (C) Miocene (INCORRECT): The entire family Discocyclinidae went completely extinct at the end of the Eocene (the Priabonian-Rupelian extinction, ~33.9 Ma)! No discocyclinids survived into the Miocene. Miocene larger foraminiferal faunas are dominated by Miogypsina, Lepidocyclina, and Austrotrillina.
  • (D) Late Miocene (INCORRECT): Completely invalid for the same reason as Option C; discocyclinids had been extinct for over 25 million years by the Late Miocene.
