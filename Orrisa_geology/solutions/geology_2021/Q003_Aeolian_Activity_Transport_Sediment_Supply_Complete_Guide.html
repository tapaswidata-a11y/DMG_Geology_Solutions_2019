Q3: Aeolian Activity Dynamic Controls (Transport Capacity, Supply & Availability)

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Sam Boggs Jr. (Sedimentology & Stratigraphy, Ch. 9, pp. 280-285); Kocurek & Lancaster (1999)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

3.1 The Kocurek-Lancaster Aeolian System Triad

In modern process sedimentology (Kocurek & Lancaster, 1999; Sam Boggs Jr., Principles of Sedimentology and Stratigraphy, Ch. 9), aeolian dynamics and dune development are governed by the interaction of three core variables:

Core Variable Physical Definition & Governing Factors
(i) Transport Capacity ($T_c$) The potential rate of sand transport by wind, governed by shear velocity ($u_*$) and fluid threshold: $q \propto u_*^3$.
(ii) Sediment Supply ($G_s$) The total inventory and volume of erodible sediment present in the upwind source basin (e.g. desiccated lake beds, outwash plains).
(iv) Sediment Availability ($A_s$) The fraction of supply susceptible to wind entrainment, limited by surface moisture, clay crusts, salt efflorescence, water table depth, and vegetation cover.

3.2 Why "Hot Desertic Conditions" is Not a Universal Physical Requirement

While vast erg systems exist in the Sahara and Arabian deserts, aeolian processes are not strictly limited to hot deserts:

  • Periglacial & Cold Deserts: Extensive active aeolian sand dunes and loess sheets form in the McMurdo Dry Valleys of Antarctica, Arctic Alaska, and the cold deserts of Tibet and Ladakh.
  • Pleistocene Loess: The massive loess plateau deposits of China and Europe were produced during cold, arid glacial maxima, not under hot climatic regimes.
  • Coastal Dune Fields: Form along temperate and sub-polar coasts regardless of temperature.

Therefore, the triad of physical control is universally (i) Transport capacity, (ii) Sediment supply, and (iv) Sediment availability.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q3. Aeolian activity is primarily controlled by: (i) Transport capacity, (ii) Sediment supply, (iii) Hot desertic conditions, (iv) Sediment availability. Choose the correct set of answer from the following options :
(A) (i), (ii), (iii)
(B) (i), (ii), (iv)
(C) (i), (iii), (iv)
(D) (i), (ii), (iii), (iv)
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) (i), (ii), (iv) — Aeolian activity is fundamentally controlled by Transport capacity, Sediment supply, and Sediment availability.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) (i), (ii), (iii) — INCORRECT: Excludes sediment availability (iv), which is mandatory to account for moisture and crusting limiting factors, while erroneously including hot desert conditions (iii).
  • (B) (i), (ii), (iv) — CORRECT: Precisely identifies the canonical triad established by Kocurek & Lancaster (1999) and textbook aeolian physics.
  • (C) (i), (iii), (iv) — INCORRECT: Omits sediment supply (ii); without a reservoir of sand, high wind capacity produces deflation lags or bedrock ventifacts rather than dune fields.
  • (D) (i), (ii), (iii), (iv) — INCORRECT: Erroneously includes (iii); temperature is a secondary modifying factor, not a primary controlling prerequisite.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

Grain Entrainment Threshold Formula & Practice

Practice Problem 3: Bagnold's Fluid Threshold for Sand Transport

According to Bagnold's equation, what is the relationship between the threshold shear velocity ($u_{*t}$) required to initiate sand grain movement and grain diameter ($D$)?

Solution:

Bagnold's fluid threshold velocity equation is:

$$u_{*t} = A \sqrt{\frac{\rho_s - \rho_a}{\rho_a} g D}$$

Where $A \approx 0.1$ for grains $> 0.1\text{ mm}$, $\rho_s$ is quartz density ($2650\text{ kg/m}^3$), $\rho_a$ is air density ($1.22\text{ kg/m}^3$), $g$ is gravity, and $D$ is grain diameter. For typical medium dune sand ($D = 0.25\text{ mm}$), $u_{*t} \approx 0.22\text{ m/s}$.
