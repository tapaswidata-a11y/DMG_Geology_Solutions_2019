Q42: Rheology of Upper Mantle & Asthenospheric Low Velocity Zone (LVZ)

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Turcotte & Schubert (Geodynamics, 3rd Ed., Ch. 7); C.M.R. Fowler (The Solid Earth, Ch. 4); Lowrie (Ch. 1)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

42.1 Rheological Stratification: Lithosphere vs. Asthenosphere

In mantle geodynamics and seismology (Turcotte & Schubert, Geodynamics, 3rd Ed., Ch. 7; Fowler, The Solid Earth, Ch. 4; Lowrie, Ch. 1), the Earth's upper $400\text{ km}$ is subdivided based on mechanical strength and rheology:

Property / Parameter Lithosphere Asthenosphere
Depth Range Surface to $100\text{–}150\text{ km}$ (beneath continents) / $0\text{–}100\text{ km}$ (oceans). $\sim 100\text{ km}$ to $410\text{ km}$ (Upper Mantle above transition zone).
Physical & Rheological State Rigid, elastic, brittle solid lithospheric lid. Ductile, plastic, mechanically weak rheid (viscous solid-state creep with $\sim 1 - 2\%$ partial melt). NOT a rigid solid!
Temperature vs. Solidus $T \ll T_{\text{solidus}}$ ($T < 1300^\circ\text{C}$ isotherm defines base). $T \approx T_{\text{solidus}}$ (Geotherm intersects/approaches mantle peridotite solidus).
Seismic Characteristic High $P$ and $S$ wave velocities; low attenuation ($Q$). Low Velocity Zone (LVZ): $v_s$ drops by $3-6\%$; high seismic attenuation ($1/Q$).
Geodynamic Function Forms the moving tectonic plates. Deformable lubricating substrate for plate motions; primary source of basaltic magma via adiabatic decompression melting.

42.2 Why "It is in solid-state" is Incorrect in Academic Testing

In standard university geological curricula and competitive exams (OPSC, UPSC GSI, GATE), the defining attribute of the Asthenosphere (from Greek asthenes, meaning "weak" or "without strength", named by Joseph Barrell in 1914) is that it is semi-molten, plastic, and capable of ductile viscous flow over geological timescales, in direct contrast to the rigid, solid lithosphere above. Stating that the asthenosphere is simply in a rigid "solid-state" is considered fundamentally misleading and incorrect in this context.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q42. Which of the following is incorrect about Asthenosphere ?
(A) It is present in the upper mantle
(B) It is in solid-state
(C) It is in a semi-molten stage
(D) It is the source of volcanic activity
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) It is in solid-state — Stating that the Asthenosphere is in a rigid "solid-state" is incorrect; the asthenosphere is characteristically a plastic, semi-molten (or partially molten rheid) layer that undergoes ductile flow.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) It is present in the upper mantle — CORRECT STATEMENT: The asthenosphere is situated within the upper mantle, immediately below the lithosphere from $\sim 100\text{ km}$ down to the $410\text{ km}$ mantle discontinuity.
  • (B) It is in solid-state — INCORRECT STATEMENT (Required Answer): The asthenosphere behaves as a plastic, semi-molten rheid with interstitial partial melting ($1-2\%$) that generates the seismic Low Velocity Zone (LVZ); describing it as rigid solid-state denies its defining rheological nature.
  • (C) It is in a semi-molten stage — CORRECT STATEMENT: Partial melting along grain boundaries gives the asthenosphere its low viscosity and seismic velocity drop.
  • (D) It is the source of volcanic activity — CORRECT STATEMENT: Upwelling and adiabatic decompression melting of the asthenosphere generates mid-ocean ridge basalts (MORB) and supplies hotspot volcanism.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

High-Yield Mantle Rheology Problem

Practice Problem 42: Viscous Relaxation Time of the Asthenosphere

Post-glacial rebound of the Fennoscandian shield yields an estimated asthenospheric dynamic viscosity of $\eta = 1.0 \times 10^{20}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ and an effective shear modulus of $\mu = 7.0 \times 10^{10}\text{ Pa}$. Calculate the Maxwell viscoelastic relaxation time ($\tau_M$) of the asthenosphere and interpret its physical meaning.

Solution:

1. The Maxwell relaxation time is given by:

$$\tau_M = \frac{\eta}{\mu} = \frac{1.0 \times 10^{20}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}}{7.0 \times 10^{10}\text{ Pa}} \approx 1.43 \times 10^9\text{ seconds}$$

2. Convert seconds to years ($1\text{ yr} \approx 3.156 \times 10^7\text{ s}$):

$$\tau_M = \frac{1.43 \times 10^9}{3.156 \times 10^7} \approx 45.3\text{ years}$$

Physical Interpretation: For loading periods much shorter than $\tau_M$ (such as high-frequency seismic waves with periods of seconds), the asthenosphere responds elastically (transmitting P and S waves). For loading periods much longer than $\tau_M$ (such as mantle convection and plate motion over millions of years), elastic stresses relax to zero and the asthenosphere behaves completely as a viscous fluid rheid.
