2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Monomineralic vs Ultrabasic Rocks

In igneous and metamorphic petrology, rock classifications depend on silica percentage ($SiO_2$) and modal mineral proportions:

  • Chemical Classification by Silica:
    • Ultrabasic: $SiO_2 < 45\%$, very rich in $Mg, Fe, Ca$ (e.g., Dunite, Pyroxenite, Peridotite).
    • Basic: $SiO_2 = 45 ext{--}52\%$ (Gabbro, Basalt, Anorthosite).
    • Intermediate: $SiO_2 = 52 ext{--}66\%$ (Diorite, Andesite).
    • Acidic: $SiO_2 > 66\%$ (Granite, Rhyolite).
  • Monomineralic Rocks: Rocks composed predominantly of a single mineral ($>90\%$):
    • Dunite: $>90\%$ Olivine $ ightarrow$ Monomineralic and Ultrabasic ($SiO_2 pprox 40\%$).
    • Pyroxenite: $>90\%$ Pyroxene $ ightarrow$ Monomineralic and Ultrabasic ($SiO_2 pprox 44 ext{--}48\%$).
    • Anorthosite: $>90\%$ Plagioclase feldspar $ ightarrow$ Monomineralic but NOT ultrabasic; it is a basic-to-felsic rock ($SiO_2 = 48 ext{--}55\%$, rich in $Al_2O_3$ and $CaO/Na_2O$).
  • Note on Leptynite: Leptynite is a granulitic metamorphic rock consisting of quartz, feldspar, and garnet (a polymineralic acid granulite). In standard Indian geology question banks (e.g., Bangar, Mahapatra), Anorthosite is universally taught as the prime example of a monomineralic rock that is not ultrabasic.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option A (Anorthosite) is correct: Anorthosite is composed of $\ge 90\%$ plagioclase, making it monomineralic, but it is basic/felsic ($SiO_2 > 45\%$, high $Al_2O_3$), NOT ultrabasic.
  • Option B (Pyroxinite): Monomineralic ultrabasic rock composed of $>90\%$ pyroxene.
  • Option D (Dunite): Monomineralic ultrabasic rock composed of $>90\%$ olivine.
  • Option C (Leptynite): Granulite-facies metamorphic rock (polymineralic quartzo-feldspathic gneiss).
