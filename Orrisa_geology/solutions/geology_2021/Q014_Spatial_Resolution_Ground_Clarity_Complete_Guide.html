Q14: Spatial Resolution & Geometric Ground Clarity in Remote Sensing

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman (Remote Sensing & Image Interpretation, Ch. 1, pp. 30-35)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

14.1 The Four Fundamental Resolutions in Remote Sensing

In digital image processing (Lillesand, Kiefer & Chipman, Remote Sensing, Ch. 1, pp. 30–35), sensor performance is defined by four orthogonal resolutions:

Resolution Dimension Physical Definition & Governing Metric Primary Impact on Image Utility
Spatial Resolution Size of the smallest ground area recorded by a detector (Instantaneous Field of View, IFOV; Ground Sampling Distance, GSD) Clarity, geometric discernibility, and edge sharpness of ground objects
Spectral Resolution Width and number of discrete wavelength bands recorded across the electromagnetic spectrum ($\Delta \lambda$) Ability to discriminate distinct rock types, minerals, and chemical compositions
Radiometric Resolution Bit-depth sensitivity to subtle differences in radiance intensity ($8\text{-bit} = 256$, $12\text{-bit} = 4096$) Contrast sensitivity in deep shadow and bright snow/salt domains
Temporal Resolution Revisit period between consecutive image acquisitions over the same geographic location Monitoring dynamic seasonal changes, floods, landslides, and volcanic eruptions

14.2 Ground Sampling Distance (GSD) & Nyquist Criterion

The ground clarity of an object requires that its dimension exceeds approximately $2.0 \times \text{GSD}$ (the two-dimensional Nyquist sampling limit) to be reliably resolved and identified without spatial aliasing.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q14. Clarity of ground object will depend upon :
(A) Spatial Resolution
(B) Spectral Resolution
(C) Temporal Resolution
(D) Radiometric Resolution
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (A)

Correct Answer: (A) Spatial Resolution — The clarity, sharpness, and geometric recognizability of individual ground objects on an image depends directly upon its Spatial Resolution.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Spatial Resolution — CORRECT: Directly dictates the minimum ground sampling distance and resolving power of individual spatial objects.
  • (B) Spectral Resolution — INCORRECT: Governs the ability to identify chemical composition and absorption bands, not geometric sharpness.
  • (C) Temporal Resolution — INCORRECT: Governs the frequency of overpasses (revisit interval), not image clarity.
  • (D) Radiometric Resolution — INCORRECT: Governs brightness quantization and bit depth, not ground detail size.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

Photogrammetric Resolution Practice Problem

Practice Problem 14: Ground Sampling Distance (GSD) Calculation

An airborne aerial camera with focal length $f = 150\text{ mm}$ flies at an altitude of $H = 3,000\text{ m}$ above mean terrain. If the physical detector pixel pitch is $p = 6.0\ \mu\text{m}$, calculate the Ground Sampling Distance (GSD).

Solution:

$$\text{GSD} = p \cdot \frac{H}{f} = (6.0 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}) \cdot \frac{3000\text{ m}}{0.150\text{ m}} = 6.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 20,000 = 0.12\text{ m} = 12\text{ cm}$$

The spatial resolution of the camera at this flying height is $12\text{ cm}$ per pixel.
