2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Transverse Strength (Modulus of Rupture)

In engineering geology, civil engineering specifications, and Indian Standards (IS: 1706 / IS: 3316), the capacity of a structural stone or rock slab to withstand transverse bending forces without fracturing is formally designated as its Transverse strength (or flexural strength / modulus of rupture):

  • Testing Principle (Three-Point Bending Test): A rectangular stone beam or slab of width $b$, depth $d$, and span length $L$ is supported at both ends and subjected to a central concentrated vertical load $W$: $$f_b = \frac{3 W L}{2 b d^2}$$ where $f_b$ is the transverse strength (modulus of rupture).
  • Engineering Importance:
    • Critical for flagstones, lintels, roof slabs, pavements, and cantilever stone structures where loads act perpendicularly to the longitudinal axis, inducing compressive stress on the upper surface and tensile stress on the lower surface.
    • Because rock is notoriously weak in tension ($\sigma_t \approx 10\%\,\sigma_c$), failure in bending always initiates at the lower tensile surface.
  • In standard engineering geology textbooks (e.g., Bangar, p. 187; Mahapatra, p. 280), this property is officially titled Transverse strength.

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option C (Transverse strength) is correct: The formal, standardized technical term in engineering geology for resistance to bending loads.
  • Option A (Bending strength): Colloquial term, but "Transverse strength" is the established engineering designation.
  • Option B (Shear strength): Resistance to sliding along internal slip planes.
  • Option D (Lateral strength): Non-standard descriptive term.
