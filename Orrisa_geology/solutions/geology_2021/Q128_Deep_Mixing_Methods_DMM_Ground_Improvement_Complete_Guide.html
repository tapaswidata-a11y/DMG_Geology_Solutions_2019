2. Detailed Geological Masterclass: Deep Mixing Methods (DMM) in Soil Stabilization

Deep Mixing Methods (DMM) represent an advanced in-situ soil stabilization technology invented in Japan and the Nordic countries, now employed globally in heavy civil engineering:

  • Operational Process: Large hollow rotating augers with mixing paddles penetrate deep into weak ground (soft clays, peats, loose sands) and mechanically blend the in-situ soil with cementitious or lime binder slurries (wet mixing) or dry powder (dry mixing).
  • Engineered Mass (Soil-Cement Columns): This hardens in-situ into overlapping soil-cement columns or continuous lattice block structures (mass stabilization), transforming weak cohesive soils into a hardened composite material.
  • Broad Multi-Functional Applications:
    • Strength & Settlement Control: Drastically elevates unconfined compressive strength and shear modulus while eliminating post-construction settlement under bridge abutments, railway embankments, and highway corridors.
    • Liquefaction Mitigation: Creates continuous stiff shear lattices that constrain seismic cyclic shear strain in loose saturated sands.
    • Seismic & Vibration Attenuation: Dissipates high-speed railway and traffic dynamic vibrations.
    • Slope Stability & Seepage Cut-off: Stabilizes deep rotational landslides and creates low-permeability ($K < 10^{-8}\text{ m/s}$) hydraulic barriers.
  • Permeation grouting only works in clean gravels/sands, hydro-fracture causes uncontrolled heave fractures, and compensation grouting is localized under buildings during tunnel mining. DMM is the comprehensive ground improvement technology matching all criteria.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

3. Critical Option Elimination & Exam Traps

  • Option C (Deep Mixing Methods - DMM) is correct: The internationally standard ground improvement technology used for foundation support, liquefaction mitigation, slope stability, and vibration reduction.
  • Option A (Permeation Grouting): Limited to porous coarse sands/gravels; cannot be mixed into soft cohesive clays.
  • Option B (Hydro Fracture Grouting): High-pressure fracturing used for targeted directional lifting, not mass soil-cement column stabilization.
  • Option D (Compensation Grouting): Real-time corrective injection to counteract tunnel excavation settlement.
