Q8: Trellis Drainage Pattern in Dipping and Folded Formations

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: K.M. Bangar (Engineering Geology, Ch. 4, pp. 58-61); Arthur N. Strahler (Physical Geology); Mahapatra (Ch. 3)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

8.1 Drainage Patterns Governed by Geological Structure

In structural geomorphology (Arthur N. Strahler, Physical Geology; K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology, Ch. 4; G.B. Mahapatra, Ch. 3), drainage patterns reflect the underlying bedrock lithology and structural deformation:

Drainage Pattern Underlying Structural & Lithologic Control Tributary Junction Angles
Trellis Alternating bands of resistant and weak dipping/folded strata. Primary streams align with strike valleys; secondary tributaries flow down dip and scarp slopes. Orthogonal / Right angles ($~90^\circ$)
Parallel Steep, uniformly dipping planar slopes (e.g. young fault blocks, volcanic cones). Parallel, acute low angles
Rectangular Heavily jointed or faulted homogeneous crystalline bedrock (granite, basalt). Sharp right-angle bends along intersecting joint sets
Dendritic Homogeneous, isotropic bedrock or flat-lying horizontal sedimentary strata. Irregular, acute branch-like branching

8.2 Strike and Dip Stream Classification

  • Subsequent Streams: Flow along the strike of softer, easily erodible formations (forming the trunk parallel valleys).
  • Consequent & Resequent Streams: Flow down the original regional dip slope of the formation.
  • Obsequent Streams: Flow down the steep scarp slope opposite to the formation dip direction.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q8. If a network of parallel streams is developed along strike and dip of the formations, the drainage pattern is referred to as :
(A) Parallel
(B) Rectangular
(C) Trellis
(D) Dendritic
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (C)

Correct Answer: (C) Trellis — When a network of parallel streams develops along the strike and dip of tilted or folded formations, the drainage pattern is designated as a Trellis drainage pattern.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Parallel — INCORRECT: Parallel networks occur on uniform steep slopes without orthogonal strike-dip tributary grids.
  • (B) Rectangular — INCORRECT: Rectangular patterns are governed by intersecting joint and fault fractures in uniform rock, not alternating strike-dip sedimentary cuestas.
  • (C) Trellis — CORRECT: The textbook definition of a trellis pattern is an orthogonal network where main subsequent streams follow the strike of soft strata and short tributaries follow the dip and scarp directions.
  • (D) Dendritic — INCORRECT: Dendritic patterns resemble branching tree roots and form only in structurally isotropic or horizontal rock formations.

Practice Problem 8: Appalachian Type Folded Belt Drainage

In the Appalachian Valley and Ridge Province, what type of drainage pattern overwhelmingly dominates, and which structural features control it?

Answer:

Trellis pattern. It is controlled by plunging anticlines and synclines where hard ridge-forming sandstones/quartzites alternate with soft valley-forming shales and limestones.
