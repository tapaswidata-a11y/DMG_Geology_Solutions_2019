Q21: Structural Geology: Thrust Fault vs. High-Angle Reverse Fault Dip Angle (< 45°)

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: Haakon Fossen (Structural Geology, Ch. 14, pp. 296-302); Van der Pluijm & Marshak (Ch. 9); Bangar (Ch. 9, pp. 175-182)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

21.1 Geometric & Kinematic Definitions of Compressional Faults

In structural geology and fault mechanics (Haakon Fossen, Structural Geology, 2010, Ch. 14, pp. 296–302; Van der Pluijm & Marshak, Earth Structure, Ch. 9; Bangar, Ch. 9), compressional dip-slip faults where the hanging wall moves upward relative to the footwall are subdivided by fault dip angle ($\theta$):

Fault Classification Fault Dip Angle ($\theta$) Tectonic Context & Typical Kinematics
Reverse Fault (Sensu Stricto) $\theta > 45^\circ$ (typically $60^\circ$) High-angle crustal shortening, basement-involved block uplifts
Thrust Fault $\theta < 45^\circ$ (typically $15^\circ - 30^\circ$) Low-angle regional contractional orogenic belts (fold-and-thrust belts, subduction accretionary prisms)
Overthrust / Nappe $\theta < 10^\circ - 15^\circ$ (often near-horizontal) Large-scale regional displacement exceeding tens to hundreds of kilometers

21.2 Anderson's Mechanical Model of Faulting

According to E.M. Anderson (1905, 1951), thrust faulting occurs when the maximum principal stress is horizontal ($\sigma_1 = \sigma_{xx}$) and the minimum principal stress is vertical ($\sigma_3 = \sigma_{zz} = \rho g z$).

The theoretical Coulomb fracture angle ($\theta_c$) with the horizontal plane (direction of $\sigma_1$) is:

$$\theta_c = 45^\circ - \frac{\phi}{2}$$

For typical crustal rocks with internal friction angle $\phi \approx 30^\circ$, the predicted thrust fault dip angle is:

$$\theta_c = 45^\circ - 15^\circ = 30^\circ < 45^\circ$$

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q21. Thrust faults are reverse faults that dip at :
(A) 45°
(B) < 45°
(C) > 30°
(D) Both (A) and (B) are correct
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) < 45° — In standard structural geology (Fossen; Billings; Bangar), a reverse fault dipping at an angle less than 45° (< 45°) is specifically designated as a Thrust fault.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) 45° — INCORRECT: 45° is the geometric boundary dividing high-angle and low-angle dip-slip faults.
  • (B) < 45° — CORRECT: The universal structural definition of a thrust fault is a reverse fault dipping at $< 45^\circ$.
  • (C) > 30° — INCORRECT: Most active thrust faults dip at $< 30^\circ$, making this option factually incorrect.
  • (D) Both (A) and (B) are correct — INCORRECT: 45° is not included in the standard definition.

Part III: Synthesis Reference Tables & High-Yield Practice

Structural Geometry Practice Problem

Practice Problem 21: Fault Shortening & Heave Calculation

A planar thrust fault dipping at $\theta = 30^\circ$ exhibits a dip-slip net slip of $S = 200\text{ meters}$. Calculate the vertical throw and horizontal heave (crustal shortening) produced by the fault.

Solution:

$$\text{Vertical Throw} = S \cdot \sin \theta = 200 \cdot \sin(30^\circ) = 200 \times 0.5 = 100\text{ meters}$$ $$\text{Horizontal Heave (Shortening)} = S \cdot \cos \theta = 200 \cdot \cos(30^\circ) = 200 \times 0.866 = 173.2\text{ meters}$$

Because $\theta < 45^\circ$, the horizontal shortening ($173.2\text{ m}$) significantly exceeds the vertical displacement ($100\text{ m}$).
