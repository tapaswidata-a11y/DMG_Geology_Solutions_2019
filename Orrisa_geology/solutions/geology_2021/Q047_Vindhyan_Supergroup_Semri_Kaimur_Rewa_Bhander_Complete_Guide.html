1. The Vindhyan Supergroup: Architecture of India's Premier Purana Basin

The Vindhyan Supergroup occupies a vast, crescent-shaped intracratonic sag basin (the Great Vindhyan Basin) covering over $104,000\text{ km}^2$ across Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Bihar. Resting unconformably over Archean granitic basement (Bundelkhand Gneiss) and Paleoproterozoic folded metasediments (Bijawar and Gwalior Groups), it represents an undeformed to gently warped Proterozoic shallow marine-to-continental sedimentary pile up to $4,500\text{ m}$ thick:

  • Classical Subdivisions: Stratigraphically, the Vindhyan Supergroup is divided into two primary divisions and four canonical groups (in ascending stratigraphic order):
    1. Lower Vindhyan:
      • Semri Group: Dominated by marine carbonate-clastic cycles (basal conglomerate, Kajrahat Limestone, Porcellanite Formation, Olive Shales, Fawn Limestone, and Rohtas Limestone). Contains diverse stromatolites, microfossils, and volcaniclastic tuffs dated between $\sim 1700\text{ Ma}$ and $1600\text{ Ma}$.
    2. Upper Vindhyan: Separated from the Semri Group by a regional erosional unconformity marked by the disappearance of extensive marine carbonates and the onset of mature clastic sedimentation:
      • Kaimur Group: Dominated by quartzose sandstones, conglomerates, and orthoquartzites (Dhandraul Quartzite) recording high-energy fluvial to coastal barrier regimes ($\sim 1150-1000\text{ Ma}$).
      • Rewa Group: Rhythmic alternation of shales and sandstones (Panna Shale, Asan Sandstone, Jhiri Shale, Govindgarh Sandstone). Famous for diamondiferous conglomerates at Panna.
      • Bhander Group: The uppermost group, marking the return of marine tidal carbonate-evaporite sedimentation (Ganurgarh Shale, Bhander Limestone, Sirbu Shale, Upper Bhander Sandstone) terminating around $\sim 650-550\text{ Ma}$ (Ediacaran).

2. Lithological Characteristics and Economic Minerals

Each of the four Vindhyan groups hosts diagnostic lithologies and critical industrial minerals:

The Ascending Quadripartite Vindhyan Succession
$$\text{Archean Basement} \xrightarrow{\text{Great Eparchean Interval}} \mathbf{Semri} \xrightarrow{\text{Unconformity}} \mathbf{Kaimur} \xrightarrow{\text{Conformity}} \mathbf{Rewa} \xrightarrow{\text{Conformity}} \mathbf{Bhander}$$

Rule: Semri (Carbonates + Volcanics) $\to$ Kaimur (Arenaceous) $\to$ Rewa (Argillaceous + Arenaceous) $\to$ Bhander (Calcareous + Arenaceous).

  • Diamondiferous Conglomerates: The famous Panna diamond mines of Madhya Pradesh occur in conglomerates at the base of the Rewa Group (Panna Diamond Belt) and the base of the Bhander Group, derived from Kimberlite/Lamproite pipes (e.g., Majhgawan pipe, dated $\sim 1070\text{ Ma}$).
  • Portland Cement Raw Material: High-grade calcitic limestones of the Semri (Rohtas Limestone, Kajrahat Limestone) and Bhander (Bhander Limestone) support India's largest cement manufacturing clusters.
  • Architectural Building Stones: The durable, uniformly grained red and buff sandstones of the Kaimur and Bhander groups provided the construction stones for India's historic monuments, including the Red Fort of Delhi, the Sanchi Stupa, and the Rashtrapati Bhavan.

Official Examination Question
Question 47
The Vindhyan Supergroup is divisible into four groups viz., Semri Group, Kaimur Group, Rewa Group and _____.
(A) Bijawar Group
(B) Bhander Group
(C) Gwalior Group
(D) None of these
Verified Correct Answer: (B) Bhander Group [The four groups of the Vindhyan Supergroup in ascending order are the Semri Group, Kaimur Group, Rewa Group, and Bhander Group.]
Correct Option: (B) Bhander Group

Detailed Stratigraphic Forensic Analysis:

  • The Vindhyan Supergroup is universally recognized across all Indian geological surveys and textbooks (Auden 1933; Krishnan 1982; Ramakrishnan & Vaidyanadhan 2008; Mahapatra 2019) as comprising exactly four constituent groups in ascending chronological order:
    1. Semri Group (Lower Vindhyan)
    2. Kaimur Group (Upper Vindhyan)
    3. Rewa Group (Upper Vindhyan)
    4. Bhander Group (Upper Vindhyan)
  • The Bhander Group is the fourth and uppermost group of the Vindhyan Supergroup, composed of the Ganurgarh Shale, Bhander Limestone, Sirbu Shale, and Upper Bhander Sandstone.
  • Therefore, the missing fourth group is indisputably the Bhander Group.
Incorrect Distractor: (A) Bijawar Group

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Bijawar Group is a Paleoproterozoic metasedimentary sequence (quartzites, dolomites, ferruginous beds, and basaltic traps) that forms the folded structural basement underlying the Vindhyan Supergroup in central India.
  • The Vindhyan Semri Group rests unconformably upon the deformed Bijawar Group with an intervening angular unconformity. Thus, Bijawar is not a constituent group of the Vindhyan Supergroup.
Incorrect Distractor: (C) Gwalior Group

Detailed Refutation:

  • The Gwalior Group (comprising the Par Formation and Morar Formation with banded iron formations and volcanic sills) is another Paleoproterozoic sedimentary-volcanic succession in northern Madhya Pradesh.
  • Like the Bijawar, the Gwalior Group unconformably underlies the Kaimur Group of the Vindhyan Supergroup. It is an older basement succession, not a division of the Vindhyan Supergroup.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) None of these

Detailed Refutation:

  • Because Option (B) Bhander Group is the exact, canonical fourth member of the Vindhyan Supergroup, selecting "None of these" is factually incorrect.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Purana Stratigraphy & Sedimentary Basins
Problem 1: Geochronological Dating of the Semri Porcellanite

High-precision U-Pb zircon SHRIMP dating of felsic volcanic tuffs in the Porcellanite Formation of the Semri Group yields an age of $1630 \pm 5\text{ Ma}$. What does this establish regarding the age of the base of the Vindhyan Supergroup?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Historically, the Vindhyan Supergroup was considered Neoproterozoic to early Cambrian. The $1630\text{ Ma}$ U-Pb zircon age from the Porcellanite Formation (located in the lower-middle Semri Group) conclusively proved that the base of the Vindhyan Supergroup initiated in the Paleoproterozoic (Statherian Period), extending the duration of Vindhyan sedimentation across nearly $1\text{ billion years}$ of Earth history.

Problem 2: The Great Boundary Fault (GBF) of Rajasthan

What major structural tectonic dislocation bounds the Vindhyan Basin along its northwestern margin in Rajasthan?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The northwestern margin of the Vindhyan Basin is truncated by the Great Boundary Fault (GBF) of Rajasthan. This is a regional, northeast-trending, post-Vindhyan reverse fault/thrust exceeding $800\text{ km}$ in length that brings deformed pre-Vindhyan rocks (Aravalli/Delhi/Bhilwara metamorphic belts) into juxtaposition against undeformed Upper Vindhyan strata (Bhander and Kaimur groups).

Problem 3: Glauconite Mineralogy in the Semri Group

Glauconitic sandstones occur prominently in the Semri and Kaimur groups. What does the presence of authigenic glauconite indicate about the paleoenvironment?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Authigenic glauconite is a potassium iron phyllosilicate mineral that precipitates exclusively in marine shelf environments with slow, starved sedimentation rates (condensed sections), mild reducing (suboxic) conditions, and normal marine salinities at water depths typically between $50\text{ m}$ and $300\text{ m}$.

Problem 4: Diamond Source in the Panna Diamond Belt

In which igneous rock body did the diamonds found in the Rewa Group conglomerates originate, and what is its age?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The primary source of the diamonds is the Majhgawan and Hinota Kimberlite/Lamproite pipes intruded into the Paleoproterozoic basement. Radiometric dating (Rb-Sr and Ar-Ar) dates the Majhgawan intrusion at $\sim 1070\text{ Ma}$. Weathering and fluvial erosion of these pipes during Rewa Group sedimentation concentrated diamonds into basal fluvial gravels, forming the diamondiferous conglomerates.

Problem 5: Ediacaran Trace Fossil Debate in the Vindhyans

What controversy arose regarding the discovery of trace fossils and carbonaceous discs (Chuaria circularis) in the Rohtas and Bhander shales?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The discovery of circular carbonaceous compressions (Chuaria, Tawuia) and putative trace fossils in the Rohtas Formation (Semri) and Bhander Group led some workers to propose a late Neoproterozoic/Cambrian age for the whole sequence. However, Chuaria is now recognized as a long-ranging megascopic eukaryotic alga that extends back to the Mesoproterozoic ($\sim 1400\text{ Ma}$), fully compatible with modern radiometric age constraints.

Problem 6: Sedimentological Maturity of the Dhandraul Quartzite

The Dhandraul Quartzite of the Kaimur Group exhibits quartz arenite composition ($>98\% \text{ quartz}$) and well-rounded grains. What does this signify about the depositional and tectonic regime?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Extreme compositional and textural maturity (quartz arenites with well-rounded, well-sorted monocrystalline quartz) indicates intense chemical weathering of the source area, prolonged polycyclic sediment recycling, and deposition on a stable, slowly subsiding cratonic shelf under high-energy wave and tidal action.

Problem 7: Distinction Between Purana Basins and Gondwana Basins

Contrast the tectonic basins of the Proterozoic Purana Supergroups (Vindhyan, Cuddapah) with the Phanerozoic Gondwana Basins.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Purana Basins: Broad intracratonic sag/flexural basins; Proterozoic in age; dominated by marine carbonates, mature orthoquartzites, and shales; devoid of coal.
  • Gondwana Basins: Linear fault-bounded grabens and half-grabens; Permian to Early Cretaceous in age; dominated by continental fluvial, lacustrine, and glacial clastics; host to India's major bituminous coal seams.
Problem 8: Identification of Evaporite Facies in the Sirbu Shale

What sedimentary structures in the Sirbu Shale and Bhander Limestone demonstrate arid sabkha depositional conditions?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The occurrence of halite pseudomorphs (hopper crystal casts), gypsum discoidal casts, desiccation mud cracks, and flat-pebble intraformational conglomerates in the Sirbu Shale and Upper Bhander strata proves episodic subaerial exposure and intense evaporation on supratidal sabkha mudflats.
