Q47: Vindhyan Supergroup Lithostratigraphy: Semri, Kaimur, Rewa & Bhander

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: J.B. Auden (Vindhyan Sedimentation, 1933); Ramakrishnan & Vaidyanadhan (Vol. 1, Ch. 12); M.S. Krishnan (Ch. 7)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

47.1 The Mesoproterozoic-Neoproterozoic Vindhyan Supergroup

In Indian Precambrian stratigraphy (Auden, 1933; Ramakrishnan & Vaidyanadhan, Geology of India, Vol. 1, Ch. 12; M.S. Krishnan, Ch. 7), the Vindhyan Supergroup represents one of the world's most spectacular, undeformed intracratonic Purana sedimentary basins, covering $> 100,000\text{ km}^2$ in central India. Resting with profound unconformity upon the Archean Bundelkhand Granite and Bijawar Group, it is subdivided into four well-defined groups:

Supergroup Division Group Name Dominant Lithologies Major Formations & Economic Minerals
UPPER VINDHYAN
(Predominantly Arenaceous)		 4. Bhander Group (Top) Shales, sandstones, stromatolitic limestones. Upper Bhander Sandstone (premier building stone: Red Fort, Sanchi Stupa), Sirbu Shale, Bhander Limestone.
3. Rewa Group Quartzites, sandstones, and carbonaceous/ferruginous shales. Govindgarh Sandstone, Jhiri Shale, Panna Shale (diamondiferous conglomerate horizons).
2. Kaimur Group Thick, massive orthoquartzites, arenites, and subordinate shales. Dhandraul Quartzite, Scarp Sandstone, Bijaigarh Shale (pyrite-bearing).
LOWER VINDHYAN
(Calc-Argillaceous)		 1. Semri Group (Base) Carbonates, volcaniclastics (porcellanites), and basal conglomerates. Rohtas Limestone, Glauconitic Sandstone, Porcellanite Formation (felsic tuffs), Kajrahat Limestone.

47.2 Structural Boundary: The Great Boundary Fault (GBF)

The western margin of the Vindhyan basin is sharply truncated against the pre-Vindhyan rocks of the Aravalli-Delhi belt by the monumental Great Boundary Fault (GBF) of Rajasthan, a regional thrust with over $800\text{ km}$ of strike length. The succession is celebrated for the world's oldest putative multicellular algae/metazoan trace fossils and pristine stromatolite assemblages (e.g. Collenia, Conophyton).

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q47. The Vindhyan Supergroup is divisible into four groups viz., Semri Group, Kaimur Group, Rewa Group and _____.
(A) Bijawar Group
(B) Bhander Group
(C) Gwalior Group
(D) None of these
VERIFIED ANSWER: OPTION (B)

Correct Answer: (B) Bhander Group — The four lithostratigraphic groups composing the Vindhyan Supergroup in ascending order are: Semri Group, Kaimur Group, Rewa Group, and Bhander Group.

Option-by-Option Critical Dissection

  • (A) Bijawar Group — INCORRECT: The Bijawar Group is a Paleoproterozoic volcanosedimentary basin sequence that forms the folded crystalline basement floor underlying the Vindhyan Supergroup.
  • (B) Bhander Group — CORRECT: The uppermost, fourth group of the Vindhyan Supergroup, completing the classical Semri-Kaimur-Rewa-Bhander succession.
  • (C) Gwalior Group — INCORRECT: The Gwalior Group is another Paleoproterozoic sequence exposed near Gwalior that unconformably underlies the Kaimur Group of the Vindhyans.
  • (D) None of these — INCORRECT: Option (B) is the universally accepted group name.

High-Yield Vindhyan Stratigraphic Mnemonic & Problem

Practice Problem 47: Chronostratigraphic Boundary & Diamondiferous Horizons

In which group of the Vindhyan Supergroup are the famous primary and secondary diamond-bearing horizons of Panna (Madhya Pradesh) located, and what is the nature of the host rock?

Solution:

1. The primary diamond source rocks in the Panna diamond belt are ultramafic kimberlite / lamproite diatreme pipes (e.g. the Majhgawan pipe, dated at $\sim 1070\text{ Ma}$), which intrude the Kaimur Group.

2. The secondary placer diamond deposits occur within the basal conglomerate beds of the Rewa Group (specifically the Panna Shale and Jhiri Shale conglomerates), derived from the erosional weathering of the kimberlite pipes prior to Rewa sedimentation.
