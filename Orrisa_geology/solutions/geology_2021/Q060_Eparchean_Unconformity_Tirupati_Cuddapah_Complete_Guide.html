1. The Eparchean Unconformity: The Great Chronological Divide of Peninsular India

In Indian stratigraphy and global Precambrian geology, the Eparchean Unconformity represents the profound, continent-wide planar erosional unconformity that separates the intensely deformed, metamorphosed, and crystalline Archean basement from the overlying undeformed to gently folded Proterozoic (Purana) sedimentary cover:

  • National Geological Monument at Tirumala (Tirupati Hills): The most spectacular, classic, and globally renowned outcrop of the Eparchean Unconformity is exposed along the Ghat road near Tirupati in the Chittoor district of Andhra Pradesh (declared a National Geological Monument by the Geological Survey of India in 2001).
  • Stratigraphic Juxtaposition:
    • Underlying Footwall: Coarse-grained, pink Archean granitic gneiss and hornblende-biotite gneisses (Peninsular Gneissic Complex, dated at $\sim 2.5\text{ Ga}$ to $2.6\text{ Ga}$) cut by basic dolerite dikes.
    • Overlying Hanging Wall: Gently dipping ($10^\circ-12^\circ$ northeast) basal quartzites and polymictic basal conglomerates of the Nagari Quartzite Formation (Nallamalai / Papaghni Groups of the Proterozoic Cuddapah Supergroup, initiated at $\sim 1.9-1.8\text{ Ga}$).
  • The Time Hiatus ($\sim 500-700\text{ Ma}$): The Eparchean Unconformity records an immense period of non-deposition, cratonic uplift, deep crustal peneplanation, and weathering spanning over 500 to 700 million years between the Archean stabilization and the initiation of Purana intracratonic sedimentation.
Figure 60.1 (G.B. Mahapatra, p. 160): The Eparchean Unconformity: Horizontal Proterozoic Cuddapah quartzites resting nonconformably on vertical Archean granite gneisses.
2. Scientific Etymology: Eparchean vs. The Great Unconformity

Question 60 tests the precise Indian geological terminology for this monument:

The Eparchean Chronological Formulation
$$\mathbf{\text{Archean Peninsular Gneiss } (\sim 2.5\text{ Ga})} \xrightarrow[\text{Hiatus } \approx 700\text{ Ma}]{\mathbf{Eparchean \text{ Unconformity}}} \mathbf{\text{Cuddapah Supergroup } (\sim 1.8\text{ Ga})}$$
  • Etymology of "Eparchean": Coined from Greek epi (meaning "upon" or "after") + Archean, denoting the fundamental transition surface capping the Archean Era across the Indian Peninsular Shield.
  • Why "The Great Unconformity" is Geographically Distinct: In international geology, "The Great Unconformity" classically refers to John Wesley Powell's (1869) discovery in the Grand Canyon (USA), separating the Paleoproterozoic Vishnu Schist from the Cambrian Tapeats Sandstone. In Indian geological literature, the specific Tirupati boundary is universally termed the Eparchean Unconformity.
Figure 60.2 (P.K. Mukherjee, p. 200): Geological cross-section documenting the Eparchean contact between the Cuddapah Supergroup and the Archean crystalline floor.

Angular Unconformity Younger horizontal strata resting on tilted or folded older sedimentary strata. Siccar Point (Hutton), Grand Canyon (Chuar over Bass), Aravalli-Delhi boundary.

Official Examination Question
Question 60
The boundary in between sedimentary rocks of the Cudappah Supergroup and Archaean rocks found near Tirupati Hills, creating a period of nondepositions and erosion of about 500 Ma has been defined as__.
(A) The Great Unconformity
(B) Eparchean Unconfornity
(C) Paraconformity
(D) None of these
Verified Correct Answer: (B) Eparchean Unconfornity [The profound unconformity between the Cuddapah Supergroup and underlying Archean granitic gneisses at Tirupati Hills is classically termed the Eparchean Unconformity.]
Correct Option: (B) Eparchean Unconfornity

Detailed Stratigraphic Forensic Analysis:

  • The boundary separating the Proterozoic sedimentary rocks of the Cuddapah Supergroup (Nagari Quartzite) from the underlying crystalline Archean basement (granites and gneisses) at Tirupati Hills (Tirumala Ghat) represents an enormous time hiatus of over 500 to 700 million years.
  • In Indian geology (Mahapatra p. 160 & 304; Mukherjee p. 200; Ramakrishnan & Vaidyanadhan Vol. 1, p. 275; GSI National Monuments), this classic contact is universally and formally designated as the Eparchean Unconformity.
  • It signifies the end of the Archean Era and the prolonged post-Archean peneplanation before the onset of Purana basin sedimentation.
  • Therefore, Option (B) Eparchean Unconfornity (spelled verbatim as in the official exam paper) is the verified, definitive correct answer.
Incorrect Distractor: (A) The Great Unconformity

Detailed Refutation:

  • While the Eparchean unconformity is physically a "great" unconformity, the formal term The Great Unconformity is historically reserved in global literature for the boundary between the Precambrian basement and Cambrian Tapeats Sandstone in the Grand Canyon of Arizona (USA).
  • In Indian stratigraphic nomenclature and national monument registers, this feature is strictly named the Eparchean Unconformity.
Incorrect Distractor: (C) Paraconformity

Detailed Refutation:

  • A paraconformity is a type of unconformity in which the strata above and below the gap are completely parallel, and the contact resembles a simple bedding plane without any physical erosional relief (recognized only through missing fossil zones).
  • At Tirupati Hills, the contact is a prominent nonconformity between crystalline igneous granite below and bedded sedimentary quartzite above, with basal conglomerates and clear erosional relief. It is not a paraconformity.
Incorrect Distractor: (D) None of these

Detailed Refutation:

  • Because Option (B) is the universally established name of the monument, Option (D) is incorrect.

High-Yield Practice Problems: Structural Unconformities & Indian Tectonics
Problem 1: Paleoweathering Crust (Saprolite) at Tirupati

What petrological feature is observed directly beneath the Eparchean Unconformity on the upper surface of the Archean granite at Tirupati?

Step-by-Step Solution:

Directly beneath the basal Nagari conglomerate, the Archean granite displays a fossil weathering zone or paleosol / saprolite profile ($1-3\text{ m}$ thick). Feldspars are chemically degraded into kaolinite clay, and biotite is oxidized into chlorite and hematite, demonstrating prolonged subaerial chemical weathering under an ancient Precambrian atmosphere prior to Cuddapah marine transgression.

Problem 2: Geochronological Calculation of Hiatus Duration

If the basement granite at Tirupati has a radiometric date of $2520 \pm 15\text{ Ma}$ and the overlying basal Cuddapah tuff has a U-Pb date of $1850 \pm 5\text{ Ma}$, calculate the exact time duration of the Eparchean hiatus.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$\text{Hiatus Duration } \Delta t = 2520\text{ Ma} - 1850\text{ Ma} = 670\text{ Ma}$$

Final Answer: The hiatus represents a time gap of $670\text{ million years}$ ($\approx 0.67\text{ billion years}$).

Problem 3: Mangampeta Barite Deposit in the Cuddapah Basin

What unique volcanogenic bedded barite deposit occurs within the Cuddapah Supergroup above the Eparchean basement?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Mangampeta Barite Deposit in the Kadapa district of Andhra Pradesh is the largest bedded barite ($\text{BaSO}_4$) deposit in the world. It occurs within the Pullampet Formation of the Cheyair/Nallamalai Group, formed by exhalative submarine barium-rich volcanic tuffs and rosettes settling in an anoxic marine basin.

Problem 4: Cuddapah Crescent Basin Geometry

What structural tectonic feature bounds the eastern convex margin of the Cuddapah Basin against the Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The eastern margin is truncated by an intense, arcuate, west-verging thrust front (Eastern Nallamalai Fold-Thrust Belt) where the high-grade metamorphic rocks of the Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt were thrust westward over the Purana sediments during the Grenvillian-age collision ($\sim 1.0\text{ Ga}$).

Problem 5: Dolerite Dike Truncation Principle

Numerous Proterozoic dolerite dikes cut through the Archean granite at Tirupati but terminate abruptly at the Eparchean contact without penetrating the overlying Nagari Quartzite. What does this demonstrate?

Step-by-Step Solution:

By the Principle of Cross-Cutting Relationships, the dolerite dike intrusion occurred *after* the crystallization of the granite, but *before* the deposition of the Nagari Quartzite. The dikes were planed off flat during the Eparchean erosional interval, proving they predate the Cuddapah Supergroup.

Problem 6: National Geological Monument Designation

Which governmental agency in India designates and preserves National Geological Monuments such as the Tirupati Eparchean Unconformity?

Step-by-Step Solution:

The Geological Survey of India (GSI), under the Ministry of Mines, Government of India, is the statutory authority that identifies, declares, and maintains National Geological Monuments for geo-heritage, educational, and scientific conservation.

Problem 7: Distinction Between Nonconformity and Disconformity

How does a nonconformity physically differ from a disconformity in engineering site investigations?

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Nonconformity: Massive, unstratified crystalline plutonic/metamorphic basement below with bedded sedimentary rock above. High shear strength contrast; potential seepage along weathered paleosol.
  • Disconformity: Stratified sedimentary rock both above and below the contact, with parallel bedding planes. Weakness is confined to the paleo-karst or erosional channel infill.
Problem 8: Peneplanation Velocity Calculation

If $15\text{ km}$ of Archean orogenic crust was stripped away by erosion to expose the deep granites at Tirupati during the $670\text{ Ma}$ Eparchean hiatus, calculate the average denudation rate in $\text{meters per million years}$ ($\text{m/Ma}$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$\text{Denudation Rate } E = \frac{15,000\text{ m}}{670\text{ Ma}} \approx 22.39\text{ m/Ma}$$

Final Answer: The average cratonic denudation rate was approximately $22.4\text{ m/Ma}$ ($0.022\text{ mm/year}$), characteristic of slow cratonic peneplanation.
