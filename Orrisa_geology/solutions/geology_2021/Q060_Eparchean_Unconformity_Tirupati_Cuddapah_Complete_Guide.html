Q60: Regional Unconformities - Eparchean Unconformity at Tirupati Hills

Exam: Geology Paper-II (2021)  |  Authoritative Reference: G.B. Mahapatra (A Text-Book of Geology, p. 304); P.K. Mukherjee (A Text Book of Geology, p. 200)

Part I: Theoretical Foundation & Concept Masterclass

1. The Eparchean Interval in Indian Stratigraphy

The term Eparchean Unconformity (first introduced into Indian geological nomenclature by Sir Thomas H. Holland and later codified by M.S. Krishnan and C.S. Fox) designates the profound, continent-wide angular unconformity and nonconformity that separates the intensely deformed, high-grade crystalline metamorphic basement of Archean age (>2.5 Ga) from the overlying unmetamorphosed to weakly deformed sedimentary and volcanic cover rocks of the Proterozoic Purana Supergroups (Cuddapah, Vindhyan, Kaladgi, Bhima, and Gwalior).

The term "Eparchean" derives from the Greek prefix epi- ("upon" or "after"), denoting the immense period of non-deposition, regional crustal stabilization, deep subaerial weathering, and peneplanation that intervened between the Archean cratonic consolidation and the initiation of Purana intracratonic basin development—a temporal hiatus spanning roughly 500 to 800 million years!

2. The Classic Type Section: Tirupati / Tirumala Hills

While the Eparchean Unconformity can be traced along the margins of several Purana basins, its most dramatic, pristine, and accessible exposure in the world is located in the sacred Tirumala-Tirupati Hills in Chittoor (Tirupati) district, Andhra Pradesh (G.B. Mahapatra, A Text-Book of Geology, Chapter 83, p. 304 / PDF p. 160; P.K. Mukherjee, A Text Book of Geology, p. 200). Recognized by the Geological Survey of India as a National Geological Monument, the exposure reveals:

  • The Basement: Highly deformed, steeply foliated, pink coarse-grained Archean granites and granodiorites (Dharwar Craton / Closepet Granite equivalents, dated at ~2.5 to 2.6 Ga) cut by basic dolerite dykes.
  • The Contact: An extraordinarily planar, horizontal erosional boundary representing an ancient Precambrian peneplain.
  • The Cover: The basal, sub-horizontal sedimentary beds of the Cuddapah Supergroup—specifically the Nagari Quartzite (and basal Gulcheru Conglomerate), consisting of massive, ripple-marked, purple to flesh-colored orthoquartzites and oligomictic conglomerates dated at ~1.7 to 1.8 Ga.

The boundary documents that the Archean mountains had been eroded completely flat to sea level before the Proterozoic Cuddapah sea transgressed across the cratonic interior.

Part II: Official Question & Critical Option Dissection

Q60. The boundary in between sedimentary rocks of the Cudappah Supergroup and Archaean rocks found near Tirupati Hills, creating a period of nondepositions and erosion of about 500 Ma has been defined as__.
(A) The Great Unconformity
(B) Eparchean Unconfornity
(C) Paraconformity
(D) None of these

Exam Question Analysis

Prompt: "The boundary in between sedimentary rocks of the Cudappah Supergroup and Archaean rocks found near Tirupati Hills, creating a period of nondepositions and erosion of about 500 Ma has been defined as________."

Option-by-Option Scientific Dissection

  • (A) The Great Unconformity (INCORRECT): In global geological literature, "The Great Unconformity" classically refers to the profound unconformity exposed in the Grand Canyon (USA), where the Cambrian Tapeats Sandstone rests upon the Paleoproterozoic Vishnu Schist / Zoroaster Granite. In Indian geology, the specific boundary between the Archean basement and Cuddapah rocks is designated the Eparchean Unconformity.
  • (B) Eparchean Unconfornity (CORRECT): The profound unconformity between the crystalline Archean basement and the basal Cuddapah sediments at Tirupati Hills is universally defined in Indian stratigraphy as the Eparchean Unconformity (Eparchean interval). It represents a critical ~500 to 800 Ma hiatus of regional uplift and erosional peneplanation.
  • (C) Paraconformity (INCORRECT): A paraconformity is a subtle stratigraphic break between parallel sedimentary strata where no erosional surface is discernable and the hiatus is recognized purely through biostratigraphic fossil gaps. The Tirupati boundary is a dramatic, visible nonconformity with crystalline granites directly underlying bedded quartzites.
  • (D) None of these (INCORRECT): Option B is the definitive historical geological terminology.
