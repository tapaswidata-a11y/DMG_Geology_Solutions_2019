Turbidite Facies: Deep-Marine Sedimentary Wackes: Fundamental Principles & Mechanics

Turbidity currents are catastrophic, subaqueous sediment-gravity density flows driven by the excess density of a sediment-water suspension relative to the surrounding ambient water. Typically initiated on continental slopes or submarine canyon heads by seismic ground shaking, storm wave loading, or sediment overloading, these turbulent slurries accelerate downslope under gravity, cutting submarine canyons and spreading out onto abyssal plains in deep-sea submarine fan complexes.

The definitive sedimentary deposit generated by a turbidity current is a graywacke (also called wacke). Petrographically, graywackes are texturally and mineralogically immature sandstones defined by poorly sorted, angular to subangular clastic framework grains (quartz, feldspar, and lithic rock fragments) suspended within an abundant microcrystalline matrix (>15% to 75%) composed of chlorite, sericite, and argillaceous clay minerals.

Deceleration of a turbidity current produces the characteristic Bouma sequence ($T_a$ through $T_e$). The basal $T_a$ division exhibits massive to normal graded bedding, succeeded upward by parallel planar lamination ($T_b$), climbing ripple and convoluted lamination ($T_c$), faint upper parallel lamination ($T_d$), and finally pelagic hemipelagic mud settling ($T_e$). Scour marks such as flute casts and groove casts are preferentially carved into underlying muds at the sole of $T_a$ graywacke beds.

""A turbidity current is a density current in water caused by the presence of suspended sediment. Turbidites characteristically form poorly sorted, matrix-rich sandstones termed graywackes, exhibiting graded bedding (the Bouma cycle) and diagnostic sole markings."" — Sam Boggs Jr., Principles of Sedimentology and Stratigraphy (5th Ed., Ch. 5, pp. 125–135)

Verified Official Answer: (D) Graywacke _
Graywacke (Turbidity currents transport poorly sorted, clay-rich detritus into deep-water basins forming graded graywackes)
(A) Shale ae Incorrect Distractor
Shale: Shale is a fine-grained, laminated, fissile siliciclastic rock formed predominantly of silt and clay minerals deposited under extremely quiescent, low-energy standing water conditions (such as deep basinal pelagic settling or quiet lagoons), not by high-velocity turbulent sediment-gravity density flows.
(B) Limestone Incorrect Distractor
Limestone: Limestone is a chemical or biogenic carbonate sedimentary rock composed largely of calcite or aragonite precipitated in warm, shallow, clear, tropical marine waters devoid of terrigenous siliciclastic mud influx, completely unlike deep-marine siliciclastic turbidite flows.
(C) Arkose oe Incorrect Distractor
Arkose: Arkose is an immature, feldspar-rich sandstone containing >25% feldspar, typically deposited in high-energy proximal continental alluvial fans or fluvial braided river systems adjacent to rapidly uplifting granitic basement horsts, rather than deep-marine basinal environments.
(D) Graywacke _ Correct Verified Option
Graywacke: Turbidity currents characteristically deposit graywackes. Rapid deceleration of the density flow dumps poorly sorted, angular sand grains simultaneously with an abundant (>15%) muddy matrix, forming diagnostic Bouma-graded turbidite beds with sole markings.

High-Yield Practice Problems with Worked Solutions
  • Practice Problem 1: Which unit of the Bouma sequence represents massive to normally graded coarse graywacke sand?
    ✔ Verified Solution: Ta division
    Mechanistic Rationale: The basal Ta division of the Bouma cycle represents the highest energy, high-concentration deposition from the head of the turbidity current, resulting in normal grading.
  • Practice Problem 2: What matrix threshold formally separates a wacke (graywacke) from an arenite in the Pettijohn classification?
    ✔ Verified Solution: 15% matrix
    Mechanistic Rationale: Sandstones containing more than 15% clay/silt matrix are classified as wackes, whereas those with less than 15% matrix are classified as arenites.
