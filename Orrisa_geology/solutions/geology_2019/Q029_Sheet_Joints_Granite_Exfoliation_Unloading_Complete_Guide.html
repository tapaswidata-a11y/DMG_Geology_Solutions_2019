Plutonic Rock Fracturing: Sheet Joints, Sheeting & Exfoliation

Granite and related massive plutonic rocks (granodiorite, diorite, gabbro) originate at deep crustal levels under high lithostatic confining pressures ($P = \rho g z$). During subsequent regional tectonic uplift and prolonged erosional unroofing (denudation), millions of tons of overlying rock overburden are removed. The massive, unfoliated granite expands elastically upward, releasing stored lithostatic strain and developing Sheet Joints (also termed sheeting or exfoliation joints).

  • Morphology & Orientation: Sheet joints are broad, gently curved planar to curviplanar fractures that develop sub-parallel to the ground surface topography. On mountain summits and domes, sheet joints curve convex upward; in valleys, they sag concave upward.
  • Spacing with Depth: Because sheeting is driven by unburdening, joint spacing is very close near the surface (a few centimeters to a meter apart) and becomes progressively wider with increasing depth (several meters to tens of meters apart), dying out completely at depths greater than $50-100\text{ m}$.
  • Landform Association: Sheeting is the primary geological mechanism driving the formation of exfoliation domes (e.g., Half Dome in Yosemite; bornhardts and whalebacks in the Peninsular Gneissic Complex and Closepet Granites of Karnataka, India).
"Sheet Joints: Sheet joints are often seen in the exposures of granite. The sheet joints are somewhat curved and are essentially parallel to the ground surface. They divide the rock mass into sheets or tabular slabs. Sheeting is caused by the release of confining pressure (unloading) when overlying rocks are removed by erosion." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Joints: Primary & Unloading Joints, p. 123) & Van der Pluijm & Marshak, p. 163
Mechanics of Sheeting: Elastic Decompression & Unloading
$$\Delta \sigma_v = -\rho g \Delta z \quad (\text{Relief of Vertical Lithostatic Overburden})$$ $$\sigma_3 \text{ becomes tensile near the free surface orthogonal to topography: } \sigma_3 \le -T_o$$ $$\text{Fracture Plane Orientation:} \quad \text{Perpendicular to } \sigma_3 \implies \text{Parallel to the surface topography}$$

Where $\rho$ is rock density, $g$ is gravity, $\Delta z$ is eroded overburden thickness, and $T_o$ is the tensile strength of the granite ($pprox 5-10\text{ MPa}$).

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q029
Granite usually exhibits :
(A) Diagonal joints
(B) Strike joints
(C) Vertical joints
(D) Sheet joints
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (D) Sheet joints
Verified against Bangar (p. 123), Billings (Structural Geology, p. 145), and Van der Pluijm & Marshak (Earth Structure, p. 163). Massive plutonic granite characteristically displays prominent sheet joints (sheeting/exfoliation) formed by the release of lithostatic pressure during unroofing.
(A) Diagonal joints INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Diagonal (or oblique) joints are defined in layered sedimentary or metamorphic rocks as joints that trend obliquely to both the strike and dip directions of the bedding planes. Massive plutonic granite possesses neither bedding nor regional strike/dip lines, making diagonal joints geometrically inapplicable as a diagnostic hallmark.
(B) Strike joints INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Strike joints are joints whose strike is parallel to the strike of the bedding planes or metamorphic foliation in stratified rocks. Because isotropic massive granites lack bedding planes, strike joints cannot serve as their primary structural attribute.
(C) Vertical joints INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: While vertical joints occur in granites (often participating as two orthogonal sets in cuboidal mural jointing), they are common to virtually every rock type on Earth (sandstones, basalts, limestones). The unique, diagnostic signature that distinguishes massive granite hills worldwide is sheet jointing (sheeting).
(D) Sheet joints CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: Sheet joints are the classic, defining physical characteristic of massive granite outcrops. Formed as deep-seated granite is exhumed and confining lithostatic overburden is removed, the granite expands toward the free topographic surface. This creates curved tensile fractures sub-parallel to the ground surface, peeling off massive curved slabs (exfoliation) and sculpting smooth domical landscapes (bornhardts and tors).

8 Fully Worked Exam-Standard Practice Problems: Joint Mechanics & Sheeting
Problem 1: Lithostatic Overburden Relief Calculation
Stress relief $\Delta \sigma_v = 53.0\text{ MPa}$
A granite pluton was emplaced at a depth of $2.0\text{ km}$ under rock density $\rho = 2700\text{ kg/m}^3$. Calculate the lithostatic confining pressure relieved upon complete erosional exhumation to the surface ($g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$).
$$\Delta \sigma_v = \rho g z = 2700 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2000 = 5.297 \times 10^7\text{ Pa} \approx 53.0\text{ MPa}$$
Problem 2: Variation of Sheet Joint Spacing with Depth
Spacing increases exponentially with depth
Field measurements show sheet joint frequency is highest near the surface ($0.2-0.5\text{ m}$ slab thickness), widening to $2-5\text{ m}$ at $20\text{ m}$ depth, and becoming virtually absent below $60-100\text{ m}$ as residual confining pressure suppresses tensile expansion.
Problem 3: Mural Jointing Terminology
Three mutually perpendicular joint sets in granite
Granite exposures frequently exhibit two vertical joint sets intersecting at approximately $90^\circ$ combined with a horizontal joint set, partitioning the pluton into cubical or rectangular blocks termed mural joints.
Problem 4: Exfoliation Domes in Indian Geology
Closepet Granite bornhardts (Ramanagara, Karnataka)
The monumental monolithic granite tors and bornhardts of Ramanagara (Closepet Granite) and Hyderabad (Granite dome inselbergs) owe their rounded, domical morphologies directly to progressive sheet jointing and exfoliation weathering.
Problem 5: Daylighting Sheet Joints on Highway Cuts
Dip of sheet joints is shallower than slope face, dipping into road cut
When road excavations cut into the flank of a sheeting dome such that sheet joints dip in the same direction as the slope cut but at a flatter angle, the joints 'daylight', predisposing the granite sheets to catastrophic planar sliding.
Problem 6: Tensile Failure Criterion for Sheeting
Tensile strain exceeds critical elastic tensile limit: $\epsilon_t > \epsilon_{\text{crit}}$
During unroofing, lateral tectonic stresses may remain high ($\sigma_h$) while vertical stress ($\sigma_v$) drops to zero. Poisson's expansion in the vertical direction produces vertical tensile strain; when this exceeds rock tensile strength, horizontal sheet fractures nucleate.
Problem 7: Columnar vs. Sheet Joints Distinction
Columnar is thermal contraction in lavas; sheeting is mechanical unloading in plutons
Columnar joints form by cooling contraction in volcanic basalts or sills (e.g., Giant's Causeway, Deccan Traps), whereas sheet joints form by mechanical decompression during unroofing of plutonic granites.
Problem 8: Quarry Exploitation of Sheeting
Used for effortless bench development and slab detachment
Granite quarrymen take advantage of pre-existing sheet joints as natural bottom-release planes (lift joints), eliminating the need for basal undercutting during dimension stone extraction.
