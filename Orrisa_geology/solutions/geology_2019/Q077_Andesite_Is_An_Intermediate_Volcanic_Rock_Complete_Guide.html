1. Theoretical Foundation: Petrography & Geological Environment of Andesite

Andesite is a fine-grained, aphanitic to porphyritic intermediate volcanic (extrusive) rock named by Christian Leopold von Buch (1826) after the Andes Mountains of South America, where it forms the dominant volcanic edifice along active subduction zone margins.

Property Petrological Specification Diagnostic Detail
Chemical Class Intermediate (52 – 63 wt% $SiO_2$) Total alkali-silica (TAS) field between basaltic andesite and dacite.
Emplacement Mode Volcanic (Extrusive) Lava flows, block-and-ash flows, pyroclastic breccias, and stratovolcano cones.
Plutonic Equivalent Diorite Shares identical chemical and bulk mineralogical composition; differs in grain size.
Essential Mineralogy Plagioclase Feldspar + Mafics Zoned plagioclase (typically Andesine $An_{30}\text{--}An_{50}$) + Augite / Hypersthene / Hornblende.
Diagnostic Texture Porphyritic with Hyalopilitic Groundmass Prominent phenocrysts of oscillatory-zoned andesine and hornblende/augite in a microcrystalline or glassy groundmass.
"Andesite is the volcanic equivalent of diorite. It is an intermediate, usually porphyritic rock consisting essentially of plagioclase feldspar (principally andesine or oligoclase) together with one or more ferromagnesian minerals such as biotite, hornblende, augite, or hypersthene. Andesites are the characteristic lavas of island arcs and active continental margins bordering the Pacific Ocean (the 'Andesite Line')."
— John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology (2nd Ed., Chapter 2, pp. 24–28)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 77
Andesite is :
A A coarse grained acid plutonic rock
B A fine grained hypabyssal rock
C An intermediate volcanic rock
D An ultrabasic volcanic rock
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION C (An intermediate volcanic rock)
OPTION C • CORRECT
An intermediate volcanic rock

Andesite has a silica content of 52% to 63% (intermediate range) and forms as an extrusive (volcanic) lava flow or pyroclastic deposit on planetary crusts. It represents the extrusive counterpart of diorite.

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
A coarse grained acid plutonic rock

A coarse-grained acid plutonic rock is Granite or Adamellite ($SiO_2 > 66\%$, phaneritic texture). Andesite is fine-grained/porphyritic, volcanic, and intermediate.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
A fine grained hypabyssal rock

A fine- to medium-grained hypabyssal rock equivalent to diorite is a microdiorite or porphyrite. Andesite is an extrusive surface lava flow (volcanic).

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
An ultrabasic volcanic rock

An ultrabasic volcanic rock ($SiO_2 < 45\%$, highly magnesian) is a Komatiite or Meimechite, characterized by spinifex texture. Andesite is neither ultrabasic nor rich in olivine.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What geological boundary separates the tholeiitic basalt province of the open Pacific basin from circum-Pacific andesitic volcanism?
Answer: The Andesite Line (Marshall Line)
The Andesite Line marks the petrological boundary between intraplate oceanic basalts and subduction-related calc-alkaline andesites.
2. What specific plagioclase composition is diagnostic of andesite?
Answer: Andesine ($An_{30}\text{--}An_{50}$)
By IUGS petrographic rules, the dominant plagioclase in andesite has an anorthite content less than $An_{50}$ (andesine/oligoclase), whereas basalt plagioclase is labradorite ($An_{50}\text{--}An_{70}$).
3. What is the typical magma series that generates the voluminous andesites of volcanic arcs?
Answer: The Calc-Alkaline Magma Series
Hydrous partial melting of mantle wedge peridotite fluxed by slab-derived fluids generates primary hydrous basaltic magmas that differentiate along calc-alkaline pathways to andesite.
4. What texture is commonly observed in andesite phenocrysts indicating magma mixing or fluctuating P-T conditions?
Answer: Oscillatory zoning and sieve (dusty) resorption rims
Repeated magma recharge in sub-volcanic chambers creates abrupt compositional jumps in plagioclase growth zones, forming oscillatory rings and sieve textures.
5. What is the volcanic equivalent of a granodiorite?
Answer: Dacite
Dacite occupies the composition field between andesite and rhyolite ($SiO_2 \approx 63\text{--}68\%$).
6. Which volcanic hazard is most typically associated with viscous andesitic to dacitic lavas?
Answer: Pyroclastic density currents (PDCs) and Peléan / Plinian explosive eruptions
The higher silica content and moderate-to-high volatile ($H_2O$) content make andesitic melts highly viscous, promoting explosive dome-collapse pyroclastic flows.
7. How does basaltic andesite differ from normal andesite in $SiO_2$ content?
Answer: Basaltic andesite has 52% to 57% $SiO_2$, while andesite has 57% to 63% $SiO_2$.
The IUGS TAS diagram formally splits the intermediate volcanic field into basaltic andesite ($52\text{--}57\%$) and andesite ($57\text{--}63\%$).
8. What is the intrusive plutonic equivalent of Andesite?
Answer: Diorite
Diorite crystallizes slowly at depth from the same intermediate magma, producing a phaneritic equigranular texture of plagioclase and amphibole.
