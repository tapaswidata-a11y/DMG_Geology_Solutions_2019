Tethyan Himalayan Geology: The Muth Quartzite Devonian Marker Horizon

In the regional geological mapping of the Tethyan Himalayan Zone across Kashmir, Zanskar, Spiti, Kinnaur, and Kumaon, the Muth Quartzite stands out as the single most distinctive, ubiquitous, and reliable lithostratigraphic marker horizon for the Devonian System.

  • Physical and Geological Characteristics:
    • Visual Appearance: Forms conspicuous, gleaming snow-white, vertical cliffs and ridges across hundreds of kilometers of barren Himalayan mountain terrain.
    • Thickness: Consistently between $150\text{ m}$ and $500\text{ m}$ thick.
    • Mineralogical & Textural Super-Maturity: Composed of $>98 - 99\%\text{ pure quartz}$ (silica). The rock is a super-mature quartz arenite consisting of exceptionally well-rounded, well-sorted, spherical quartz grains bonded by syntaxial quartz overgrowths into an impervious, vitreous orthoquartzite. Heavy minerals are restricted entirely to the ultrastable ZTR trio (Zircon, Tourmaline, Rutile).
    • Sedimentary Structures & Environment: Displays giant tabular and trough cross-bedding, ripple marks, and localized Skolithos trace fossil burrows, indicating coastal eolian dune and shallow-marine shoreface deposition across a stable, peneplaned northern Gondwanan passive shelf.
  • Forensic Refutation of Other Stratigraphic Formations:
    • Lipak Formation: Lower Carboniferous (Tournaisian-Viséan) sequence of dark grey to black fossiliferous limestones and dolomites rich in brachiopods (Syringothyris).
    • Po Formation: Upper Carboniferous succession of alternating quartzites and black shales (Fenestella Shales) with bryozoans and the Rhacopteris flora.
    • Syringothyris Limestone: Lower Carboniferous limestone marker bed restricted to the Kashmir Basin, characterized by the index brachiopod Syringothyris cuspidata.
"Muth Quartzite (Devonian Marker): The Muth Quartzite is the most remarkable and easily recognized formation in the whole Himalayan sequence. It is a massive, snow-white, granular orthoquartzite of high purity ($>98\%\text{ SiO}_2$), forming conspicuous white cliffs. It serves as an infallible marker horizon for the Devonian System throughout Spiti, Kashmir, and Kumaon." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (The Devonian System: Muth Quartzite, p. 286)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 53
Marker horizon for Devonian in Himalaya is called ;
(A) Lipak formation
(B) Po formation
(C) Muth quartzites
(D) Syringothyris limestone
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (C) Muth quartzites
(A) Lipak formation INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Lipak Formation represents the Early Carboniferous (Mississippian) succession of fossiliferous limestones and dolomites resting conformably above the Muth Quartzite.
(B) Po formation INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Po Formation is of Carboniferous (Pennsylvanian) age, famed for its Fenestella Shales and the Rhacopteris plant beds of the Thabo stage.
(C) Muth quartzites CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: The Muth Quartzite is universally documented in Himalayan geology as the paramount regional marker horizon for the Devonian Period. Its pure snow-white color, vitreous luster, high resistance to weathering, and massive thickness make it an unmistakable regional structural marker across Kashmir, Spiti, Zanskar, and Kumaon.
(D) Syringothyris limestone INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Syringothyris Limestone is the marker horizon for the Lower Carboniferous (Tournaisian) in the Kashmir Valley, named after the diagnostic spiriferid brachiopod Syringothyris cuspidata.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Tethyan Himalayan Stratigraphy
Problem 1: Textural Super-Maturity of Muth Quartzite
Grains are well-rounded and well-sorted with zero clay matrix (<1% matrix)
Following Folk's textural classification, the Muth Quartzite is a true 'super-mature' quartz arenite, requiring prolonged eolian abrasion and wave reworking in a high-energy coastal marine realm.
Problem 2: Underlying Formation Below Muth Quartzite in Spiti
Pin Formation (Silurian System)
The Muth Quartzite rests conformably upon the Silurian Pin Formation (Sanugba Group), which consists of grey-green shales, siltstones, and coral-bearing biostromal limestones.
Problem 3: Type Locality of the Muth Quartzite
Near the village of Muth (Mudh) in the Pin Valley, Spiti
Named by Ferdinand Stoliczka in 1865, the vertical cliffs surrounding the village of Muth expose the complete, pristine ~150-meter-thick stratotype section.
Problem 4: ZTR Index Calculation for Muth Quartzite
ZTR Index approaches 100%
Non-opaque heavy mineral concentrates contain exclusively Zircon, Tourmaline, and Rutile, with less stable minerals (amphiboles, pyroxenes, epidote) completely eliminated by chemical weathering and multiple recycling cycles.
Problem 5: Fossils Found in the Muth Quartzite
Extremely rare body fossils; contains *Skolithos* and *Diplocraterion* trace fossils
Due to high-energy wave washing and intense diagenetic syntaxial quartz overgrowth, body fossils are rarely preserved, though localized cast impressions of Devonian brachiopods (*Schellwienella*) and vertical pipe-rock burrows occur.
Problem 6: Regional Equivalent of Muth Quartzite in Kashmir
Muth Quartzite of the Anantnag and Liddar Valley
The unit extends continuously westward into the Kashmir synclinorium, maintaining its identical snow-white orthoquartzite lithology between the Silurian slates and the Syringothyris Limestone.
Problem 7: Paleoclimate Inferred from Muth Quartzite
Warm, tropical to subtropical climate on northern Gondwana margin
Intense chemical weathering of the Gondwana craton interior under warm conditions destroyed all unstable feldspars and mafic silicates, delivering only pure quartz grains to the coastal eolian-dune and shelf complex.
Problem 8: Diagenetic Cementation Mechanism
Syntaxial silica overgrowths in optical continuity with detrital quartz nuclei
Petrographic thin sections reveal that circulating silica-rich porewaters precipitated secondary quartz directly onto detrital sand grains, interlocking them into an ultra-tough polygonal granoblastic mosaic.
