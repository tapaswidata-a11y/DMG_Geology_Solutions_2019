Kinematics of Intersection Lineations in Tectonic Shear Zones

In structural geology and metamorphic petrology (Ramsay & Huber, 1983; Hobbs, Means & Williams, 1976; Billings, 1972; Fossen, 2016), when two penetrative planar fabric surfaces intersect in three-dimensional space, their line of intersection geometrically generates a prominent linear fabric termed an intersection lineation. Specifically, the intersection of primary sedimentary bedding ($S_0$) with a secondary tectonic cleavage or foliation plane ($S_1$) produces an $S_0 \times S_1$ intersection lineation (denoted $L_0^1$ or $L_{1/0}$).

"Any linear feature developed in a rock as a result of tectonic deformation is called a lineation. When a rock possessing primary bedding planes is subjected to folding and develops a secondary tectonic cleavage, the line of intersection of the bedding planes ($S_0$) with the cleavage planes ($S_1$) produces a conspicuous lineation on both surfaces, known as an intersection lineation. In cylindrical folds, this lineation is parallel to the fold axis." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Chapter 7: Secondary Structures & Lineations, p. 235)

The mathematical and geometric properties of intersection lineations are of foundational importance in structural mapping:

Vector Cross-Product Formulation of Intersection Lineation
$$\vec{L}_{1/0} = \vec{n}_{S0} \times \vec{n}_{S1}$$

Where $ ec{n}_{S0}$ is the normal vector to the bedding plane ($S_0$) and $ ec{n}_{S1}$ is the normal vector to the cleavage plane ($S_1$). In cylindrical folds, the intersection lineation vector $ ec{L}_{1/0}$ is co-axial with the fold hinge line ($ ec{B}$)!

Official Question Statement — OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 Paper-II (Question 23)
23. The intersection of bedding and cleavage produces :
(A) Lineation  ✔
(B) Boudin
(C) Mullion structure
(D) Joint
✔
Correct Answer: (A) Lineation
In structural geology (K.M. Bangar, p. 235; Ramsay & Huber, 1983; Marland P. Billings, p. 340; Haakon Fossen, p. 272), the linear trace produced by the geometric intersection of bedding ($S_0$) and cleavage ($S_1$) is universally termed an Intersection lineation.
(A) Lineation Correct Verified Option
Why this option is correct: When a secondary planar cleavage intersects a primary sedimentary bedding plane, their intersection creates a linear fabric visible on both surfaces as fine stripes, color bandings, or fine crinkles, termed an intersection lineation.
(B) Boudin Incorrect Distractor
Why this distractor is incorrect: A boudin (sausage structure) is formed by the longitudinal tensile stretching and necking of a competent layer (such as quartzite or amphibolite) embedded in an incompetent ductile matrix (such as marble or shale), not by the simple geometric intersection of cleavage and bedding.
(C) Mullion structure Incorrect Distractor
Why this distractor is incorrect: Mullion structures are large-scale cylindrical, fluted rock columns formed at the competency boundary between contrasting rock layers under ductile shortening, where cusps point toward the more competent rock. They are not produced by the intersection of bedding and cleavage.
(D) Joint Incorrect Distractor
Why this distractor is incorrect: A joint is a brittle planar fracture across which negligible shear displacement has occurred. It is a planar surface, not a linear intersection fabric.

Field Application: Structural Mapping using $S_0 \times S_1$ Lineations

The intersection lineation is one of the most powerful diagnostic tools in structural field mapping:

  • Parallelism to Regional Fold Axes ($B$-Lineation): In cylindrical folds, the intersection lineation ($L_{1/0}$) is strictly parallel to the major fold axis. Thus, by measuring the plunge and trend of intersection lineations in a single small hand specimen, a geologist immediately determines the orientation of a regional mountain-scale fold!
  • Pencil Cleavage: In mudstones and siltstones where bedding ($S_0$) and tectonic cleavage ($S_1$) intersect with equal fracture frequency, the rock breaks into elongated, slender polygonal rods termed "pencil structure" or pencil cleavage.
High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Problems)
  • Practice Q1: What is the difference between an "intersection lineation" and a "mineral stretching lineation"?
    ✔ Correct Answer: An intersection lineation is a purely geometric intersection of two planes (parallel to fold axes, $B$-axis); a mineral stretching lineation reflects finite plastic extension of minerals parallel to tectonic transport ($X$-axis).
    Explanation: Intersection lineations generally form perpendicular to shortening ($B \perp \sigma_1$), whereas stretching lineations elongate parallel to the transport direction ($a$-lineation).
  • Practice Q2: What causes "pencil cleavage" in slightly deformed shales?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The mutual intersection of bedding fissility ($S_0$) and incipient tectonic cleavage ($S_1$) breaking the rock into long pencil-like prismatic rods.
    Explanation: Characteristic of early-stage tectonic deformation in foreland fold-and-thrust belts (e.g., Appalachian Valley and Ridge).
  • Practice Q3: What is a "crenulation lineation"?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Microscopic fold hinges formed when a second-generation cleavage ($S_2$) micro-folds an earlier foliation ($S_1$).
    Explanation: Produces fine, corrugated crinkle lineations across schistosity planes in poly-deformed metamorphic schists.
  • Practice Q4: In stereographic projection, how is an intersection lineation determined from the strikes and dips of bedding and cleavage?
    ✔ Correct Answer: By finding the point of intersection between the two great circles representing $S_0$ and $S_1$; the plunge and trend of this point gives the lineation.
    Explanation: The intersection of two planar great circles on a Wulff or Schmidt net is a point representing a line in space.
  • Practice Q5: What is a "rodded structure" (rodding)?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Cylindrical rods of quartz formed by the folding, shearing, and isolation of quartz veins parallel to the fold hinge.
    Explanation: Competent quartz veins are severed and rolled into linear rods within weaker mica schist matrices.
  • Practice Q6: What is the difference between an $S$-tectonite and an $L$-tectonite?
    ✔ Correct Answer: An $S$-tectonite is dominated by planar foliation (flattening strain, $k < 1$); an $L$-tectonite is dominated by linear fabric (constrictional strain, $k > 1$).
    Explanation: In Flinn's strain diagram, cigar-shaped constrictional strain ellipsoids produce pure $L$-tectonites devoid of planar foliation.
  • Practice Q7: Can intersection lineation occur in undeformed horizontal sedimentary rocks?
    ✔ Correct Answer: No; development of cleavage requires tectonic compressive strain exceeding the ductile yield strength of the rock.
    Explanation: Undeformed rocks only possess bedding ($S_0$). Cleavage ($S_1$) requires regional tectonic shortening.
  • Practice Q8: Why does cleavage refraction occur when cleavage passes from a shale layer into a sandstone layer?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Competency contrast causes the strain ellipse orientation to rotate, causing cleavage to steepen in competent sandstone and flatten in incompetent shale.
    Explanation: The refraction of cleavage creates differing intersection lineation characteristics across alternating beds.
