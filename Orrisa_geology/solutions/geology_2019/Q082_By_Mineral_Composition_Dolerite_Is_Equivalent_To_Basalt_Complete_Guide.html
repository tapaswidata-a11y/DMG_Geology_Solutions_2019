1. Theoretical Foundation: The Plutonic-Hypabyssal-Volcanic Equivalence Triad

In igneous petrology, rocks sharing identical chemical and mineralogical compositions are subdivided into three structural categories based on their cooling rate, depth of consolidation, and resulting crystalline texture:

Structural Setting Cooling Regime & Depth Grain Size & Diagnostic Texture Representative Mafic Rock
Plutonic (Deep Intrusive) Slow cooling at deep crustal levels ($> 3\text{--}5\text{ km}$) Coarse-grained, phaneritic equigranular Gabbro
Hypabyssal (Subvolcanic / Shallow Intrusive) Moderate cooling in dykes, sills, and cone sheets ($< 2\text{ km}$) Medium-grained, ophitic / subophitic (laths of plagioclase enclosed by pyroxene) DOLERITE (Diabase)
Volcanic (Extrusive) Rapid quenching at Earth's surface as lava flows Fine-grained, aphanitic, glassy, intersertal / hyalopilitic BASALT (Option D)
"By mineralogical and chemical composition, dolerite is precisely equivalent to basalt and gabbro. All three consist essentially of calcic plagioclase (labradorite or bytownite) and clinopyroxene (augite), with or without olivine and titanomagnetite. Dolerite represents the medium-grained hypabyssal facies (forming dykes and sills), basalt is the fine-grained extrusive volcanic facies, and gabbro is the coarse-grained plutonic facies."
— K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Petrology, p. 126)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 82
By mineral composition, dolerite is equivalent to :
A Pitchstone
B Syenite
C Peridotite
D Basalt
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION D (Basalt)
OPTION D • CORRECT
Basalt

Dolerite (termed diabase in North American literature) and basalt share identical mineral compositions: both are basic rocks composed of calcic plagioclase (labradorite) and augite (clinopyroxene). Dolerite is the medium-grained hypabyssal intrusive equivalent of extrusive volcanic basalt.

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Pitchstone

Pitchstone is a volcanic glass of acidic to intermediate composition ($SiO_2 > 65\%$, with 4-10% water) characterized by a dull, pitch-like resinous luster. It is highly felsic, unlike basic dolerite.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Syenite

Syenite is an intermediate plutonic rock dominated by alkali feldspar (orthoclase/microcline) with minor hornblende or biotite. It is rich in alkalis ($K_2O + Na_2O$) and low in lime/magnesia, totally distinct from mafic dolerite.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Peridotite

Peridotite is an ultrabasic rock ($SiO_2 < 45\%$) composed almost entirely of olivine and pyroxenes with little or no feldspar. Dolerite contains 50% or more plagioclase feldspar.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What diagnostic texture is characteristic of dolerites under the petrological microscope?
Answer: Ophitic (or Subophitic) texture
In ophitic texture, elongated, euhedral laths of plagioclase are completely or partially enclosed within larger, anhedral plates of augite pyroxene.
2. What is the North American synonymous term for dolerite?
Answer: Diabase
In the United States and Canada, medium-grained basic hypabyssal rocks are called diabase, whereas in British and Indian usage, they are called dolerite.
3. What prominent geological formation in Peninsular India contains thousands of dolerite dykes cutting Archaean basements?
Answer: Newer Dolerite dyke swarms of the Singhbhum Craton (Odisha-Jharkhand) and Cuddapah dykes
The Singhbhum craton is criss-crossed by the spectacular Palaeoproterozoic Newer Dolerite dyke swarm trending NNE-SSW and NW-SE.
4. What is the fine-grained volcanic equivalent of Granite?
Answer: Rhyolite
Just as basalt is to dolerite/gabbro, rhyolite is the volcanic equivalent of granitic intrusive rocks.
5. What alteration process converts calcic plagioclase in dolerite to epidote, zoisite, and albite?
Answer: Saussuritization
Hydrothermal alteration or low-grade metamorphism breaks down calcic plagioclase into a fine-grained aggregate of zoisite, epidote, calcite, and albite (saussurite).
6. What alteration process transforms pyroxene in dolerite to fibrous green amphibole (actinolite/tremolite)?
Answer: Uralitization
Uralitization is the hydrothermal replacement of primary clinopyroxene (augite) by secondary fibrous amphibole (uralite).
7. What is an olivine-rich dolerite called?
Answer: Olivine dolerite
When olivine exceeds 5-10% of modal volume, the rock is specifically termed olivine dolerite.
8. What is the characteristic weathering pattern displayed by dolerite dyke outcrops in the field?
Answer: Spheroidal weathering (onion-skin spalling) and 'tombstone' topography
Jointed dolerite blocks weather inward along moisture-rich corners, peeling off in concentric shells and leaving rounded corestones resembling giant boulders or tombstones.
