1. Theoretical Foundation: Defining Porosity & Volumetric Phase Relations

In hydrogeology, petroleum geology, and rock/soil mechanics, a natural geological material is conceptualized as a multi-phase system consisting of solid mineral grains ($V_s$) and open interstitial pore spaces (voids, $V_v$) occupied by water and/or air. The fundamental volumetric parameters are defined as follows:

Volumetric Property Mathematical Definition Physical Meaning & Reference Base Range of Values
POROSITY ($n$) (Option B) $$n = \frac{V_v}{V_t} \times 100\%$$ Ratio between the total volume of pore spaces ($V_v$) and the TOTAL volume of the rock ($V_t = V_s + V_v$). Always expressed as a fraction ($0 < n < 1$) or percentage ($0\% \text{ to } 100\%$). Cannot exceed 100%.
Void Ratio ($e$) (Option A) $$e = \frac{V_v}{V_s}$$ Ratio between the total volume of pore spaces ($V_v$) and the volume of SOLID mineral grains only ($V_s$). Expressed as a decimal ratio; can exceed 1.0 (or $> 100\%$) in soft clays and loose silts.
Bulk Density ($\rho_b$) (Option D) $$\rho_b = \frac{M_{\text{total}}}{V_t}$$ Total mass of rock (solids + moisture) per unit total volume. Units: $\text{g/cm}^3$ or $\text{kg/m}^3$ (typically 1.8 to 3.0).
"Porosity is the ratio between the total volume of pore spaces (voids) to the total volume of the rock, generally expressed as a percentage. It indicates the storage capacity of the rock for fluids like water, oil, or gas. In contrast, void ratio is the ratio of the volume of voids to the volume of the solid grains alone."
— K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Properties of Building Stones, p. 284)
$$\mathbf{Porosity\ (n)} = \frac{\mathbf{Volume\ of\ Pore\ Spaces\ (V_v)}}{\mathbf{Total\ Volume\ of\ the\ Rock\ (V_t)}} \times 100\% \equiv \mathbf{Option\ B}$$ $$\text{Inter-relationship: } n = \frac{e}{1 + e} \quad \text{and} \quad e = \frac{n}{1 - n}$$

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 109
Ratio between the total volume of pore spaces to the total volume of the rock is a measure of:
A Void ratio
B Porosity
C Pores ratio
D Bulk density
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION B (Porosity)
OPTION B • CORRECT
Porosity

By fundamental definition across physics, hydrogeology, and rock mechanics (IS: 13030; Bangar, p. 284; Todd; Freeze & Cherry), the ratio of the total volume of pore spaces ($V_v$) to the total volume of the rock ($V_t$) is defined as Porosity ($n$).

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Void ratio

Void ratio ($e$) is the ratio of the volume of pore spaces ($V_v$) to the volume of solid mineral grains ($V_s$), NOT to the total volume ($V_t$).

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Pores ratio

'Pores ratio' is a fictitious non-geological distractor without physical or mathematical definition.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
Bulk density

Bulk density is the ratio of mass to volume ($\rho = M/V$), expressed in units of $\text{g/cm}^3$ or $\text{kg/m}^3$, not a dimensionless volume-to-volume ratio.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. If a soil has a void ratio $e = 0.50$, what is its porosity ($n$)?
Answer: $n = 33.33\%$ (or 0.333)
$n = \frac{e}{1 + e} = \frac{0.50}{1 + 0.50} = \frac{0.50}{1.50} = 0.333 \implies 33.33\%$.
2. If a sandstone has a porosity $n = 20\%$, what is its void ratio ($e$)?
Answer: $e = 0.25$
$e = \frac{n}{1 - n} = \frac{0.20}{1 - 0.20} = \frac{0.20}{0.80} = 0.25$.
3. What is the theoretical maximum porosity for an aggregate of uniform spherical grains packed in cubic packing?
Answer: $47.64\%$
In cubic packing, volume of solids in a unit cell of edge $2R$ is $\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 / 8 R^3 = \frac{\pi}{6} \approx 0.5236$, leaving $n = 1 - 0.5236 = 47.64\%$.
4. What is the theoretical minimum porosity for uniform spheres in rhombohedral (hexagonal close) packing?
Answer: $25.95\%$ (or $\approx 26\%$)
Rhombohedral packing represents the densest possible geometric packing of uniform spheres without crushing.
5. What is the difference between total porosity and effective porosity?
Answer: Total porosity includes all voids (isolated + interconnected); effective porosity includes only interconnected pores that allow fluid flow.
Pumice and clay have very high total porosity, but low effective porosity because pores are either isolated (pumice) or bound by molecular adhesion (clay).
6. Why does a clay have higher porosity (50-70%) than a well-sorted sand (30-40%), yet far lower permeability?
Answer: Clay has immense total pore volume due to open card-house fabric, but pore throat diameters are sub-microscopic, creating colossal viscous drag.
Permeability is governed by pore throat size ($r^2$), not by total pore volume ($n$).
7. What is secondary porosity in limestone reservoirs?
Answer: Porosity formed after deposition by groundwater dissolution (vugs, caverns) or tectonic fracturing.
Dissolution along joints creates cavernous and karst porosity holding major hydrocarbon reserves.
8. What laboratory instrument is used to measure the grain volume of rock cores for porosity determination?
Answer: Helium Pycnometer (Boyle's Law Porosimeter)
Helium gas penetrates the finest interconnected pores, enabling highly accurate measurement of solid grain volume ($V_s$).
