Marine Geomorphology: Seamounts, Guyots & Thermal Subsidence

The deep ocean basin floor is populated by thousands of isolated underwater volcanic edifices rising from the abyssal plains. While active or recently extinct submarine volcanoes retain their conical, pointed summit peaks (termed Seamounts), vast numbers of ancient underwater volcanic mountains possess remarkably flat, horizontal summit plateaus. These flat-topped submarine mountains are termed Guyots (or Tablemounts).

  • Discovery & Naming: During World War II, Princeton geologist and naval commander Harry H. Hess utilized newly invented echo sounders on the transport ship USS Cape Johnson to survey the Pacific basin. He discovered hundreds of truncated, flat-topped volcanic peaks lying submerged at depths of $1,000 - 2,000\text{ meters}$ below sea level. Hess named them Guyots in honor of Arnold Henry Guyot, the 19th-century Swiss-American geographer and founding geology professor at Princeton University.
  • Evolutionary Genesis of a Guyot: The formation of a guyot follows a rigorous four-stage geodynamic cycle:
    1. Subaerial Volcanic Island Stage: Hotspot or mid-ocean ridge volcanism builds a massive basaltic volcano that rises above sea level to form a volcanic island (analogous to Hawaii or Tahiti).
    2. Wave Planation at Sea Level: Once volcanic activity ceases, relentless ocean wave attack and surf abrasion bevel and plane the volcanic summit completely flat at the sea level interface, creating a truncated wave-cut platform. Shallow-water fringing coral reefs and rounded basalt beach pebbles accumulate on this platform.
    3. Thermal Contraction & Subsidence: As plate motion carries the extinct volcano away from the warm, buoyant spreading ridge or mantle plume, the underlying oceanic lithosphere conductively cools, thickens, and increases in density ($d(t) \propto \sqrt{t}$). This causes the entire seafloor to undergo massive isostatic thermal subsidence ($1,000 - 2,500\text{ m}$).
    4. Submerged Guyot: The flat, wave-beveled summit sinks below the photic zone. Corals die (drowned reefs), and the flat platform is preserved in the cold, deep ocean abyssal realm as a permanent flat-topped seamount (guyot).
"Guyot: A flat-topped seamount is called a guyot. It was once a volcanic island whose top was leveled and cut flat by wave action when it was at sea level. With the cooling and subsidence of the ocean floor, the flat-topped volcanic mountain was drowned and submerged to great depths." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (The Oceans: Features of Ocean Floor, p. 36)
Thermal Subsidence Formulation of Guyots
$$d_{\text{guyot}}(t) = d_0 + C_s \sqrt{t} \quad (\text{Depth increases with square root of crustal age } t)$$ $$\Delta z_{\text{subsidence}} = 350 \sqrt{t_{\text{Ma}}} \text{ meters} \quad (\text{Typical thermal contraction of oceanic lithosphere})$$ $$\text{For an extinct volcano aged } t = 64\text{ Ma:} \quad \Delta z = 350 \times \sqrt{64} = 350 \times 8 = \mathbf{2,800\text{ meters}}$$

Drowned Cretaceous rudist bivalves and reef corals recovered from guyot tops at $1,500 ext{ m}$ depth provide empirical verification that these summits were once at sea level.

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q043
Guyot is a :
(A) Ridge of a fold mountain
(B) Major depression in a continental area
(C) Flat topped seamount
(D) Flat topped hill on the surface
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (C) Flat topped seamount
Verified against Bangar (p. 36), Harry Hess (1946), and Thurman & Trujillo (Essentials of Oceanography). A guyot (tablemount) is by definition a flat-topped submarine volcanic mountain (seamount) submerged beneath the sea.
(A) Ridge of a fold mountain INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The ridge of a fold mountain is a linear subaerial or submarine tectonic crest produced by regional horizontal crustal compression (e.g., an anticlinorium crest or Appalachian hogback ridge), bearing zero relation to isolated volcanic guyots.
(B) Major depression in a continental area INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Major continental depressions include rift valleys, grabens, or structural basins (e.g., the Dead Sea basin or Lake Baikal). A guyot is an elevated positive submarine mountain, not a depression.
(C) Flat topped seamount CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: A guyot (tablemount) is an isolated volcanic seamount whose peak was planed perfectly flat by marine surf erosion when it stood at sea level as an island, and subsequently drowned to great depths ($1,000-2,500 ext{ m}$) as the cooling oceanic lithosphere subsided isostatically. Harry Hess recognized hundreds of these flat-topped volcanic seamounts in the Pacific, providing a cornerstone proof of seafloor spreading and oceanic subsidence.
(D) Flat topped hill on the surface INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: A flat-topped hill on the continental land surface is known as a mesa or butte, formed by subaerial fluvial and scarp-retreat erosion of horizontal sedimentary strata capped by a resistant caprock. Guyots are exclusively submarine features.

8 Fully Worked Exam-Standard Practice Problems: Marine Geology & Guyots
Problem 1: Drowned Coral Reefs on Guyots
Dredged fossils confirm Cretaceous reef corals at $1,500\text{ m}$ depth
Oceanographic dredging of Pacific guyot summits recovered shallow-water Cretaceous rudist bivalves and reef corals (*Actinastrea*). Because hermatypic corals cannot grow below $50\text{ m}$ depth (photic zone limit), this proves guyot summits were once at sea level.
Problem 2: Emperor Seamount-Hawaiian Ridge Bend
Bend at $\approx 47\text{ Ma}$ represents change in Pacific Plate motion from NNW to WNW
The Hawaiian-Emperor chain consists of active islands (Hawaii), conical seamounts, and ancient drowned guyots (Detroit Guyot, Meiji Guyot near the Aleutians). The prominent $60^\circ$ bend at $47\text{ Ma}$ marks a major kinematic plate reorganization.
Problem 3: Minimum Relief Criterion for Seamounts
Seamount elevation must exceed $1,000\text{ meters}$ above surrounding abyssal floor
By international hydrographic definition (IHO/IOC), an isolated submarine elevation is classified as a seamount (or guyot, if flat-topped) only if its vertical relief is $1,000\text{ m}$ or greater. Features under $1,000\text{ m}$ are termed abyssal hills or knolls.
Problem 4: Role of Harry Hess in Seafloor Spreading
Guyots proved that ocean crust moves laterally away from ridges while subsiding
Harry Hess reasoned in 1962 that if the ocean floor were static, guyots would remain at sea level. Their systematic increase in depth and age away from spreading ridges provided a fundamental pillar of his "History of Ocean Basins" paper proposing seafloor spreading.
Problem 5: Ferromanganese Crusts on Guyots
Cobalt-rich ferromanganese crusts precipitate from seawater on flat summits
Because strong deep-sea currents sweep guyot summits free of sediment, dissolved metals precipitate directly from seawater over millions of years, forming $5-15\text{ cm}$ thick crusts highly enriched in cobalt, nickel, and tellurium.
Problem 6: Ocean Current Taylor Columns Over Guyots
Upwelling and closed anticyclonic circulation vortices over flat summits
When deep ocean currents encounter a steep guyot, Coriolis forces create a closed circulating vortex termed a *Taylor Column*, which traps nutrients, driving localized biological upwelling and rich pelagic fisheries.
Problem 7: Distinction Between Mesa and Guyot
Mesa is a subaerial sedimentary hill; Guyot is a submarine volcanic mountain
While both feature flat tops, a mesa forms on land by stream erosion cutting horizontal sedimentary strata, whereas a guyot is an isolated basaltic volcano planed by sea waves and drowned in the deep ocean.
Problem 8: Subduction of Guyots at Deep Sea Trenches
Causes tectonic erosion and seismic asperities in subduction zones
When plate motion carries a guyot into an oceanic trench (e.g., Daiichi-Kashima Guyot in the Japan Trench), the massive volcanic block acts as a giant asperic wedge that scours the overriding plate and triggers high-magnitude megathrust earthquakes.
