Gastropod Paleontology: Coiling Chirality & The Genus Physa

In molluscan paleontology and malacology, the Class Gastropoda (snails and slugs) is characterized by a single univalve, helically or planispirally coiled calcareous shell. A fundamental taxonomic criterion in gastropod morphology is shell chirality (coiling direction). In standard textbooks and Indian paleontology, the freshwater pulmonate genus Physa is the quintessential textbook representative of a sinistrally (left-handed) coiled shell.

  • Determination of Coiling Chirality (Dextral vs. Sinistral):
    • Standard Orientation Rule: Hold the gastropod shell vertically with the apex pointing upward (dorsal) and the aperture (mouth) facing directly toward the observer.
    • Dextral Coiling (Right-Handed):
      • If the aperture lies on the RIGHT-HAND SIDE of the central axis (columella), the shell is dextral.
      • Over $90 - 95\%\text{ of all gastropod species}$ (e.g., *Turritella*, *Helix*, *Conus*, *Murex*, *Cerithium*) are dextrally coiled.
    • Sinistral Coiling (Left-Handed — Question 71):
      • If the aperture lies on the LEFT-HAND SIDE of the central columella, the shell is sinistral.
      • The freshwater pond snail genus Physa (e.g., *Physa prinsepii* of the Indian Deccan Intertrappeans, and modern *Physa fontinalis*) is globally famous as the diagnostic, benchmark example of a sinistrally coiled gastropod.
  • Stratigraphic Importance of Physa in India:
    • Physa prinsepii: An exceptionally large (up to 7-9 cm long), robust, sinistrally coiled freshwater gastropod with swollen body whorls and an elongated left-handed aperture.
    • Horizon Marker: It occurs in phenomenal abundance within the Late Cretaceous Intertrappean beds of the Deccan Traps (e.g., Nagpur, Malabar Hill Mumbai, Rajahmundry, Sagar), proving that the basalt flows erupted over freshwater continental lakes.
"Gastropoda (Coiling and Physa): In gastropods, coiling is usually dextral (right-handed), where the aperture lies to the right when the apex is pointing upwards. However, in certain genera like Physa, the shell is sinistrally coiled (left-handed), with the aperture situated on the left side of the axis. *Physa prinsepii* is a prominent index fossil in the Deccan Intertrappean beds of India." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Invertebrate Paleontology: Gastropoda - Coiling, p. 262)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 71
Physa-the gastropod genus shows :
(A) Planispirally coiled
(B) Dextrally coiled
(C) Uncoiled nature
(D) Sinistrally coiled (Sinisterly coiled)
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (D) Sinistrally coiled
(A) Planispirally coiled INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Planispiral coiling occurs when whorls coil in a single flat plane symmetrical about the axis (e.g., the freshwater snail *Planorbis* and cephalopod *Bellerophon*). *Physa* is helicospiral, not planispiral.
(B) Dextrally coiled INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Dextral coiling (aperture on the right) characterizes the vast majority of gastropods (*Turritella*, *Lymnaea*, *Helix*). *Physa* is the classic biological opposite, exhibiting left-handed coiling.
(C) Uncoiled nature INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Uncoiled or cup-like non-spiral gastropods include limpets (*Patella*) and fissurellids. *Physa* possesses an elongate, turbinate, multi-whorled spiral shell.
(D) Sinistrally coiled CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: Physa is universally cited in paleontology as the prime textbook representative of sinistral (left-handed) coiling. When held with the apex uppermost and aperture facing the observer, its aperture opens exclusively on the left side of the columella.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Gastropod Paleobiology & Morphology
Problem 1: Genetic Basis of Sinistral Coiling in Snails
Maternal effect gene controlling the cleavage angle of the spindle at the 4-cell embryonic stage
A.D. Boycott and C. Diver proved that shell chirality is determined by maternal cytoplasm mRNA directing spiral blastomere cleavage during early zygotic division.
Problem 2: The Columella of a Gastropod Shell
Central solid or hollow axis of pillar around which the whorls are coiled
Where inner whorl walls do not fuse completely, a central vertical cavity opens at the base termed the umbilicus.
Problem 3: Anatomical Process of 'Torsion' in Gastropods
180° counter-clockwise rotation of the visceral mass bringing mantle cavity and anus forward above the head
Torsion occurs during the veliger larval stage, distinguishing gastropods from all other molluscs by placing the respiratory mantle cavity and gills anteriorly.
Problem 4: Indian Deccan Intertrappean Fossil Fauna Associated with Physa
Paludina (Viviparus), Unio, Indobatrachus pusillus (fossil frogs), and charophyte gyrogonites
At Worli Hill, Mumbai, lake shales interbedded with basalt flows contain millions of crushed *Physa prinsepii* alongside fossil frogs and freshwater ostracods.
Problem 5: Difference Between Holostomatous and Siphonostomatous Apertures
Holostomatous has smooth unbroken continuous margins (herbivores); Siphonostomatous has anterior canal notch (carnivores)
*Physa* is holostomatous (grazes on algae). Marine predatory gastropods (*Murex*, *Conus*) possess a siphonostomatous notch to project an inhalant sensory water siphon.
Problem 6: The Operculum
Horny or calcified door-like plate attached to the foot that seals the shell aperture
When the snail retracts its soft foot into the shell, the operculum seals the aperture, preventing desiccation and protecting against predators.
Problem 7: What is the 'Protoconch'?
The embryonic apical shell formed in the larval stage prior to hatching
Preserved at the very tip of the spire, the protoconch lacks adult ornamentation, providing critical developmental clues regarding larval feeding modes.
Problem 8: Modern Ecological Role of *Physa fontinalis*
Freshwater bladder snail acting as a bioindicator of organic pollution
Possessing secondary vascularized lung tissues, *Physa* survives in stagnant, low-dissolved-oxygen pond environments by breathing atmospheric air at the water surface.
