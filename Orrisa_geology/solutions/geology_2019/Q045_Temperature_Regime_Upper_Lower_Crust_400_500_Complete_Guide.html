Crustal Geothermics: Temperature Regimes Across the Continental Crust

The thermal regime of the continental crust is governed by the 1D conductive heat transfer equation, sustained by two primary sources: radiogenic heat production ($A_0$) from radioactive decay of $^{238}\text{U}, ^{235}\text{U}, ^{232}\text{Th},$ and $^{40}\text{K}$ concentrated within felsic upper crustal rocks, and mantle basal heat flux ($q_m$) conducted into the base of the crust from the underlying asthenosphere.

  • Crustal Geothermal Gradient: In stable continental shields and cratons, the geothermal gradient near the surface averages approximately $20^\circ - 25^\circ\text{C / km}$ (or $25^\circ - 30^\circ\text{C / km}$ in younger tectonic provinces). Because radiogenic elements are strongly concentrated in the upper $10-15\text{ km}$, the gradient curves and decreases with depth.
  • Depth of the Lower Crust:
    • Upper Crust (Sial): Extends from surface to the Conrad Discontinuity at depths of approximately $15 - 20\text{ km}$.
    • Lower Crust (Sima): Extends from the Conrad Discontinuity ($pprox 20\text{ km}$) down to the Mohorovicic Discontinuity (Moho) at $pprox 35 - 40\text{ km}$.
  • Temperature at the Upper Part of the Lower Crust:
    • At the top of the lower crust (depth $z \approx 20 - 22\text{ km}$), conductive thermal integration yields: $$T(z) = T_0 + \int_0^z \left( \frac{q_m + A(z')}{k} \right) dz' \approx \mathbf{400^\circ - 500^\circ\text{C}}$$
    • Rheological Significance: This $400^\circ - 500^\circ\text{C}$ temperature regime marks the critical Brittle-to-Ductile Transition Zone for plagioclase feldspar. While quartz transitions from brittle to crystal-plastic dislocation creep at $pprox 300^\circ\text{C}$, plagioclase feldspars (the dominant framework mineral of the lower crust) become plastic only above $450^\circ - 500^\circ\text{C}$. Thus, the upper part of the lower crust marks the threshold where the entire crust becomes fully ductile.
"Temperature increases continuously with depth within the earth. In the continental crust, the average geothermal gradient is about 20° to 30°C per kilometer depth. At the upper boundary of the lower crust, at depths of about 20 km, the temperature regime reaches approximately 400° to 500°C. At the base of the crust (Moho), temperatures reach about 600° to 800°C." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Interior of the Earth: Temperature Regime, p. 22) & John D. Winter, Ch. 1, p. 5
1D Steady-State Continental Geotherm Equation
$$T(z) = T_0 + \frac{q_0 z}{k} - \frac{A_0 D^2}{k} \left( 1 - e^{-z/D} \right)$$ $$\text{At depth } z = 20\text{ km:} \quad T(20) \approx 20^\circ\text{C} + (20\text{ km} \times 22^\circ\text{C/km}) \approx \mathbf{440^\circ - 460^\circ\text{C}}$$ $$\text{Brittle-Ductile Transition: } \quad T_{\text{quartz plasticity}} \approx 300^\circ\text{C} \quad \big| \quad T_{\text{feldspar plasticity}} \approx 450^\circ - 500^\circ\text{C}$$

Where $q_0$ is surface heat flow ($pprox 60 ext{ mW/m}^2$), $k$ is thermal conductivity ($pprox 2.5-3.0 ext{ W}/( ext{m}\cdot ext{K})$), $A_0$ is surface radiogenic heat production ($pprox 2 ext{ }\mu ext{W/m}^3$), and $D$ is radiogenic scale depth ($pprox 10 ext{ km}$).

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q045
The temperature regime in the upper part of the lower crust is:
(A) 400-500°C
(B) 300-400°C
(C) 100-200°C
(D) 200-250°C
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (A) 400-500°C
Verified against Bangar (p. 22), Winter (Principles of Igneous & Metamorphic Petrology, p. 5), and Fowler (The Solid Earth, p. 235). Under standard continental geothermal gradients ($20-25^\circ ext{C/km}$), temperatures at depths of $20-25 ext{ km}$ (the upper lower crust) reach precisely 400° to 500°C.
(A) 400-500°C CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: The lower continental crust begins at the Conrad discontinuity at a depth of approximately $20 ext{ km}$. Multiplying this depth by standard continental geothermal gradients ($20^\circ-25^\circ ext{C/km}$) demonstrates that the temperature regime prevailing at the top of the lower crust is precisely $400^\circ - 500^\circ ext{C}$. This temperature matches the onset of crystal-plasticity in feldspars and corresponds to middle amphibolite facies metamorphic conditions.
(B) 300-400°C INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The $300^\circ - 400^\circ ext{C}$ temperature regime is characteristic of the middle to lower part of the UPPER crust (depths of $12 - 16 ext{ km}$), where greenschist facies metamorphism occurs and quartz undergoes plastic deformation while feldspar remains brittle.
(C) 100-200°C INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: $100^\circ - 200^\circ ext{C}$ corresponds to the shallow upper crust at depths of only $4 - 8 ext{ km}$, characteristic of sedimentary diagenesis and early zeolite facies burial metamorphism.
(D) 200-250°C INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Temperatures of $200^\circ - 250^\circ ext{C}$ prevail at depths of $8 - 10 ext{ km}$ within the upper crust, where oil generation windows terminate and brittle micro-fracturing dominates.

Problem 1: Temperature Calculation from Geothermal Gradient
Temperature $T = 460^\circ\text{C}$ at $z = 20\text{ km}$
Assuming a mean surface temperature $T_0 = 20^\circ\text{C}$ and an average geothermal gradient $\nabla T = 22^\circ\text{C/km}$ through the upper crust, calculate the temperature at depth $z = 20\text{ km}$.
$$T(20) = T_0 + z \cdot \nabla T = 20 + 20 \times 22 = 20 + 440 = 460^\circ\text{C}$$
Problem 2: Plagioclase Brittle-Ductile Transition Temperature
Plagioclase transitions to crystal-plasticity at $450^\circ - 500^\circ\text{C}$
In quartz-feldspathic crustal rocks, quartz flows ductilely above $300^\circ\text{C}$, but the rock retains macroscopic strength until plagioclase feldspars soften at $450-500^\circ\text{C}$, enabling pervasive ductile shearing in the lower crust.
Problem 3: The Conrad Discontinuity as a Petrological Boundary
Transition from granodioritic upper crust to mafic granulitic lower crust
V. Conrad (1925) identified a mid-crustal seismic velocity jump from $6.0$ to $6.8\text{ km/s}$ at $15-20\text{ km}$ depth, marking the compositional change from hydrous granitic rocks to dry, dense mafic granulites.
Problem 4: Heat Generation in the Lower Crust vs. Upper Crust
Lower crust is depleted in radioactive isotopes ($A_{\text{lower}} \approx 0.2 - 0.4\text{ }\mu\text{W/m}^3$)
Large-ion lithophile elements (U, Th, K) are strongly incompatible in mantle melting and partition preferentially into upper crustal granites ($A_{\text{upper}} \approx 1.5 - 3.0\text{ }\mu\text{W/m}^3$), leaving the lower crust chemically depleted in radiogenic heat production.
Problem 5: Maximum Depth of Continental Earthquakes
Restricted to upper $15 - 20\text{ km}$ (the seismogenic zone)
Because rocks below $20\text{ km}$ ($>400^\circ\text{C}$) deform by ductile plastic creep rather than brittle fracture, elastic strain cannot accumulate, confining continental earthquakes strictly to the brittle upper crust.
Problem 6: Dehydration Melting of Biotite in the Lower Crust
Biotite dehydration melting initiates at $\approx 750^\circ - 800^\circ\text{C}$
At depths approaching the Moho ($30-35\text{ km}$), temperatures exceed $750^\circ\text{C}$, causing biotite and hornblende to break down via dehydration melting, generating granitic leucosomes (migmatites) and anhydrous granulite restites.
Problem 7: Underplating of Mafic Magma at the Moho
Ponding of dense mantle basalt at crust-mantle boundary due to neutral buoyancy
Ascending mantle basaltic melts ($ ho pprox 2.8 ext{ g/cm}^3$) are more buoyant than mantle ($3.3 ext{ g/cm}^3$) but denser than upper crust ($2.7 ext{ g/cm}^3$), causing them to pond and crystallize at the Moho, underplating the lower crust with high-temperature gabbroic cumulates.
Problem 8: Electrical Conductivity of the Lower Crust
High conductivity layers caused by saline metamorphic fluids or graphite films
Magnetotelluric (MT) surveys worldwide reveal zones of anomalous high electrical conductivity at $20-30 ext{ km}$ depth ($400-500^\circ ext{C}$), attributed to interconnected saline brines released by prograde metamorphic dehydration.
