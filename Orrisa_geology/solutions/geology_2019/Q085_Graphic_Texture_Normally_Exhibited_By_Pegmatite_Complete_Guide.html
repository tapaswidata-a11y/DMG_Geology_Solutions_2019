1. Theoretical Foundation: Origin & Morphology of Graphic Texture

Graphic texture (also termed runic texture or micrographic texture) is a distinctive magmatic intergrowth texture where angular, wedge-shaped, or hook-shaped rods of quartz are enclosed within a single optically continuous host crystal of alkali feldspar (microcline or orthoclase). In cross-section, the skeletal quartz crystals strikingly resemble ancient cuneiform, Arabic, or runic writing:

Feature Petrological Characteristic Physicochemical Significance
Host Rock Type Granitic Pegmatite (Option B) Exhibited magnificently in coarse pegmatite border and wall zones (often termed graphic granite).
Host Mineral Microcline / Orthoclase (Alkali Feldspar) Forms large, single-crystal optically continuous background plates (pink, buff, or white).
Intersertal Mineral Quartz (Ichthyoglypts / Skeletal Rods) Parallel three-dimensional rods of quartz that extinguish simultaneously under crossed polars.
Petrogenetic Mechanism Simultaneous Eutectic Crystallization Precipitates at the granite eutectic minimum in volatile-rich, undercooled residual pegmatitic melts.
Phase Ratio $\sim 72\text{--}75\%$ Feldspar : $25\text{--}28\%$ Quartz Matches precisely the eutectic proportions in the binary $SiO_2 - KAlSi_3O_8 - H_2O$ phase system at water-vapor pressures.
"Graphic texture is an intimate intergrowth of quartz and alkali feldspar in which the quartz forms angular, skeletal grains resembling runic inscriptions or ancient cuneiform characters. It is the hallmark texture of pegmatites (graphic granite), resulting from the simultaneous, rapid, eutectic crystallization of quartz and feldspar from a volatile-rich, fluxed residual magmatic liquid."
— K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Petrology Section, p. 115)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 85
Graphic texture is normally exhibited by :
A Komatiite
B Pegmatite
C Phonolite
D Anorthosite
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION B (Pegmatite)
OPTION B • CORRECT
Pegmatite

Graphic texture is the textbook diagnostic feature of granitic pegmatites (often known commercially as graphic granite). It forms by the simultaneous, eutectic precipitation of skeletal quartz rods within large plates of alkali feldspar from volatile-saturated, fluxed residual fluids.

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Komatiite

Komatiite is an Archaean ultramafic volcanic rock characterized by spinifex texture (skeletal, bladed, or platy crystals of olivine and pyroxene caused by rapid surface supercooling). It never contains graphic quartz-feldspar intergrowths.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Phonolite

Phonolite is an undersaturated alkaline volcanic rock composed of nepheline, sanidine, and aegirine. Because phonolite is silica-undersaturated (foid-bearing), free quartz cannot form, making graphic texture chemically impossible.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
Anorthosite

Anorthosite is a coarse-grained monomineralic plutonic rock consisting of >90% plagioclase feldspar displaying cumulate and adcumulate textures, not graphic intergrowths.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. How does micrographic texture differ from granophyric texture?
Answer: Granophyric texture exhibits a radiating, plumose, or spherulitic intergrowth of quartz and feldspar, often in subvolcanic felsic intrusions.
Granophyric texture occurs at shallower depths and higher undercooling than classic coarse graphic pegmatite.
2. What optical proof confirms that all quartz rods in a graphic granite field belong to a single continuous crystal network?
Answer: They extinguish simultaneously under crossed nicols (birefringence extinction).
Even though cross-sections appear as disconnected angular rods, all quartz units share a single three-dimensional crystallographic orientation.
3. What is the difference between graphic texture and myrmekitic texture?
Answer: Graphic texture is magmatic quartz in K-feldspar; myrmekite is vermicular quartz in sodic plagioclase replacing K-feldspar.
Myrmekite forms by subsolidus fluid-driven metasomatism: $KAlSi_3O_8 + Na^+/Ca^{2+} \rightarrow (Na,Ca)AlSi_3O_8 + SiO_2 (quartz) + K^+$.
4. What is a complex pegmatite with distinct concentric zones called?
Answer: Zoned pegmatite (e.g., Border zone $\rightarrow$ Wall zone $\rightarrow$ Intermediate zone $\rightarrow$ Quartz core)
Cameron et al. (1949) demonstrated that graphic texture typically develops in the intermediate wall zone of zoned pegmatites.
5. What volatile components lower the crystallization temperature of pegmatite melts to $500\text{--}650^\circ\text{C}$?
Answer: Water ($H_2O$), Fluorine ($F$), Boron ($B$), and Lithium ($Li$)
These network-modifying fluxing components depolymerize the silicate melt, reducing viscosity and enabling giant crystal growth.
6. In which Indian state are the world-famous Bihar Mica Belt pegmatites located?
Answer: Jharkhand (Koderma, Giridih, Hazaribagh districts)
The Koderma pegmatite belt is renowned for giant muscovite 'books', beryl, and exquisite graphic granites.
7. What is an aplite in relation to a pegmatite?
Answer: Aplite is a fine-grained, sugary (saccharoidal) felsic dyke rock of identical granitic composition to pegmatite.
Aplites crystallize rapidly due to sudden pressure-release quenching (loss of volatiles), whereas pegmatites crystallize slowly from volatile-rich fluids.
8. What is the term for the skeletal fish-hook shaped quartz grains in graphic texture?
Answer: Ichthyoglypts
Classical petrographers termed the hieroglyphic, hook-shaped quartz rods in graphic granite 'ichthyoglypts' (fish carvings).
