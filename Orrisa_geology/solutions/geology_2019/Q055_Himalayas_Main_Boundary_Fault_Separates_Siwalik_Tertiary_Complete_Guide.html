Himalayan Tectonics: Regional Longitudinal Fault Architecture & The MBT

The Himalayan Orogenic Belt—formed by the ongoing collision of the Indian tectonic plate with the Eurasian plate—is structurally segmented into four classic longitudinal morphotectonic belts bounded by giant, crustal-scale northward-dipping thrust fault systems. Among these fundamental dislocations, the Main Boundary Thrust (MBT), also termed the Main Boundary Fault, is a paramount regional tectonic boundary.

  • Structural Architecture of the Himalaya (North to South):
    1. Indus-Tsangpo Suture Zone (ITSZ): The fundamental collision zone between India and Asia, marked by ophiolitic mélanges and Cretaceous-Eocene flysch.
    2. South Tibetan Detachment System (STDS): North-dipping low-angle normal fault separating the Higher Himalayan Crystalline basement from the overlying Tethyan Himalayan sedimentary zone.
    3. Main Central Thrust (MCT): Major crustal shear zone placing the high-grade, kyanite-sillimanite Higher Himalayan Crystalline (HHC) basement over the lower-grade Lesser Himalayan metasediments.
    4. Main Boundary Thrust (MBT / Main Boundary Fault):
      • Structural Definition: A prominent north-dipping reverse/thrust fault system extending continuously for over $2,400\text{ km}$ along the entire Himalayan arc.
      • Juxtaposed Terranes: It thrusts the Lesser Himalayan sedimentary and Early Tertiary belt (Pre-Tertiary metasediments along with Paleogene Subathu, Dagshai, and Kasauli formations) southward over the Sub-Himalayan Neogene Siwalik molasse.
    5. Main Frontal Thrust (MFT / Himalayan Frontal Thrust): The southernmost active blind thrust fault placing the folded Sub-Himalayan Siwalik molasse directly over the modern Quaternary Indo-Gangetic Alluvium.
"The Main Boundary Fault (MBT): The Main Boundary Fault is a major structural feature extending all along the outer ranges of the Himalayas. It is a great reverse fault (thrust) that separates the Lesser Himalayan formations (and Early Tertiary beds) from the younger Sub-Himalayan Siwalik rocks. The older rocks of the Lesser Himalaya have been thrust southward along this plane over the Siwaliks." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Structural Features of the Himalayas: MBT, pp. 306-307)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 55
In Himalayas, the main boundary fault separates ;
(A) Siwalik and Tertiary
(B) Siwalik and Archean
(C) Vindhyan and Aravalli
(D) Siwalik and Delhi Group
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (A) Siwalik and Tertiary
(A) Siwalik and Tertiary (Lesser Himalaya) CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: The Main Boundary Thrust (MBT / Main Boundary Fault) separates the Sub-Himalayan Siwalik Group from the older Lesser Himalayan rock units, which include the early Tertiary Paleogene sequence (Subathu, Dagshai, Kasauli Formations) and thrust sheets of the Lesser Himalaya. In standard examination phrasing, this is categorized as separating the Siwaliks from the Tertiary / Lesser Himalayan belt.
(B) Siwalik and Archean INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The footwall and hanging wall of the MBT do not involve Archean cratonic rocks. The hanging wall contains Proterozoic-Paleozoic Lesser Himalayan metasediments and Paleogene strata; high-grade Archean-Paleoproterozoic crystallines lie far to the north in the Higher Himalaya above the MCT.
(C) Vindhyan and Aravalli INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The fault separating the Vindhyan Supergroup from the deformed Aravalli belt is the Great Boundary Fault (GBF) of Rajasthan, located in the Peninsular Shield over 500 km south of the Himalayas.
(D) Siwalik and Delhi Group INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Delhi Group is confined to the Aravalli-Delhi orogen in Rajasthan, Haryana, and Delhi. It does not bound the Siwaliks along the Himalayan MBT.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Himalayan Structural Architecture
Problem 1: Dip Angle and Geometry of the MBT
Dips northward at 30° to 45° near surface, flattening into the Main Himalayan Décollement (MHD) at depth
Seismic reflection and receiver function profiles show that the MBT, MCT, and MFT all sole out into a single basal detachment fault—the Main Himalayan Thrust (MHT)—at 10-15 km crustal depth.
Problem 2: The Paleogene (Early Tertiary) Himalayan Belt
Subathu Formation (Eocene), Dagshai Formation (Oligocene), and Kasauli Formation (Early Miocene)
This succession documents the transition from shallow-marine foreland basin conditions (*Subathu* nummulitic limestones) to brackish (*Dagshai* red beds) and finally freshwater fluvial molasse (*Kasauli* plant sandstones).
Problem 3: Medlicott's Original Concept of the 'Main Boundary Fault'
Interpreted by H.B. Medlicott (1864) as the depositional boundary basin margin for Siwalik sedimentation
Medlicott originally believed the MBF marked the original coastline/cliff against which Siwaliks were deposited; later mapping proved it is an active tectonic thrust that cut through and displaced older strata.
Problem 4: Metamorphic Discontinuity Across the MCT
Inverted metamorphism with grade increasing upward into the hanging wall
Across the MCT, metamorphic grade paradoxically increases upwards from chlorite-sericite phyllites in the Lesser Himalayan footwall to kyanite-sillimanite gneisses in the Higher Himalayan hanging wall.
Problem 5: Dun Valleys in the Sub-Himalaya
Broad synclinal longitudinal valleys (e.g., Dehradun) developed between the MBT and MFT
Duns are structural depressions filled with gravel and alluvium, trapped between the rising Lesser Himalayan front to the north and the Siwalik outer ridges to the south.
Problem 6: Krol Thrust and MBT Relationship
The Krol Thrust is a frontal branch/nappe of the MBT system bringing Neoproterozoic carbonates over Tertiary beds
In the Garhwal and Kumaon foothills, the Krol Nappe carries Infra-Krol shales and Krol limestones over the Subathu/Dagshai Tertiary sequence along the Krol Thrust.
Problem 7: Great Earthquake Nucleation Along the Main Himalayan Thrust
Slip is locked along the ramp of the MHT beneath the Lesser Himalaya, breaking toward the MBT and MFT
Megathrust events (e.g., 1905 Kangra, 1934 Bihar-Nepal, 2015 Gorkha) nucleate on the locked MHT ramp, releasing strain by propagating slip toward the frontal MBT/MFT fault traces.
Problem 8: Modern Active Shortening Rate Accommodated Across the Himalaya
~15 to 20 mm/year of total ~40 mm/year Indo-Eurasian convergence
GPS geodesy establishes that approximately half of the total plate collision velocity is absorbed by crustal shortening and seismic strain accumulation across the MFT/MBT belt.
