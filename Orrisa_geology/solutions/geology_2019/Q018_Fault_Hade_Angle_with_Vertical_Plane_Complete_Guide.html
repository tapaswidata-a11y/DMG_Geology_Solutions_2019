Geometric Elements of Fault Planes: Dip vs. Hade

In structural geology and mining geometry, a fault plane is a planar fracture along which measurable displacement has occurred. To fully specify the spatial attitude of an inclined fault plane, structural geologists and mining engineers utilize two complementary angular measurements measured relative to orthogonal reference planes:

  • Dip ($\delta$): The maximum angle of inclination that the fault plane makes with a horizontal plane, measured perpendicular to the strike in a vertical plane ($0^\circ \le \delta \le 90^\circ$).
  • Hade ($ heta_h$): The angle that the fault plane makes with a vertical plane, measured perpendicular to the strike ($0^\circ \le \theta_h \le 90^\circ$).
"The angle which an inclined fault plane makes with the vertical plane is known as the hade of the fault. It is the complement of the dip angle of the fault plane, such that: $$\text{Hade} + \text{Dip} = 90^\circ \implies \text{Hade} = 90^\circ - \text{Dip}$$ For example, if a fault plane has a dip of $60^\circ$, its hade is $30^\circ$. The hade is always measured in a direction perpendicular to the strike of the fault." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Structural Geology: Fault Terminology, p. 208)
Fundamental Angular Relationship for Fault Plane Geometry
$$\text{Hade } (\theta_h) = 90^\circ - \text{Dip } (\delta)$$ $$\text{Heave } (H) = \text{Dip Slip } (S_d) \cdot \cos \delta = S_d \cdot \sin \theta_h$$ $$\text{Throw } (T) = \text{Dip Slip } (S_d) \cdot \sin \delta = S_d \cdot \cos \theta_h$$

Where $\text{Heave}$ is the horizontal displacement component perpendicular to strike, and $\text{Throw}$ is the vertical displacement component.

Official Question Statement — OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 Paper-II (Question 18)
18. The angle which the fault plane makes with vertical plane is known as :
(A) Net slip
(B) Heave
(C) Strike slip
(D) Hade  ✔
✔
Correct Answer: (D) Hade
In structural geology and mining geometry (K.M. Bangar, p. 208; Marland P. Billings, p. 138; Fossen, 2016; Davis & Reynolds, 1996), the angle which the fault plane makes with a vertical plane is universally defined as the Hade.
(A) Net slip Incorrect Distractor
Why this distractor is incorrect: Net slip is a distance (linear displacement vector measured in meters), representing the total true displacement between two formerly contiguous piercing points on opposing sides of the fault plane. It is not an angle made with a vertical plane.
(B) Heave Incorrect Distractor
Why this distractor is incorrect: Heave is the horizontal component of dip slip (measured in meters), representing the horizontal gap or separation between the displaced ends of a faulted bed measured perpendicular to strike. It is a linear distance, not an angle.
(C) Strike slip Incorrect Distractor
Why this distractor is incorrect: Strike slip is the component of net slip measured parallel to the fault strike within the fault plane. It is a linear displacement component, not an angle.
(D) Hade Correct Verified Option
Why this option is correct: By standard international definition, hade is the exact angle that an inclined fault plane (or vein/bed) makes with the vertical plane. It is the geometric complement of the dip angle ($\text{Hade} = 90^\circ - \text{Dip}$).

Comprehensive Coordinate Transformations: Dip, Hade, Throw & Heave

Given an inclined fault with dip angle $\delta$ and true dip-slip displacement $S_d$:

$$\text{Hade } \theta_h = 90^\circ - \delta$$ $$\text{Throw } T = S_d \cdot \sin \delta = S_d \cdot \cos \theta_h$$ $$\text{Heave } H = S_d \cdot \cos \delta = S_d \cdot \sin \theta_h$$ $$\tan \delta = \frac{\text{Throw}}{\text{Heave}} \implies \tan \theta_h = \frac{\text{Heave}}{\text{Throw}}$$
High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Problems)
  • Practice Q1 (Numerical): A normal fault in a coal mine dips at an angle $\delta = 55^\circ$. (a) Calculate the hade of the fault plane. (b) If the measured throw of a faulted coal seam is $T = 32.0\text{ m}$, calculate the heave ($H$) and the dip-slip ($S_d$) along the fault plane.
    ✔ Worked Answer: (a) $\text{Hade} = 35^\circ$; (b) $\text{Heave} = 22.4\text{ m}$; $\text{Dip-slip} = 39.1\text{ m}$.
    Derivation:
    (a) $\text{Hade } \theta_h = 90^\circ - \delta = 90^\circ - 55^\circ = 35^\circ$.
    (b) $\text{Heave } H = \frac{T}{\tan \delta} = \frac{32.0}{\tan 55^\circ} = \frac{32.0}{1.4281} \approx 22.41\text{ m}$.
    $$\text{Dip-slip } S_d = \frac{T}{\sin \delta} = \frac{32.0}{\sin 55^\circ} = \frac{32.0}{0.8192} \approx 39.06\text{ m}$$
  • Practice Q2: If a fault plane is perfectly vertical ($\delta = 90^\circ$), what is its hade?
    ✔ Correct Answer: $ ext{Hade} = 0^\circ$.
    Explanation: Because the fault plane is coincident with the vertical plane, the angle between them is zero ($ ext{Hade} = 90^\circ - 90^\circ = 0^\circ$).
  • Practice Q3: If a detachment fault has a hade $ heta_h = 75^\circ$, classify the fault as high-angle or low-angle based on its dip.
    ✔ Correct Answer: Low-angle fault ($ ext{Dip } \delta = 15^\circ < 45^\circ$).
    Explanation: $ ext{Dip} = 90^\circ - ext{Hade} = 90^\circ - 75^\circ = 15^\circ$. Since $\delta < 45^\circ$, it is a low-angle detachment or thrust fault.
  • Practice Q4: In historical Cornish mining terminology, why did miners prefer measuring "hade" rather than "dip"?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Because miners suspended a plumb-bob (vertical plumb-line) inside shafts; measuring the angle from the plumb-line directly gave the hade.
    Explanation: A plumb-line gives a precise vertical reference; measuring deviation of the mineral vein from the string gave the hade without needing an artificial horizontal bubble level.
  • Practice Q5: What is the "apparent dip" of a fault plane, and how does it compare to the "true dip"?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Apparent dip is the inclination measured in any vertical plane not perpendicular to strike; it is always strictly less than the true dip ($ an lpha = an \delta \cdot \cos eta$).
    Explanation: True dip is measured perpendicular to strike and represents the maximum inclination; in any oblique cross-section, apparent dip ($lpha$) is smaller.
  • Practice Q6: What is a "fault scrap" vs. a "fault-line scarp"?
    ✔ Correct Answer: A fault scarp is generated directly by active tectonic displacement; a fault-line scarp is carved by differential erosion along an ancient, dormant fault line.
    Explanation: Fault-line scarps often face in the opposite direction of the original tectonic displacement if the downthrown side contains more resistant rock (obsequent fault-line scarp).
  • Practice Q7: In structural contour mapping of a planar fault, how does the spacing between strike contours relate to dip and hade?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Horizontal contour spacing $S = \Delta h / an \delta = \Delta h \cdot an heta_h$.
    Explanation: For a given contour interval $\Delta h$, steeper faults (smaller hade) have closely spaced contours; gently dipping faults (larger hade) have widely spaced contours.
  • Practice Q8: What is the "Rake" (or Pitch) of a line on an inclined fault plane?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The angle that a linear feature (e.g., striations/slickenlines) makes with the horizontal strike line within the fault plane ($0^\circ \le \phi \le 90^\circ$).
    Explanation: Measured on the inclined plane itself using a protractor; $ ext{Rake} = 90^\circ$ represents pure dip-slip; $ ext{Rake} = 0^\circ$ represents pure strike-slip.
