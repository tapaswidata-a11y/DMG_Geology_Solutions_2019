1. Theoretical Foundation: Geometric Classification of Inclined Strata

In structural geology and geological mapping, planar sedimentary strata and metamorphic foliations are described by their attitude (strike and dip). The angle of inclination of a bedding plane with the true horizontal plane is termed its true dip ($\theta$). In standard geological engineering classifications (Billings, 1972; Bangar, p. 162), inclined strata are classified based on the magnitude of dip angle:

Inclination Class Dip Angle Range ($\theta$) Outcrop Character & Topographic Expression Engineering Significance (Slopes & Tunnels)
Gently Inclined (Gentle Dip) $0^\circ < \theta < 15^\circ$ (or $0^\circ\text{--}10^\circ$) Forms mesas, buttes, and gentle cuestas; broad outcrop widths in valleys. Extremely stable flat foundations; horizontal tunnel drives.
MODERATELY INCLINED STRATA (Option A) $15^\circ \le \theta \le 45^\circ$ (Angles upto $45^\circ$) Asymmetrical ridges (cuestas) with distinct dip slope and escarpment. Critical dip range for translational planar rockslides (daylighting on slope cuts).
Steeply Inclined Strata (Option B) $45^\circ < \theta < 85^\circ$ Symmetrical razor-backed ridges (hogbacks); narrow outcrop bands. Prone to toppling failures; tunnels cutting normal to strike are most stable.
Vertical Strata $\theta \approx 90^\circ$ Straight linear bands cutting straight across contour lines regardless of topography. No dip-slope sliding; high vertical bearing capacity.
Overturned Strata (Option D) Inverted past $90^\circ$ Oldest beds lie on top of younger beds; confirmed by geopetal structures. Indicates intense recumbent nappe folding.
"According to the angle of inclination (dip), stratified rocks are categorized into: Gently inclined strata (dipping at angles less than 15°), Moderately inclined strata (dipping at angles between 15° and 45°, or angles up to 45°), and Steeply inclined strata (dipping at angles greater than 45°). Plunge (Option C) refers specifically to the inclination of a linear fold axis, not a planar stratum."
— K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Structural Geology, pp. 162–164)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 113
Strata dipping at angles upto 45° are called :
A Moderately inclined strata
B Steeply inclined strata
C Plunge
D Overturned
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION A (Moderately inclined strata)
OPTION A • CORRECT
Moderately inclined strata

By standard structural geology conventions (Billings; Bangar, p. 162; Mahapatra), stratified rock layers dipping at angles between 15° and 45° (or angles upto 45°) are formally designated as moderately inclined strata.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Steeply inclined strata

Steeply inclined strata have dip angles greater than 45° up to nearly vertical ($45^\circ\text{--}85^\circ$), forming symmetrical hogback ridges.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Plunge

'Plunge' is the inclination of a linear feature (such as a fold hinge line or lineation) with the horizontal plane, not the planar dip of a bedding surface.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
Overturned

Overturned strata have been rotated through tectonic folding past the vertical (over $90^\circ$), such that original young beds underlie older beds.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What is the difference between a cuesta and a hogback?
Answer: A cuesta has a gentle dip slope ($< 30^\circ\text{--}40^\circ$) and a steep scarp slope; a hogback has nearly equal steep slopes on both sides ($> 40^\circ\text{--}45^\circ$).
Moderately inclined strata produce cuestas; steeply inclined strata produce symmetrical hogbacks.
2. Why are cut slopes excavated in moderately inclined strata dipping into the roadway exceptionally dangerous?
Answer: If daylighted, the bedding plane acts as a planar slide surface where dip angle ($\theta$) exceeds the friction angle ($\phi$).
Translational planar rockslides occur spontaneously along moderately inclined beds ($20^\circ\text{--}45^\circ$) when the toe is excavated.
3. What is the Strike of an inclined stratum?
Answer: The compass direction of a horizontal line drawn on the bedding plane (perpendicular to true dip).
Strike is always exactly perpendicular to the direction of maximum (true) dip.
4. What is Apparent Dip ($\alpha$) versus True Dip ($\theta$)?
Answer: True dip is the maximum inclination perpendicular to strike; apparent dip is the inclination along any other oblique direction ($\alpha < \theta$).
$\tan \alpha = \tan \theta \cdot \cos \beta$, where $\beta$ is the angle between strike and apparent dip direction.
5. What does the Rule of 'V's state when strata dip upstream?
Answer: The outcrop pattern in the valley forms a 'V' pointing upstream.
Whenever beds dip upstream (against the valley gradient), the outcrop 'V' invariably points upstream.
6. What is the relation between true bed thickness ($T$), outcrop width ($W$), and dip angle ($\theta$) on horizontal ground?
Answer: $T = W \cdot \sin \theta$
As dip angle increases, the outcrop width for the same true bed thickness narrows.
7. In tunnel construction, which alignment relative to strike of inclined strata provides the most stable conditions?
Answer: Tunneling perpendicular to strike (cutting across beds), preferably driving into the dip.
Driving parallel to strike leaves entire sidewalls supported on a single dipping bedding plane, promoting sidewall sliding.
8. What is a clinometer used for in field geology?
Answer: Measuring the angle of dip of a geological plane or the plunge of a lineation.
A Brunton compass combines a magnetic needle for strike azimuth with a spirit-level clinometer for dip angle measurement.
