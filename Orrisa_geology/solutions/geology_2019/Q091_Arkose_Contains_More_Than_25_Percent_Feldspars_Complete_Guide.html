1. Theoretical Foundation: Sandstone Classification & Arkose Systematics

In sedimentary petrology, terrigenous clastic sandstones are classified on the ternary QFL Diagram (Quartz – Feldspar – Lithic rock fragments) pioneered by R.L. Folk (1968) and F.J. Pettijohn (1975). A sandstone is formally designated as an Arkose (or arkosic arenite) when its detrital framework contains more than 25% feldspar:

ARKOSE (Option C) < 75% > 25% Feldspar ($F / L > 3:1$) < 25% Lithics Rapid erosion of granitic/gneissic basements in fault-bounded rift basins (graben); arid or cold climate.

Quartzarenite > 95% < 5% < 5% Ultra-mature stable cratonic platforms; multiple cycles of reworking and chemical leaching.
Lithic Arenite < 75% < 25% > 25% Lithics ($L / F > 3:1$) Active orogenic belts, island arcs, and collision suture zones (recycled orogen provenance).
"An arkose is a sandstone composed of quartz and more than 25 percent feldspar (chiefly orthoclase, microcline, or sodic plagioclase). The grains are generally poorly sorted, angular to sub-angular, and cemented by calcite, iron oxides, or silica. The presence of abundant fresh feldspar indicates that the source rocks (granites or gneisses) underwent rapid mechanical disintegration under arid, semi-arid, or high-relief tectonic conditions before chemical decomposition could destroy the feldspars."
— F.J. Pettijohn, Sedimentary Rocks (3rd Ed., Harper & Row, p. 214)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 91
Arkose contain more than 25% of :
A Micas
B Amphiboles
C Feldspars
D Chlorite
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION C (Feldspars)
OPTION C • CORRECT
Feldspars

By international consensus across all modern sandstone classifications (Folk, 1968; Pettijohn, 1975; Dickinson, 1985; Bangar, p. 136), an arkose is strictly defined as a sandstone containing more than 25% feldspar (mostly pink potash feldspars such as microcline and orthoclase). This gives arkose its typical pink, red, or salmon-colored appearance.

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Micas

Micas (biotite and muscovite) may occur as subordinate accessories (typically 1% to 5%) in arkosic sandstones, but never exceed 25%. A sandstone rich in micas is termed a micaceous sandstone or flagstone.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Amphiboles

Amphiboles are unstable ferromagnesian minerals that weather rapidly during surface transport. They occur only in trace quantities as heavy mineral accessories, never exceeding 25% in sandstones.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Chlorite

Chlorite is a common green sheet-silicate mineral found in the matrix of greywackes and low-grade metamorphic rocks. It does not constitute the framework grain defining an arkose.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What two climatic/tectonic factors preserve high percentages of feldspar in arkose against chemical weathering?
Answer: Arid / frigid cold climate and high topographic relief with rapid tectonic uplift and burial.
Chemical weathering hydrolyzes feldspars to clay; an arid or freezing climate, combined with rapid erosion and burial, halts hydrolysis.
2. Which potash feldspar is recognized by its distinctive 'tartan' or 'cross-hatched' twinning in arkose thin sections?
Answer: Microcline
Microcline exhibits prominent gridiron (albite + pericline) cross-hatched twinning under crossed nicols, diagnostic of granitic basement provenance.
3. What prominent Indian sedimentary formation contains basal arkosic sandstones overlying Archaean granitic basements?
Answer: The Talchir Formation (basal Gondwana Supergroup) and Cuddapah Basin basal Gulcheru quartzites/arkoses.
Glacial and periglacial erosion of granites during early Gondwana times formed boulder beds and feldspathic arkoses.
4. What is the difference between an arkosic arenite and an arkosic wacke?
Answer: Arkosic arenite has <15% fine matrix; arkosic wacke has 15% to 50% fine matrix.
Gilbert (1954) and Dott (1964) divide all sandstones at the 15% matrix boundary into arenites and wackes.
5. What is 'grus' in weathering petrology?
Answer: Coarse, angular granitic sand produced by in-situ granular disintegration of granite, forming the direct precursor to arkose.
Mechanical spalling of quartz and feldspar crystals in desert granites generates grus, which upon transport lithifies into arkose.
6. What provenance field on the Dickinson (1985) QFL diagram plots typical arkoses?
Answer: Continental Block Provenance (specifically Basement Uplift)
Dickinson's triangular provenance model links arkoses directly to fault-bounded continental basement uplifts exposing granitic plutons.
7. What cement commonly binds arkose grains giving it a red or brownish color?
Answer: Hematite (iron oxide) and Calcite
Oxidizing continental groundwater precipitates iron oxide coatings (hematite) around feldspar grains, producing red-bed arkoses.
8. What is the feldspar threshold for classifying a sandstone as a 'subarkose'?
Answer: 5% to 25% feldspar
Subarkose occupies the transitional field between quartzarenite (<5% feldspar) and true arkose (>25% feldspar).
