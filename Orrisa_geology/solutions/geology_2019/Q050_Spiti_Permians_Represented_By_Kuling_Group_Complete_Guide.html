Tethyan Himalayan Stratigraphy: Spiti Paleozoic & The Permian Kuling Group

The Spiti Valley in Himachal Pradesh preserves the most complete, continuously fossiliferous marine sedimentary sequence in the entire Indian subcontinent—from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous. Because of its unbroken record, it is globally known as the Museum of Indian Geology. Within this succession, the Permian System is represented exclusively by the Kuling Group (historically termed the Productus Shales).

  • Stratigraphic Context in the Spiti Basin:
    1. Muth Quartzite (Devonian): Pure snow-white orthoquartzite regional marker horizon.
    2. Kanawar Group (Carboniferous): Composed of the Lipak Formation (Tournaisian-Viséan fossiliferous limestones with brachiopods Syringothyris) and the Po Formation (Namurian Fenestella Shales and plant-bearing Thabo Stage).
    3. The Permian Gap & Kuling Group: Above an Early Permian unconformity (reflecting Gondwanan rifting and Panjal volcanic event), the Middle to Late Permian is represented by the Kuling Group (~60 to 100 m thick).
    4. Lithostratigraphic Subdivisions of the Kuling Group:
      • Gechang Formation (Lower Kuling): Calcareous sandstone and sandy limestone (~5-20 m), containing cold-water marine brachiopods (Spiriferella rajah, Neospirifer) deposited during the post-glacial Gondwana marine transgression.
      • Gungri Formation (Upper Kuling / Productus Shale): Monotonous succession of dark grey to black, splintery, highly carbonaceous and phosphatic shales (~50-80 m) containing abundant calcareous concretions.
        Fossil Fauna: Famous for the ammonoid Cyclolobus walkeri, xenodiscid ammonoids, and productid brachiopods (Marginifera himalayensis, Costiferina indica).
    5. Overlying Lilang Group (Triassic): The Gungri Formation is conformably overlain by the basal Triassic Otoceras woodwardi bed (Mikila/Lilang Group), capturing the globally crucial Permian-Triassic (P-Tr) mass extinction boundary.
"Permian of Spiti (Kuling System / Productus Shales): The Permian rocks in Spiti rest with an unconformity over the Carboniferous Kanawar Group. They are known as the Kuling Group (or Productus Shales). The lower division consists of calcareous sandstone (Gechang formation), and the upper division consists of black carbonaceous shales (Gungri formation) containing concretions. Diagnostic fossils include Marginifera himalayensis, Spiriferella rajah, and the Late Permian ammonoid Cyclolobus." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (The Permian System: Spiti, pp. 287-288)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 50
In Spiti, the Permians are represented by :
(A) Muth quartzite
(B) Kanawar Group
(C) Kuling group
(D) Agglomerate shale
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (C) Kuling group
(A) Muth quartzite INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Muth Quartzite is the world-famous marker horizon for the Devonian System in the Himalayas, completely pre-dating the Permian by over 100 million years.
(B) Kanawar Group INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Kanawar Group in Spiti represents the Carboniferous System, comprising the Lipak Formation (Early Carboniferous) and Po Formation (Late Carboniferous Fenestella Shales).
(C) Kuling group CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: The Permian rocks in Spiti are designated lithostratigraphically as the Kuling Group. It is subdivided into the lower Gechang Formation (calcareous sandstone) and upper Gungri Formation (Productus Shales) containing *Cyclolobus walkeri* and *Marginifera himalayensis*.
(D) Agglomerate shale (Agglomeratic Slate) INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Agglomeratic Slate represents the Carboniferous-Permian volcanic-pyroclastic rift deposits of the Kashmir Basin associated with the Panjal Traps, not the marine sedimentary sequence of the Spiti basin.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Himalayan Paleozoic Stratigraphy
Problem 1: Diagnostic Value of *Cyclolobus walkeri*
Index ammonoid for the uppermost Late Permian (Changhsingian stage)
*Cyclolobus* features complex ammonitic suture lines with numerous auxiliary lobes, serving globally as the definitive ammonoid marker for the closing stages of the Permian Period prior to the mass extinction.
Problem 2: Nature of the Permian-Triassic Boundary in Spiti
Apparent paraconformity/conformity between Gungri black shales and Otoceras limestone
At the classic Guling and Attargoo sections in Spiti, the top of the Gungri Formation is directly overlain by the *Otoceras woodwardi* zone with minimal physical discordance, recording the global P-Tr extinction event in continuous marine strata.
Problem 3: Depositional Environment of the Gungri Formation
Anoxic to euxinic deep-water basinal shelf
High organic carbon content, dark black colour, lack of bioturbation, and widespread phosphatic concretions indicate deposition under stagnant, oxygen-depleted marine bottom waters.
Problem 4: Kashmir Equivalent of the Spiti Kuling Group
The Zewan Formation
In Kashmir, the marine Permian capping the Gangamopteris beds and Panjal Traps is the *Zewan Formation*, containing an identical brachiopod fauna (*Marginifera*, *Spiriferella*).
Problem 5: Plant Fossils in the Carboniferous Po Formation
Rhacopteris ovata flora in the Thabo Stage
The Thabo plant beds in the Po Formation yield *Rhacopteris*, representing the Culm (Early Carboniferous) flora of Gondwanaland, demonstrating temporary emergence before marine submergence.
Problem 6: Why is the Kuling Group Absent in Parts of the Lesser Himalaya?
Due to the Hercynian (Late Paleozoic) epeirogenic uplift and erosion
The Late Paleozoic Hercynian orogenic phase uplifted much of the Indian peninsular margin, causing a regional unconformity where Permian rocks directly overlie older Proterozoic basements or are absent.
Problem 7: Brachiopod Morphological Adaptation of *Marginifera*
Strong internal marginal ridge (flange) to prevent soft-tissue fouling in muddy sediment
*Marginifera himalayensis* possessed a concave brachial valve and a thick internal marginal rim that elevated the lophophore above fluid bottom muds in the quiet Gungri depositional basin.
Problem 8: Origin of Phosphatic Nodules in Kuling Shales
Upwelling of nutrient-rich cold oceanic waters along the northern Gondwanan margin
Coastal upwelling along the southern margin of the Neo-Tethys fostered intense biological productivity, leading to high biogenic silica and phosphate precipitation during early diagenesis.
