1. Theoretical Foundation: Bowen's Reaction Principle (1928)

In his seminal work The Evolution of the Igneous Rocks (1928), Canadian experimental petrologist Norman L. Bowen demonstrated that minerals crystallizing from a cooling basaltic magma do not precipitate independently at single freezing points, but rather react continuously with the residual liquid along two distinct branches:

Reaction Series Branch Crystallization Mechanism Sequence from High-T to Low-T Mineral Structural Evolution
Discontinuous Series (Fe-Mg Silicates) Incongruent reaction at fixed temperatures; early crystal reacts with melt to form a new mineral with a fundamentally different crystal structure. $Mg\text{-Olivine} \rightarrow \text{Orthopyroxene} \rightarrow \text{Clinopyroxene} \rightarrow \text{Amphibole (Hornblende)} \rightarrow \text{Biotite}$ Nesosilicate (isolated tetrahedra) $\rightarrow$ Inosilicate (single chains) $\rightarrow$ Inosilicate (double chains) $\rightarrow$ Phyllosilicate (sheets).
Continuous Series (Feldspar Solid Solution) Continuous ionic exchange without changing the underlying crystal lattice (tectosilicate framework); $Ca^{2+} + Al^{3+} \rightleftharpoons Na^+ + Si^{4+}$. Calcic Plagioclase (Anorthite) $\rightarrow$ Bytownite $\rightarrow$ Labradorite $\rightarrow$ Andesine $\rightarrow$ Oligoclase $\rightarrow$ Sodic Plagioclase (Albite) $CaAl_2Si_2O_8$ (pure calcic anorthite at $\sim 1553^\circ\text{C}$) progressively enriches in $NaAlSi_3O_8$ (sodic albite at $\sim 1118^\circ\text{C}$).
"In the continuous reaction series of the plagioclase feldspars, the first mineral to form at the highest temperature from a basaltic liquid is calcic plagioclase (anorthite, $CaAl_2Si_2O_8$). As cooling proceeds, this early-formed calcic plagioclase reacts continuously with the soda-enriched residual liquid, shifting its solid-solution composition gradually through bytownite, labradorite, and andesine toward sodic plagioclase (albite)."
— N.L. Bowen, The Evolution of the Igneous Rocks (Princeton University Press, 1928)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 78
The first mineral formed in the continuous series of Bowen reaction principle is :
A Sodic-Calcic plagioclase
B Potash feldspar
C Calcic-sodic plagioclase
D Calcic plagioclase
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION D (Calcic plagioclase)
OPTION D • CORRECT
Calcic plagioclase

The continuous series is defined by the plagioclase feldspar binary solid-solution ($An\text{--}Ab$). Because anorthite ($CaAl_2Si_2O_8$) has a much higher melting point ($1553^\circ\text{C}$) than albite ($1118^\circ\text{C}$), the first crystal to precipitate from a cooling basic magma at the highest temperature is calcic plagioclase (anorthite-rich, $An_{90}\text{--}An_{100}$).

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Sodic-Calcic plagioclase

Sodic-calcic plagioclase (such as andesine or oligoclase) forms intermediate to late in the continuous series as the melt becomes progressively depleted in calcium and enriched in sodium.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Potash feldspar

Potash feldspar (orthoclase/microcline) does not belong to the continuous plagioclase series; it precipitates late from the residual felsic liquid at low temperatures ($\sim 700\text{--}650^\circ\text{C}$) after both the continuous and discontinuous series converge.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Calcic-sodic plagioclase

Calcic-sodic plagioclase (such as labradorite, $An_{50}\text{--}An_{70}$) represents a middle stage of continuous crystallization, after initial precipitation of pure calcic plagioclase (anorthite/bytownite).

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What is the first mineral to form in the DISCONTINUOUS series of Bowen's reaction principle?
Answer: Forsteritic Olivine ($Mg_2SiO_4$)
Magnesium-rich olivine (forsterite) has the highest crystallization temperature among mafic silicates ($\sim 1890^\circ\text{C}$ pure, $\sim 1200\text{--}1300^\circ\text{C}$ in basaltic melt).
2. Why does normal zoning in plagioclase phenocrysts always exhibit a calcic core and a sodic rim?
Answer: Because the melt becomes progressively depleted in Ca and enriched in Na during fractional crystallization.
As temperature drops, the plagioclase liquidus and solidus dictate that successive crystal layers become richer in albite ($Na$), leaving the high-T calcic core enclosed by a sodic rim.
3. What happens to early-formed calcic plagioclase crystals if they are rapidly isolated from the melt by sinking?
Answer: Fractional crystallization occurs; the crystals cannot react with the liquid, forming cumulate anorthosite.
Bowen termed this fractional crystallization: removing crystals prevents equilibrium reaction, driving the residual melt toward extreme silicic/alkaline compositions.
4. What is the final mineral to crystallize when both the continuous and discontinuous series converge?
Answer: Quartz ($SiO_2$)
After potash feldspar and muscovite crystallize, residual pure silica precipitates as quartz at $\sim 650\text{--}600^\circ\text{C}$.
5. What type of solid solution occurs in the plagioclase feldspar series?
Answer: Coupled substitution solid solution ($Ca^{2+} + Al^{3+} \rightleftharpoons Na^+ + Si^{4+}$)
Because $Ca^{2+}$ and $Na^+$ have different valencies, charge neutrality is maintained by simultaneously substituting $Al^{3+}$ for $Si^{4+}$ in the tetrahedral framework.
6. Which mineral crystallizes immediately after orthopyroxene in Bowen's discontinuous reaction series?
Answer: Clinopyroxene (Augite), followed by Amphibole (Hornblende)
The discontinuous sequence proceeds: Olivine $\rightarrow$ Orthopyroxene/Clinopyroxene $\rightarrow$ Amphibole $\rightarrow$ Biotite.
7. What is a peritectic reaction in the discontinuous series?
Answer: An incongruent reaction where an early crystal reacts with melt to produce a new crystal phase: Olivine + Liquid $\rightarrow$ Pyroxene.
At the peritectic point ($1557^\circ\text{C}$ in the pure $Mg_2SiO_4-SiO_2$ system), forsterite reacts with silica-rich liquid to form enstatite.
8. Which plagioclase feldspar is most susceptible to chemical weathering under humid surface conditions according to Goldich's Weathering Series?
Answer: Calcic Plagioclase (Anorthite)
Goldich's dissolution series exactly mirrors Bowen's reaction series: high-temperature minerals formed furthest from surface P-T conditions (anorthite, olivine) weather the fastest.
