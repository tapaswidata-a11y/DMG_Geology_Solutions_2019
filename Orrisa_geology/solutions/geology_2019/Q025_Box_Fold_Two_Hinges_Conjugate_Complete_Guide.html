Anatomy of Conjugate Folds: Box Folds & Multiple Hinges

In structural geology, simple cylindrical folds typically possess a single hinge zone where the curvature of the folded surface is maximized, separating two opposing limbs. However, under specific kinematic and mechanical conditions—particularly when two conjugate sets of shear zones or kink bands intersect—structures with more than one hinge line develop. The most prominent example is the Box Fold (also termed a conjugate fold or polyclinal fold).

  • Definition: A box fold is a fold characterized by a broad, relatively flat crest (or trough) bounded on either side by two distinct hinge zones. Beyond each hinge, the outer limbs dip steeply in opposite directions.
  • Conjugate Axial Surfaces: Unlike conventional folds with a single axial plane, a box fold possesses two separate axial surfaces that converge downward (in an anticlinal box fold) or upward (in a synclinal box fold).
  • Polyclinal Geometry: Because there are multiple axial surfaces and hinges within the same profile, the fold is classified as polyclinal. In cross section, the fold profile resembles a three-sided rectangular or trapezoidal box.
"Box Fold: A fold in which the crest is broad and flat, and the limbs dip steeply on both sides. A box fold possesses two distinct hinges and two axial surfaces dipping towards each other. They are common in Jura-type fold belts where competent limestone beds overlie incompetent evaporite decollements." — Marland P. Billings, Structural Geology (Folds: Morphology & Classification, p. 61) & Van der Pluijm & Marshak, p. 259
Kinematic Condition for Conjugate / Box Folding
$$\theta_{\text{conjugate}} \approx 60^\circ \quad \text{between conjugate shear bands/kink planes}$$ $$\sigma_1 \text{ bisects the obtuse or acute angle between the two conjugate axial surfaces}$$ $$\text{Number of Hinges } (N_h) = 2 \quad \text{in a standard box fold profile}$$

The flat crestal domain maintains bedding orientation parallel to regional unshortened strata, while deformation is concentrated along the two flanking hinge zones.

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q025
In which fold there could be two hinges ?
(A) Tight fold
(B) Isoclinal fold
(C) Overturned fold
(D) Box fold
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (D) Box fold
Verified against Billings (p. 61), Van der Pluijm & Marshak (p. 259), and Ramsay & Huber (Modern Structural Geology Vol. 2). A box fold uniquely develops two distinct hinge zones and two conjugate axial surfaces bounding a flat crest or trough.
(A) Tight fold INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Fleuty's (1964) geometric classification defines a tight fold strictly on the basis of its interlimb angle ($0^\circ < \theta \le 30^\circ$). A tight fold possesses only a single continuous hinge zone where maximum curvature occurs. It has one axial surface and two convergent limbs.
(B) Isoclinal fold INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: An isoclinal fold is characterized by parallel limbs (interlimb angle $\theta \approx 0^\circ$) that dip in the same direction at equal angles. Like all simple buckle folds, an isoclinal fold has only one hinge zone (often razor-sharp or hairpin-like) and a single axial surface bisecting the parallel limbs.
(C) Overturned fold INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: An overturned fold has an inclined axial plane such that both limbs dip in the same general direction, with one limb rotated past the vertical (inverted stratigraphic polarity). Despite the asymmetric tilt, it possesses only one hinge line connecting the normal limb to the overturned limb.
(D) Box fold CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: A box fold (conjugate fold) is the classic structural example of a fold profile featuring two distinct hinges. The crestal region between the two hinges remains flat and tabular, while the outer limbs dip steeply away from the hinge lines. It is formed by the intersection or cooperative propagation of two conjugate kink bands or shear zones.

Problem 1: Interlimb Angle Classification
Fleuty Classification: Tight Fold
A fold profile reveals an interlimb angle of $\theta = 24^\circ$. According to Fleuty's (1964) fold classification scheme, folds with $0^\circ < \theta \le 30^\circ$ are categorized as tight folds ($180^\circ - 120^\circ$ gentle, $120^\circ - 70^\circ$ open, $70^\circ - 30^\circ$ close, $30^\circ - 0^\circ$ tight, $0^\circ$ isoclinal).
Problem 2: Axial Surface Geometry of a Conjugate Box Fold
Conjugate Axial Angle = $60^\circ$; $\sigma_1$ horizontal
In a symmetrical box anticline, the eastern axial surface dips $60^\circ\text{W}$ and the western axial surface dips $60^\circ\text{E}$. The acute angle between the two surfaces is $60^\circ$. The maximum compressive stress $\sigma_1$ bisects this angle horizontally, driving simultaneous inward movement along both conjugate hinges.
Problem 3: Polyclinal vs. Monoclinal Folds
Polyclinal refers to multiple axial surfaces in cross section
Folds that show several axial surfaces within the same profile (such as box folds and accordion folds) are termed polyclinal, whereas folds with a single step-like flexure in otherwise horizontal strata are termed monoclines.
Problem 4: Hinge Curvature Analysis
Curvature is concentrated at the two hinges; zero on the flat crest
In a box fold, the rate of change of bedding dip with respect to arc length ($d\phi / ds$) is zero across the flat top ($d\phi/ds = 0$) and reaches two localized maxima at the two sharp hinge lines.
Problem 5: Stratigraphic Verification in Fold Crests
Flat crest displays normal stratigraphic polarity
In an anticlinal box fold, despite the steep or overturned outer limbs, the broad flat plateau between the two hinges retains right-side-up beds, with the oldest rocks at the base of the plateau.
Problem 6: Mechanical Prerequisite for Box Folding
Strong anisotropic layering over ductile decollement
Box folds form preferentially in stratified sedimentary sequences where thick, competent layers (e.g., massive limestone) overlie a ductile, highly lubricating basal detachment horizon (e.g., rock salt or shale).
Problem 7: Inversion of Limb Dip in Overturned Folds
Overturned fold has 1 hinge, but 1 inverted limb
An overturned fold has both limbs dipping in the same direction, but it still maintains exactly one hinge. Only a box fold possesses two independent hinges separating two limbs from a central platform.
Problem 8: Identification on Aerial / Satellite Imagery
Truncated flat mesa bounded by two parallel strike ridges
On geological maps, a plunging box fold appears as a broad tabular plateau flanked by two parallel or outward-diverging linear strike ridges where beds plunge steeply along the two hinge zones.
