1. Theoretical Foundation: Adamellite in Granitoid Systematics

Adamellite was named in 1898 by A. Cathrein after Mount Adamello in the Italian Alps. In classical petrography and the standard IUGS classification, adamellite is synonymous with Quartz Monzonite (Field 3b of the QAPF classification), defined as a plutonic rock where quartz comprises 20% to 60% of the felsic minerals and the ratio of plagioclase to total feldspar is roughly equal ($35\% \le P/(A+P) \le 65\%$):

Rock Type IUGS QAPF Field Plagioclase : Alkali Feldspar Ratio Dominant Feldspar Species Characteristic Ferromagnesian Minerals
Alkali-Feldspar Granite Field 2 $P/(A+P) < 10\%$ (Alkali Feldspar dominant) Orthoclase, Microcline, Perthite >> Albite Biotite, Riebeckite, Aegirine
Syenogranite Field 3a $10\% \le P/(A+P) < 35\%$ K-Feldspar > Plagioclase Biotite ± Muscovite
ADAMELLITE (Quartz Monzonite) Field 3b $35\% \le P/(A+P) \le 65\%$ (Roughly EQUAL) Roughly equal amounts of Oligoclase & K-feldspar Biotite, Hornblende
Granodiorite Field 4 $65\% < P/(A+P) \le 90\%$ (Plagioclase dominant) Oligoclase/Andesine > K-Feldspar Hornblende > Biotite
Tonalite (Trondhjemite) Field 5 $P/(A+P) > 90\%$ (Extreme Plagioclase) Andesine/Oligoclase >> K-Feldspar Hornblende, Biotite
"Adamellite is a variety of granite containing roughly equal amounts of orthoclase (K-feldspar) and plagioclase (normally oligoclase), with 20% to 60% quartz. In Brøgger's terminology and modern IUGS usage, it is referred to as quartz monzonite. When plagioclase exceeds two-thirds of total feldspar, the rock grades into granodiorite; when potash feldspar exceeds two-thirds, it becomes normal granite (syenogranite)."
— K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Petrology Section, pp. 122–124)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 84
Adamellite contains :
A Roughly equal amounts of Oligoclase and K-feldspar
B Roughly equal amounts of Quartz and Oligoclase
C Roughly equal amounts of Quartz and K-feldspar
D Roughly equal amounts of Quartz and Albite
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION A (Roughly equal amounts of Oligoclase and K-feldspar)
OPTION A • CORRECT
Roughly equal amounts of Oligoclase and K-feldspar

By international and Indian petrological definition, adamellite (quartz monzonite) is specifically distinguished from granite sensu stricto and granodiorite by possessing roughly equal proportions of plagioclase (oligoclase) and potash feldspar (orthoclase/microcline) ($P/(A+P)$ ratio of approximately 1:1, or between 35:65 and 65:35).

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Roughly equal amounts of Quartz and Oligoclase

Granitoid classifications are fundamentally based on the ratio between the two feldspar phases ($A$ vs $P$), not the ratio between quartz and plagioclase. In adamellite, total feldspar comprises 50% to 70% of the rock, while quartz makes up only 20% to 35%.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Roughly equal amounts of Quartz and K-feldspar

This is incorrect. Quartz and K-feldspar do not define adamellite; a rock with equal quartz and K-feldspar but little plagioclase is a syenogranite or alkali-feldspar granite.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
Roughly equal amounts of Quartz and Albite

Albite ($An_0\text{--}An_{10}$) is the sodic alkali feldspar characteristic of rare peralkaline granites. Adamellite typically contains calcic-sodic plagioclase in the range of oligoclase ($An_{10}\text{--}An_{30}$) alongside microcline/orthoclase.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What is the volcanic extrusive equivalent of Adamellite (Quartz Monzonite)?
Answer: Quartz Latite (or Rhyodacite)
Quartz latite contains phenocrysts of both plagioclase and sanidine in roughly equal amounts within a fine-grained silicic groundmass.
2. Who introduced the term 'monzonite' and from which type area is it derived?
Answer: Albert de Lapparent (1864), from Mount Monzoni in the Italian Dolomites.
Monzoni in Northern Italy is the type locality for intermediate plutonic rocks having equal alkali feldspar and plagioclase.
3. What is the difference between monzonite and quartz monzonite (adamellite)?
Answer: Monzonite has <5% modal quartz; quartz monzonite (adamellite) has 5% to 20% (or 20-60% in QAPF) quartz.
Monzonite is silica-saturated/intermediate, while quartz monzonite is oversaturated and acidic.
4. What is the specific composition of oligoclase plagioclase found in adamellites?
Answer: $An_{10}$ to $An_{30}$ ($10\%\text{--}30\%$ anorthite, $70\%\text{--}90\%$ albite).
Oligoclase is the classic intermediate-sodic plagioclase of acid plutonic rocks.
5. Which batholith in North America contains enormous volumes of adamellite/quartz monzonite?
Answer: The Sierra Nevada Batholith and Idaho Batholith
Mesozoic subduction-related cordilleran batholiths are predominantly composed of granodiorite, adamellite, and tonalite.
6. What is the characteristic dark mineral in adamellites?
Answer: Biotite mica ± common hornblende
Adamellites are calc-alkaline rocks typically carrying 10-15% mafics, dominated by brown biotite and dark green hornblende.
7. Why did the IUGS Subcommission recommend replacing 'adamellite' with 'quartz monzonite'?
Answer: To create a consistent, systematic root-name hierarchy based on QAP modal parameters.
IUGS (1973, 2002) preferred 'quartz monzonite' for Field 3b, but recognized adamellite as an established historical synonym.
8. What texture is produced when plagioclase cores in adamellite are mantled by alkali feldspar?
Answer: Anti-rapakivi texture
Rapakivi texture is K-feldspar mantled by plagioclase; the reverse (plagioclase mantled by K-feldspar) is anti-rapakivi.
