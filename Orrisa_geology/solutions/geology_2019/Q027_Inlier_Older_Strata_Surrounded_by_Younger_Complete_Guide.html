Map Patterns of Denudation: Inlier, Outlier, Klippe & Window

In geological mapping and structural geology, the intersection of eroded topography with folded, faulted, or unconformable rock strata produces distinctive outcrop configurations. Two fundamental erosional structures defined by relative stratigraphic age are the Inlier and the Outlier.

  • Inlier: An outcrop of older strata completely surrounded by younger strata on a geological map. Inliers are typically produced when erosion unroofs the crest of an anticline, dome, or deeply incises an active river canyon through sub-horizontal younger cover beds.
  • Outlier: An outcrop of younger strata completely surrounded by older strata. Outliers are typically formed when erosion isolates a mesa, butte, or the down-folded core of a syncline from the main stratigraphic sequence.
  • Mnemonic:
    • INlier = INside older rocks are exposed!
    • OUTlier = OUTside younger rocks are stranded!
"An inlier may be defined as a patch of older strata which is surrounded on all sides by younger strata (Fig. 7.49). An outlier is a patch of younger rocks surrounded by older rocks on all sides. It is formed when a part of a bed gets separated from the main mass by erosion." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Structures Due to Denudation, p. 124)
Stratigraphic Age Rules for Inlier and Outlier
$$\text{Inlier:} \quad \text{Age}_{\text{Center}} > \text{Age}_{\text{Periphery}} \quad (\text{Older enclosed by Younger})$$ $$\text{Outlier:} \quad \text{Age}_{\text{Center}} < \text{Age}_{\text{Periphery}} \quad (\text{Younger enclosed by Older})$$

In thrust terranes: a tectonic window (fenster) is an inlier of the footwall, while a klippe is an outlier of the hanging wall (nappe).

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q027
On a geological map, if older strata are found to be surrounded by younger strata then it could be :
(A) An inlier
(B) An outlier
(C) An overlap
(D) Nappe structure
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (A) An inlier
Verified against Bangar (p. 124), Billings (Structural Geology, p. 89), and Mukherjee (p. 182). An area where older rocks are surrounded on all sides by younger strata is by definition an inlier.
(A) An inlier CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: By classic geomorphological and structural definition, an inlier is a geographic exposure of older strata entirely encircled by younger strata. Inliers are commonly generated when fluvial or glacial erosion breaches the protective roof of an anticline or structural dome, exposing the older core strata below the surrounding younger cover.
(B) An outlier INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: An outlier is the exact converse: a patch of younger strata surrounded on all sides by older strata. Outliers develop when erosional processes strip away younger strata from an upland, leaving an isolated residual cap (such as a mesa or the trough of a syncline) resting atop older rocks.
(C) An overlap INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: An overlap (or onlap) is a stratigraphic term describing a marine transgression where younger sedimentary layers extend successively further landward over an unconformity, concealing the older underlying strata. It does not describe an enclosed circular or elliptical outcrop map pattern.
(D) Nappe structure INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: A nappe is a vast allochthonous rock mass (recumbent fold or thrust sheet) that has been displaced laterally over distances greater than 5 km along a low-angle thrust fault. While erosional holes in a nappe (fensters) expose inliers, the nappe itself refers to the displaced sheet, not the enclosed map relationship.

8 Fully Worked Exam-Standard Practice Problems: Map Interpretation & Inliers
Problem 1: Dips in an Eroded Dome (Inlier)
Bedding dips radially away from the center (Quaquaversal dip)
A circular outcrop on a map has Precambrian gneiss in the center surrounded concentric rings of Cambrian, Ordovician, and Silurian strata. Bedding dips radially outward ($360^\circ$ away from center). This constitutes an anticlinal dome inlier.
Problem 2: Dips in a Structural Basin (Outlier)
Bedding dips centripetally inward toward the center
If Jurassic strata occupy the center of an elliptical ring of Triassic and Permian rocks, with all beds dipping toward the center, the Jurassic rocks form a synclinal basin outlier.
Problem 3: The Black Hills of South Dakota
Massive regional structural inlier
The Black Hills uplift features an eroded core of Precambrian granite and metamorphic rocks surrounded by younger Paleozoic and Mesozoic sedimentary hogbacks dipping outward, making it a classic continental-scale inlier.
Problem 4: Fluvial Incision Across Horizontal Strata
Deep valley exposes an inlier; mountain plateau forms an outlier
In the Colorado Plateau, the Grand Canyon exposes older Proterozoic Vishnu Schist surrounded on top by younger Paleozoic strata (inlier in valley floor), while isolated mesas support younger Mesozoic caps surrounded by eroded Permian strata (outliers).
Problem 5: Distinguishing Klippe from Sedimentary Outlier
A klippe is bounded by a fault contact; an outlier by bedding/unconformity
In field mapping, a klippe is separated from the underlying rock by a thrust fault (often marked by mylonite, gouge, or slickensides), whereas a sedimentary outlier is bounded by normal conformable or unconformable depositional contacts.
Problem 6: Rule of V's for Inliers along Valleys
The outcrop V points downstream for beds dipping downstream steeper than valley gradient
When an inlier is exposed along a river valley cutting through gently dipping strata, the boundary lines form V-shaped patterns governed by the interaction of topography with the 3D strike and dip of the contact planes.
Problem 7: Age Determination via Index Fossils
Center contains Paradoxides (Cambrian); margin contains Productus (Permian)
If the core contains trilobite fossils (*Paradoxides*, Middle Cambrian) and the surrounding strata contain brachiopod fossils (*Productus*, Permian), the central rock is significantly older, definitively confirming an inlier.
Problem 8: Fault-Bounded Inliers
A horst block forms a structural inlier
When a central block is uplifted between two normal faults and eroded flat, older basement rocks are juxtaposed against younger downthrown graben sediments on both sides, creating a fault-bounded inlier.
