1. Theoretical Foundation: The Udden-Wentworth Scale & Silt Fraction

The quantitative classification of detrital (clastic) sediments is based on the geometric particle-size scale formulated by J.A. Udden (1898) and modified by Chester K. Wentworth (1922). In 1934, W.C. Krumbein introduced the logarithmic transformation phi ($\phi$) scale ($\phi = -\log_2 d_{\text{mm}}$) to enable rigorous statistical analysis:

Sand (Very Coarse to Very Fine) 2 mm – 1/16 mm 2000 – 62.5 $\mu\text{m}$ $-1.0\ \phi$ to $+4.0\ \phi$ Sandstone (Arenite / Wacke)

• Medium Silt 1/32 – 1/64 mm 31.2 – 15.6 $\mu\text{m}$ $+5.0\ \phi$ to $+6.0\ \phi$ Medium Siltstone
• Fine Silt 1/64 – 1/128 mm 15.6 – 7.8 $\mu\text{m}$ $+6.0\ \phi$ to $+7.0\ \phi$ Fine Siltstone
• Very Fine Silt 1/128 – 1/256 mm 7.8 – 3.9 $\mu\text{m}$ $+7.0\ \phi$ to $+8.0\ \phi$ Very Fine Siltstone
Clay Grade < 1/256 mm < 3.9 $\mu\text{m}$ $ > +8.0\ \phi$ Claystone / Shale / Mudstone
"According to the Wentworth grade scale, silt comprises all mineral fragments with diameters between 1/16 mm and 1/256 mm (or 0.0625 mm to 0.0039 mm). Particles larger than 1/16 mm are sand, while particles finer than 1/256 mm are classified as clay. Silt-grade grains are barely visible under a hand lens but feel gritty between the fingers or teeth, distinguishing them from smooth, plastic clays."
— K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Sedimentary Rocks, pp. 130–132)
$$\text{Krumbein Phi Transformation: } \phi = -\log_2 (d_{\text{mm}})$$ $$\text{Upper Limit of Silt: } d = \frac{1}{16}\text{ mm} = 0.0625\text{ mm} \implies \phi = -\log_2(2^{-4}) = \mathbf{+4.0\ \phi}$$ $$\text{Lower Limit of Silt: } d = \frac{1}{256}\text{ mm} = 0.0039\text{ mm} \implies \phi = -\log_2(2^{-8}) = \mathbf{+8.0\ \phi}$$

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 92
The silt grade detrital sediments would have a size range of :
A 1/16 mm to 1/256 mm
B Less than 1/256 mm
C 1/16 mm to 2 mm
D 2 mm to 1 mm
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION A (1/16 mm to 1/256 mm)
OPTION A • CORRECT
1/16 mm to 1/256 mm

By international geological standards (Wentworth, 1922; Krumbein, 1934; Pettijohn; Bangar), the silt grade detrital sediment is defined as particles ranging in diameter from 1/16 mm to 1/256 mm (0.0625 mm to 0.0039 mm, or $+4\phi$ to $+8\phi$).

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Less than 1/256 mm

Particles having a diameter less than 1/256 mm ($< 0.0039\text{ mm}$, or $> +8\phi$) belong to the clay grade, which consists of colloidal phyllosilicates and mud.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
1/16 mm to 2 mm

The size range from 1/16 mm to 2 mm defines the sand grade (from very fine sand at 0.0625 mm up to very coarse sand at 2 mm), not silt.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
2 mm to 1 mm

This range corresponds exclusively to very coarse sand (1 mm to 2 mm), which lies near the gravel boundary.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What is the phi value ($\phi$) of a grain having a diameter of exactly 0.0625 mm (1/16 mm)?
Answer: $+4.0\ \phi$
$\phi = -\log_2(0.0625) = -\log_2(2^{-4}) = -(-4) = +4.0\ \phi$. This marks the exact sand-silt boundary.
2. What aeolian wind-blown deposit consists almost entirely of well-sorted quartz silt?
Answer: Loess
Loess is an unstratified, buff-colored, calcareous deposit of wind-blown glacial or peridesert quartz silt (20 to 50 $\mu\text{m}$).
3. What is the primary mineral constituent of detrital silt?
Answer: Quartz ($SiO_2$)
Quartz is chemically inert and mechanically durable; physical abrasion crushes quartz grains down to a natural cleavage limit of ~20 to 4 $\mu\text{m}$ (silt size).
4. How can a field geologist immediately distinguish siltstone from claystone without a microscope?
Answer: The 'bite test' (chewing a tiny speck): siltstone feels distinctly gritty between the teeth, whereas claystone feels buttery smooth.
Human teeth are exceptionally sensitive tactile sensors capable of detecting particles down to ~10 $\mu\text{m}$ (silt), whereas clay (<4 $\mu\text{m}$) cannot be felt.
5. What physical law governs the settling velocity of silt particles in calm water?
Answer: Stokes' Law ($v = \frac{2}{9}\frac{(\rho_s - \rho_f) g r^2}{\eta}$)
Particles smaller than 0.1 mm (including all silt and clay) settle via laminar viscous drag described by Stokes' Law.
6. What is the millimeter size equivalent of $-1\ \phi$?
Answer: 2.0 mm (the gravel-sand boundary)
$d = 2^{-\phi} = 2^{-(-1)} = 2^1 = 2.0\text{ mm}$.
7. What is mud in sedimentary terminology?
Answer: A mixture of silt and clay grade particles (all detritus finer than 1/16 mm).
Mudrock / mudstone is the collective term for fine-grained clastic rocks containing both silt (1/16-1/256 mm) and clay (<1/256 mm).
8. What is the millimeter diameter of the boundary separating granules from pebbles in Wentworth's scale?
Answer: 4.0 mm
Granules range from 2 mm to 4 mm; pebbles range from 4 mm to 64 mm.
