Density Distribution within the Earth: PREM Model & Core Physics

The radial density distribution within the Earth was historically solved through a combination of astronomical geodesy (Earth's total mass $M_E = 5.972 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$, average density $\bar{\rho} = 5.515\text{ g/cm}^3$, and moment of inertia factor $I / M R^2 = 0.3307$) and the Adams-Williamson equation applied to free oscillation and body-wave travel times (synthesized in the Preliminary Reference Earth Model - PREM by Dziewonski & Anderson, 1981).

  • Why Surface Rocks Cannot Explain Earth's Mass: Typical surface continental rocks have densities of $2.7 - 2.8\text{ g/cm}^3$, and upper mantle peridotites have densities of $3.3 - 3.4\text{ g/cm}^3$. Because the Earth's average density is $5.515\text{ g/cm}^3$ and $I / MR^2 < 0.4$ (the value for a homogeneous sphere), high-density metallic material must be concentrated at the core.
  • Density Profile across Earth's Layers:
    • Crust: $2.7 - 3.0\text{ g/cm}^3$ (granite to basalt/gabbro).
    • Upper Mantle: $3.3 - 3.5\text{ g/cm}^3$ (olivine/pyroxene peridotite).
    • Transition Zone: $3.7 - 4.0\text{ g/cm}^3$ (wadsleyite/ringwoodite phase transitions).
    • Lower Mantle (Mesosphere): $4.4 - 5.6\text{ g/cm}^3$ (dense bridgmanite + ferropericlase).
    • Outer Core: $9.9 - 12.2\text{ g/cm}^3$ (Averaging precisely $\approx 10.7\text{ g/cm}^3$ at mid-outer-core depths).
    • Inner Core: $12.8 - 13.1\text{ g/cm}^3$ (crystalline solid iron-nickel alloy).
  • Outer Core Composition: The outer core is a molten iron-nickel liquid alloy ($pprox 85\%\text{ Fe}, \approx 5\%\text{ Ni}$) alloyed with $8-10\%$ light elements (such as sulfur, oxygen, silicon, and carbon) which depress the density by $pprox 10\%$ relative to pure liquid iron at core pressures ($136 - 330\text{ GPa}$).
"The density of rocks increases with depth due to changes in chemical composition and enormous overlying pressure. The average density of crustal rocks is about 2.7 to 3.0 g/cm³, while the mantle has an average density of 3.3 to 5.7 g/cm³. In the core, the density increases abruptly to 9.9 to 12.2 g/cm³, with an average density of about 10.7 g/cm³ prevailing within the outer core." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Interior of the Earth: Density and The Core, p. 24)
Adams-Williamson Equation for Radial Density Variation
$$\frac{d\rho}{dr} = -\frac{\rho(r) g(r)}{\Phi(r)} = -\frac{\rho(r) g(r)}{V_p^2(r) - \frac{4}{3}V_s^2(r)}$$ $$\text{Seismic Parameter: } \Phi = \frac{K_S}{\rho} = V_p^2 - \frac{4}{3}V_s^2 \quad (K_S = \text{adiabatic bulk modulus})$$ $$\bar{\rho}_{\text{crust}} \approx 2.8\text{ g/cm}^3 \quad \big| \quad \bar{\rho}_{\text{mantle}} \approx 4.5\text{ g/cm}^3 \quad \big| \quad \mathbf{\bar{\rho}_{\text{outer core}} \approx 10.7\text{ g/cm}^3} \quad \big| \quad \bar{\rho}_{\text{inner core}} \approx 13.0\text{ g/cm}^3$$

Across the Core-Mantle Boundary (CMB at $2900 ext{ km}$), density jumps discontinuously from $5.6 ext{ g/cm}^3$ to $9.9 ext{ g/cm}^3$ ($\Delta ho pprox +4.3 ext{ g/cm}^3$).

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q035
An average density of 10.7 gm/cm3 prevails at:
(A) Upper mantle
(B) Core
(C) Lower crust
(D) Lower mantle
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (B) Core
Verified against Bangar (p. 24), Fowler (The Solid Earth, p. 110), and Dziewonski & Anderson (PREM, 1981). An average density of $10.7 ext{ g/cm}^3$ prevails uniquely within Earth's liquid outer core, where densities range between $9.9$ and $12.2 ext{ g/cm}^3$.
(A) Upper mantle INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The upper mantle is composed of ultramafic peridotite with an average density of only $3.3 - 3.5\text{ g/cm}^3$. A density of $10.7\text{ g/cm}^3$ is more than three times higher than the actual density of upper mantle rocks.
(B) Core CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: Under the PREM model, density across the Core-Mantle Boundary jumps from $5.6\text{ g/cm}^3$ in the deep mantle to $9.9\text{ g/cm}^3$ at the top of the outer core, progressively increasing under immense hydrostatic compression to $12.2\text{ g/cm}^3$ at the inner core boundary. The average density throughout the outer core is precisely $10.7\text{ g/cm}^3$, confirming Option (B) as the correct answer.
(C) Lower crust INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The lower continental crust consists of mafic granulites and amphibolites with a density of $2.8 - 3.0\text{ g/cm}^3$. It is almost four times less dense than the core.
(D) Lower mantle INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The lower mantle (mesosphere) extends from $660\text{ km}$ to $2900\text{ km}$ depth and has densities ranging from $4.4\text{ g/cm}^3$ at its top to $5.6\text{ g/cm}^3$ at the base of $D''$. Even under the extreme pressure of the lowermost mantle, its silicate mineralogy (bridgmanite) prevents density from exceeding $5.6\text{ g/cm}^3$.

8 Fully Worked Exam-Standard Practice Problems: Density & Deep Earth Geophysics
Problem 1: Core Mass Fraction of the Earth
Core constitutes $\approx 32.5\%$ of Earth mass but only $\approx 16.2\%$ of volume
Despite occupying less than a sixth of Earth's total volume ($V_{\text{core}} / V_{\text{Earth}} \approx 0.162$), the core's high density ($9.9 - 13.1\text{ g/cm}^3$) accounts for nearly one-third of the planet's total mass ($M_{\text{core}} = 1.94 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg} \approx 32.5\%$ of $M_E$).
Problem 2: Density Deficit of the Outer Core
Outer core is $8-10\%$ less dense than pure liquid iron at core P-T
Shock-wave compression experiments on pure iron at $136-330\text{ GPa}$ show that liquid iron is denser than the seismically observed core. This "density deficit" requires the presence of $\approx 8-10\text{ wt}\%$ light elements (likely S, O, Si, C, and H).
Problem 3: Moment of Inertia Factor Proof of Dense Core
$I / (M R^2) = 0.3307 < 0.4$ proves mass is concentrated toward center
A homogeneous sphere has a moment of inertia factor of $0.400$. The Earth's observed value of $0.3307$ (determined from satellite orbital precession) proves mathematically that mass density must increase drastically toward the center.
Problem 4: Hydrostatic Pressure at the Center of the Earth
Center pressure $P_c \approx 364\text{ GPa}$ ($3.64\text{ million atmospheres}$)
Integrating $dP = -\rho(r) g(r) dr$ from surface to center yields $P_{\text{CMB}} \approx 136\text{ GPa}$ at the core-mantle boundary, $P_{\text{ICB}} \approx 330\text{ GPa}$ at the inner core boundary, and $P_c \approx 364\text{ GPa}$ at Earth's center.
Problem 5: Gravitational Acceleration Profile in the Mantle vs Core
$g(r)$ remains nearly constant at $\approx 10\text{ m/s}^2$ through mantle, peaks at CMB ($pprox 10.7\text{ m/s}^2$)
Because core density is so high, the enclosed mass drops rapidly inside the core, causing gravity to decrease linearly from $10.7\text{ m/s}^2$ at the CMB to exactly $0\text{ m/s}^2$ at Earth's center ($r = 0$).
Problem 6: The Geodynamo in the Liquid Outer Core
Thermal and compositional convection of liquid Fe-Ni drives planetary magnetic field
Crystallization of the inner core expels buoyant light elements into the liquid outer core. This compositional buoyancy, combined with Coriolis forces from planetary rotation, powers vigorous helical convection that generates the geomagnetic field (geodynamo).
Problem 7: S-Wave Shadow Zone Angle
Direct S-waves are absent beyond $103^\circ$ ($103^\circ - 180^\circ$)
Because shear waves cannot propagate through the liquid outer core ($V_s = 0$), stations located at angular distances greater than $103^\circ$ from an earthquake epicenter record no direct $S$ arrivals, producing an $S$-wave shadow zone from $103^\circ$ to $180^\circ$.
Problem 8: Birch's Law Relating Velocity and Density
Linear relation: $V_p = a + b \rho$ for rocks of constant mean atomic weight $\bar{M}$
Francis Birch (1961) demonstrated experimentally that compressional wave velocity is linearly related to density for materials of similar mean atomic weight ($\bar{M} \approx 21$ for silicates; $\bar{M} \approx 48$ for iron-rich core material), providing the basis for deciphering deep Earth composition.
