Mesozoic Stratigraphy: The Kutch Basin as Global Jurassic Type Area

The Kutch (Kachchh) Basin in Gujarat is the world-renowned type area for the Jurassic System in India and a benchmark reference for global Tethyan-peri-Gondwanan ammonite biostratigraphy. Initiated during the Late Triassic to Early Jurassic rifting of Gondwanaland along the western continental margin of India, the basin accumulated over $2,500\text{ m}$ of exceptionally fossiliferous marine to deltaic sediments.

  • Classic Four-Fold Stratigraphic Division (Stoliczka, Waagen, Rajnath):
    1. Patcham Formation (~300 m; Middle Jurassic: Bathonian):
      • Lithology: Basal yellow limestones, calcareous sandstones, and rich coral patch reefs (exposed in Patcham Island and Kutch Mainland).
      • Diagnostic Fossils: Cephalopods such as Macrocephalites triangularis and Sivajiceras congener, with brachiopods (Kutchithyris).
    2. Chari Formation (~360 m; Callovian to Oxfordian):
      • Lithology: Highly fossiliferous shales, oolitic limestones, and the famous golden-weathering Dhosa Oolite Member at the top.
      • Diagnostic Biozones: Standard global ammonite zones: Macrocephalites macrocephalus (Callovian base), Reineckeia, Peltoceras athleta, and Mayaites maya (Oxfordian).
    3. Katrol Formation (~300 m; Kimmeridgian to Early Tithonian):
      • Lithology: Red sandstones, gritty shales, and ironstone nodules reflecting shallowing marine conditions.
      • Diagnostic Fossils: Ammonites Katroloceras, Waagenia, Streblites, and Hildoglochiceras.
    4. Umia Formation (~500 m; Tithonian to Early Cretaceous Aptian):
      • Lithology: Marine sandstones (Trigonia beds with Trigonia ventricosa), grading upward into terrestrial deltaic sandstones with Gondwana plant fossils (Ptilophyllum flora), capped by the marine transgressive Ukra Member (Aptian ammonite Australiceras).
      • Ammonites: Virgatosphinctes densiplicatus and Micracanthoceras.
"The Jurassic of Kutch: Kutch is the type area of the Jurassic rocks in India. The Jurassic strata are magnificently developed, showing complete exposure from Bathonian to Lower Cretaceous. They are divided into four groups: (1) Patcham Group (Bathonian), (2) Chari Group (Callovian-Oxfordian), (3) Katrol Group (Kimmeridgian), and (4) Umia Group (Tithonian to Lower Cretaceous). The cephalopod fauna of Kutch is famous all over the world for its richness and variety." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Jurassic System: Kutch, pp. 291-293)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 48
Kutch is the type Area in India for :
(A) Jurassic formation
(B) Early archean rocks
(C) Late Archean rocks
(D) Cambrian formation
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (A) Jurassic formation
(A) Jurassic formation CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: Kutch is universally recognized in Indian and international geology as the classic type area for the Jurassic System in India. The Patcham, Chari, Katrol, and Umia Formations preserve an unmatched marine sequence spanning Bathonian, Callovian, Oxfordian, Kimmeridgian, and Tithonian stages, populated with world-standard ammonite zones.
(B) Early archean rocks INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Early Archean rocks (Paleoarchean >3.5 Ga) are located in the Singhbhum Craton (Older Metamorphic Group), Dharwar Craton (Sargur Group), and Bastar Craton. Kutch contains no exposed Archean basement.
(C) Late Archean rocks INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Late Archean rocks (Neoarchean ~2.7 - 2.5 Ga) are typified by the Dharwar Supergroup in Karnataka and the Bundelkhand Granite Complex. Kutch is an exclusively Mesozoic-Cenozoic pericratonic rift basin.
(D) Cambrian formation INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The type areas for the Cambrian System in the Indian subcontinent are the Spiti Valley (Parahio Formation / Haimanta Group) and the Salt Range (Salt Pseudomorph and Magnesian Sandstone beds), not Kutch.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Kutch Stratigraphy & Cephalopod Biozones
Problem 1: Lithological Character of the Dhosa Oolite Member
Green to brownish ferruginous and oolitic limestone marking a condensed stratigraphic section
The Dhosa Oolite occurs at the top of the Chari Formation (Oxfordian). It is an iron-rich oolitic grainstone packed with ammonites, representing a period of very slow sedimentation (condensation horizon) during maximum marine flooding.
Problem 2: Significance of the Ukra Member in Umia Formation
Aptian marine transgressive horizon containing ammonites within an otherwise continental sequence
The Ukra bed contains the diagnostic Lower Cretaceous ammonite *Australiceras*, proving that marine conditions briefly returned to the Kutch basin in the Aptian stage before regional emergence.
Problem 3: Paleogeographic Affinity of Kutch Plant Beds
Gondwana Upper Mesozoic flora (*Ptilophyllum* flora)
The plant beds interstratified within the upper Umia sandstones contain cycads (*Ptilophyllum acutifolium*), conifers, and ferns identical to the Rajmahal and Jabalpur floras, demonstrating continental land connection to the Gondwana mainland.
Problem 4: Index Fossil for the Base of the Callovian Stage
Macrocephalites macrocephalus
The basal macrocephalus zone of the Chari Formation is the global bio-chronological marker for the Bathonian-Callovian boundary.
Problem 5: Major Structural Features of the Kutch Rift Basin
E-W trending faulted folds (Kachchh Mainland Fault, Island Belt Fault, Katrol Hill Fault)
The Kutch basin formed by Mesozoic extensional rifting along primordial Precambrian basement faults, later inverted into E-W trending asymmetric anticlinal ridges during Cenozoic Himalayan collision compression.
Problem 6: Golden Oolite Occurrence in Kutch
Basal part of the Chari Formation (Keera dome and Chari dome)
The 'Golden Oolite' is a distinct calc-arenite composed of carbonate ooids coated with thin films of golden iron-oxide/pyrite, formed in high-energy shallow agitated marine shoals.
Problem 7: Type Area of Jurassic in Rajasthan (Adjacent Basin)
Jaisalmer Basin (Lathi Formation, Jaisalmer Limestone, Baisakhi, Bedesar)
The Jaisalmer Basin to the north contains a lateral marine equivalent, notably the fossiliferous golden Jaisalmer Limestone (*Pseudocidaris*, ammonites) correlating with the Patcham-Chari interval.
Problem 8: Trigonia Beds of Kutch
Bivalve-rich sandstones in the lower Umia Formation characterized by Trigonia ventricosa
The genus *Trigonia* (thick-shelled schizodont bivalve) occurs in coquina beds, serving as a reliable Tithonian marker throughout peri-Gondwanan basins (India, Madagascar, South Africa).
