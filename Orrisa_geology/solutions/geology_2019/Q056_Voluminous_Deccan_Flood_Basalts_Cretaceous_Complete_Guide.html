Igneous Provinces: The Deccan Large Igneous Province & Cretaceous Volcanism

The Deccan Traps of western and central India constitute one of the most monumental Continental Flood Basalt (CFB) Large Igneous Provinces (LIPs) in planetary geological history. Covering over $500,000\text{ km}^2$ of the Indian subcontinent (with an estimated original erupted volume exceeding $1.5\text{ million km}^3$), this massive volcanic catastrophe occurred predominantly during the Late Cretaceous Period (specifically spanning the Cretaceous-Paleogene / K-Pg boundary).

  • Geochronological & Tectonomagmatic Parameters:
    1. Plume Trigger: Deccan volcanism erupted as the northward-drifting Indian continent passed directly over the deep-seated Reunion Mantle Plume hotspot in the proto-Indian Ocean.
    2. Geochronological Constraints ($^{40}\text{Ar}/^{39}\text{Ar}$ & U-Pb Zircon):
      • High-precision U-Pb dating of zircon in intertrappean volcanic ash beds constrains the entire main volcanic eruptive phase to between 66.4 Ma and 64.5 Ma.
      • Over $80 - 90\%\text{ of the colossal volume}$ poured out in an exceptionally rapid, hyper-voluminous magmatic pulse lasting less than 750,000 years, right across the Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian) and earliest Paleocene.
    3. Lithostratigraphic Classification of the Western Ghats:
      • Kalsubai Subgroup (Base, Late Cretaceous): Jawhar, Igatpuri, Neral, and Thakurvadi formations (thick picritic and tholeiitic flows).
      • Lonavala Subgroup (Late Cretaceous): Khandala and Bushe formations (contaminated by granitic continental crust).
      • Wai Subgroup (Top, Late Cretaceous to Earliest Paleogene): Poladpur, Ambenali, and Mahabaleshwar formations (comprising $>50\%$ of the total trap volume, erupting directly across the K-Pg boundary).
    4. Infratrappean & Intertrappean Sediments:
      • Infratrappean (Lameta Formation): Fluvial/lacustrine Maastrichtian Cretaceous sediments beneath the lowest basalt flow, containing titanosaurian dinosaurs (Isisaurus, Jainosaurus).
      • Intertrappean Beds: Thin fossiliferous sedimentary intervals sandwiched between basalt flows containing freshwater fossils (Physa prinsepii), plants, and fossil frogs (Indobatrachus).
"Deccan Traps (Age and Extent): The Deccan Traps constitute an immense succession of flat-bedded basaltic lava flows that poured out through fissures during the closing stages of the Cretaceous Period. The eruptive activity began in the Upper Cretaceous and continued across the boundary into the early Tertiary. The underlying fossiliferous beds (Lameta beds) are definitely Upper Cretaceous, confirming the Cretaceous age of the volcanism." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (The Deccan Traps, pp. 297-299)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 56
The Volumincus Deccan Flood Basalts occurred during :
(A) Ordovician
(B) Triassic
(C) Cretaceous
(D) Pleistocene
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (C) Cretaceous
(A) Ordovician INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Ordovician Period occurred between ~485 Ma and ~443 Ma in the Early Paleozoic, hundreds of millions of years prior to the breakup of Gondwanaland and the eruption of the Deccan basalts.
(B) Triassic INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Triassic Period (~252 - 201 Ma) witnessed the deposition of the Panchet Formation in the Gondwana basins and the Lilang Group in Spiti. It predates Deccan flood volcanism by nearly 140 million years.
(C) Cretaceous CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: The voluminous Deccan Flood Basalt province erupted during the Late Cretaceous Period (~66 - 65 Ma) as the Indian plate drifted northward over the Reunion mantle hotspot. The basalts overlie the fossiliferous Cretaceous Lameta beds and encompass the Cretaceous-Paleogene boundary.
(D) Pleistocene INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Pleistocene Epoch (~2.58 Ma to 11.7 ka) represents the Quaternary ice ages. Only modern volcanic centers like Barren Island in the Andaman Sea show Quaternary-Holocene activity; Deccan volcanism was entirely extinct by the Eocene.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Deccan Volcanology & Petrology
Problem 1: Dominant Rock Type in the Deccan Traps
Quartz-normative Tholeiitic Basalt
Over 95% of the Deccan lava flows are uniform subalkaline tholeiitic basalts composed of augite, calcic plagioclase (labradorite), titanomagnetite, and interstitial devitrified glass.
Problem 2: Nature of Volcanic Eruption for Deccan Basalts
Quiet, non-explosive fissure eruptions via regional dyke swarms
Lavas issued from linear fissure vents fed by immense doleritic dyke swarms (Narmada-Tapi and Western Coast swarms), spreading laterally as low-viscosity, flat-lying compound and simple pahoehoe sheets.
Problem 3: Giant Plagioclase Basalts (GPBs)
Phenocryst-rich marker horizons with plagioclase crystals exceeding 5 cm length
GPB horizons occur at stratigraphic breaks between major subgroups in the Western Ghats, formed by prolonged stagnation and crystal accumulation in deep crustal magma chambers.
Problem 4: Diagnostic Fossil Gastropod of Deccan Intertrappeans
Physa (Bullinus) prinsepii
This large, left-coiling (sinistral) freshwater pulmonate gastropod is an index fossil identifying late Cretaceous intertrappean lake sediments across Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra.
Problem 5: Secondary Minerals Filling Vesicles in Deccan Basalt
Zeolites (stilbite, heulandite, apophyllite), chalcedony, agate, and calcite
Hydrothermal fluids circulating through cooling amygdaloidal basalt vesicles precipitated world-class crystalline zeolites and quartz geodes (renowned around Pune and Jalgaon).
Problem 6: Step-Like Geomorphology of the Western Ghats
Differential weathering between hard, massive flow cores and soft, vesicular/amygdaloidal flow tops
The word 'Trap' is derived from the Swedish 'Trapp' (meaning stairs or steps), reflecting the monumental terraced stair-case escarpment of the Western Ghats.
Problem 7: Paleomagnetic Polarity of Deccan Basalts Across K-Pg Boundary
Chron 29R (Reversed) transitioning into Chron 29N (Normal)
The main volume of the Deccan Traps erupted during the reversed magnetic chron 29R, which contains the precise K-Pg extinction boundary layer.
Problem 8: Modern Hotspot Volcanism Sourced from the Deccan Plume
Piton de la Fournaise volcano on Réunion Island in the Indian Ocean
The trace of the mantle plume forms the Chagos-Laccadive Ridge and Mascarene Plateau, terminating at the presently active shield volcano on Réunion Island.
