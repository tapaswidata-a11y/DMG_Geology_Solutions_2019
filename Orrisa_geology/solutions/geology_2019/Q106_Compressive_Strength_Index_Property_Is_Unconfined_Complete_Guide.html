1. Theoretical Foundation: Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS) in Engineering Geology

In rock engineering and geotechnical design, the Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS) (also known as uniaxial compressive strength) is universally recognized by the International Society for Rock Mechanics (ISRM) and the Bureau of Indian Standards (IS: 9143) as the single most important index property of intact rock and building stones:

Testing Regime Confining Pressure ($\sigma_3$) Pore Fluid Drainage Condition Standard Laboratory & Field Role
UNCONFINED (Uniaxial) COMPRESSIVE STRENGTH (UCS) (Option D) Zero Lateral Confinement ($\sigma_2 = \sigma_3 = 0$) Tested dry or at natural moisture content; air-jacketed cylinder. Primary index property for rock classification (Deere & Miller, 1966), RMR (Bieniawski), Q-system (Barton), and GSI.
Confined (Triaxial) Strength (Option A) $\sigma_3 > 0$ (Hydraulic cell pressure) Drained or undrained; simulates deep burial stress. Determines failure envelopes, internal friction angle ($\phi$), and cohesion ($c$).
Drained Compressive Strength (Option B) $\sigma_3 > 0$ Pore water allowed to drain; excess pore pressure $u = 0$. Long-term slope and foundation stability analysis.
Undrained Compressive Strength (Option C) $\sigma_3 > 0$ Pore water prevented from escaping; $u \ne 0$. Short-term sudden loading (e.g., rapid dam filling or earthquake shock).
"The unconfined compressive strength (UCS) is by far the most widely used and important mechanical property of rocks. It represents the ultimate compressive stress an unconfined cylindrical specimen can bear before failing along shear fractures. Because it is simple to determine, highly reproducible, and correlates directly with rock hardness, durability, drillability, and modulus of elasticity, UCS forms the backbone of all engineering classifications of building stones."
— K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Properties of Building Stones, pp. 286–288)
$$\text{Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS): } \sigma_c = \frac{P_{\text{failure}}}{A} = \frac{4 P_{\text{failure}}}{\pi D^2}$$ $$\text{Standard Specimen Geometry: } \text{Cylinder with Height-to-Diameter Ratio } \frac{L}{D} = 2.0 \text{ to } 2.5 \quad (D \approx 54\text{ mm, NX core})$$ $$\text{Lateral Boundary Condition: } \sigma_2 = \sigma_3 = 0 \implies \mathbf{UNCONFINED\ (Option\ D)}$$

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 106
Which type of compressive strength is taken as the most important index property of stones ?
A Confined
B Drained
C Undrained
D Unconfined
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION D (Unconfined)
OPTION D • CORRECT
Unconfined

By international and Indian engineering geology standards (IS: 9143; ISRM; Deere & Miller; Bangar, p. 286), the Unconfined compressive strength (UCS) is universally accepted as the premier and most fundamental index property of building stones and intact rock masses.

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Confined

Confined compressive strength requires complex triaxial cells to apply lateral confining fluid pressures ($\sigma_3$). While essential for deep underground tunnel analysis, it is too complex and costly to serve as a routine index property for building stones.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Drained

Drained strength is a specialized soil/rock mechanics parameter where pore water is allowed to escape during shear, used in effective stress analysis rather than as a baseline stone index property.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Undrained

Undrained compressive strength tests low-permeability rocks/clays where pore pressures build up during rapid loading; it is not the general index property used to grade stone quarries.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What is the standard height-to-diameter ($L/D$) ratio specified for laboratory UCS testing of rock cores?
Answer: 2.0 to 2.5 (IS: 9143 / ISRM)
An $L/D$ ratio of 2.0 to 2.5 eliminates end-platen frictional confinement effects while avoiding buckling instability.
2. If a rock specimen has an $L/D$ ratio of less than 2.0, what correction formula is applied to obtain standard UCS ($\sigma_{c}$)?
Answer: $\sigma_{c} = \frac{\sigma_{c\text{-measured}}}{0.778 + 0.222 (D/L)}$ (ASTM / Protodyakonov correction)
Shorter specimens exhibit falsely elevated strengths due to platen restraint across the entire core height.
3. What indirect field test correlates strongly with UCS and can be conducted directly on irregular rock lumps?
Answer: The Point Load Strength Index test ($I_{s(50)}$)
ISRM and IS: 8764 relate point load index to compressive strength via the empirical formula: $\text{UCS} \approx 20\text{ to } 25 \times I_{s(50)}$.
4. What is the typical UCS value of fresh, unweathered granite?
Answer: 150 to 250 MPa (approximately 1500 to 2500 $kg/cm^2$).
Granite's dense interlocking hypidiomorphic quartz-feldspar fabric provides massive compressive load-bearing capability.
5. What non-destructive rebound test measures surface hardness and estimates UCS in the field?
Answer: The Schmidt Rebound Hammer Test (L-type hammer)
The rebound number ($R$) calibrated against rock dry density provides a rapid, empirical estimation of in-situ UCS.
6. How does moisture saturation affect the UCS of sedimentary sandstones and shales?
Answer: Saturation reduces UCS by 30% to 65% compared to dry strength.
Water weakens clay cements, reduces surface energy (Rehbinder effect), and develops pore pressure, sharply diminishing compressive strength.
7. What is the Brazilian Test used for in rock mechanics?
Answer: Determining the indirect tensile strength of rock discs.
Compressing a circular rock disc diametrically induces uniform tensile stress across the loaded diameter ($\sigma_t = \frac{2P}{\pi D t}$).
8. What is the typical ratio between tensile strength and compressive strength for intact rock?
Answer: Tensile strength is approximately 1/10th to 1/20th (5% to 10%) of compressive strength.
Griffith's crack theory proves that pre-existing microscopic microcracks open rapidly in tension, making rocks 10 to 20 times weaker in tension.
