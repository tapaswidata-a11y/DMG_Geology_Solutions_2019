1. Theoretical Foundation: Coarse Clastic Granulometry

In the Udden-Wentworth classification of coarse detrital sediments (Wentworth, 1922), rudaceous (gravel-grade) clasts ($> 2\text{ mm}$) are precisely categorized based on intermediate diameter measurements. A rounded to sub-rounded clast with a diameter between 64 mm and 256 mm is designated as a Cobble:

"In petrology, coarse detrital fragments are classified by size. A boulder has a diameter greater than 256 mm. A cobble has a diameter ranging from 64 mm to 256 mm (roughly grapefruit to human-head size). A pebble ranges from 4 mm to 64 mm, and a granule from 2 mm to 4 mm. A sedimentary rock formed of consolidated, rounded cobbles is a cobble conglomerate."
— F.J. Pettijohn, Sedimentary Rocks (Gravels and Conglomerates, p. 153)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 94
Rounded to sub-rounded clast with a diameter between 64 and 256 mm is generally known as :
A Gravel
B Pebble
C Cobble
D Boulder
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION C (Cobble)
OPTION C • CORRECT
Cobble

By Wentworth's (1922) authoritative sedimentological scale, clasts having a diameter between 64 mm and 256 mm are uniquely designated as cobbles. Rounded clasts in this size range form cobble conglomerates, typical of high-energy braided rivers and storm-tossed beaches.

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Gravel

'Gravel' is an overarching collective term encompassing all unconsolidated detrital clasts larger than 2 mm in diameter (including granules, pebbles, cobbles, and boulders), not the specific 64–256 mm size class.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Pebble

A pebble is strictly defined as a clast having a diameter between 4 mm and 64 mm. Clasts larger than 64 mm are cobbles.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
Boulder

A boulder is defined as a clast with a diameter greater than 256 mm ($> 10.1\text{ inches}$).

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What is the fundamental difference between a conglomerate and a sedimentary breccia?
Answer: A conglomerate is composed of rounded to sub-rounded clasts; a breccia is composed of sharp, angular clasts.
Rounding reflects prolonged fluvial or beach transport; angularity indicates minimal transport (talus scree, faulting).
2. What hydrodynamic transport regime is required to transport 64 to 256 mm cobbles?
Answer: High-energy upper flow regime (torrential flash floods, steep mountain streams, or severe marine storm breakers).
Hjulström's diagram demonstrates that entraining and rolling cobbles requires bottom current velocities exceeding 2 to 4 m/s.
3. What is an oligomictic conglomerate vs. a polymictic conglomerate?
Answer: Oligomictic contains clasts of a single rock type (e.g., pure vein quartz); polymictic contains diverse clasts (granite, basalt, quartzite).
Oligomictic conglomerates reflect intense chemical weathering destroying unstable clasts; polymictic reflects rapid mechanical erosion.
4. What is the term for a conglomerate where clasts touch each other, with sand/mud filling the interstices?
Answer: Clast-supported (or Orthoconglomerate)
When clasts float in a muddy matrix without touching, it is matrix-supported (paraconglomerate or diamictite).
5. What is imbrication in cobble gravels?
Answer: The preferred shingle-like overlapping alignment of flat cobbles dipping gently upstream against the current.
Imbrication provides a definitive palaeocurrent vector: the long $a-b$ planes dip upstream into the paleoflow.
6. What is the phi ($\phi$) value corresponding to the 64 mm cobble-pebble boundary?
Answer: $-6.0\ \phi$
$\phi = -\log_2(64) = -\log_2(2^6) = -6.0\ \phi$.
7. What famous Siwalik group member in the Himalayan foothills consists of massive boulder and cobble conglomerates?
Answer: The Upper Siwalik Boulder Conglomerate Formation (Pleistocene)
The Upper Siwalik Boulder Conglomerate represents syn-orogenic alluvial fans shed off the rising Greater and Lesser Himalayas.
8. What is Zingg's clast shape classification based on?
Answer: Ratios of the three orthogonal axes: $a$ (long), $b$ (intermediate), and $c$ (short).
Zingg (1935) categorized cobbles into Equant (spherical), Oblate (discoid), Prolate (rod-shaped), and Triaxial (bladed).
