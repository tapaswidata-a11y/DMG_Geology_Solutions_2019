1. Theoretical Foundation: Color Index ($M'$) Variation in Intermediate Rocks

In classical petrography, intermediate plutonic rocks of the diorite family consist essentially of intermediate plagioclase (andesine or oligoclase) and ferromagnesian minerals (hornblende, augite, and biotite). Variations in the modal percentage of ferromagnesian (mafic) minerals dictate their nomenclature across the color index spectrum:

Rock Variety Ferromagnesian Content (Mafic Modal %) Feldspar Content (Plagioclase %) Petrological Definition & Mineralogy
Leucodiorite 0% – 35% Mafic 65% – 100% Andesine Light-colored, feldspar-rich variety of diorite.
Diorite (sensu stricto) 35% – 65% Mafic 35% – 65% Andesine Normal mesocratic diorite; balanced hornblende and andesine plagioclase.
Meladiorite 65% – 90% Mafic 10% – 35% Andesine Dark-colored, melanocratic variety of diorite enriched in hornblende/pyroxene.
Perkinite (Option A) 90% – 100% Mafic (100% Ferromagnesian) 0% – 10% (Feldspar-free extreme) Coarse-grained ultramafic rock composed essentially of amphibole (hornblende) and pyroxene without olivine.
"If the ferromagnesian content of diorite increases until plagioclase disappears entirely, the rock becomes 100% ferromagnesian. Such an ultramafic rock consisting essentially of hornblende and pyroxene is termed Perkinite. It stands as the ultramafic, feldspar-free end-member of the dioritic-gabbroic clan."
— P.K. Mukherjee, A Textbook of Geology (World Press, Igneous Rocks, p. 249)
$$\text{Diorite} = \text{Andesine Plagioclase} + \text{Ferromagnesian Minerals (Hornblende, Biotite, Augite)}$$ $$\text{When Ferromagnesian Content } \rightarrow 100\% \implies \text{Feldspar} \rightarrow 0\% \implies \mathbf{Perkinite}\ (\text{Hornblende} + \text{Pyroxene Ultramafic})$$

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 81
If the ferromagnesian content of diorite is 100%, then it is called :
A Perkinite
B Leptinite
C Meladiorite
D Leucodiorite
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION A (Perkinite)
OPTION A • CORRECT
Perkinite

By standard classical petrographic nomenclature (Duparc, 1907; P.K. Mukherjee, p. 249; K.M. Bangar), when the ferromagnesian mineral percentage of diorite reaches 100% (meaning plagioclase feldspar is completely absent), the resulting ultramafic amphibole-pyroxene rock is designated as Perkinite.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Leptinite

Leptinite (or leptite) is a fine-grained, light-colored granoblastic metamorphic rock (granulite or felsic gneiss) dominated by quartz and feldspar. It is leucocratic and metamorphic, not an ultramafic rock.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Meladiorite

Meladiorite is a dark diorite that still contains 10% to 35% plagioclase feldspar ($M' = 65\text{--}90\%$). It does NOT have 100% ferromagnesian minerals.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
Leucodiorite

Leucodiorite is the exact polar opposite: it is a light-colored diorite dominated by plagioclase feldspar with only 0% to 35% ferromagnesian minerals.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. Who originally defined 'perkinite' and what were its primary constituents?
Answer: L. Duparc (1907); defined as an ultramafic rock composed essentially of amphibole and pyroxene.
Duparc introduced perkinite for coarse-grained rocks containing amphibole and pyroxene in varying proportions, with little to no olivine.
2. What is an ultramafic rock composed of greater than 90% olivine called?
Answer: Dunite
Dunite is a monomineralic ultramafic peridotite containing ≥90% modal olivine.
3. What is an ultramafic rock composed essentially of pyroxene called?
Answer: Pyroxenite
Pyroxenites contain >90% pyroxene (orthopyroxene, clinopyroxene, or both).
4. What is the distinguishing mineralogical difference between perkinite and peridotite?
Answer: Peridotite contains essential olivine (>40%), whereas perkinite is dominated by hornblende and pyroxene without significant olivine.
Peridotites are olivine-bearing ultramafic rocks (lherzolite, harzburgite, dunite), whereas perkinite belongs to the amphibole-pyroxene ultramafic group.
5. What prefix is added to an igneous rock name when the mafic mineral content is between 65% and 90%?
Answer: Mela- (e.g., meladiorite, melagabbro)
IUGS uses 'mela-' for melanocratic varieties with color index $M' = 65\text{--}90\%$, and 'leuco-' for leucocratic varieties with $M' = 0\text{--}35\%$.
6. What is the volcanic equivalent of an ultramafic rock?
Answer: Komatiite
Komatiites are Archaean ultramafic volcanic lavas with >18 wt% $MgO$ displaying spinifex texture.
7. What is an anorthosite in terms of plagioclase content?
Answer: An ultra-leucocratic plutonic rock containing greater than 90% plagioclase feldspar.
Anorthosite is essentially monomineralic plagioclase ($M' < 10\%$).
8. What is the characteristic plagioclase in diorite?
Answer: Andesine ($An_{30}\text{--}An_{50}$) or Oligoclase
If the plagioclase is more calcic than $An_{50}$ (labradorite/bytownite), the rock is classified as a gabbro rather than a diorite.
