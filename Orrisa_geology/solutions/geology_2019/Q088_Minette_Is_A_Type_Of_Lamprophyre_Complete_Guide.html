1. Theoretical Foundation: The Lamprophyre Clan & Minette Systematics

Lamprophyres (from Greek lampros = bright/glistening, and phyro = mixed/porphyritic; von Gümbel, 1874) are a distinctive group of melanocratic, volatile-rich ($H_2O, CO_2, F$) hypabyssal dyke rocks characterized by panidiomorphic texture, in which euhedral phenocrysts of ferromagnesian minerals (biotite/phlogopite, amphibole, or pyroxene) are set in a fine-grained groundmass of feldspars ± feldspathoids. Critically, felsic minerals NEVER form phenocrysts in true lamprophyres.

"Minette is a variety of lamprophyre characterized by an abundance of glistening bronze-colored flakes of biotite (or phlogopite) set in a fine-grained groundmass consisting predominantly of orthoclase feldspar. 2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 88
Minette is a type of :
A Diorite
B Tonalite
C Aplite
D Lamprophyre
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION D (Lamprophyre)
OPTION D • CORRECT
Lamprophyre

By international IUGS classification (Rock, 1991; Le Maitre, 2002; Bangar), minette is the classic type of calc-alkaline lamprophyre containing essential phenocrystic biotite/phlogopite and groundmass alkali feldspar (orthoclase).

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Diorite

Diorite is a coarse-grained plutonic intermediate rock composed of andesine plagioclase and hornblende. It lacks the distinctive porphyritic-panidiomorphic texture and alkali-rich lamprophyric chemistry of minette.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Tonalite

Tonalite is a quartz-rich, plagioclase-dominated plutonic rock ($Q > 20\%$, $P/(A+P) > 90\%$). Minette is a melanocratic dyke rock dominated by K-feldspar and mica, not a quartz-plagioclase pluton.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Aplite

Aplite is a leucocratic (light-colored), fine-grained, equigranular (saccharoidal) dyke rock dominated by quartz and feldspars ($M' < 5\%$). Minette is melanocratic ($M' > 35\text{--}50\%$), dark, and intensely porphyritic.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. What is the fundamental textural rule that distinguishes lamprophyres from all ordinary porphyries?
Answer: In lamprophyres, only mafic minerals form phenocrysts; felsic minerals (quartz and feldspar) are restricted to the groundmass.
In normal porphyries (like granite porphyry), feldspar or quartz form the phenocrysts, whereas lamprophyres exhibit strictly mafic phenocrysts.
2. Which lamprophyre has biotite phenocrysts with PLAGIOCLASE in the groundmass?
Answer: Kersantite
Kersantite is the plagioclase-bearing counterpart of orthoclase-bearing minette.
3. Which lamprophyre has HORNBLENDE phenocrysts with ORTHOCLASE in the groundmass?
Answer: Vogesite
Vogesite is characterized by amphibole/clinopyroxene phenocrysts and alkali feldspar groundmass.
4. What is panidiomorphic texture?
Answer: A texture in which almost all constituent mineral grains possess well-developed crystal faces (euhedral/idiomorphic).
Lamprophyres are renowned for panidiomorphic texture, especially prominently zoned euhedral phlogopite and hornblende.
5. What volatile components are unusually enriched in lamprophyre magmas?
Answer: $H_2O, CO_2, F, Cl, P_2O_5$, and $SO_3$
Abundant primary carbonates, apatite, and hydrous micas reflect deep mantle derivation with metasomatic volatile enrichment.
6. Which Indian coalfield is famous for lamprophyre (mica-peridotite) dykes that have heat-altered coal beds into 'jhama' (natural coke)?
Answer: The Jharia and Raniganj Coalfields (Damodar Valley Basin)
Cretaceous lamprophyre dykes intruded Permian Gondwana coal seams in Jharia, pyrolyzing valuable coal into natural coke (jhama).
7. What is an ocellus (plural: ocelli) in a lamprophyre?
Answer: A small, spherical or rounded leucocratic globule consisting of carbonates, quartz, or feldspar formed by liquid immiscibility or late-stage gas-bubble segregation.
Ocelli are classic diagnostic features of lamprophyric melts, representing separated silicate or carbonate liquid droplets.
8. What is a kimberlite or lamproite in relation to lamprophyres?
Answer: Kimberlites and lamproites are ultra-deep potassic/ultramafic mantle rocks closely related to the lamprophyre clan that host diamonds.
Lamproites and kimberlites share deep volatile-rich mantle genesis and phlogopite-rich mineralogy with lamprophyres.
