Archean Tectonics: Dharwar Craton Lithostratigraphy & Cratonization

The Dharwar Craton of southern peninsular India represents one of the premier Archean cratonic nuclei on Earth. Its evolution spans from the Paleoarchean (~3.5 Ga) to the Neoarchean-Paleoproterozoic boundary (~2.5 Ga). Geologically, the craton is divided by the linear, crustal-scale Closepet Granite batholith into the Western Dharwar Craton (WDC) and the Eastern Dharwar Craton (EDC).

  • Chronostratigraphic Superposition (Oldest to Youngest):
    1. Sargur Group (~3.35 - 3.58 Ga): Oldest recognized supracrustal remnants, occurring as linear, high-grade amphibolite-to-granulite facies enclaves engulfed within the gneissic complex. Composed of komatiites, layered mafic-ultramafic complexes, high-alumina pelites (kyanite-sillimanite-staurolite schists), banded iron formations (BIF), and calc-silicate marbles.
    2. Peninsular Gneissic Complex (PGC, ~3.0 - 3.4 Ga): Polyphase migmatitic basement complex dominated by tonalite-trondhjemite-granodiorite (TTG) suites, representing multiple episodes of plutonism and crustal accretion forming the basement floor for the Dharwar Supergroup.
    3. Dharwar Supergroup (~2.6 - 3.0 Ga): The signature Neoarchean greenstone belt volcano-sedimentary succession, unconformably overlying the PGC. Divided into:
      • Bababudan Group (~3.0 - 2.7 Ga): Basal oligomictic quartz-pebble conglomerate (QPC), thick amygdaloidal mafic volcanic flows, and prominent titaniferous BIF.
      • Chitradurga Group (~2.7 - 2.6 Ga): Upper group marked by Talya polymictic conglomerate, deep-marine greywacke-shale turbidites, pillow basalts, and chemogenic chert-BIF associations.
    4. Closepet Granite (~2.51 Ga): A monumental ~400-km-long, 20-30 km wide, N-S trending polyphase calc-alkaline to potassic granite-adamellite batholith. It sharply intrudes across both the migmatitic basement (PGC) and the folded Dharwar supracrustal belts, marking the youngest major tectonomagmatic and cratonization event of the Dharwar Craton.
"Dharwar Supergroup & Closepet Granite: The Dharwar rocks represent the oldest metamorphosed sedimentary and volcanic succession of Southern India. This vast succession resting upon the Peninsular Gneisses was folded, metamorphosed, and finally intruded by the massive Closepet Granite at approximately 2,500 million years ago, representing the terminal plutonic event that stabilized the Mysore Archean craton." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Stratigraphy of India: Dharwar System, pp. 277-279)
Geochronological Sequence of Dharwar Cratonization
$$T_{\text{Sargur}} (3.58\text{ Ga}) > T_{\text{PGC}} (3.36-3.00\text{ Ga}) > T_{\text{Dharwar SG}} (2.95-2.60\text{ Ga}) > T_{\text{Closepet}} (2.51\text{ Ga})$$

Single-zircon U-Pb TIMS and SHRIMP geochronology constrains the Closepet Granite crystallization age precisely to $2513 \pm 5\text{ Ma}$, proving unequivocally that it represents the youngest event in Dharwarian stratigraphy.

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 46
Which of the following represents the youngest event in the Dharwarian stratigraphy ?
(A) Sargur Group
(B) Closepet Granite
(C) Dharwar SuperGroup
(D) Peninsular Gneissic Complex
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (B) Closepet Granite
(A) Sargur Group INCORRECT (OLDEST)
Forensic Analysis: The Sargur Group (~3.35 - 3.58 Ga) is the oldest recognizable supracrustal unit in the craton, preserved as highly deformed, amphibolite-to-granulite facies metamorphic enclaves within the Peninsular Gneissic Complex.
(B) Closepet Granite CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: The Closepet Granite ($2.51\text{ Ga}$) is a late-to-post-kinematic calc-alkaline batholith that cuts across both the Peninsular Gneisses and the folded greenstone belts of the Dharwar Supergroup. Radiometric dating (U-Pb zircon: $2510 \pm 10\text{ Ma}$) confirms it represents the final plutonic cratonization event, making it the youngest stratigraphic element listed.
(C) Dharwar SuperGroup INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Dharwar Supergroup (~2.6 - 3.0 Ga) consists of the Bababudan and Chitradurga Groups. It was deposited, deformed, and metamorphosed prior to the intrusion of the Closepet Granite, which cross-cuts Dharwar folds and regional fabrics.
(D) Peninsular Gneissic Complex INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The PGC (~3.0 - 3.4 Ga) forms the vast crystalline migmatitic basement upon which the Dharwar Supergroup sediments and lavas were laid down unconformably. It is older than both the Dharwar Supergroup and the Closepet Granite.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Archean Geology & Stratigraphy
Problem 1: Boundary Dividing Western and Eastern Dharwar Cratons
The Closepet Granite batholith and the Chitradurga shear zone
The Western Dharwar Craton (WDC) is characterized by ancient 3.0-3.4 Ga TTG basement and mature quartzites/BIF, whereas the Eastern Dharwar Craton (EDC) is dominated by young 2.7-2.5 Ga juvenile magmatism and thin gold-bearing greenstone slivers (e.g., Kolar, Ramagiri). The Closepet Granite acts as the tectonic stitch dividing them.
Problem 2: Lithological Difference Between Bababudan and Chitradurga Groups
Bababudan is dominated by subaerial continental flood basalts and quartzites; Chitradurga is deep-marine turbidites, pillow lavas, and greywackes
Bababudan represents intracratonic continental rifting (oligomict QPC, amygdaloidal basalt flows), whereas Chitradurga reflects progressive ocean basin deepening (polymict Talya conglomerate, flysch-type greywackes, chemogenic chert-BIF).
Problem 3: Petrological Nature of the Closepet Granite
Polyphase anatectic calc-alkaline to potassic granite produced by crust-mantle interaction
Closepet Granite comprises four distinct facies: clinopyroxene-bearing monzonite, porphyritic monzogranite, equigranular granite, and late aplitic dykes, formed by anatexis of lower crust triggered by mantle-derived basic magmas.
Problem 4: Index Minerals in Sargur Metasediments
Kyanite, sillimanite, staurolite, and almandine garnet
Sargur pelites record upper amphibolite to granulite facies metamorphism ($P \approx 7-9\text{ kbar}, T \approx 700-800^\circ\text{C}$), distinguishing them from the lower-grade greenschist facies rocks of the Dharwar Supergroup.
Problem 5: Economic Mineral Deposits Associated with Dharwar Greenstones
Banded Iron Formations (Hematite/Magnetite), Gold (Kolar, Hutti), and Manganese (Sandur)
Dharwar greenstone belts host India's primary gold lodes (orogenic quartz-carbonate veins at Kolar and Hutti) and massive iron ore reserves in the Bababudan, Kudremukh, and Sandur belts.
Problem 6: Metamorphic Grade Transition Across Dharwar Craton
Greenschist facies in the north grading continuously to Granulite facies (Charnockites) in the south
The Dharwar craton displays an oblique crustal section: northern shallow crust (greenschist greenstones) exposes deep crust in the south (Kabbaldurga incipient charnockite transition at ~2.5 Ga).
Problem 7: Significance of Quartz-Pebble Conglomerate (QPC) in Bababudan Group
Marks an anoxic Archean atmosphere and hosts paleo-placer uranium/gold
The presence of detrital uraninite and rounded detrital pyrite pebbles in the basal Bababudan QPC proves that oxygen levels were $<10^{-5}\text{ PAL}$ (Pre-Great Oxidation Event).
Problem 8: U-Pb Dating Method Used on Closepet Zircons
Isotope Dilution Thermal Ionization Mass Spectrometry (ID-TIMS) and SHRIMP
High-precision zircon concordia intercept ages of $2513 \pm 5\text{ Ma}$ pin the Closepet Granite as the benchmark Archean-Proterozoic boundary chronometer in Peninsular India.
