Northwest Himalayan Stratigraphy: The Archean-Precambrian Salkhala Series

The Northwestern Himalayan syntaxis (encompassing the Kashmir Valley, Hazara, and the Nanga Parbat-Haramosh massif) preserves an ancient crystalline metamorphic basement that underpins the Phanerozoic Tethyan succession. In the geological literature of Kashmir and Hazara, these ancient Archean to Early Precambrian crystalline basement rocks are designated as the Salkhala Series (or Salkhala Formation).

  • Stratigraphic and Lithological Characteristics:
    1. Naming & Type Area: Named by D.N. Wadia after the village of Salkhala situated on the Kishanganga (Neelum) River in northern Kashmir.
    2. Lithological Complex: The Salkhala Series is a highly folded, regionally metamorphosed complex comprising:
      • Carbonaceous and graphitic schists (often containing bedded crystalline graphite).
      • Chlorite, sericite, and biotite phyllites and schists.
      • Banded white and grey crystalline limestones, calc-granulites, and marbles.
      • Quartzites, quartz-schists, and intrusive sheets of granitic gneisses.
    3. Stratigraphic Superposition in the Kashmir Basin:
      • Salkhala Series (Archean-Paleoproterozoic Basement): The fundamental crystalline foundation.
      • Dogra Slate (Late Neoproterozoic): Thick, unfossiliferous cleaved slates resting unconformably on the eroded Salkhala floor.
      • Paleozoic Tethyan Succession: Fossiliferous Cambrian beds (with Redlichia noetlingi), Ordovician-Silurian slates, Devonian Muth Quartzite, Early Carboniferous Syringothyris Limestone, Fenestella Shales, Permian Panjal Volcanics, Gangamopteris beds, and Zewan Formation.
  • Forensic Refutation of Alternative Series:
    • Buxa Series: Located exclusively in the Eastern Himalayas (Sikkim, Bhutan, and Arunachal Pradesh foothills), composed of Neoproterozoic dolostones, cherts, and quartzites.
    • Sakarsanahalli Series: High-grade metamorphic calc-silicate and manganiferous enclaves inside the Archean basement of the Dharwar Craton (Kolar schist belt, Karnataka).
    • Aravalli Series: Paleoproterozoic metasedimentary fold belt located in Rajasthan, northwestern peninsular India.
"Salkhala Series of Kashmir: In the Kashmir-Hazara area, the oldest crystalline rocks are known as the Salkhala Series. Named after the village Salkhala on the Kishanganga river, it consists of an intensely folded complex of graphitic phyllites, schists, marbles, quartzites, and gneisses. It represents the ancient Archean to Pre-Cambrian basement upon which the younger sedimentary systems of Kashmir were deposited." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Precambrian of Extra-Peninsula: Salkhala Series, p. 281)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 58
In Kashmir-Hazara area, the Archean rocks are called:
(A) Buxa series
(B) Salkhala series
(C) Sakarsanahalli series
(D) Aravalli series
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (B) Salkhala series
(A) Buxa series INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Buxa Series belongs to the Eastern Himalayas (exposed in North Bengal, Sikkim, Bhutan, and Western Arunachal Pradesh) and consists of Neoproterozoic stromatolitic dolostones and quartzites.
(B) Salkhala series CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: In the Kashmir-Hazara sector of the northwest Himalayas, the ancient crystalline metamorphic basement complex is universally termed the Salkhala Series. It consists of graphitic schists, carbonaceous marbles, phyllites, and gneisses, forming the crystalline floor beneath the Dogra Slates and Paleozoic strata.
(C) Sakarsanahalli series INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Sakarsanahalli Series is an Archean supracrustal metamorphic enclave sequence (calc-silicates, banded manganiferous quartzites) situated in the Dharwar Craton near Kolar, Karnataka.
(D) Aravalli series INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Aravalli Supergroup/Series is a Paleoproterozoic mountain belt located in the state of Rajasthan in western peninsular India, hundreds of kilometers south of Kashmir.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Kashmir Geology & Himalayan Basements
Problem 1: Economic Graphite Occurrences in Kashmir
Hosted in the carbonaceous graphitic phyllites of the Salkhala Series
Amorphous to crystalline flake graphite deposits in Uri, Karnah, and Baramulla districts occur directly within the graphitic schist horizons of the Salkhala Series.
Problem 2: Tectonic Contact of the Salkhala Series with Younger Strata
Panjal Thrust (juxtaposing Salkhala crystalline nappe over younger Paleozoic/Mesozoic strata)
The Salkhala crystalline rocks are thrust southwestward as a regional nappe sheet over the younger autochthonous sedimentary belt along the Panjal Thrust.
Problem 3: Lithological Characteristics of the Dogra Slate
Dark green, purple, and black fissile slates with subordinate greywacke sandstones
The Dogra Slate represents a thick Neoproterozoic marine argillaceous basin sequence resting upon the Salkhalas, devoid of body fossils.
Problem 4: Hazara Equivalent of the Dogra Slate
The Hazara Slate Formation
In the Hazara syntaxis (Pakistan), the Dogra Slate is represented by the lithologically identical, turbiditic Hazara Slate ('Attock Slate').
Problem 5: Index Trilobite of the Kashmir Cambrian (Overlying Strata)
Redlichia noetlingi
Fossiliferous Lower Cambrian greenish-grey silty shales of the Lolab Formation (Kashmir) yield abundant specimens of the index polymerid trilobite *Redlichia noetlingi*.
Problem 6: Metamorphic Grade of Salkhala Rocks
Greenschist to Amphibolite facies
Salkhala metasediments display progressive metamorphism from chlorite-biotite phyllites to garnet-, staurolite-, and kyanite-bearing schists near granitic gneiss contacts.
Problem 7: Syntaxial Bend of the Northwest Himalayas
Acute hairpin loop around the Nanga Parbat promontory mapped by D.N. Wadia
The regional structural trend swings abruptly from NW-SE in Kashmir to N-S and NE-SW in Hazara around the protruding Archean-Precambrian Salkhala/Nanga Parbat basement spur.
Problem 8: Origin of Salkhala Bedded Marbles
Recrystallized Precambrian marine carbonate platforms
Coarsely crystalline white, grey, and banded marbles represent original limestone and dolostone reefs subjected to regional tectonothermal recrystallization during Himalayan and pre-Himalayan orogenies.
