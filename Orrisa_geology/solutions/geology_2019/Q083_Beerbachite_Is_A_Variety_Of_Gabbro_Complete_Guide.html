1. Theoretical Foundation: Origin & Petrographic Nature of Beerbachite

Beerbachite was originally described in 1892 by German petrologist C. Chelius from Beerbach in the Odenwald, Germany. In classical and modern petrology, it designates a fine-grained, equigranular (aplitic to granoblastic) variety of Gabbro (microgabbro) consisting essentially of granular calcic plagioclase and pyroxene:

Petrographic Attribute Scientific Characteristic Diagnostic Mineralogy & Texture
Parent Rock Group Gabbro Clan (Basic Igneous Rock) Compositionally identical to basic gabbro/norite; $SiO_2 \approx 45\text{--}52\%$.
Essential Mineralogy Calcic Plagioclase + Pyroxene Labradorite/Bytownite + Orthopyroxene (Hypersthene) + Clinopyroxene (Diallage/Augite) + Magnetite.
Microscopic Texture Granular / Saccharoidal (Aplitic) Evenly sized, polygonal, mosaic-like grains without porphyritic or ophitic textures; resembles a pyroxene-hornfels.
Geological Occurrence Dykes & Contact Aureoles Occurs as aplitic basic dykes cutting larger gabbro massifs, or as intensely recrystallized basaltic xenoliths/walls in contact metamorphic aureoles.
"Beerbachite is a fine-grained, granular variety of gabbro (or aplitic microgabbro) characterized by a sugary, saccharoidal texture. It consists of an equigranular mosaic of labradorite and pyroxene (both augite and hypersthene). Originally regarded as a basic aplite dyke rock, many beerbachites are now recognized as basaltic or doleritic inclusions contact-metamorphosed to pyroxene-hornfels facies by host gabbroic intrusions."
— IUGS Subcommission on the Systematics of Igneous Rocks; P.K. Mukherjee, A Textbook of Geology (p. 251)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 83
Beerbachite is a variety of :
A Gabbro
B Basalt
C Dolerite
D Monzonite
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION A (Gabbro)
OPTION A • CORRECT
Gabbro

Beerbachite is historically and systematically classified as a fine-grained granular or aplitic variety of Gabbro (microgabbro). It shares the identical mineral chemistry of gabbro (calcic plagioclase + pyroxene) while displaying a distinctive saccharoidal, equigranular mosaic texture.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Basalt

Basalt is an extrusive volcanic lava flow exhibiting aphanitic, glassy, or intersertal textures. Beerbachite forms as intrusive dykes or high-grade thermal hornfelses with completely holocrystalline granular texture.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
Dolerite

Dolerite is characterized by ophitic or subophitic texture with subhedral plagioclase laths. Beerbachite lacks ophitic texture, displaying instead an equigranular (aplitic) mosaic texture.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
Monzonite

Monzonite is an intermediate plutonic rock containing roughly equal amounts of alkali feldspar and plagioclase ($SiO_2 \approx 55\text{--}62\%$). Beerbachite contains no alkali feldspar and is strictly basic.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. From which European locality was beerbachite originally named?
Answer: Beerbach in the Odenwald, Hesse, Germany.
C. Chelius named the rock in 1892 from dyke-like occurrences cutting gabbros in the Odenwald mountain range.
2. What type of gabbro is characterized by essential orthopyroxene rather than clinopyroxene?
Answer: Norite
When orthopyroxene (enstatite/hypersthene) is the dominant pyroxene alongside calcic plagioclase, the rock is formally termed norite.
3. What is troctolite composed of?
Answer: Calcic plagioclase and olivine (essentially pyroxene-free).
Troctolite (from Greek for 'trout rock') is an olivine-plagioclase cumulate member of the gabbro family.
4. What is a basic aplite?
Answer: A fine-grained, saccharoidal dyke rock of basic composition (such as beerbachite).
While aplites are typically granitic, fine-grained sugary dykes of gabbroic composition are historically called basic aplites or beerbachites.
5. Under what metamorphic facies do basaltic country rocks recrystallize to produce beerbachite-like textures?
Answer: Pyroxene-hornfels facies (or Sanidinite facies)
Extreme contact thermal metamorphism by mafic magma dehydrates basalts into an equigranular mosaic of pyroxene and calcic plagioclase.
6. What is the diallage variety of clinopyroxene commonly found in gabbros?
Answer: Augite exhibiting prominent (100) parting due to microscopic exsolution lamellae of orthopyroxene.
Diallage is a historical petrographic name for augite with a distinctive platy parting parallel to {100} and pearly lustre.
7. What is an olivine gabbro?
Answer: A gabbro containing calcic plagioclase, clinopyroxene, and >5% modal olivine.
Olivine gabbro is an abundant member of ocean floor crust (layer 3) and continental layered mafic complexes.
8. What is the typical anorthite content ($An\%$) of plagioclase in gabbroic rocks?
Answer: $An_{50}\text{--}An_{85}$ (Labradorite to Bytownite)
By definition, gabbros contain calcic plagioclase with $An > 50\%$, which separates them from diorites ($An < 50\%$).
