Ductile Flow, Competency Contrasts & Disharmonic Folding Mechanics

When stratified rock sequences undergo compressional deformation, individual layers respond according to their intrinsic rheological properties (viscosity, yield strength, and ductility). If layers of strongly differing competencies (e.g., massive sandstones alternating with incompetent shales or evaporites) are folded together, the uniform geometric continuity across layers breaks down, producing disharmonic folding.

  • Disharmonic Fold Definition: Folds in which the shape, amplitude, and wavelength change abruptly from one layer to the adjacent layer across the stratigraphic section. The folds in the incompetent layers are not harmonic with those in the competent layers.
  • Hinge Thickening & Limb Thinning: In incompetent beds subjected to high confining pressure and tectonic stress, material behaves pseudo-plastically and flows ductilely from high-stress regions (the limbs) into low-stress regions (crests and troughs). Consequently, the incompetent units show drastically thickened hinges (crests and troughs) and heavily attenuated, thinned limbs.
  • Ramsay Class 2 Similar Fold Model: In Ramsay's dip isogon classification, a fold where the orthogonal thickness $t_\alpha$ varies such that $t_\alpha = t_0 \cdot \cos \alpha$, maintaining identical profile shapes down axial surfaces, is a Similar Fold (Class 2). The thickness measured parallel to the axial trace ($T_\alpha$) remains perfectly constant, requiring substantial flow of rock mass into hinges.
"In close and disharmonic folds, the folding is so tight that incompetent strata flow plastically towards the crests and troughs. This causes thinning at the limbs and thickening at the crests and troughs. The disharmonic fold is produced when competent and incompetent beds fold with different wavelengths." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Folds: Types of Fold, p. 114)
Ramsay Fold Geometry: Parallel vs. Similar Folds
$$\text{Parallel / Class 1B Fold (Buckling):} \quad t_\alpha = t_0 = \text{constant} \implies \text{No hinge thickening}$$ $$\text{Similar / Class 2 Fold (Ductile Flow):} \quad t_\alpha = t_0 \cos \alpha \implies T_\alpha = \text{constant}$$ $$\text{Hinge Thickening Ratio } (\Delta t) = \frac{t_{\text{hinge}}}{t_{\text{limb}}} = \frac{1}{\cos \alpha} > 1.0$$

Where $\alpha$ is the limb dip, $t_0$ is the thickness at the hinge, and $t_\alpha$ is the orthogonal thickness along the limb.

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q026
Thickened crests and troughs with thinner limbs are noticed in:
(A) Open fold
(B) Disharmonic fold
(C) Buckle fold
(D) Over fold
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (B) Disharmonic fold
Verified against Bangar (p. 114) and Fossen (Structural Geology, p. 248). Disharmonic and similar-type close folds exhibit plastic ductile flow of incompetent layers into crests and troughs, leading to pronounced hinge thickening and limb thinning.
(A) Open fold INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Open folds are defined by Fleuty as having an interlimb angle between $70^\circ$ and $120^\circ$. The degree of deformation is mild; strain is insufficient to drive plastic ductile flow of incompetent material into hinges. Consequently, layer thickness remains essentially uniform throughout.
(B) Disharmonic fold CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: In disharmonic folds (and associated close/similar folds), alternating competent and incompetent layers deform inhomogeneously. The incompetent strata cannot accommodate the large buckles of competent layers without severe plastic flow, causing the incompetent material to flow from the high-stress limbs into low-stress crests and troughs, resulting in dramatically thickened crests and troughs and thinned limbs.
(C) Buckle fold INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Pure buckle folding of competent layers is governed by flexural slip and Biot-Ramberg mechanics, which classically generates parallel folds (Ramsay Class 1B). In a parallel fold, layer thickness measured perpendicular to bedding ($t_\alpha$) remains strictly uniform from crest to limb ($t_\alpha = t_0$). It does not produce thickened hinges.
(D) Over fold INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: An over fold (or overturned fold) describes a geometric attitude where one limb is overturned past vertical. This geometric descriptor refers purely to axial plane and limb inclination, not to layer thickness variations or plastic flow into crests and troughs.

8 Fully Worked Exam-Standard Practice Problems: Fold Mechanics & Thickness Variations
Problem 1: Calculation of Similar Fold Limb Thickness
Orthogonal thickness on limb $t_{60} = 5.0\text{ m}$
A similar fold (Ramsay Class 2) has a hinge thickness of $t_0 = 10.0\text{ m}$. If the limb dips at an angle of $\alpha = 60^\circ$, calculate the orthogonal limb thickness $t_\alpha$.
$$t_\alpha = t_0 \cdot \cos \alpha = 10.0 \cdot \cos(60^\circ) = 10.0 \cdot 0.5 = 5.0\text{ m}$$
Problem 2: Dip Isogon Convergence Criterion
Parallel dip isogons define Class 2 Similar Folds
Dip isogons are lines connecting points of equal dip on adjacent surfaces. When dip isogons are parallel to each other and to the axial surface, the fold is mathematically a Class 2 (Similar) fold with constant axial thickness $T_\alpha$.
Problem 3: Competency Contrast & Wavelength Selection
Biot-Ramberg equation: $\lambda_d = 2\pi h \sqrt[3]{\mu_1 / (6\mu_2)}$
Competent layers of thickness $h$ and viscosity $\mu_1$ embedded in incompetent matrix $\mu_2$ select a dominant wavelength $\lambda_d$. If $\mu_1 / \mu_2$ is high, buckling dominates; if low, ductile thickening and disharmony prevail.
Problem 4: Space Problem in Parallel Folds
Inward curvature forces cusp formation and thrusting in cores
Because parallel folds maintain constant orthogonal thickness, the radius of curvature decreases toward the core of an anticline, eventually reaching zero ($R = 0$) and generating space problems solved by disharmonic folding or detachment thrusts.
Problem 5: Identification of Ptygmatic Folding
Ptygmatic folds represent disharmonic buckle veins in incompetent host
Tortuous, meandrine quartz-feldspathic veins folded within ductile gneisses or migmatites are termed ptygmatic folds, representing extreme disharmonic folding driven by high competency contrast.
Problem 6: Cleavage Development in Hinges vs Limbs
Axial planar cleavage is most intense and penetrative in thickened hinges
In incompetent layers undergoing flow into crests and troughs, the strain ellipsoid experiences maximum flattening perpendicular to the axial surface, producing dense, pervasive axial-planar slaty cleavage in the thickened hinge zones.
Problem 7: Ramsay Class 1C Characterization
Intermediate between Parallel (1B) and Similar (2)
Most natural buckle folds modified by flattening belong to Ramsay Class 1C, where dip isogons converge weakly toward the inner arc, exhibiting modest hinge thickening compared to ideal 1B folds.
Problem 8: Competency Inversion in Metamorphism
Carbonates flow plastically at lower temperatures than quartzites
Under greenschist to amphibolite facies metamorphism, calcite and dolomite undergo crystal-plastic dislocation creep at $250-350^\circ\text{C}$, behaving as incompetent flowing units that heavily thicken in fold hinges between rigid quartzites.
