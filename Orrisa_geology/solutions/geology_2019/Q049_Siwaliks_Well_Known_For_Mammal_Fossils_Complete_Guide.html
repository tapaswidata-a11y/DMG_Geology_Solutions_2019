Cenozoic Vertebrate Paleontology: Siwalik Foreland Basin & Mammalian Radiation

The Siwalik Supergroup (or Siwalik Group) is a vast, continuous, $6,000\text{ m}$ thick synorogenic molasse sequence deposited in the Himalayan foreland basin from Middle Miocene to Early Pleistocene (~18 Ma to 0.6 Ma). It was formed by rivers draining the rising Himalayan orogen (paleo-Indo-Brahm river system). Globally, the Siwaliks are celebrated as the premier terrestrial vertebrate fossil repository in the world, famed above all for their rich and evolutionary mammalian fauna.

  • Stratigraphic Divisions & Mammalian Faunal Succession:
    1. Lower Siwalik (~18 - 10.5 Ma; Kamlial & Chinji Formations):
      • Sedimentology: Hard grey sandstones and bright red/purple floodplain shales.
      • Fauna: Primitive proboscideans (Deinotherium, Gomphotherium), anthracotheres, early suids (Listriodon), primitive giraffids (Giraffokeryx), and primitive primates.
    2. Middle Siwalik (~10.5 - 3.5 Ma; Nagri & Dhok Pathan Formations):
      • Sedimentology: Coarse multistory micaceous sandstones ('pepper-and-salt' texture) and brown clays.
      • Fauna — Golden Age of Mammals:
        • Hominoids (Primates): World-famous discovery of Sivapithecus indicus, Sivapithecus punjabicus (and Ramapithecus), crucial in ape-human evolutionary lineages.
        • Equids: Abundant radiation of the tridactyl (three-toed) grazing horse Hipparion (Hipparion theobaldi, H. antilopinum), marking the arrival of C4 grasslands.
        • Giant Herbivores: Bramatherium, Hydaspitherium (giant giraffids), Mastodon, and rhinocerotids.
    3. Upper Siwalik (~3.5 - 0.6 Ma; Tatrot, Pinjor & Boulder Conglomerate):
      • Sedimentology: Coarse conglomerates, gravel beds, silts, and loess.
      • Fauna — Advanced Quaternary Mammals:
        • Arrival of monodactyl true horses (Equus sivalensis).
        • Advanced proboscideans: Stegodon ganesa (with massive 3-meter curved tusks) and Elephas hysudricus.
        • Hippopotamids (Hexaprotodon sivalensis), camelids (Camelus sivalensis), giant bovids, sabre-toothed cats (Machairodus), and primates (Cercopithecus).
"Siwalik System & Mammalian Fossils: The Siwalik rocks are globally famous for their exceptionally rich assemblage of mammalian fossils. The collection includes practically every mammalian order: Primates (such as the extinct man-like ape Sivapithecus), Proboscidea (primitive elephants like Stegodon, Mastodon, Deinotherium), Equidae (the horse ancestor Hipparion and modern Equus), Rhinocerotidae, Bovidae, Suidae, and Carnivora. No other geological formation in the world presents such an extraordinary and continuous record of mammalian evolution." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (The Tertiary System: Siwalik Group, pp. 302-304)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 49
Siwaliks are well known for:
(A) Dinosaur fossil
(B) Invertebrate fossils
(C) Mammal fossils
(D) Plant fossils
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (C) Mammal fossils
(A) Dinosaur fossil INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Dinosaurs went extinct at the end of the Cretaceous (~66 Ma), whereas the Siwaliks are Neogene-Quaternary (~18 to 0.6 Ma). Indian dinosaur fossils (Titanosaurs, Rajasaurus narmadensis) are recovered from the Cretaceous Lameta Formation and Ariyalur beds, not Siwaliks.
(B) Invertebrate fossils INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: While minor freshwater bivalves (*Unio*) and gastropods occur in Siwalik muds, the Siwaliks are globally renowned specifically for vertebrates, in complete contrast to marine formations like Kutch or Spiti which are famed for invertebrates.
(C) Mammal fossils CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: The Siwalik Supergroup is universally celebrated as the world's finest Neogene terrestrial fossil treasury for mammals. It records the complete evolutionary history of hominoid primates (Sivapithecus), three-toed and single-toed horses (Hipparion, Equus), proboscideans (Stegodon, Elephas), giraffes, hippos, rhinos, and bovids.
(D) Plant fossils INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Fossil wood and leaf impressions occur in the Siwaliks, but plant fossils are the signature hallmark of the Gondwana Supergroup (Glossopteris, Gangamopteris) and Rajmahal Intertrappeans, not the Siwalik Group.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Siwalik Biostratigraphy & Evolution
Problem 1: The 'Hipparion Datum' in Siwalik Chronology
First appearance datum (FAD) of Hipparion at ~10.7 Ma (Base of Nagri Formation)
The migration of the three-toed horse *Hipparion* from North America into Eurasia via the Bering land bridge marks a global chronostratigraphic datum (~10.7 Ma) defining the base of the Middle Siwaliks.
Problem 2: Significance of Sivapithecus Jaw Fragments
Demonstrated that Sivapithecus is sister-taxon to modern Orangutan (*Pongo*)
Facial and gnathic anatomy (narrow interorbital distance, oval orbits, airorhynchous facial profile) proved *Sivapithecus* was ancestral to Asian orangutans rather than a direct human ancestor.
Problem 3: Carbon Isotope Shift in Siwalik Paleosols (~7 - 8 Ma)
Shift from C3 woodland vegetation to C4 grassland savannas
Pedogenic carbonate $\delta^{13} ext{C}$ shifts from $-12‰$ to $+1‰$ between 7.5 and 6.0 Ma, recording the intensification of the Asian Monsoon and the replacement of C3 forests by C4 drought-adapted grasses.
Problem 4: Diagnostic Tusk Feature of *Stegodon ganesa*
Enormous, closely spaced, parallel curved tusks exceeding 3 meters length
Discovered in the Pinjor Formation near Saketi, *Stegodon ganesa* possessed the most massive tusks of any known proboscidean, displayed today in the Indian Museum Kolkata.
Problem 5: Difference Between Pinjor and Tatrot Formations
Tatrot represents Pliocene (~3.5-2.6 Ma); Pinjor represents Early Pleistocene (~2.6-0.6 Ma) with *Equus* and *Elephas*
The Pinjor stage is marked by the first appearance of the true modern horse (*Equus sivalensis*) and modern elephant (*Elephas hysudricus*), alongside colder Pleistocene glacial-influenced climate.
Problem 6: Sedimentological Difference Between Kamlial and Chinji Formations
Kamlial is dominated by hard grey sandstones; Chinji is dominated by thick bright-red floodplain clays
Kamlial sandstones were deposited by large bedload channel networks, whereas Chinji reflects meandering river channels wandering across wide, oxidized, red muddy floodplains.
Problem 7: The Genus *Hexaprotodon*
Primitive hippopotamid possessing six incisor teeth in both upper and lower jaws
Modern African hippos (*Hippopotamus amphibius*) are tetraprotodont (4 incisors), whereas the Siwalik *Hexaprotodon* retained the primitive mammalian condition of 6 incisors.
Problem 8: Tectonic Boundary Terminating the Siwalik Basin to the South
Main Frontal Thrust (MFT / Himalayan Frontal Fault)
The MFT places the folded Siwalik molasse over the modern Indo-Gangetic alluvium along the southernmost active front of the Himalayan collision zone.
