Indian Gondwana Paleobotany: The Conifer Buriadia & Permian Flora

The Gondwana Supergroup of peninsular India preserves an exceptional fossil plant record documenting the floral evolution of the southern supercontinent from the late Paleozoic post-glacial Permian into the Cretaceous. Within the rich assemblages of the Lower Gondwana coal measures, Buriadia (specifically Buriadia sewardii) represents a true primitive gymnospermous conifer.

  • Paleobotanical Characteristics of Buriadia:
    • Taxonomic Classification: Division Gymnospermae, Order Coniferales / Voltziales (primitive walchian-type conifers).
    • Vegetative Morphology: Highly branched woody gymnospermous shoots bearing spirally arranged, polymorphic, linear needle-like leaves. A distinctive diagnostic feature is that the leaves frequently bifurcate (fork) at their tips into two divergent lobes, alongside single linear needles.
    • Reproductive Structures: Unlike modern conifers that bear cone scales, *Buriadia* bore solitary, terminal, anatropous (inverted) ovules/seeds attached directly to the leafy shoots on short stalks, demonstrating the primitive transitional state of early conifer female fructifications.
    • Stratigraphic Horizon: Confined to the Karharbari Formation and Upper Talchir Formation of the Lower Gondwana (Early Permian / Sakmarian-Artinskian), where it forms a signature biostratigraphic marker in the Giridih and Damodar Valley coalfields.
  • Forensic Refutation of Other Gondwana Floral Taxa:
    • Glossopteris: The dominant signature plant of the Gondwana Supergroup. It is an extinct Pteridosperm (Seed Fern) belonging to the order Glossopteridales, characterized by tongue-shaped leaves with an anastomosing reticulate venation network and a prominent central midrib.
    • Gangamopteris: Also a Pteridosperm (Seed Fern), closely related to *Glossopteris* but lacking a central midrib (bearing arched, sub-parallel bifurcating veins radiating from the leaf base). Dominant in Early Permian Talchir and Karharbari beds.
    • Schizoneura (Schizoneura gondwanensis): An extinct Sphenopsid / Equisetalean (Horsetail) belonging to the articulate pteridophytes. It possesses hollow, fluted jointed stems bearing nodal whorls of ribbon-like leaves.
"Gondwana Flora (Buriadia): The Gondwana flora of India is subdivided into the Lower Gondwana (*Glossopteris* flora) and Upper Gondwana (*Ptilophyllum* flora). In the Lower Gondwana Karharbari beds, alongside *Glossopteris* and *Gangamopteris*, occurs the primitive conifer Buriadia (represented by *Buriadia sewardii*), characterized by branched leafy twigs with bifurcated needle-like leaves and single seeds." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Gondwana System: Paleobotany, pp. 290-291)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 68
Amongst the following flora which represents a conifer ?
(A) Schizoneura
(B) Buriadia
(C) Gangamopteris
(D) Glossopteris
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (B) Buriadia
(A) Schizoneura INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Schizoneura is a sphenopsid (equisetalean horsetail / articulate pteridophyte) featuring fluted jointed stems, not a conifer.
(B) Buriadia CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: Buriadia (Buriadia sewardii) is a primitive gymnospermous conifer (Order Coniferales / Voltziales) discovered in the Early Permian Karharbari Formation of the Lower Gondwana. Its woody branched twigs bear needle-like bifid leaves and solitary terminal seeds, marking India's earliest known conifer.
(C) Gangamopteris INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Gangamopteris is an extinct seed fern (Pteridosperm / Glossopteridales) characterized by tongue-shaped leaves lacking a midrib, not a conifer.
(D) Glossopteris INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Glossopteris is the iconic seed fern (Pteridosperm / Glossopteridales) of the Gondwana coal measures, featuring reticulate venation with a distinct midrib. It is not a conifer.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Indian Gondwana Paleobotany
Problem 1: Paleobotanist Who Decoded Buriadia Fructifications
Professor Birbal Sahni and Divya Darshan Pant
Birbal Sahni (1928) and D.D. Pant demonstrated that *Buriadia* bore non-coniferous single ovules directly on leafy shoots, solving the evolutionary puzzle of conifer seed attachment.
Problem 2: The Two Great Floras of the Indian Gondwana
The Lower Gondwana Glossopteris Flora and Upper Gondwana Ptilophyllum Flora
The Paleozoic Lower Gondwana is dominated by cold-climate seed ferns (*Glossopteris*), transitioning in the Mesozoic Upper Gondwana to warm-climate cycadophytes (*Ptilophyllum*, *Nilssonia*).
Problem 3: Female Reproductive Organ of Glossopteris
Scutum, Ottokaria, and Dictyopteridium
Seed-bearing fructifications were attached directly to the midrib of *Glossopteris* leaves, proving they were seed-bearing gymnospermous pteridosperms rather than true ferns.
Problem 4: Index Fossil Plant Defining the Karharbari Formation
Gondwanidium validum and Buriadia sewardii
The co-occurrence of the neuropterid frond *Gondwanidium* and the conifer *Buriadia* defines the Karharbari biozone, separating it from the underlying Talchir and overlying Barakar formations.
Problem 5: Vertebraria as an Internal Plant Organ
The segmented, chambered subterranean root (rhizome) of Glossopteris
*Vertebraria indica* consists of radiating longitudinal wedge-like septa separated by air-filled cavities, an anatomical adaptation for aeration in waterlogged swamp soils.
Problem 6: Rajmahal Intertrappean Plant Fossils
Fossiliferous chert beds yielding silicified bennettitalean gymnosperms (*Williamsonia sewardiana*)
Silicified volcanic ash beds between Early Cretaceous Rajmahal basalt flows preserve cellular anatomy of *Williamsonia* cones, researched extensively by the Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP).
Problem 7: Distinguishing Glossopteris from Gangamopteris
Glossopteris has a well-defined persistent central midrib; Gangamopteris lacks a midrib
In *Gangamopteris*, subparallel arched veins emerge directly from the basal attachment and anastomose without ever coalescing into a median midrib vein.
Problem 8: Fossil Wood of Lower Gondwana Gymnosperms
Dadoxylon (silicified pycnoxylic secondary gymnospermous wood)
Massive silicified tree trunks in the Raniganj and Barakar coal measures (*Dadoxylon*) exhibit prominent annual growth rings, indicating strong seasonal climatic fluctuations in Permian Gondwanaland.
