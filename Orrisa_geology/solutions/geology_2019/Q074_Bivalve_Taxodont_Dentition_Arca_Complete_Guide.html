Bivalve Paleontology: Hinge Dentition Architecture & The Genus Arca

In the Class Bivalvia (Lamellibranchiata / Pelecypoda, Phylum Mollusca), the shell consists of two lateral, calcareous valves hinged dorsally. The inner dorsal margin features a specialized interlocking system of teeth and sockets known as the hinge dentition, which guides valve closure and prevents lateral shearing. Among the classic dentition types, the genus Arca (ark clams) is the world-renowned textbook archetype of TAXODONT dentition.

  • Characteristics of Taxodont Dentition (Archetypal Genus: Arca):
    • Anatomical Definition: Taxodont dentition consists of a long, straight or gently curved hinge plate bearing a continuous row of numerous, small, similar, comb-like alternating transverse teeth and sockets.
    • Mechanical Function: Because the teeth are uniform, transverse, and tightly interlocking along the entire cardinal margin, they provide exceptional resistance against rotational twisting and shearing, adapted for thick-shelled epifaunal or shallow endobenthic bivalves.
    • Subclass & Orders: Typical of the primitive Subclass Pteriomorphia (Order Arcoida, Family Arcidae: Arca, Barbatia, Anadara) and Subclass Protobranchia (Family Nuculidae: Nucula).
  • Forensic Review of Distractor Genera and Their Dentition:
    • Venus (Heterodont Dentition): Subclass Heterodonta. The hinge is differentiated into a few (typically 3) distinct, radiating cardinal teeth directly beneath the beak (umbone) and elongated anterior/posterior lateral teeth.
    • Mytilus (Dysodont Dentition): Subclass Pteriomorphia (Mussels). Hinge teeth are virtually absent, reduced to tiny, weak denticles near the acute umbone, because the valves are anchored to rocks by strong fibrous byssus threads.
    • Pholadomya (Desmodont Dentition): Subclass Anomalodesmata. True hinge teeth are absent; the hinge mechanism is replaced by a large, internal spatulate plate (chondrophore or resilifer) supporting an elastic internal ligament.
"Bivalve Hinge Dentition (Taxodont): The arrangement of teeth and sockets along the hinge margin is of great taxonomic importance: (1) Taxodont Dentition: Consists of a continuous row of numerous, small, similar teeth arranged in a straight or curved line along the hinge plate. The classic example is Arca. (2) Heterodont: Differentiated into cardinal and lateral teeth (e.g., Venus). (3) Dysodont: Teeth reduced or absent (e.g., Mytilus)." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Invertebrate Paleontology: Bivalvia - Dentition, p. 261)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 74
Which amongst the following is a bivalve genus having taxodont type dentition ?
(A) Arca
(B) Pholadomya
(C) Venus
(D) Mytilus
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (A) Arca
(A) Arca CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: Arca (the ark clam) is the universally documented textbook archetype of taxodont dentition. Its straight hinge margin bears a continuous comb-like row of numerous, small, uniform, transverse alternating teeth and sockets.
(B) Pholadomya INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Pholadomya possesses desmodont dentition, where true teeth are absent and replaced by an internal ligament process (chondrophore).
(C) Venus INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Venus possesses heterodont dentition, characterized by differentiated cardinal teeth beneath the beak and lateral teeth along the margin.
(D) Mytilus INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Mytilus (marine mussel) possesses dysodont dentition, where teeth are reduced to tiny vestigial denticles or completely absent, with valve closure governed by byssal anchoring.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Bivalve Paleobiology & Shell Anatomy
Problem 1: Ligament Function in Bivalve Opening
Antagonistic passive spring opening the valves when adductor muscles relax
The uncalcified proteinaceous ligament is under compression (internal resilium) or tension (external lamellar ligament) when closed. Relaxation of adductor muscles automatically pops the valves open.
Problem 2: The Byssal Notch in *Arca*
Ventral gap between closed valves for the emergence of tough byssal anchor fibers
Many species of *Arca* (*Arca noae*) anchor to rocky substrates using a stout, chitinous byssus projecting through a broad median ventral hiatus.
Problem 3: Pallial Line and Pallial Sinus Significance
Pallial line marks mantle attachment; Pallial sinus indicates retractable siphons (infaunal burrowers)
Infaunal siphonate bivalves (*Mya*, *Tellina*) have a deep indentation (sinus) in the pallial line. Epifaunal non-siphonate bivalves (*Arca*) have an entire, unbroken pallial line (integripalliate).
Problem 4: Dimyarian vs. Monomyarian Adductor Scars
Dimyarian has 2 adductor muscle scars (anterior & posterior); Monomyarian has only 1 large central scar
*Arca* is isomyarian (two equal-sized adductor scars). Oysters (*Ostrea*) and scallops (*Pecten*) are monomyarian, having lost the anterior adductor.
Problem 5: Cardinal Area (Amphidetic Ligament Area) in *Arca*
Broad, flat, triangular ligament area between the umbones bearing chevron-shaped grooves
The wide dorsal spacing between the recurved umbones exposes a large cardinal area scored with diamond or chevron-shaped ligament attachment furrows (duplivincular ligament).
Problem 6: Pachydont Dentition in Rudists
Extreme specialization with colossal peg-like teeth in conical reef-building Cretaceous bivalves
Cretaceous rudists (*Hippurites*) evolved giant peg teeth and deep sockets, functioning like box lids on horn-coral-like calcite cones.
Problem 7: Mineralogy of the *Arca* Shell
Cross-lamellar aragonite
The shell wall of *Arca* is constructed of interlocking cross-lamellar aragonite laths, providing exceptional mechanical toughness against crushing by durophagous predators.
Problem 8: Modern Indian Occurrence of Arcidae
Anadara granosa (blood clam) in coastal muddy estuaries and mangrove lagoons of Odisha and Bengal
*Anadara granosa* possesses hemoglobin in its blood (giving red flesh) and classic taxodont teeth, harvested commercially along the eastern Indian seaboard.
