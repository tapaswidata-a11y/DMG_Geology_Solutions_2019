Chronostratigraphy: Indian Stratigraphic Units & Age Correlations

Accurate correlation between lithostratigraphic formations and global chronostratigraphic epochs requires rigorous biostratigraphic and isotopic grounding. In Indian geology, competitive exams frequently test key diagnostic formations from the Purana Proterozoic Basins, the Mesozoic-Cenozoic Marine Basins, and Island Arc Successions.

  • Forensic Review of Tested Formations:
    1. Niniyur Formation (Cauvery Basin, South India):
      • Geographic & Geologic Setting: Exposed in the Ariyalur-Trichinopoly area of Tamil Nadu.
      • Stratigraphic Placement: The Cauvery Basin Cretaceous-Tertiary succession comprises in ascending order: Uttatur Group (Upper Albian - Cenomanian), Trichinopoly Group (Turonian - Coniacian), Ariyalur Group (Campanian - Maastrichtian), and the Niniyur Formation (Danian, earliest Paleocene).
      • Curriculum Classification: In classic Indian geology textbooks (M.S. Krishnan, D.N. Wadia, K.M. Bangar), the Niniyur beds are traditionally categorized and taught as the uppermost terminal member of the famous "Cretaceous of South India (Trichinopoly)" succession because they represent the continuous marine capping sequence of the Ariyalur Cretaceous marine transgression.
    2. Bhander Group (Vindhyan Supergroup): Uppermost group of the Upper Vindhyan sequence (comprising Ganurgarh Shale, Bhander Limestone, Sirbu Shale, and Upper Bhander Sandstone). Radiometric dates (detrital zircon and kimberlite pipes) constrain it to the Neoproterozoic (~650 - 850 Ma), completely ruling out 'Early Proterozoic' (Paleoproterozoic).
    3. Long Formation (Andaman Islands): A prominent carbonate-rich unit of the Archipelago Group on Long Island. It is bio-zoned with globorotalid foraminifera and calcareous nannofossils dating unequivocally to the Middle to Late Miocene, not the Oligocene.
    4. Bheema (Bhima) Group (Karnataka): One of the premier Neoproterozoic "Purana" basins (~800 - 900 Ma) resting upon the Eastern Dharwar granites. It consists of unmetamorphosed shales and massive Shahabad limestones, completely distinct from the Cambrian.
"Cretaceous of Trichinopoly: The Cretaceous rocks of the Cauvery basin are subdivided into four well-defined stages: (1) Utatur Stage (Cenomanian), (2) Trichinopoly Stage (Turonian-Senonian), (3) Ariyalur Stage (Senonian-Maestrichtian), and (4) Niniyur Stage (Danian). The Niniyur stage is characterized by the absence of ammonites and belemnites, and the abundance of algae and the gastropod Nautilus danicus and Cardita beaumonti." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Cretaceous System of India, p. 296)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 47
Choose the correct pair of stratigraphic formation and its corresponding age :
(A) Bhander Group — Early Proterozoic
(B) Niniyur Formation — Cretaceous
(C) Long Formation — Oligocene
(D) Bheema Group — Cambrian
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (B) Niniyur Formation — Cretaceous
(A) Bhander Group — Early Proterozoic INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Bhander Group represents the youngest division of the Upper Vindhyan Supergroup and is firmly dated to the Neoproterozoic (~650 - 850 Ma). The 'Early Proterozoic' (Paleoproterozoic, 2500 - 1600 Ma) corresponds to the Lower Vindhyan Semri Group or Bijawar Group, making this pair completely false.
(B) Niniyur Formation — Cretaceous CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: In standard Indian stratigraphy curricula (M.S. Krishnan, D.N. Wadia, K.M. Bangar), the South Indian Cretaceous of the Cauvery Basin is universally taught as four divisions: Utatur, Trichinopoly, Ariyalur, and Niniyur. Although modern international chronostratigraphy classifies the Danian stage as basal Paleocene, in Indian examination keys and classic textbooks, Niniyur is designated as the terminal Cretaceous division representing the Danian marine sequence.
(C) Long Formation — Oligocene INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Long Formation belongs to the Archipelago Group of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. Its foraminiferal assemblage (Globorotalia fohsi zone) dates it firmly to the Middle to Late Miocene. The Oligocene in the Andaman belt is represented by the Andaman Flysch Group.
(D) Bheema Group — Cambrian INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Bheema (Bhima) Group is a classical Neoproterozoic Purana basin (~800 - 900 Ma) developed on the Archean granitic basement in Karnataka. It is devoid of Phanerozoic shelly macrofossils (trilobites) and is Proterozoic, not Cambrian.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Indian Stratigraphic Chronology
Problem 1: Diagnostic Fossil Disappearance in Niniyur Formation
Extinction of Belemnites and Ammonites; appearance of Danian fauna
The underlying Ariyalur beds contain abundant ammonites (*Pachydiscus*, *Baculites*) and belemnites. In the Niniyur beds, all ammonites and belemnites are completely extinct, replaced by *Nautilus danicus*, *Cardita beaumonti*, and coralline algae, capturing the K-Pg boundary.
Problem 2: Oldest Group of the Vindhyan Supergroup
Semri Group (Lower Vindhyan, ~1.6 - 1.7 Ga)
The Semri Group represents the Paleoproterozoic to Mesoproterozoic base (containing Kajrahat limestone and Porcellanite beds), whereas the Bhander Group sits at the very top of the Upper Vindhyan in the Neoproterozoic.
Problem 3: Age of the Andaman Flysch Group
Late Eocene to Oligocene
The Andaman Flysch represents a thick sequence of deep-water turbiditic sandstones and shales deposited by longitudinal turbidity currents in the Sunda subduction trench during Late Eocene to Oligocene times.
Problem 4: Subdivisions of the Bhima Group
Sedam Subgroup (Rabanpalli Formation, Shahabad Limestone) and Andola Subgroup
The Bhima succession is divided into basal clastics (Rabanpalli), a middle carbonate platform (Shahabad limestone—the backbone of South Indian cement manufacture), and upper shales/limestones.
Problem 5: Type Area of Marine Cambrian in Peninsular India
Peninsular India has NO Marine Cambrian; Salt Range and Spiti are Extra-Peninsular
The Indian peninsula was an emergent craton during the Cambrian. Marine Cambrian rocks are confined strictly to the Extra-Peninsula (Tethyan belt of Spiti and Kashmir, and Salt Range Pakistan).
Problem 6: Age of the Ariyalur Group
Upper Cretaceous: Campanian to Maastrichtian
The Ariyalur Group is divided into the Sillakkudi, Kallankurichi (rich in gryphaeid oysters), and Ottakovil formations, dated to Campanian-Maastrichtian via *Globotruncana* foraminifera.
Problem 7: Significance of the Porcellanite Stage in Semri Group
Silicic felsic pyroclastic volcanic ash beds (~1600 Ma)
The Porcellanite Formation of the Semri Group comprises rhyolitic to dacitic tuffs, tuffaceous shales, and silicified ash layers, dated by zircon U-Pb to ~1630 Ma.
Problem 8: Correlation of Niniyur with Narmada Valley Cretaceous
Bagh Beds of Narmada Valley are Cenomanian-Turonian, older than Niniyur
The marine Bagh Beds (Nimar Sandstone, Nodular Limestone, Deola Marl, Coralline Limestone) correlate with the Uttatur and Trichinopoly groups, not the terminal Niniyur beds.
