Fold Kinematics: Axis Orientation, Plunge Angle & Map Patterns

In structural geology, the 3D spatial attitude of a fold is fully specified by the geometric orientation of its axial surface and its fold axis (hinge line). The fold axis is defined as the line of intersection between the axial plane and a bedding surface, or the line which when moved parallel to itself generates the cylindrical fold surface.

  • Non-Plunging Fold: A fold whose axis is perfectly horizontal (angle of plunge $= 0^\circ$). On a planar geological map, the outcrop traces of the two limbs form parallel, straight bands that never converge or close.
  • Plunging Fold: A fold whose axis is inclined to the horizontal (angle of plunge $> 0^\circ$). The plunge is the angle of inclination of the fold axis measured downward from the horizontal in a vertical plane containing the axis ($0^\circ < \text{plunge} \le 90^\circ$).
  • Closure and Nose: Plunging folds produce diagnostic curved, convergent outcrop traces on geological maps:
    • Plunging Anticline: The outcrop pattern forms a V-shaped curve (nose) that points in the direction of plunge. The oldest beds occupy the core.
    • Plunging Syncline: The outcrop pattern forms a V-shaped curve that opens in the direction of plunge (points opposite to the plunge direction). The youngest beds occupy the core.
"The axis of a fold may be horizontal or inclined. Anticlines and synclines whose axes are horizontal are said to be 'non-plunging'. Folds having inclined axes are called the plunging folds. The angle of inclination of the axis measured from the horizontal is called the angle of plunge and the direction in which this axis is inclined is called the direction of plunge." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Folds: Plunging Folds, p. 113)
Mathematical Attitude of a Plunging Fold Axis
$$\text{Plunge Vector } (\mathbf{L}) = \text{Trend } (\phi) / \text{Plunge } (\beta) \quad \text{e.g., } 045^\circ / 30^\circ$$ $$\text{Condition for Plunge:} \quad \beta > 0^\circ \quad (\text{Axis is not horizontal})$$ $$\text{True Dip of Limb } (\delta), \text{ Plunge } (\beta), \text{ Angle between Strike and Axis } (\alpha): \quad \sin \beta = \sin \delta \cdot \sin \alpha$$

When $\beta = 90^\circ$, the fold axis is vertical and the fold is classified as a vertical fold.

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q030
The fold is said to have plunge if :
(A) The axis is horizontal
(B) The axis is not horizontal
(C) It is asymmetrical
(D) It is tightly folded
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (B) The axis is not horizontal
Verified against Bangar (p. 113), Billings (Structural Geology, p. 53), and Fossen (Structural Geology, p. 238). A fold possesses a plunge if and only if its fold axis (hinge line) is inclined to the horizontal plane.
(A) The axis is horizontal INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: If the fold axis is horizontal, the angle of inclination with the horizontal is zero ($\text{plunge} = 0^\circ$). By definition, this represents a non-plunging fold. Its outcrop pattern consists of parallel strike bands that do not converge.
(B) The axis is not horizontal CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: Plunge is defined as the vertical angle between a linear geologic feature (the fold axis) and the horizontal plane. If the axis is not horizontal (i.e., $\text{plunge} > 0^\circ$), the fold is by definition a plunging fold. On geological maps, this tilt forces the outcrops to wrap around the hinge line, generating diagnostic converging nose closures.
(C) It is asymmetrical INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Asymmetry describes the inequality of limb dips or the inclination of the axial plane relative to the bisector of the interlimb angle. A fold can be asymmetrical while having a completely horizontal axis (an asymmetrical non-plunging fold), or symmetrical while plunging steeply.
(D) It is tightly folded INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Tightly folded refers strictly to the interlimb angle ($0^\circ < \theta \le 30^\circ$ under Fleuty's scheme). Tightness describes the degree of buckling and strain, completely independent of whether the fold axis is horizontal, inclined, or vertical.

8 Fully Worked Exam-Standard Practice Problems: Fold Plunge & Attitude Analysis
Problem 1: Apparent Dip & Plunge Relationship
Plunge $\beta = 25.66^\circ$
A fold axis lies in an axial plane dipping $40^\circ$. The angle between the fold axis and the strike of the axial plane (rake/pitch) is $\alpha = 45^\circ$. Calculate the true plunge of the fold axis $\beta$.
$$\sin \beta = \sin \delta \cdot \sin \alpha = \sin(40^\circ) \cdot \sin(45^\circ) = 0.6428 \cdot 0.7071 = 0.4545$$ $$\beta = \arcsin(0.4545) = 27.03^\circ$$
Problem 2: Reading Outcrop Noses on a Geological Map
Plunging anticline plunging towards the North
On a map, an outcrop pattern of folded sandstone forms a V-shaped nose pointing due North. Stratigraphic logs confirm that the oldest shale unit is located within the interior of the V. Because the nose of an anticline points in the direction of plunge, the fold is an anticline plunging North ($000^\circ$).
Problem 3: Reclined Fold Attitude
Plunge of axis equals the dip of the axial plane
A reclined fold is defined as a plunging fold where the pitch (rake) of the fold axis on the axial plane is $90^\circ$. Consequently, the trend of the plunge is parallel to the dip direction of the axial plane, and the plunge angle equals the axial plane dip ($\beta = \delta$).
Problem 4: Stereographic Projection of Fold Axis
Intersection point of two limb great circles
On a Schmidt or Wulff stereonet, when the great circles representing the two planar limbs of a cylindrical fold are plotted, their mutual point of intersection directly defines the plunge and trend of the fold axis ($\beta$-axis).
Problem 5: Culmination and Depression in Plunging Folds
Reversal of plunge direction along fold axis
When a fold axis does not maintain a constant plunge but undulates along its strike, the crestal high point where plunge diverges in opposite directions is called a culmination (dome-like), while the low point where plunges converge is called a depression (saddle/basin).
Problem 6: Pi ($\pi$) Diagram for Hinge Determination
Normal to the great circle containing poles to bedding
In a $\pi$-diagram, poles to folded bedding surfaces form a great circle girdle (the $\pi$-girdle). The pole to this girdle (the $\pi$-axis) corresponds precisely to the cylindrical fold axis.
Problem 7: Zig-zag Outcrop Patterns in Repeated Plunging Folds
Alternating plunging anticlines and synclines
A sequence of plunging anticlines and synclines dissected by a horizontal erosional plain produces a classic repeating zig-zag or S-shaped sinuous outcrop pattern, prominent in the Appalachian Valley and Ridge Province.
Problem 8: Vertical Fold Classification
Plunge $\beta = 90^\circ$; axial plane dip $\delta = 90^\circ$
Under Fleuty's classification, a fold whose axis plunges at $90^\circ$ (vertical) within a vertical axial plane is termed a vertical fold. In map view, the hinge is a single point and both limbs appear as parallel vertical walls.
