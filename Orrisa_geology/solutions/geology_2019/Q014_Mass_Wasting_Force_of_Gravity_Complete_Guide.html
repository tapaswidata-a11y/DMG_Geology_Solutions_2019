Mechanics of Slope Stability & Gravitational Driving Forces

Mass wasting (or mass movement) is the comprehensive geological term defining the downslope movement of rock, regolith, soil, or debris under the direct driving influence of the force of gravity, without requiring the dynamic kinetic transport of a fluid medium (such as running water, glacial ice, or wind) to carry the particles in suspension or traction.

"Mass movement or mass wasting is the term applied to the movement of rock fragments and soil down a slope under the direct influence of gravity. It includes movements of all kinds—from the slow, imperceptible downhill creep of soil to catastrophic rockfalls and landslides. Gravity is the fundamental driving force behind all mass wasting." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Chapter 10: Mass Movements, p. 377)

The mechanics of slope stability under gravity are formalized via the Limit Equilibrium Principle and the Mohr-Coulomb Failure Criterion:

Gravitational Stress Resolution on an Inclined Slope (Terzaghi, 1943)
$$W = m \cdot g = \gamma \cdot V \quad (\text{Total Weight of Sliding Mass})$$ $$\text{Driving Shear Force } T = W \cdot \sin \alpha$$ $$\text{Normal Force } N = W \cdot \cos \alpha$$ $$\text{Factor of Safety } FS = \frac{\text{Resisting Shear Strength } R}{\text{Driving Shear Force } T} = \frac{c' \cdot A + (W \cos \alpha - u \cdot A) \tan \phi'}{W \sin \alpha}$$

Where $\alpha$ is slope inclination, $c'$ is effective cohesion, $\phi'$ is effective internal friction angle, $u$ is pore water pressure, and $A$ is failure plane area. Slope failure occurs when gravity causes $FS < 1.0$.

Official Question Statement — OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 Paper-II (Question 14)
14. Mass wasting is primarily affected by :
(A) Force of gravity  ✔
(B) Intensity of oxidation
(C) Intensity of carbonization
(D) Strength of streams
✔
Correct Answer: (A) Force of gravity
In engineering geology and geomorphology (K.M. Bangar, p. 377; Arthur Holmes, p. 347; Varnes, 1978; Bloom, p. 165), mass wasting is explicitly and fundamentally defined as the downslope movement of slope-forming materials under the direct driving influence of the force of gravity.
(A) Force of gravity Correct Verified Option
Why this option is correct: The force of gravity is the primary, indispensable physical engine driving all mass wasting. Gravitational acceleration ($g$) acts upon the mass of rock and soil, resolving into a downslope driving shear component ($W \sin \alpha$). Whenever this driving stress exceeds the resisting shear strength of the slope, mass wasting occurs.
(B) Intensity of oxidation Incorrect Distractor
Why this distractor is incorrect: Oxidation is a chemical weathering reaction (e.g., $4\text{Fe}^{2+} + 3\text{O}_2 \to 2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$) that alters iron-bearing silicates into rust-colored oxides. While chemical weathering can gradually weaken rock strength over millenia, it does not provide the dynamic physical driving force for mass displacement.
(C) Intensity of carbonization Incorrect Distractor
Why this distractor is incorrect: Carbonization (or coalification / carbonation) refers either to the geochemical concentration of carbon in organic matter forming coal, or the chemical reaction of dissolved carbon dioxide with minerals. It is not the driving physical agent of downslope mass movement.
(D) Strength of streams Incorrect Distractor
Why this distractor is incorrect: The strength of streams governs fluvial erosion and transport, where sediment particles are entrained within moving water. Mass wasting is fundamentally demarcated from fluvial erosion precisely because mass wasting moves under direct gravitational body force without being entrained as sediment load in a river.

Triggering Mechanisms vs. Primary Driving Force in Mass Wasting

It is vital to distinguish between the singular driving force (Gravity) and the transient triggering mechanisms that precipitate failure:

  • Rainfall & Pore Water Pressure ($u$): Water acts by reducing effective normal stress ($\sigma' = \sigma - u$) and lubricating shear planes; it does not replace gravity as the driving force.
  • Seismic Ground Acceleration (Earthquakes): Dynamic seismic shear waves ($k_h W$) temporarily augment gravitational driving shear stress.
  • Toe Undercutting (River or Road Excavation): Removes kinematic support at the base of the slope, leaving the upper block unsupported against gravitational collapse.
High-Yield Practice Questions & Worked Derivations (8 Problems)
  • Practice Q1 (Numerical): A planar rock block weighing $W = 600\text{ kN}$ rests on a joint plane inclined at $\alpha = 35^\circ$. The basal contact area is $A = 25\text{ m}^2$. The joint plane has an effective cohesion $c' = 12\text{ kPa}$ and an internal friction angle $\phi' = 30^\circ$. If water pressure is zero, calculate the Factor of Safety ($FS$) against sliding.
    ✔ Worked Answer: $FS = 1.69$ (Stable Slope).
    Derivation:
    Driving force $T = W \sin \alpha = 600 \sin 35^\circ = 600(0.5736) = 344.15\text{ kN}$.
    Normal force $N = W \cos \alpha = 600 \cos 35^\circ = 600(0.8192) = 491.49\text{ kN}$.
    Resisting shear force $R = c' A + N \tan \phi'$:
    $$R = (12\text{ kPa} \times 25\text{ m}^2) + 491.49 \tan 30^\circ = 300 + 491.49(0.5774) = 300 + 283.77 = 583.77\text{ kN}$$
    $$FS = \frac{R}{T} = \frac{583.77}{344.15} \approx 1.696$$
  • Practice Q2: What happens to the Factor of Safety ($FS$) of the slope in Q1 if heavy monsoon rains generate a pore water pressure $u = 15\text{ kPa}$ along the joint plane?
    ✔ Worked Answer: $FS = 1.07$ (Marginal Stability / Imminent Failure).
    Derivation:
    Total pore water force $U = u \times A = 15\text{ kPa} \times 25\text{ m}^2 = 375\text{ kN}$.
    Effective normal force $N' = N - U = 491.49 - 375 = 116.49\text{ kN}$.
    New resisting force $R' = 300 + 116.49(0.5774) = 300 + 67.26 = 367.26\text{ kN}$.
    $$FS = \frac{367.26}{344.15} \approx 1.067$$
  • Practice Q3: For a completely dry, cohesionless sand slope ($c'=0, u=0$), what is the maximum stable slope angle?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The angle of internal friction of the sand ($lpha_{ ext{max}} = \phi'$), termed the angle of repose.
    Explanation: When $c'=0$, $FS = rac{W \cos lpha an \phi'}{W \sin lpha} = rac{ an \phi'}{ an lpha}$. For $FS \ge 1.0$, $ an lpha \le an \phi' \implies lpha \le \phi'$.
  • Practice Q4: In what type of mass movement does the displaced block rotate backward toward the head scarp?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Rotational slide (Slump).
    Explanation: In a rotational slump, the failure surface is curved concave-upward (spoon-shaped). As the mass slides, the center of gravity rotates, tilting trees and bedding backward into the hill.
  • Practice Q5: What is "solifluction", and in what climatic zone is it the dominant mass wasting agent?
    ✔ Correct Answer: The slow downslope flow of water-saturated soil over an impermeable permafrost layer, dominant in periglacial/subarctic regions.
    Explanation: During summer, the active layer thaws into a saturated slurry that cannot drain downward through the frozen permafrost, flowing slowly downslope under gravity.
  • Practice Q6: What field evidence diagnoses active "soil creep" on a hillside?
    ✔ Correct Answer: J-shaped (pistol-butted) tree trunks, tilted utility poles, displaced fences, and terracettes.
    Explanation: Because surface soil creeps faster than subsoil, tree roots move slower than trunks, forcing trees to curve vertically toward sunlight as they grow.
  • Practice Q7: In the 1963 catastrophic Vajont Dam disaster in Italy, what form of mass wasting occurred?
    ✔ Correct Answer: A catastrophic translational rock block slide along clay interbeds, mobilizing 270 million cubic meters of rock into the reservoir.
    Explanation: Reservoir filling raised pore water pressure along bedding planes that dipped directly toward the valley, triggering instantaneous planar failure under gravity.
  • Practice Q8: What is the most cost-effective engineering stabilization measure for preventing rain-induced mass wasting?
    ✔ Correct Answer: Subsurface drainage (horizontal drain holes and French drains) to reduce pore water pressure ($u$).
    Explanation: Dewatering restores effective normal stress ($\sigma' = \sigma - u$) and maximizes frictional shear resistance without expensive retaining walls.
