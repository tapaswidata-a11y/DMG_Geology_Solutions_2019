Precambrian Orogenesis: Aravalli-Delhi Belt & Erinpura Granite Plutonism

The Aravalli-Delhi Orogenic Belt in northwestern India (Rajasthan and northern Gujarat) preserves a multi-phase Proterozoic Wilson Cycle extending over 700 km from Delhi to Palanpur. The youngest structural supergroup in this fold belt is the Delhi Supergroup (Mesoproterozoic, ~1.7 - 1.0 Ga), which underwent polyphase deformation, regional metamorphism, and was syn- to post-tectonically intruded by the massive Erinpura Granite (~850 - 800 Ma).

  • Tectonostratigraphic Succession of Rajasthan:
    1. Banded Gneissic Complex (BGC / Mewar Gneiss; ~3.3 - 2.8 Ga): Archean basement of tonalite-trondhjemite-granite gneisses, amphibolites, and granulites, unconformably overlain by Proterozoic basins.
    2. Aravalli Supergroup (Paleoproterozoic; ~2.2 - 1.8 Ga): Rifting and passive margin shelf sequence (basal volcanics, Raialo dolomites and phosphorites at Jhamarkotra, phyllites, and deep-water flysch).
    3. Delhi Supergroup (Mesoproterozoic; ~1.7 - 1.0 Ga):
      • Alwar Group: Lower, arenaceous-dominated sequence of thick quartz arenites, arkoses, and conglomerates deposited in shallow-marine coastal to graben environments.
      • Ajabgarh Group: Upper, deeper-water argillaceous-calcareous sequence of mica schists, calc-silicates, marbles (e.g., Makrana marble), and carbonaceous phyllites.
    4. Erinpura Granite (~850 - 800 Ma): A monumental suite of late- to post-tectonic, S-type to I-type granites and granite-gneisses named after the town of Erinpura (Sirohi district). The plutons (including the Abu batholith, Sirohi, and Sendra granites) cross-cut the folded Delhi metasediments, marking the terminal Neoproterozoic tectonothermal stabilization of the Delhi Orogen.
    5. Post-Delhi Volcanics: The younger Malani Igneous Suite (~750 Ma) represents anorogenic felsic volcanism (rhyolites and peralkaline granites) that erupted unconformably over the eroded Erinpura and Delhi floor in western Rajasthan.
"Delhi Supergroup & Erinpura Granite: The Delhi rocks were subjected to intense folding and regional metamorphism. After the folding of the Delhi System was completed, the whole area was intruded on an extensive scale by granitic rocks known as the Erinpura Granite. It forms the prominent hills of Mount Abu and Erinpura. It is intrusive into the Delhi Supergroup and represents the last major plutonic event in the Aravalli mountain belt." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (The Delhi System, pp. 280-281)

OPSC Assistant Geologist 2019 • Paper-II • Question 51
Dalhi system is intruded by:
(A) Erinpura Granite
(B) Malani Rhyolite
(C) Singhbhum Granite
(D) Bundelkhand Granite
✔ OFFICIALLY VERIFIED ANSWER: (A) Erinpura Granite
(A) Erinpura Granite CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: The Delhi Supergroup is extensively intruded by the Erinpura Granite (~850 - 800 Ma). These late-to-post-kinematic granites (including the Mount Abu batholith and Sendra complex) produce prominent contact metamorphic aureoles and truncate the regional structural fold axes of the Delhi rocks.
(B) Malani Rhyolite INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Malani Igneous Suite (~750 Ma) consists of extrusive volcanic flows (felsic rhyolites and ash-flow tuffs) that rest unconformably over the eroded, beveled surface of the folded Delhi rocks and Erinpura granite. They are extrusive cover rocks, not an intrusive plutonic system into the Delhi sequence.
(C) Singhbhum Granite INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Singhbhum Granite (~3.1 - 3.4 Ga) is an Archean composite batholith restricted geographically to the Singhbhum Craton in Jharkhand and Odisha, completely unrelated to the Delhi belt of Rajasthan.
(D) Bundelkhand Granite INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The Bundelkhand Granite Complex (~2.5 - 2.6 Ga) is an Archean cratonic basement block in central India (Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh) characterized by quartz reefs and dolerite dykes, formed prior to the Proterozoic Delhi System.

8 Fully Worked Practice Problems: Proterozoic Magmatism & Rajasthan Stratigraphy
Problem 1: Mount Abu Batholith Composition
Porphyritic microcline-bearing granite belonging to the Erinpura Suite
Mount Abu, the highest peak of the Aravalli Range (Guru Shikhar: 1,722 m), is a massive monolithic horst carved entirely out of the Neoproterozoic Erinpura Granite batholith.
Problem 2: Major Mineral Deposit Formed by Post-Delhi Magmatism
Tungsten (Wolframite) mineralization at Degana (Nagaur district)
High-level granite intrusions related to the terminal Delhi magmatic phase generated greisenized quartz-vein networks hosting India's premier wolframite (tungsten) deposits at Degana.
Problem 3: Distinction Between Alwar and Ajabgarh Groups
Alwar is coarse arenaceous (sandstones/quartzites); Ajabgarh is fine argillaceous and calcareous (schists, marbles)
Alwar group quartzites form the rugged, cliff-forming ridges of northeastern Rajasthan, while Ajabgarh rocks occupy valleys and host the marble belts (Makrana, Rajnagar).
Problem 4: Sendra Granite Tectonic Setting
Syn- to late-tectonic sheet intrusions along the western margin of the South Delhi Fold Belt
Sendra granites (~960-850 Ma) intrude the Barotia and Sendra sequences of the Delhi Supergroup, displaying deformational gneissic foliation at margins.
Problem 5: Great Boundary Fault (GBF) of Rajasthan
Regional reverse fault separating folded Aravalli/Berach rocks from flat-lying Vindhyan strata
The GBF trends NE-SW for over 400 km, acting as the structural boundary that juxtaposes deformed Pre-Vindhyan rocks against the unmetamorphosed Bhander and Kaimur groups.
Problem 6: Age of the Malani Igneous Suite
Neoproterozoic (~750 - 770 Ma)
The Malani suite represents one of the largest felsic anorogenic volcanic provinces in the world (~50,000 km²), younger than and unconformable upon the Erinpura Granite.
Problem 7: Khetri Copper Belt Host Rocks
Ajabgarh Group metasediments of the Delhi Supergroup
The world-famous Khetri Copper Belt (Kolihan, Madhan-Kudhan) is hosted in garnet-chlorite schists, phyllites, and amphibolites of the Ajabgarh Group, mineralized by syn-tectonic hydrothermal fluids.
Problem 8: S-type Character of Certain Erinpura Granites
Presence of primary muscovite, garnet, high normative corundum (>1%), and high initial ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr
These geochemical markers indicate that substantial portions of the Erinpura magmas originated via partial melting (anatexis) of pelitic metasediments of the Delhi Supergroup during crustal thickening.
