1. Theoretical Foundation: Carbonatite Petrogenesis & Geochemistry

Carbonatites are unique, mantle-derived igneous rocks defined by the International Union of Geological Sciences (IUGS) as containing more than 50% modal primary carbonate minerals (calcite, dolomite, ankerite, and siderite) with less than 20% $SiO_2$.

Classification Dominant Carbonate Mineral Chemical Designation Associated Silicate Rocks & Fenitization
Calcio-carbonatite (Sövite / Alvikite) Calcite ($CaCO_3$) Rich in $CaO$; Sövite is coarse plutonic, Alvikite is hypabyssal/volcanic. Surrounded by ijolite, nephelinite, and extensive fenitization (alkali metasomatic halo).
Magnesio-carbonatite (Beforsite) Dolomite ($CaMg(CO_3)_2$) Rich in $MgO$; often forms medium- to fine-grained dykes and cone sheets. Enriched in high-field strength elements (HFSE) and LREEs.
Ferro-carbonatite Ankerite / Siderite ($FeCO_3$) Rich in $FeO + Fe_2O_3 + MnO$; late-stage low-temperature magmatic differentiates. Highest concentration of Rare Earth Elements (monazite, bastnäsite).
Natro-carbonatite Nyerereite / Gregoryite ($(Na,K)_2Ca(CO_3)_2$) Extremely rich in $Na_2O$ and $K_2O$; active lava at Oldoinyo Lengai, Tanzania. Water-soluble, rapidly altering to hydrous carbonates at surface conditions.
"Carbonatites are igneous rocks containing more than 50 percent by volume of carbonate minerals. Although rare, carbonatites are of immense economic importance because they host the world's richest deposits of Rare Earth Elements (REEs: La, Ce, Pr, Nd), Niobium (in pyrochlore), Tantalum, Phosphate (in apatite), and Fluorite. The Amba Dongar complex in Gujarat is India's most famous carbonatite-alkaline ring complex."
— Keith Bell, Carbonatites: Genesis and Evolution (Unwin Hyman)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 79
A magmatic rock rich in calcium carbonate and often containing rare earth minerals is :
A Alkali basalt
B Syenite porphyry
C Carbonatite
D Granodiorite
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION C (Carbonatite)
OPTION C • CORRECT
Carbonatite

Carbonatite is an igneous (magmatic) rock composed of greater than 50% carbonate minerals, predominantly calcium carbonate (calcite in sövite). It is world-renowned as the primary geological host for economic concentrations of Rare Earth Elements (bastnäsite, monazite) and niobium (pyrochlore).

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Alkali basalt

Alkali basalt is a silicate volcanic rock composed of calcic plagioclase, titanaugite, and olivine with normative nepheline. It does not contain primary calcium carbonate minerals.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Syenite porphyry

Syenite porphyry is an intermediate felsic silicate rock dominated by alkali feldspar phenocrysts. While often associated with carbonatite ring complexes, it consists of aluminosilicates, not calcium carbonate.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
Granodiorite

Granodiorite is a common acid plutonic silicate rock consisting of quartz, plagioclase (oligoclase/andesine), K-feldspar, and biotite/hornblende. It has negligible carbonate content.

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. Which active volcano in the East African Rift erupts natrocarbonatite lava at approximately $500\text{--}600^\circ\text{C}$?
Answer: Oldoinyo Lengai (Tanzania)
Oldoinyo Lengai is the only active volcano on Earth erupting sodium-potassium carbonate lavas (nyerereite and gregoryite).
2. What is the coarse-grained, plutonic variety of calcite carbonatite called?
Answer: Sövite
Coarse-grained plutonic calcite-carbonatite is termed sövite; its fine-grained hypabyssal or volcanic equivalent is alvikite.
3. What is the process of intensive alkali metasomatism of wall rocks surrounding a carbonatite called?
Answer: Fenitization
Fenitization involves pervasive infiltration of hyper-alkaline fluids ($Na, K, Fe^{3+}$), transforming granitic country rocks into fenites rich in aegirine and alkali feldspar.
4. Where is India's most prominent carbonatite complex located, renowned for fluorite and REE mineralization?
Answer: Amba Dongar (Chhota Udepur district, Gujarat)
Amba Dongar is a Cretaceous-Palaeocene ring complex related to Deccan plume magmatism, hosting India's largest fluorite deposit and rich REE reserves.
5. What primary niobium-bearing ore mineral is extracted almost exclusively from carbonatite complexes?
Answer: Pyrochlore [$(Na,Ca)_2Nb_2O_6(OH,F)$]
Over 90% of global niobium production (e.g., Araxá in Brazil) is mined from weathered pyrochlore-bearing carbonatites.
6. How does liquid immiscibility contribute to the origin of carbonatitic magmas?
Answer: A parent carbonated nephelinite/melilitite melt unmixes into two immiscible liquids: a silicate melt and a carbonate melt.
Experimental petrology shows that at crustal pressures, $CO_2$-saturated alkaline silicate melts split into conjugate silicate and carbonate fractions.
7. What is a dolomite-dominated carbonatite termed in classical petrography?
Answer: Beforsite
Beforsite is the magnesio-carbonatite variety, consisting primarily of magmatic dolomite with minor apatite and phlogopite.
8. Which world-class REE deposit in China is hosted within Proterozoic carbonatite dykes?
Answer: Bayan Obo (Inner Mongolia, China)
Bayan Obo is the world's largest known REE deposit, containing massive reserves of bastnäsite and monazite associated with dolomitic carbonatites.
