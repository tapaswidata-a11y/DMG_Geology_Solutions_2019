Paleogeography & Continental Drift: The Breakup of Pangea

The theory of Continental Drift, pioneered by Alfred Wegener (1912, 1915) and subsequently synthesized into modern Plate Tectonics by Arthur Holmes, Harry Hess, and J. Tuzo Wilson, demonstrated that the Earth's present continents were formerly assembled into a single late Paleozoic supercontinent: Pangea (from Greek, meaning "All-Earth").

  • Supercontinental Rifting: During the Late Triassic to Early Jurassic ($pprox 200-180\text{ Ma}$), Pangea began to rift and separate along an east-west trending equatorial seaway known as the Tethys Ocean.
  • The Two Mega-Continents: This rifting event split Pangea into two colossal continental landmasses:
    • Laurasia (Northern Supercontinent): Comprising present-day North America (Laurentia), Greenland, Europe, and Asia (excluding the Indian subcontinent and Arabia).
    • Gondwanaland (Southern Supercontinent): Named by Eduard Suess after the ancient Gond kingdom of central India, comprising present-day South America, Africa, Madagascar, the Indian Peninsula, Australia, and Antarctica.
  • Panthalassa: The vast, singular global ocean that surrounded Pangea was termed Panthalassa (meaning "All-Sea"), the ancestral precursor to the modern Pacific Ocean.
"According to the Continental Drift Theory, in the Carboniferous period all the landmasses were united into one huge supercontinent called Pangea, surrounded by a universal ocean called Panthalassa. Later on, Pangea broke up into two super-continental blocks: Laurasia in the north and Gondwanaland in the south, separated by the east-west trending Tethys Sea." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Plate Tectonics & Continental Drift, p. 28) & Van der Pluijm & Marshak, p. 580
Supercontinental Kinematic Partitioning Formula
$$\text{Pangea (Late Paleozoic)} \xrightarrow[\approx 200\text{ Ma}]{\text{Tethyan Rifting}} \mathbf{\text{Laurasia (North)}} + \mathbf{\text{Gondwanaland (South)}}$$ $$\text{Laurasia} = \text{North America} + \text{Eurasia (excluding India & Arabia)} + \text{Greenland}$$ $$\text{Gondwanaland} = \text{South America} + \text{Africa} + \text{Madagascar} + \text{India} + \text{Antarctica} + \text{Australia}$$

Gondwanaland is characterized by a distinctive Permo-Carboniferous stratigraphic succession: Talchir boulder beds (glacial), Glossopteris flora, and thick coal seams.

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q031
The two super continental blocks separated due to drifting are :
(A) Pangea and Laurasia
(B) Pangea and Gondwanaland
(C) Laurasia and Gondwanaland
(D) Laurasia and Panthalassa
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (C) Laurasia and Gondwanaland
Verified against Bangar (p. 28), Van der Pluijm & Marshak (Earth Structure, p. 580), and Tarbuck & Lutgens (Earth Science). When the ancestral supercontinent Pangea broke apart in the early Mesozoic, it split into two daughter supercontinental blocks: northern Laurasia and southern Gondwanaland.
(A) Pangea and Laurasia INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Pangea was the original, unified supercontinent before rifting took place. Laurasia was not a coexisting entity that drifted away from Pangea; rather, Laurasia was one of the two pieces resulting from the breakup of Pangea.
(B) Pangea and Gondwanaland INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Similar to distractor (A), Gondwanaland was the southern daughter fragment derived from Pangea. Pangea ceased to exist once the breakup was initiated; the two supercontinental blocks that drifted apart from each other were Laurasia and Gondwanaland.
(C) Laurasia and Gondwanaland CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: During the Triassic-Jurassic rifting phase of Pangea, the opening of the east-west Tethys seaway bifurcated the global landmass into two vast supercontinents: Laurasia in the north (North America, Europe, Asia) and Gondwanaland in the south (South America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica). These two giant continental blocks drifted independently until their eventual collisions in the Cenozoic (e.g., India colliding into Eurasia to form the Himalayas).
(D) Laurasia and Panthalassa INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: Panthalassa was the universal ancestral ocean, not a continental block. The question specifically inquires about the two super continental blocks that separated due to continental drift.

8 Fully Worked Exam-Standard Practice Problems: Paleogeography & Continental Drift
Problem 1: Identification of Gondwana Continental Fragments
India, Australia, Antarctica, Africa, South America, Madagascar
Which modern landmasses constituted Gondwanaland? All southern hemisphere continents plus peninsular India and the Arabian Peninsula formed Gondwana, linked by shared Dwyka/Talchir glacial tillites and *Glossopteris* flora.
Problem 2: The Tethys Ocean's Role in Plate Tectonics
Separated Laurasia from Gondwana; closed during Indo-Eurasian collision
The Neotethys Ocean opened as the Cimmerian blocks rifted from northern Gondwana during the Permo-Triassic, and eventually closed as India moved northward, with marine Tethyan sediments uplifted into the Tibetan Plateau and Tethyan Himalayas.
Problem 3: Paleomagnetic Apparent Polar Wander Paths (APWP)
Divergent APWP curves prove continental drift rather than true polar wander
When magnetic remanence from rocks of identical age in North America and Europe was measured, their polar wander paths diverged. When the Atlantic Ocean was closed mathematically, the two curves coincided, providing quantitative proof of drift.
Problem 4: Glossopteris Flora Distribution
Tongue-shaped leaves of seed-ferns restricted to Gondwanaland
The distinctive seed-fern *Glossopteris* occurs across Permian coal basins in India (Damodar), Australia, South Africa (Karoo), Antarctica, and South America, proving these lands were contiguous and shared a subpolar climate.
Problem 5: Eduard Suess & the Naming of Gondwanaland
Named after the Gond kingdom in central India (Madhya Pradesh)
Austrian geologist Eduard Suess coined the name "Gondwana-Land" in 1885, derived from the Gond kingdom of central India where the Gondwana sedimentary rock system was originally surveyed by H.B. Medlicott (1873).
Problem 6: Drift Velocity of the Indian Plate
Peak velocity $\approx 15-20\text{ cm/year}$ during Late Cretaceous
Following the breakup of eastern Gondwana, the Indian plate detached from Antarctica and Madagascar and accelerated across the Indian Ocean at extraordinary rates exceeding $15-20\text{ cm/yr}$, driven by slab pull and the Reunion mantle plume head.
Problem 7: Origin of the Panthalassic Ocean Floor
Ancestral Pacific Ocean basin
Panthalassa occupied over $70\%$ of the Earth's surface during the Permian. As Pangea rifted and the Atlantic and Indian Oceans opened, Panthalassa shrank, leaving the Pacific Ocean basin as its surviving relic.
Problem 8: Wilson Cycle Duration
Supercontinent assembly and dispersal cycle takes $\approx 400 - 500\text{ Ma}$
The Wilson Cycle describes the cyclical opening and closing of ocean basins (embryonic rift $\to$ mature ocean $\to$ subduction $\to$ collision), requiring roughly $400-500$ million years to transition from one supercontinent to the next.
