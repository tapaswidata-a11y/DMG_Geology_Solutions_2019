Paleomagnetism & Geotectonics: Geomagnetic Polarity Reversals & Seafloor Striping

Paleomagnetism is the study of the Earth's ancient magnetic field preserved in ferromagnetic minerals (chiefly titanomagnetite and hematite) within igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic rocks. When basaltic lava cools below the Curie temperature of magnetite ($T_C \approx 580^\circ\text{C}$), its magnetic domains lock in the exact direction and intensity of the ambient geomagnetic field at that instant, acquiring Thermo-Remanent Magnetization (TRM).

  • Discovery of Geomagnetic Field Reversals: In 1906, French physicist Bernard Brunhes discovered volcanic rocks whose magnetization pointed opposite to the present-day magnetic field. In 1929, Japanese geophysicist Motonori Matuyama confirmed that the Earth's magnetic dipole has spontaneously flipped its polarity countless times throughout geological history:
    • Normal Polarity: Magnetic field vectors point north (as in the present day).
    • Reversed Polarity: Magnetic field vectors point south (a compass needle would point to the South Pole).
  • Geomagnetic Polarity Timescale (GPTS): By radiometric K-Ar dating of terrestrial basalt lava flows, geophysicists established the standard polarity epochs (chrons):
    1. Brunhes Normal Chron: $0 - 0.78\text{ Ma}$ (Present day).
    2. Matuyama Reversed Chron: $0.78 - 2.58\text{ Ma}$ (Includes the Jaramillo and Olduvai normal subchrons).
    3. Gauss Normal Chron: $2.58 - 3.58\text{ Ma}$.
    4. Gilbert Reversed Chron: $3.58 - 5.33\text{ Ma}$.
  • The Vine-Matthews-Morley Hypothesis (1963): Fred Vine, Drummond Matthews, and Lawrence Morley combined geomagnetic pole reversals with Harry Hess's seafloor spreading concept. As new oceanic crust forms at mid-ocean ridges, it cools and records the prevailing magnetic polarity. Periodic pole reversals create symmetrical alternating positive and negative magnetic anomaly stripes parallel to ridge crests, providing undeniable, quantitative empirical proof of seafloor spreading and plate tectonics.
"Palaeomagnetism is the study of the fossil magnetism retained by rocks. Rocks containing magnetic minerals record the direction of the earth's magnetic field at the time of their formation. Studies of magnetized basalt lavas revealed that the earth's magnetic field has reversed its polarity repeatedly in the past. Thus, palaeomagnetic data is primarily useful for understanding magnetic pole reversals and continental drift." — K.M. Bangar, Principles of Engineering Geology (Interior of Earth & Plate Tectonics, p. 28) & Van der Pluijm & Marshak, p. 445
Paleomagnetic Dipole Formula & Seafloor Spreading Rate
$$\tan I = 2 \tan \lambda \quad (\text{Geocentric Axial Dipole Equation: Magnetic Inclination } I \text{ vs. Paleolatitude } \lambda)$$ $$v_{\text{spreading}} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \quad (\text{Distance to magnetic stripe } \Delta x \text{ divided by reversal age } \Delta t)$$ $$B_{\text{total}} = B_{\text{core}} + B_{\text{crustal anomaly}} \implies \Delta B = \pm 200 - 1000\text{ nT}$$

Positive anomalies occur where remnant crustal magnetization reinforces the present normal field; negative anomalies occur where reversed remnant magnetization cancels part of the present field.

Official Examination Question · OPSC 2019 Paper-II (GGM - 2/2019) · Q042
Palaeomagnetic data is useful for understanding :
(A) Pole reversals
(B) Composition of mantle
(C) Internal structure of the earth
(D) Origin of life on earth
✓
CORRECT OPTION: (A) Pole reversals
Verified against Bangar (p. 28), Van der Pluijm & Marshak (Earth Structure, p. 445), and Fowler (The Solid Earth, p. 43). Paleomagnetic records locked in cooled volcanic rocks and marine sediments provide direct, definitive empirical evidence of Earth's geomagnetic pole reversals and seafloor spreading.
(A) Pole reversals CORRECT VERIFIED
Forensic Analysis: The most revolutionary discovery produced by paleomagnetic data was the realization that Earth's magnetic field periodically undergoes complete geomagnetic polarity reversals (pole reversals), switching between normal and reversed polarity states. When mapped over the oceanic floor, these reversals produce symmetrical zebra-like magnetic anomaly stripes that proved Harry Hess's seafloor spreading theory and formed the bedrock of modern Plate Tectonics.
(B) Composition of mantle INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The composition of the mantle is determined through mantle xenolith petrology (peridotites in kimberlites and alkali basalts), ophiolite field complexes, high-pressure diamond anvil cell experiments, and cosmochemical meteorite comparisons (CI chondrites), not through paleomagnetism. Because the mantle is above the Curie point throughout most of its depth, it cannot retain permanent remanent paleomagnetism.
(C) Internal structure of the earth INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The internal concentric shell structure of the Earth (crust, mantle, outer core, inner core) was resolved through observational earthquake seismology (body wave travel-time curves, shadow zones, and seismic tomography), not paleomagnetism.
(D) Origin of life on earth INCORRECT
Forensic Analysis: The origin of life on Earth is investigated via organic geochemistry (carbon isotopic fractionation $\delta^{13} ext{C}$), paleobiology (Archean stromatolites and microfossils), prebiotic chemical synthesis experiments, and hydrothermal vent biochemistry, bearing zero connection to paleomagnetic measurements.

8 Fully Worked Exam-Standard Practice Problems: Paleomagnetism & Geotectonics
Problem 1: Paleolatitude from Magnetic Inclination
Paleolatitude $\lambda = 30.0^\circ\text{N}$
A Cretaceous basalt flow in peninsular India has a measured magnetic inclination of $I = 49.1^\circ$. Calculate the paleolatitude $\lambda$ of India when this basalt solidified.
$$\tan I = 2 \tan \lambda \implies \tan \lambda = \frac{\tan(49.1^\circ)}{2} = \frac{1.1544}{2} = 0.5772$$ $$\lambda = \arctan(0.5772) = 30.0^\circ$$
Problem 2: Seafloor Spreading Rate Calculation
Half spreading rate $v = 2.5\text{ cm/year}$; full opening rate $= 5.0\text{ cm/year}$
The Brunhes-Matuyama magnetic reversal boundary ($0.78\text{ Ma}$) is identified at a distance of $\Delta x = 19.5\text{ km}$ from the crest of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. Calculate the half spreading rate.
$$v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{19.5\text{ km}}{0.78\text{ Ma}} = \frac{19.5 \times 10^5\text{ cm}}{7.8 \times 10^5\text{ yr}} = 2.5\text{ cm/year}$$
Problem 3: The Cretaceous Long Normal Quiet Zone
37 million years without a single magnetic reversal ($121 - 84\text{ Ma}$)
During the Cretaceous Long Normal Superchron (Chron C34n), Earth's magnetic field remained continuously in normal polarity without flipping for 37 million years, creating broad smooth oceanic zones devoid of magnetic stripe anomalies.
Problem 4: Curie Temperature of Common Magnetic Minerals
Magnetite ($580^\circ\text{C}$), Hematite ($675^\circ\text{C}$), Pyrrhotite ($320^\circ\text{C}$)
Above the Curie temperature, thermal kinetic vibrations overcome quantum exchange coupling, converting ferromagnetic minerals into paramagnetic materials incapable of retaining remanent magnetization.
Problem 5: Apparent Polar Wander Path (APWP) Intersection
Convergence of APWPs proves the assembly of supercontinents
When APWP curves from two distinct continents coincide, it demonstrates mathematically that the two landmasses were joined and drifted as a single unified tectonic plate (e.g., Laurasia during the Permian).
Problem 6: Magnetic Inclination at Equator vs. Magnetic Poles
Inclination $I = 0^\circ$ at magnetic equator; $I = \pm 90^\circ$ at magnetic poles
Under the GAD equation ($\tan I = 2 \tan \lambda$): at the equator ($\lambda = 0$), $I = 0^\circ$ (horizontal field lines); at the poles ($\lambda = 90^\circ$), $I = 90^\circ$ (vertical field lines).
Problem 7: Duration of a Geomagnetic Reversal Event
Field flip transition takes approximately $2,000 - 7,000\text{ years}$
High-resolution sedimentary core records demonstrate that while polarity chrons last hundreds of thousands of years, the actual polarity reversal transition occurs very rapidly over $2,000$ to $7,000$ years, accompanied by a $90\%$ collapse in dipole field strength.
Problem 8: Thermal Demagnetization Procedure
Stepwise heating in zero-field oven to isolate primary Characteristic Remanent Magnetization (ChRM)
Paleomagnetic laboratories heat rock specimens incrementally (e.g., $100^\circ, 200^\circ, \dots, 580^\circ\text{C}$) inside mu-metal magnetic shields to unblock low-coercivity secondary viscous overprints and isolate the pristine primary ChRM vector.
