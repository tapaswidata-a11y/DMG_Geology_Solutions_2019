1. Theoretical Foundation: The Charnockite Series & Holland's Definition (1900)

The term Charnockite was coined in 1900 by Sir Thomas H. Holland, Director of the Geological Survey of India, to designate an orthopyroxene (hypersthene)-bearing quartzofeldspathic rock quarried from St. Thomas Mount in Chennai (formerly Madras) that had been used to construct the tombstone of Job Charnock, the historical founder of Kolkata.

"Charnockite is a hypersthene granite consisting essentially of quartz, microcline (perthite), plagioclase, and hypersthene, with or without iron ores and garnet. Its most striking physical character is an olive-green, greasy, or resinous waxy luster, imparted by microscopic carbonaceous and fluid inclusions in quartz and feldspar under high-pressure granulite facies metamorphism ($T = 750\text{--}900^\circ\text{C}, P = 5\text{--}10\text{ kbar}$, ultra-low $a_{H_2O}$)."
— Sir Thomas Holland, The Charnockite Series: A Group of Archaean Hypersthenic Rocks in Peninsular India (Memoirs of the GSI, Vol. XXVIII, Part 2, 1900)

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 80
Generally, a charnockite has a mineral composition of :
A Quartz-Hornblende-Oligoclase
B Quartz-Epidote-Andesine
C Quartz-Feldspar-Hypersthene
D Quartz-Feldspar-Augite
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION C (Quartz-Feldspar-Hypersthene)
OPTION C • CORRECT
Quartz-Feldspar-Hypersthene

By Holland's classical definition and international nomenclature, charnockite is an orthopyroxene granite composed essentially of Quartz, Feldspar (microperthitic K-feldspar and plagioclase), and Hypersthene. The presence of hypersthene (orthopyroxene) is the non-negotiable diagnostic signature that separates charnockite from all ordinary granites.

OPTION A • DISTRACTOR
Quartz-Hornblende-Oligoclase

This assemblage corresponds to a typical Tonalite or Diorite. It lacks the essential orthopyroxene (hypersthene) and K-feldspar.

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
Quartz-Epidote-Andesine

Epidote is a hydrous low- to medium-grade mineral diagnostic of greenschist to epidote-amphibolite facies. It is completely unstable in the dry, ultra-high-temperature granulite facies regime of charnockites.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
Quartz-Feldspar-Augite

Augite is a clinopyroxene. While clinopyroxene may occur as a minor subordinate accessory, the essential diagnostic ferromagnesian mineral of charnockite is strictly orthopyroxene (Hypersthene).

High-Yield Practice Problems (8 Exam-Standard Questions)
1. Who named the charnockite series and after whom was it named?
Answer: Sir Thomas H. Holland (1900), after Job Charnock.
Holland named the rock after Job Charnock, founder of Calcutta, whose tombstone in St. John's Churchyard was made of this rock brought from St. Thomas Mount, Madras.
2. What pleochroic colors are typically displayed by hypersthene in a charnockite thin section?
Answer: Pink / reddish-pink ($\alpha$) to yellowish-green ($\beta$) to green ($\gamma$)
Hypersthene displays intense and diagnostic pleochroism from salmon-pink to pale green under plane-polarized light.
3. What causes the characteristic waxy, greasy bluish-green luster of charnockites in hand specimens?
Answer: Submicroscopic fluid inclusions ($CO_2$-rich) and exsolved micro-needles of rutile/iron oxides in quartz and feldspar.
These submicroscopic inclusions scatter light, producing the famous greasy appearance and opalescent blue quartz.
4. What is the process called where pre-existing amphibolite-facies gneisses are transformed in-situ into patchy charnockite by flushing of $CO_2$ fluids?
Answer: Charnockitization (or 'Incipient Charnockite' formation)
Demonstrated famously at Kabbaldurga (Karnataka), where carbonic fluids lowered water activity ($a_{H_2O}$), breaking down hornblende/biotite to hypersthene.
5. Which tectonic mobile belt in Eastern India is world-renowned for extensive charnockite-khondalite massifs?
Answer: The Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt (EGMB)
The Eastern Ghats belt (extending through Odisha and Andhra Pradesh) represents a deeply eroded Proterozoic granulite terrain dominated by charnockites and khondalites.
6. How does enderbite differ mineralogically from charnockite sensu stricto?
Answer: Enderbite is plagioclase-dominant (tonalitic), while charnockite s.s. has K-feldspar $\ge$ plagioclase (granitic).
In the charnockite series, enderbite is the hypersthene-bearing tonalite/trondhjemite equivalent.
7. What is the metamorphic facies condition required to generate hypersthene in quartzofeldspathic rocks?
Answer: Granulite Facies
Orthopyroxene in felsic rocks is the defining index mineral of the granulite facies ($T > 750^\circ\text{C}$).
8. What accessory mineral is often present alongside hypersthene in garnetiferous charnockites?
Answer: Almandine-Pyrope Garnet
High-pressure granulites frequently contain almandine-rich garnet formed via the reaction: $Biotite + Sillimanite + Quartz \rightarrow Garnet + K-feldspar + Liquid$.
