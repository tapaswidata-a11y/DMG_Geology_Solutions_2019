1. Theoretical Foundation: Planck's Quantum Hypothesis and Photon Energy

In classical electrodynamics, James Clerk Maxwell modeled electromagnetic radiation as continuous transverse waves governed by Maxwell's equations. However, classical theory failed to explain the spectral distribution of blackbody radiation (leading to the catastrophic "ultraviolet catastrophe" predicted by the Rayleigh-Jeans law).

In 1900, Max Planck revolutionized modern physics by postulating that electromagnetic energy is not emitted or absorbed continuously, but in discrete, indivisible packets or packets of energy termed quanta (later called photons by Gilbert Lewis, 1926). Planck's fundamental equation states:

$$E = h \cdot \nu$$

where:

  • $E$ = Energy of a single quantum (photon), in Joules ($\text{J}$) or electron-volts ($\text{eV}$).
  • $h$ = Planck's constant ($h = 6.62607015 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s} = 4.135667 \times 10^{-15}\text{ eV}\cdot\text{s}$).
  • $\nu$ = Frequency of the electromagnetic radiation, in Hertz ($\text{Hz}$ or $\text{s}^{-1}$).

Since the frequency $\nu$ is related to wavelength $\lambda$ by the speed of light $c = \nu \lambda$, the energy quantum can equivalently be expressed in terms of wavelength:

$$E = \frac{h \cdot c}{\lambda}$$

2. Official Examination Question & Forensic Option Analysis
QUESTION 126
The value of energy quantum for radiation of any frequency is proportional to :
A The frequency
B The reciprocal of the energy
C The square of the frequency
D The square root of the frequency
CORRECT ANSWER: OPTION A (The frequency)
OPTION A • CORRECT
The frequency

By Planck's law ($E = h\nu$), the energy of an electromagnetic photon is directly proportional to its frequency $\nu$, with Planck's constant $h$ acting as the invariant constant of proportionality. Higher frequency radiation (such as UV or X-rays) carries proportionally higher energy per quantum than lower frequency radiation (such as infrared or radio waves).

OPTION B • DISTRACTOR
The reciprocal of the energy

Self-referential and physically meaningless. Energy cannot be proportional to its own reciprocal ($E \propto 1/E \implies E^2 = \text{const}$). The energy quantum is inversely proportional to wavelength ($E \propto 1/\lambda$), but not to the reciprocal of energy.

OPTION C • DISTRACTOR
The square of the frequency

Incorrect power law. Wave intensity or radiated power from an accelerating dipole scales with frequency squared or fourth power, but the fundamental quantum energy packet $E$ scales strictly linearly with $\nu^1$, not $\nu^2$.

OPTION D • DISTRACTOR
The square root of the frequency

Incorrect exponent. Energy depends on the first power of frequency ($\nu^1$), not the square root ($\nu^{1/2}$).

1. What is the energy in electron-volts ($\text{eV}$) of a green photon with a wavelength of $\lambda = 500\text{ nm}$?
Answer: Approximately $2.48\text{ eV}$.
$$E(\text{eV}) \approx \frac{1240}{\lambda(\text{nm})} = \frac{1240}{500} = 2.48\text{ eV}$$.
2. Why do optical satellite detectors need cooling when operating in the thermal infrared ($8\text{--}14\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) but not in the visible band?
Answer: Because thermal infrared photons have very low quantum energy ($\approx 0.1\text{ eV}$), ambient room-temperature thermal vibrations ($k_B T \approx 0.026\text{ eV}$) can excite noise electrons unless cooled to cryo-temperatures (~77 K).
Visible photons have high energy (~2-3 eV), far above ambient thermal excitation thresholds.
3. What is the value of Planck's constant in SI units ($\text{J}\cdot\text{s}$)?
Answer: $h = 6.62607015 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$ (an exact defined fundamental constant in SI since 2019).
It fixes the scale of quantum mechanical phenomena throughout the universe.
4. If the frequency of an electromagnetic wave is tripled, what happens to the energy of each of its photons?
Answer: The energy of each photon triples ($E_2 = 3 E_1$).
This follows directly from the linear proportionality $E \propto \nu$.
5. Which experimental discovery by Albert Einstein in 1905 provided definitive proof for Planck's quantum hypothesis?
Answer: The Photoelectric Effect (for which Einstein was awarded the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics).
Einstein demonstrated that electrons are ejected from a metal surface only if the incident photon frequency exceeds a threshold frequency ($\nu_0$), regardless of beam intensity.
6. How does quantum energy explain why microwaves cannot cause chemical ionization?
Answer: Microwave photon energies are tiny ($\sim 10^{-5}\text{ eV}$), millions of times lower than the energy required to break chemical bonds or ionize atoms ($\sim 1\text{--}10\text{ eV}$).
Microwaves only cause rotational thermal motion of dipolar water molecules.
7. What is the relationship between the energy of a quantum and its momentum ($p$)?
Answer: $p = \frac{E}{c} = \frac{h}{\lambda}$ (de Broglie / Einstein momentum relation).
Even though photons have zero rest mass, they carry momentum and exert radiation pressure on planetary bodies.
8. What is a "quantum detector" in satellite remote sensing?
Answer: A semiconductor sensor (photodiode or photoconductor) where individual absorbed photons directly excite electron-hole pairs across the bandgap.
Quantum detectors (e.g. Si, InGaAs, HgCdTe) offer ultra-fast response times and high sensitivity compared to thermal detectors (bolometers).
