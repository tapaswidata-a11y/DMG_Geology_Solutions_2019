Verified Answer Key

Correct Option: (b) Bathyal zone — In the classical marine bathymetric ichnofacies model established by Adolf Seilacher (1967), the Zoophycos ichnofacies is characteristic of low-energy, quiet-water continental slope settings corresponding to the bathyal depth zone (typically 200 m to 2000 m depth, below storm wave base), situated between the shallow shelf (Cruziana ichnofacies) and deep ocean abyss (Nereites ichnofacies).

1. Principles of Ichnology & Seilacherian Ichnofacies

An ichnofacies is an association of trace fossils that recurs through geological time under similar environmental, depositional, and bathymetric conditions. Pioneered by Adolf Seilacher (1967), trace fossil classification reflects organism behavior (ethology) in response to hydrodynamic energy, sedimentation rate, substrate consistency, food availability, and oxygenation.

The classic depth-related softground marine ichnofacies succession across a continental margin includes:

  1. Psilonichnus Ichnofacies: Supratidal and backshore dunes, beaches, and washover fans. Characterized by crab burrows (e.g., Psilonichnus) and root traces.
  2. Skolithos Ichnofacies (Littoral to shallow sublittoral): High-energy intertidal to sandy upper shoreface. Dominance of vertical, cylindrical dwelling burrows (Domichnia) constructed by suspension feeders (e.g., Skolithos, Ophiomorpha, Diplocraterion) to withstand shifting sands and wave agitation.
  3. Cruziana Ichnofacies (Sublittoral zone / Shelf): Moderate-to-low energy shelf below fair-weather wave base but above storm wave base. Diverse locomotion traces (Repichnia), resting traces (Cubichnia), and feeding burrows (Fodinichnia) of deposit and suspension feeders (e.g., Cruziana, Asteriacites, Rhizocorallium, Teichichnus).
  4. Zoophycos Ichnofacies (Bathyal zone / Continental Slope): Quiet, low-energy continental slope and rise environments with muddy substrates and dysaerobic (low oxygen) pore-water conditions. Complex planar to helical deposit-feeding structures (Fodinichnia), predominantly Zoophycos, Phycosiphon, and Spirophyton.
  5. Nereites Ichnofacies (Abyssal zone / Deep Ocean Plain): Deep abyssal plains and distal turbidite basins (>2000 m). Dominance of intricate, highly systematic grazing trails (Pascichnia) and farming burrows / graphoglyptids (Agrichnia) designed to maximize foraging efficiency in food-starved settings (e.g., Nereites, Paleodictyon, Spirorhaphe, Cosmorhaphe).

2. Ethology & Environmental Dynamics of Zoophycos

Zoophycos consists of an elaborate, three-dimensional spiral or planar spreite structure radiating from a central axis. A worm-like deposit-feeding organism systematically mined nutritious organic-rich mud below the sediment-water interface, creating a corkscrew-like spreite through repetitive back-and-forth passes.

As documented by Boggs (2006, Chapter 4, pp. 105–108):

  • Bathymetric Locus: Predominantly continental slope, toe of slope, and outer shelf settings (bathyal depths, ~200–2000 m).
  • Oxygen Conditions: Commonly associated with oxygen-deficient (dysaerobic) conditions where surface-grazing epifauna cannot survive, leaving deep sediment tiers to specialized Zoophycos miners.
  • Substrate: Fine-grained, cohesive mud and silty hemipelagic softgrounds.

3. Comprehensive Option Analysis

Option Marine Bathymetric Zone Characteristic Seilacherian Ichnofacies Evaluation
(a) Sublittoral zone Continental shelf (low-tide line to ~200 m depth) Cruziana ichnofacies Incorrect. Characterized by diverse resting (Asteriacites) and crawling (Cruziana) traces of shelf fauna.
(b) Bathyal zone Continental slope and rise (~200 m to 2000 m depth) Zoophycos ichnofacies Correct. Classical home of the Zoophycos assemblage; low-energy, dysaerobic muddy slope.
(c) Abyssal zone Deep ocean floor (>2000 m to 6000 m depth) Nereites ichnofacies Incorrect. Dominated by farming networks (Paleodictyon) and systematic grazing spirals (Spirorhaphe).
(d) Littoral zone Intertidal / foreshore zone (between high and low tides) Skolithos ichnofacies Incorrect. Dominated by robust vertical tubes (Skolithos, Diplocraterion) in wave-swept sands.
