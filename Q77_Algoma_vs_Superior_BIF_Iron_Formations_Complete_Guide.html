Question 77: Algoma-Type vs. Superior-Type Banded Iron Formations

Official Examination Guide • Precambrian Secular Evolution & Depositional Tectonic Settings
Official Examination Question
Consider the following statements:
  • 1. Algoma-type iron formations are sedimentary units in sequences of submarine volcanic rocks in greenstone belts, most commonly of Cambrian age.
  • 2. Superior-type iron formations occur in sedimentary basins of marine strata of late Archaean or Palaeoproterozoic age.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only
(b) Both 1 and 2
(c) 2 only CORRECT ANSWER
(d) Neither 1 nor 2
1. Executive Summary & Core Principle

Banded Iron Formations (BIFs) are chemical sedimentary rocks composed of alternating millimeter- to centimeter-scale layers of iron-rich minerals (magnetite, hematite, siderite, greenalite) and silica-rich chert. Following the classic classification by G.A. Gross (1965, 1980), Precambrian iron formations are subdivided primarily into:

  • Algoma-Type Iron Formations: Deposited in volcanic-arc and back-arc environments within Archean greenstone belts (>2.5 Ga, mostly 2.7–3.5 Ga). They are intimately interbedded with submarine volcanic rocks (pillowed tholeiitic basalts, andesites, felsic tuffs) and volcanogenic turbiditic greywackes. They are typically of limited aerial extent (a few kilometers long, tens of meters thick) and originated from local submarine volcanic exhalative hydrothermal venting. They are definitively ARCHEAN, NOT Cambrian! The Cambrian (541–485 Ma) is a Phanerozoic system during which typical BIFs were completely absent from Earth's oceans.
  • Superior-Type Iron Formations: Deposited in broad, shallow-marine epicontinental shelf basins and passive continental margins during the Late Archean to Paleoproterozoic (2.45 to 1.8 Ga). They are associated with platformal sedimentary strata (quartzites, dolostones, black shales) without direct volcanic rock associations. They extend over hundreds of square kilometers and represent basin-scale oxidation of upwelling ferruginous deep oceanic waters during the Great Oxidation Event (GOE).

Because Statement 1 incorrectly assigns a Cambrian age to Algoma-type iron formations, Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 accurately describes the Late Archean to Paleoproterozoic age and marine sedimentary association of Superior-type BIFs. Thus, the correct option is (c) 2 only.

3. Secular Evolution of BIFs and the "Cambrian Fallacy"

The temporal distribution of iron formations in Earth history is one of the most famous milestones in Precambrian geochemistry:

  1. Archean (3.8–2.5 Ga): Dominated by Algoma-type BIFs. Under an anoxic atmosphere and ocean, dissolved ferrous iron ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$) was abundant in seawater. Local hydrothermal vents provided iron and silica, which precipitated as iron-bearing sediments.
  2. Paleoproterozoic (2.45–1.8 Ga): Peak of Superior-type BIFs. The advent of oxygenic photosynthesis by cyanobacteria triggered the Great Oxidation Event (GOE) (~2.4 Ga). When upwelling deep ferrous-rich marine waters contacted oxygenated surface waters, dissolved $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ oxidized to insoluble $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ oxyhydroxides, blanketing continental shelves with colossal BIF deposits.
  3. Mesoproterozoic to Neoproterozoic Hiatus (1.8–0.8 Ga): After ~1.8 Ga, oceans became euxinic (sulfidic, "Canfield Ocean") or deep waters became oxygenated, stripping dissolved $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ from seawater and causing the global disappearance of BIFs.
  4. Neoproterozoic Glaciogenic (Rapitan-type, ~0.7 Ga): Re-appeared briefly during "Snowball Earth" events (Rapitan Group, Canada; Sturtian glaciation), where global sea-ice cover caused ocean anoxia, allowing $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ accumulation until ice melted and rapid aeration precipitated iron.
  5. Phanerozoic (Cambrian to Recent, 541 Ma – Present): Banded Iron Formations NEVER formed during the Cambrian! Phanerozoic sedimentary iron ores are exclusively minette-type or Clinton-type ironstones (oolitic goethite-chamosite-hematite beds), which are mineralogically, texturally, and genetically distinct from BIFs.
4. Systematic Evaluation of Exam Statements
  • Statement 1 is INCORRECT: "Algoma-type iron formations are sedimentary units in sequences of submarine volcanic rocks in greenstone belts, most commonly of Cambrian age."
    Why: The first part of the statement is true (they are sedimentary units in submarine volcanic sequences in greenstone belts), but the age clause—"most commonly of Cambrian age"—is factually false. They are Archean (>2.5 Ga).
  • Statement 2 is CORRECT: "Superior-type iron formations occur in sedimentary basins of marine strata of late Archaean or Palaeoproterozoic age."
    Why: This is completely accurate. Superior-type BIFs represent shelf sedimentary sequences that peaked between 2.45 Ga and 1.8 Ga in the Paleoproterozoic.
Key Takeaway: Algoma-type BIF = Archean submarine volcanics in greenstone belts; Superior-type BIF = Paleoproterozoic marine shelf sediments. Hence, (c) 2 only is the correct choice.
