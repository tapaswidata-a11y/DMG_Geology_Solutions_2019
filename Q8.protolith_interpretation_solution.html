Comprehensive Chapter Masterclass: Metamorphism of Carbonate Rocks
The CaO–MgO–SiO₂–H₂O–CO₂ (CMS-HC) System, Decarbonation Sequences & T-X(CO₂) Phase Equilibria
1. The Chemical System of Carbonate Metamorphism

Sedimentary carbonate rocks range from pure limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and dolostone ($\text{CaMg(CO}_3)_2$) to siliceous dolomitic limestones and marls containing varying amounts of detrital quartz ($\text{SiO}_2$) and clay minerals (illite, smectite).

The phase equilibria of these rocks are modeled within the fundamental five-component system $\text{CaO} - \text{MgO} - \text{SiO}_2 - \text{H}_2\text{O} - \text{CO}_2$ (CMS-HC). When quartz is present, progressive metamorphism drives a series of dramatic decarbonation and mixed-volatile reactions, liberating gaseous $\text{CO}_2$ (and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and forming a sequence of calcium-magnesium silicates.

"Metamorphosed calcareous rocks are derived from carbonate-rich sedimentary precursors... Metamorphosed siliceous dolomites are particularly interesting because the reactions that occur during progressive metamorphism produce a sequence of Ca-Mg silicates that serve as sensitive indicators of metamorphic grade and fluid composition." — John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology (2nd Ed., Chapter 29, p. 670)
2. The Progressive Mineral Sequence in Siliceous Dolomites

As temperature rises from greenschist through amphibolite to granulite facies, the initial sedimentary assemblage Calcite + Dolomite + Quartz undergoes sequential reactions as portrayed in Winter's $T-X_{\text{CO}_2}$ phase diagram:

Reaction 1: Appearance of Diopside (Medium-to-High Grade)
At amphibolite facies conditions, dolomite reacts with quartz (or earlier tremolite + calcite) to generate the calcic pyroxene diopside: Dolomite + 2 Quartz = Diopside + 2 CO₂ Tremolite + 3 Calcite + 2 Quartz = 5 Diopside + 3 CO₂ + H₂O
Reaction 2: Appearance of Forsterite (High Grade)
In quartz-depleted, dolomite-rich bulk compositions, diopside and dolomite react to form Mg-rich olivine (forsterite): Diopside + 3 Dolomite = 2 Forsterite + 4 Calcite + 2 CO₂

Notice that forsterite in marbles coexists stably with calcite, but is strictly isolated from free quartz by the intervening diopside tie-line!

Reaction 3: Appearance of Wollastonite (Highest Grade / Granulite Facies)
At peak temperatures and low $X_{\text{CO}_2}$ (or very high T in granulites), calcite and quartz directly react to produce the pyroxenoid wollastonite: Calcite + Quartz = Wollastonite + CO₂
Reaction 4: K-Feldspar from Illitic Clays
In impure dolomites containing minor detrital illite/potassium clay, phlogopite forms at lower grades and breaks down at granulite facies to generate K-feldspar: Phlogopite + 3 Calcite + 6 Quartz = K-feldspar + 3 Diopside + 3 CO₂ + H₂O

Therefore, an assemblage containing calcite + dolomite + quartz + diopside + wollastonite + K-feldspar + forsterite represents a classic, high-grade, partially equilibrated siliceous dolomitic limestone (carbonate rock protolith) undergoing granulite-facies metamorphism!

II
Official Exam Question Statement & In-Depth Critical Analysis
Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) 2019 · Junior Scientific Officer Exam · Question 8
Question Statement
Q.8: Presence of calcite, dolomite, quartz, diopside, wollastonite, K-feldspar, forsterite in a regionally metamorphosed rock, under granulite facies suggests which one of the following types of protoliths?
(a) Mafic
(b) Pelitic
(c) Carbonate rock
(d) Ultramafic
✓
Correct Answer: (c) Carbonate rock
The combination of carbonate minerals (calcite, dolomite) with calcium-magnesium silicates (diopside, wollastonite, forsterite) uniquely fingerprints an impure siliceous dolomitic limestone protolith.
Exhaustive Option-by-Option Critical Analysis
(c) Carbonate rock CORRECT
Why it is uniquely correct: The mineral assemblage listed in the question is the textbook signature of a siliceous dolomitic limestone or marl (Winter, 2010, Chapter 29; Bucher & Grapes, 2011; Yardley, 1989). Primary sedimentary calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$), dolomite ($\text{CaMg(CO}_3)_2$), and detrital quartz ($\text{SiO}_2$) react during progressive metamorphism to yield diopside ($\text{CaMgSi}_2\text{O}_6$), forsterite ($\text{Mg}_2\text{SiO}_4$), and wollastonite ($\text{CaSiO}_3$). Minor clay/shale impurities (illite) provide potassium and aluminum to generate K-feldspar via phlogopite breakdown. No other geological protolith possesses this combination of abundant Ca-Mg carbonates and calc-silicate phases.
(a) Mafic INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: A mafic protolith (metabasite such as basalt or gabbro) under granulite facies conditions produces a "two-pyroxene granulite" dominated by orthopyroxene + clinopyroxene + calcic plagioclase ± garnet ± quartz (as examined in Question 5). Mafic rocks have low CO₂ contents; primary calcite and dolomite do not exist in equilibrium in peak mafic granulites, nor does wollastonite form because calcium is strongly partitioned into plagioclase and clinopyroxene.
(b) Pelitic INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: Pelitic protoliths (shales, mudstones) are characterized by very high Al₂O₃ and FeO/MgO, with very low CaO. Under granulite facies, metapelites form assemblages of sillimanite + garnet (almandine) + cordierite + biotite + K-feldspar + plagioclase + quartz. Pelites completely lack the calcium required to form calcite, dolomite, diopside, or wollastonite.
(d) Ultramafic INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: While ultramafic rocks (peridotites, serpentinites) contain abundant Mg-olivine (forsterite) and minor pyroxene, they are extremely depleted in CaO and SiO₂. They never contain free quartz in equilibrium with forsterite, nor do they develop wollastonite ($\text{CaSiO}_3$) or significant calcite/dolomite in regional granulite facies terranes.

8 High-Yield Practice Questions on Carbonate Metamorphism & Protolith Diagnosis
1. The reaction Calcite + Quartz = Wollastonite + CO₂ is an example of which type of metamorphic reaction?
(a) Continuous hydration    (b) Discontinuous decarbonation    (c) Polymorphic inversion    (d) Oxidation reaction
Answer: (b) Discontinuous decarbonation — It is a univariant devolatilization reaction releasing CO₂ gas.
2. Why does wollastonite form at much lower temperatures in the presence of an H₂O-rich metamorphic fluid than in pure CO₂?
(a) H₂O increases the activation energy
(b) Dilution of the fluid phase by H₂O lowers the activity of CO₂ ($a_{\text{CO}_2}$), driving decarbonation to lower temperatures
(c) H₂O transforms wollastonite into tremolite
(d) H₂O reacts with quartz to form coesite
Answer: (b) — Reducing the mole fraction of CO₂ ($X_{\text{CO}_2}$) reduces the partial pressure and chemical potential of CO₂, shifting the equilibrium boundary toward lower temperatures.
3. In a pure dolomitic marble undergoing metamorphism, what mineral appears before diopside under moderate temperatures if quartz is present?
(a) Tremolite    (b) Sillimanite    (c) Kyanite    (d) Omphacite
Answer: (a) Tremolite — The reaction 5 Dolomite + 8 Quartz + 1 H₂O = Tremolite + 3 Calcite + 7 CO₂ introduces tremolite at lower amphibolite grade before diopside appears.
4. The presence of forsterite in a metamorphic marble indicates that:
(a) The rock was originally an aluminum-rich bauxite
(b) The protolith was dolomite-rich and quartz was completely consumed by earlier reactions
(c) The metamorphism occurred at 0°C
(d) The rock was subjected to hydrothermal gold mineralization
Answer: (b) — Forsterite forms by reaction between diopside and excess dolomite; it cannot coexist with quartz.
5. A metamorphosed rock consisting essentially of garnet (almandine-pyrope), sillimanite, cordierite, quartz, and biotite is derived from which protolith?
(a) Basalt    (b) Pelite (shale)    (c) Limestone    (d) Dunite
Answer: (b) Pelite (shale) — High Al and K with low Ca produce sillimanite, cordierite, and almandine garnet.
6. Which chemical component distinguishes a siliceous dolomite from a pure limestone?
(a) Abundance of both MgO and SiO₂    (b) Total absence of CaO    (c) High Na₂O    (d) High P₂O₅
Answer: (a) Abundance of both MgO and SiO₂ — Dolomite provides Mg and Ca, while detrital sand/chert provides silica.
7. The chemical formula of wollastonite is:
(a) CaSiO₃    (b) CaMgSi₂O₆    (c) Mg₂SiO₄    (d) Ca₂Al₃Si₃O₁₂(OH)
Answer: (a) CaSiO₃ — Wollastonite is the calcium metasilicate pyroxenoid.
8. What is the role of potassium in the formation of K-feldspar in impure calc-silicate rocks?
(a) It comes from detrital illite/muscovite and forms phlogopite, which breaks down to K-feldspar at high grades
(b) It replaces silicon in the quartz lattice
(c) It forms liquid native potassium metal
(d) It acts as a catalyst without entering any mineral
Answer: (a) — Illite clay supplies potassium to form phlogopite at medium grades; at higher grades, phlogopite + calcite + quartz react to form K-feldspar + diopside.
