Question 73: Groundwater Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) & Electrical Conductance (EC)

Aqueous Electrolytic Conduction, Units of Specific Conductance & Temperature Coefficients
Official Examination Question
Which of the following statements regarding the Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) and Electrical Conductance (EC) of the groundwater are correct?
1. A rapid determination of the TDS can be made by measuring the EC of the groundwater sample.
2. The specific conductance is measured in microsiemens/cm.
3. The specific conductance decreases with increase in temperature.
Select the correct answer using the codes given below:
(a) 2 and 3 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 2 only [CORRECT]
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Verified Answer Key

Correct Option: (c) 1 and 2 only — Statement 1 is true because electrical conductance is directly proportional to ion concentration ($\text{TDS} \approx 0.55\text{--}0.75 \times \text{EC}$). Statement 2 is true because the standard international unit of specific electrical conductance is microsiemens per centimeter ($\mu\text{S/cm}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$). Statement 3 is false because in aqueous electrolytic solutions, warming decreases water viscosity and accelerates ionic mobility, causing specific conductance to increase by approximately $2\%$ per $^\circ\text{C}$.

(Right) Measured electrical conductance as a function of sample temperature; conductance rises steadily (~2% per $^\circ\text{C}$) because lower water viscosity promotes faster ionic migration, requiring standard reporting at $25^\circ\text{C}$.

1. Statement-by-Statement Diagnostic Breakdown

Statement 1: "Rapid determination of TDS via EC measurement" — CORRECT

Pure deionized water is an insulator. The electrical conductivity of groundwater arises exclusively from the presence of dissolved, dissociated ionic solutes (cations: $\mathrm{Na^+, Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}, K^+}$; anions: $\mathrm{HCO_3^-, Cl^-, SO_4^{2-}, NO_3^-}$).

  • Gravimetric laboratory determination of TDS requires evaporating a filtered water sample to dryness at $180^\circ\text{C}$ and weighing the residual salts, which takes hours.
  • In contrast, a portable field conductivity meter instantly measures electrical current between platinum electrodes. In natural groundwater, TDS is related to EC by the well-established empirical linear equation: $$\text{TDS (mg/L)} = A \times \text{EC } (\mu\text{S/cm at } 25^\circ\text{C})$$ where conversion factor $A$ typically ranges between 0.55 and 0.75 (average $\approx 0.64$ for bicarbonate-chloride freshwaters, rising to $0.80$ for sulfate-rich waters).

Statement 2: "Specific conductance is measured in microsiemens/cm" — CORRECT

Conductance ($G$) is the reciprocal of electrical resistance ($G = 1 / R$, unit: Siemens, $\text{S}$, formerly mho). Specific conductance ($\sigma$) normalizes conductance to a standard geometry (a 1 cm cube of solution):

  • In the SI metric system and standard international hydrogeological practice (USGS, WHO, BIS), specific electrical conductance of water is expressed in microsiemens per centimeter ($\mu\text{S/cm}$) or millisiemens per meter ($\text{mS/m}$, where $1\ \mu\text{S/cm} = 0.1\ \text{mS/m}$).
  • Fresh drinking water typically registers $100\text{ to }1000\ \mu\text{S/cm}$, while seawater registers $\sim 50,000\ \mu\text{S/cm}$.

Statement 3: "Specific conductance decreases with increase in temperature" — INCORRECT

Unlike metallic conductors (where electrical resistance increases with heat due to thermal lattice vibrations scattering electrons), aqueous electrolytic solutions behave in the exact opposite manner:

  • In water, electric charge is transported by the physical migration of dissolved hydrated ions through liquid molecules.
  • As temperature rises, thermal energy reduces water viscosity ($\mu$) and weakens solvent-solute hydrogen bonds.
  • Consequently, ionic drift velocity and mobility increase dramatically! The electrical conductivity of water INCREASES by approximately $1.9\%\text{ to }2.2\%$ per degree Celsius increase.
  • To eliminate thermal variations, all specific conductance readings must be corrected to a standard reference temperature of $25^\circ\text{C}$ using the temperature compensation formula: $$\text{EC}_{25} = \frac{\text{EC}_T}{1 + \alpha (T - 25)}$$ where $\alpha \approx 0.02\ ^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$.

2. Groundwater Classification by Salinity & EC

Water Quality Class Total Dissolved Solids (TDS, mg/L) Specific Conductance (EC at 25°C, μS/cm) Suitability & Use
Freshwater < 1,000 mg/L < 1,500 μS/cm Potable drinking water; excellent for all irrigation.
Brackish Water 1,000 – 10,000 mg/L 1,500 – 15,000 μS/cm Requires desalination (RO); tolerable only for salt-resistant crops.
Saline Water 10,000 – 35,000 mg/L 15,000 – 50,000 μS/cm Unfit for human consumption or agriculture (coastal intrusion).
Brine > 35,000 mg/L > 50,000 μS/cm Deep sedimentary basin formation fluids / evaporite pans.
