1. The Metamorphic Facies Concept & Mafic Protoliths

The concept of metamorphic facies was formulated by the Finnish petrologist Pentti Eskola (1915, 1920, 1939) to provide a rigorous, chemical-thermodynamic framework for correlating metamorphic rocks of widely differing bulk compositions. Eskola recognized that while pelites, carbonates, and basalts yield vastly different minerals under the same physical conditions, all rocks that equilibrated under identical temperature and pressure ranges belong to the same metamorphic facies.

"A metamorphic facies is a set of metamorphic mineral assemblages, repeatedly associated in time and space and showing a regular relationship between mineral composition and bulk chemical composition, such that they were formed under similar ranges of temperature and pressure... Because mafic rocks (basalts and gabbros) are chemically sensitive to P and T across almost the entire spectrum of crustal conditions, their mineral assemblages serve as the definitive standard for naming and defining metamorphic facies." — John D. Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology (2nd Ed., Chapter 25, pp. 568–571)
2. The Amphibolite-to-Granulite Transition: Hornblende Breakdown

In mafic protoliths (metabasites), the lower-grade amphibolite facies is characterized by the essential hydrous mineral assemblage hornblende + plagioclase (oligoclase–andesine) ± quartz ± garnet.

As temperature rises beyond approximately 700°C to 750°C in the deep continental crust, hydrous amphibole becomes thermodynamically unstable and undergoes prograde dehydration-devolatilization (and dehydration-melting if quartz and plagioclase are present). The breakdown of hornblende produces anhydrous pyroxenes:

Dehydration Breakdown of Hornblende (Low to Moderate Pressure Granulites)
At moderate crustal pressures (3–8 kbar), hornblende reacts with quartz to generate orthopyroxene, clinopyroxene, and calcic plagioclase: Hornblende + Quartz = Orthopyroxene + Clinopyroxene + Plagioclase + H₂O (or melt)

This reaction generates the classic two-pyroxene granulite (orthopyroxene + clinopyroxene + plagioclase), marking the definitive boundary of the granulite facies.

Dehydration Breakdown of Hornblende (High Pressure Granulites)
At higher lithostatic pressures (> 8–12 kbar in thickened orogenic roots), garnet joins the product assemblage: Hornblende + Plagioclase + Quartz = Clinopyroxene + Orthopyroxene + Garnet + H₂O (or melt)

High-pressure mafic granulites are thus characterized by the 5-phase anhydrous paragenesis: Clinopyroxene + Orthopyroxene + Plagioclase + Garnet + Quartz.

3. ACF Chemographic Topology of Granulite Facies Mafic Rocks

In Eskola's ACF diagram (where A = Al₂O₃ + Fe₂O₃ - (Na₂O + K₂O), C = CaO, F = FeO + MgO + MnO), projected from quartz and fluid, the granulite facies metabasites occupy the central three-phase and four-phase sub-triangles connecting Plagioclase (Anorthite), Diopside (Cpx), Enstatite-Ferrosilite (Opx), and Almandine-Pyrope (Grt).

Key petrological features of granulite facies metabasites:

  • Coexistence of Two Pyroxenes: The essential defining hallmark of granulite facies metabasites is the stable coexistence of orthopyroxene (hypersthene/enstatite) and clinopyroxene (diopside/augite) with plagioclase.
  • Anhydrous Character: Hydrous minerals (hornblende, biotite, epidote) are absent or present only as minor retrograde/relict phases or F/Cl-enriched accessory crystals.
  • Plagioclase Composition: Plagioclase is intermediate to calcic (andesine to labradolite/bytownite). At even higher pressures (transitioning to eclogite), plagioclase breaks down completely into omphacite + garnet + kyanite + quartz.

Official Exam Question Statement & In-Depth Critical Analysis
Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) 2019 · Junior Scientific Officer Exam · Question 5
Question Statement
Q.5: Which one of the following is a characteristic assemblage of granulite facies metamorphism of mafic protolith?
(a) Hornblende + Plagioclase + Quartz + Garnet
(b) Forsterite + Orthopyroxene + Clinopyroxene + Epidote
(c) Clinopyroxene + Orthopyroxene + Plagioclase + Garnet + Quartz
(d) Forsterite + Orthopyroxene + Clinopyroxene + Staurolite
✓
Correct Answer: (c) Clinopyroxene + Orthopyroxene + Plagioclase + Garnet + Quartz
The stable coexistence of two pyroxenes (Cpx + Opx) with plagioclase, garnet, and quartz represents the quintessential high-pressure/high-temperature anhydrous assemblage of mafic granulites.
Exhaustive Option-by-Option Critical Analysis
(c) Clinopyroxene + Orthopyroxene + Plagioclase + Garnet + Quartz CORRECT
Why it is uniquely correct: In metamorphic petrology (Winter, 2010, Table 25-1, p. 571; Yardley, 1989; Bucher & Grapes, 2011), the entry into the granulite facies in mafic rocks is universally defined by the dehydration of hornblende to produce orthopyroxene (hypersthene) in the presence of clinopyroxene and plagioclase. At medium to high pressures, almandine-pyrope garnet and quartz coexist stably with both pyroxenes. This anhydrous 5-phase assemblage (Cpx + Opx + Plag + Grt + Qtz) is the textbook definition of a mafic "two-pyroxene granulite."
(a) Hornblende + Plagioclase + Quartz + Garnet INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: This assemblage is the classic paragenesis of the Amphibolite Facies (specifically, garnet amphibolite). Hornblende is a hydrous amphibole whose upper thermal stability limit defines the boundary between amphibolite and granulite facies. When a mafic rock enters the granulite facies, hornblende breaks down completely by dehydration reactions to generate pyroxenes. Therefore, an assemblage dominated by hornblende cannot represent the granulite facies.
(b) Forsterite + Orthopyroxene + Clinopyroxene + Epidote INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: This option contains two severe petrological impossibilities for mafic granulites:
  1. Forsterite (Mg-olivine): Mafic rocks (basalts/gabbros) are silica-saturated to oversaturated; olivine cannot coexist in equilibrium with quartz-bearing mafic granulites. Forsterite is restricted to silica-undersaturated ultramafic rocks (peridotites) or siliceous dolomitic marbles.
  2. Epidote: Epidote is a hydrous Ca-Al sorosilicate characteristic of low-to-medium grades (greenschist and epidote-amphibolite facies, T < 500°C–550°C). It completely decomposes at temperatures hundreds of degrees below the onset of granulite facies conditions.
(d) Forsterite + Orthopyroxene + Clinopyroxene + Staurolite INCORRECT
Why it is incorrect: This assemblage is a chemically contradictory mixture:
  1. Staurolite: Staurolite is strictly confined to highly aluminous, Fe-rich pelitic protoliths (metamorphosed shales) at amphibolite facies (520°C–650°C). It never occurs in mafic bulk compositions because basalts lack sufficient Al₂O₃ relative to Ca, Mg, and Fe.
  2. Forsterite + Staurolite incompatibility: Forsterite represents extreme silica-undersaturation and ultramafic compositions, whereas staurolite requires high Al and excess silica. They are chemically mutually exclusive in nature.

Diagnostic Petrographic Criteria: Identifying Mafic Granulites
  • Presence of Orthopyroxene: The definitive petrographic criterion separating granulite facies from amphibolite facies is the appearance of hypersthene/orthopyroxene (distinguished by faint pink-to-green pleochroism, parallel extinction, and low birefringence).
  • Two-Pyroxene Geothermometry: Granulite metabasites preserve equilibrium compositions of coexisting clino- and orthopyroxenes that record peak metamorphic temperatures between 750°C and 950°C.
  • Granoblastic Polygonal Texture: Mafic granulites typically display a granoblastic polygonal (or granoblastic mosaic) texture with straight grain boundaries meeting at ~120° triple junctions, indicating prolonged static annealing at high temperature.
  • Plagioclase Twinning: Plagioclase exhibits well-developed albite and pericline twinning, frequently showing antiperthitic exsolution lamellae of K-feldspar.
8 High-Yield Practice Questions on Granulite Facies & Mafic Metamorphism
1. The critical mineral whose first appearance in metabasites defines the onset of the granulite facies is:
(a) Almandine garnet    (b) Orthopyroxene (hypersthene)    (c) Diopside    (d) Sillimanite
Answer: (b) Orthopyroxene (hypersthene) — In mafic rocks, the transition from amphibolite to granulite facies is definitively marked by the dehydration of hornblende to produce orthopyroxene in the presence of clinopyroxene and plagioclase.
2. Which reaction represents the dehydration breakdown of hornblende at the amphibolite-to-granulite facies boundary?
(a) Hornblende + Quartz = Orthopyroxene + Clinopyroxene + Plagioclase + H₂O
(b) Actinolite + Epidote = Hornblende + Albite + H₂O
(c) Glaucophane + Quartz = Jadeite + Talc + H₂O
(d) Tremolite + Calcite + Quartz = Diopside + CO₂
Answer: (a) — Reaction (a) is the fundamental devolatilization reaction responsible for the classic two-pyroxene granulite assemblage.
3. Mafic granulites are often called "two-pyroxene granulites" because they contain:
(a) Diopside and Augite
(b) Orthopyroxene (hypersthene/enstatite) and Clinopyroxene (augite/diopside)
(c) Omphacite and Aegirine
(d) Wollastonite and Hedenbergite
Answer: (b) — Two-pyroxene granulites are defined by the coexistence of an orthopyroxene (Ca-poor) and a clinopyroxene (Ca-rich).
4. Why is epidote completely absent from equilibrium granulite facies mafic rocks?
(a) Epidote only forms in granite pegmatites
(b) Epidote is a hydrous mineral that breaks down via dehydration at T > 550°C–600°C
(c) Epidote requires CO₂ to be stable
(d) Epidote is converted directly to chlorite
Answer: (b) — Epidote is thermally limited to greenschist and epidote-amphibolite facies; it dehydrates to form anorthite-rich plagioclase, grossular, and hornblende well before reaching granulite temperatures.
5. At ultra-high temperatures (UHT metamorphism, T > 900°C–1000°C) in pelitic granulites, which diagnostic anhydrous assemblage is recognized?
(a) Sapphirine + Quartz    (b) Chlorite + Muscovite    (c) Glaucophane + Jadeite    (d) Prehnite + Pumpellyite
Answer: (a) Sapphirine + Quartz — The coexistence of sapphirine + quartz or osumilite + quartz is the definitive indicator of ultra-high temperature (UHT) granulite metamorphism.
6. In mafic rocks, the transition from granulite facies to eclogite facies at high pressure is characterized by the disappearance of:
(a) Garnet    (b) Plagioclase    (c) Clinopyroxene    (d) Quartz
Answer: (b) Plagioclase — At high lithostatic pressures (> 12–15 kbar), plagioclase breaks down completely into jadeite/omphacite + grossular + kyanite + quartz, defining the eclogite facies.
7. Who first established the concept of metamorphic facies based on mineral assemblages in mafic rocks?
(a) George Barrow    (b) Pentti Eskola    (c) N. L. Bowen    (d) F. J. Turner
Answer: (b) Pentti Eskola — Eskola (1915, 1920) pioneered the metamorphic facies classification based on his study of Orijärvi and Oslo rocks.
8. What texture is most typical of mafic granulites that have undergone extensive static thermal annealing in the deep crust?
(a) Nematoblastic    (b) Slaty cleavage    (c) Granoblastic polygonal    (d) Poikilitic
Answer: (c) Granoblastic polygonal — High-temperature static recrystallization (annealing) drives grain-boundary migration to minimize interfacial surface energy, producing equant grains with ~120° dihedral angles.
