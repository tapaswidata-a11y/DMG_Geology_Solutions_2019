Question 89: Water Pollution & Organic Matter Loading: BOD vs. COD

Official Examination Guide • Biochemical Oxygen Demand, Streeter-Phelps Model & Dissolved Oxygen Deficit
Official Examination Question
The organic matter load in a water body is described as
(a) Dissolved oxygen
(b) Biochemical oxygen demand CORRECT ANSWER
(c) Chemical oxygen demand
(d) Total hardness
1. Executive Summary & Core Environmental Principle

In environmental geology, aquatic chemistry, and limnology (Masters & Ela, Introduction to Environmental Engineering and Science; Metcalf & Eddy, Wastewater Engineering):

  • Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD): The primary parameter used to quantify the biodegradable organic matter load in natural water bodies (rivers, lakes, aquifers) and sewage effluents. It is defined as the mass of dissolved oxygen ($\text{O}_2$, expressed in $\text{mg/L}$) consumed by aerobic microorganisms during the biochemical oxidation and stabilization of organic carbon over a standardized incubation period (typically 5 days at 20°C, denoted as $\text{BOD}_5$):
    Organic Matter (CH₂O) + O₂ ➔ [Microorganisms] ➔ CO₂ + H₂O + Cellular Biomass
    A high BOD value directly reflects a high concentration of decomposing organic matter (e.g., untreated sewage, agricultural runoff), which depletes dissolved oxygen (DO) and leads to aquatic hypoxia or fish mortality.
  • Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD): Quantifies the total oxygen equivalent required to chemically oxidize all organic matter (both biologically degradable and biologically inert/refractory compounds) using a boiling mixture of concentrated sulfuric acid and a strong chemical oxidizing agent (potassium dichromate, $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$). For any given water sample, $\text{COD} \ge \text{BOD}$.
  • Dissolved Oxygen (DO): The concentration of free, non-compound $\text{O}_2$ gas dissolved in water. It represents the oxygen resource available for aquatic fauna, which is consumed by the incoming BOD load.
  • Total Hardness: The concentration of dissolved multivalent metallic cations, primarily calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) and magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$), expressed in $\text{mg/L as CaCO}_3$.

Therefore, the biodegradable organic matter load in a natural water body is universally described as (b) Biochemical oxygen demand.

2. Comparative Characterization of Water Quality Parameters
Parameter Definition & Principle Test Duration / Condition Role in Water Quality Assessment
Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) $\text{O}_2$ consumed by aerobic bacteria decomposing biodegradable organics. 5 days at 20°C ($\text{BOD}_5$); standard dilution method. Standard descriptor of organic pollution load in natural rivers and lakes. Pristine water < 2 mg/L; polluted water > 10 mg/L.
Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD) $\text{O}_2$ consumed by chemical digestion of all organics using boiling $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ / $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. 2 to 3 hours at 150°C. Measures total oxidizable chemical and industrial waste load (biodegradable + refractory).
Dissolved Oxygen (DO) Actual molecular oxygen dissolved in water. Instantaneous electrochemical sensor or Winkler titration. Critical for survival of fish and aerobic ecology (optimum 6–9 mg/L; lethal < 2 mg/L).
Total Hardness Sum of $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]$ and $[\text{Mg}^{2+}]$ polyvalent cations. EDTA titration method. Controls soap lathering and scaling in industrial boilers; unrelated to organic matter load.
3. Evaluation of Options
  • (a) Dissolved oxygen: INCORRECT. Represents the dissolved gas available for respiration, which is consumed by the organic load.
  • (b) Biochemical oxygen demand: CORRECT ANSWER. By universal definition in environmental science, BOD quantifies the organic matter load in water bodies.
  • (c) Chemical oxygen demand: INCORRECT. A laboratory chemical oxidation metric predominantly used for industrial wastewater, whereas the biological load in natural water bodies is characterized by BOD.
  • (d) Total hardness: INCORRECT. Measures calcium and magnesium mineral content, not organic matter.
Key Takeaway: Organic matter load in a water body = Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD). Hence, (b) Biochemical oxygen demand is the correct answer.
