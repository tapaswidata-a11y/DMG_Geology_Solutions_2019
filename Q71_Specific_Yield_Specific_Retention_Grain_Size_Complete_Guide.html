Question 71: Specific Yield ($S_y$), Specific Retention ($S_r$) & Grain Size Controls

Aquifer Storage Fundamentals, Gravity Drainage Physics, and Pore-Size Surface Tension Dynamics
Official Examination Question
Consider the following statements regarding specific yield of an aquifer:
1. It is the ratio of the volume of water that after saturation can be drained by gravity to its own volume.
2. Its value does not depend on the grain size.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only [CORRECT]
(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2
Verified Answer Key

Correct Option: (a) 1 only — Statement 1 is the exact classical thermodynamic definition of Specific Yield ($S_y = V_{wd} / V$). Statement 2 is completely false because specific yield depends profoundly on grain size: fine-grained sediments (clays) have very small pore throats and huge surface areas where molecular capillary forces retain water ($S_r \approx 48\%$, $S_y \approx 2\%$), whereas coarse sands and gravels release water freely under gravity ($S_y \approx 25\text{--}30\%$).

Figure 71.1 | Dependence of Specific Yield ($S_y$) and Specific Retention ($S_r$) on Sediment Grain Size: In an unconfined aquifer, total porosity is partitioned into drainable water ($S_y$) and capillary-retained water ($S_r$): $n = S_y + S_r$. In clay, high total porosity ($n \approx 50\%$) yields almost no gravity drainage ($S_y \approx 2\%$) because molecular attraction binds pore water. In medium-to-coarse sand, wide pores allow gravity to overcome capillary forces, maximizing specific yield ($S_y \approx 27\text{--}28\%$). (Source: Todd & Mays, Groundwater Hydrology; Davis & DeWiest, 1966).

1. Mathematical Definitions in Aquifer Storage

When an unconfined saturated porous medium is subjected to gravity drainage (e.g., during pumping or water table decline), not all water drains out:

  • Specific Yield ($S_y$): The ratio of the volume of water drained by gravity ($V_{wd}$) to the total bulk volume of the rock or soil ($V$): $$S_y = \frac{V_{wd}}{V}$$
  • Specific Retention ($S_r$): The ratio of the volume of water retained against gravity by molecular attraction and surface tension ($V_{wr}$) to the total volume ($V$): $$S_r = \frac{V_{wr}}{V}$$
  • Porosity Conservation Law: The sum of specific yield and specific retention equals the total porosity ($n$): $$n = S_y + S_r = \frac{V_v}{V}$$

2. Why Specific Yield Depends Strongly on Grain Size

The retention of water in a porous skeleton is governed by capillary forces and adsorption along mineral grain surfaces.

  • The capillary rise height ($h_c$) in a cylindrical pore is inversely proportional to the pore throat radius ($r$): $$h_c = \frac{2\gamma \cos \theta}{\rho_w g r}$$ where $\gamma$ is surface tension and $\theta$ is contact angle.
  • In fine-grained sediments (clay and fine silt), pore radii are microscopic ($r < 2\ \mu\text{m}$), and specific surface area is enormous ($ > 10\text{ m}^2/\text{g}$). Capillary suction pressures exceed gravity by orders of magnitude, causing virtually all pore water to be held as pellicular and capillary water ($S_r \approx 45\text{--}50\%$). Consequently, $S_y$ drops to an insignificant 1% to 3%, despite clay having the highest total porosity of all sedimentary rocks!
  • In coarse-grained sediments (medium sand, coarse sand, gravel), pore radii are large ($r > 100\ \mu\text{m}$), specific surface area is low, capillary forces are weak, and gravity drains water freely ($S_y \approx 20\text{--}30\%$).

4. Comprehensive Statement Analysis

  • Statement 1: Correct. "It is the ratio of the volume of water that after saturation can be drained by gravity to its own volume." This is the verbatim classical definition of specific yield.
  • Statement 2: Incorrect. "Its value does not depend on the grain size." As proved by capillary theory and empirical testing across all sediments, grain size is the primary physical control on the partition between specific yield and specific retention.
