🖼️ Textbook Evidence: Dana's Chemical Classification System

Figure 42.1: Dana Classification of Non-Silicate Minerals (Dexter Perkins, Mineralogy, 3rd ed., Chapter 2 & Appendix). Classification based on the dominant anionic group: Halides ($\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{F}^-$), Hydroxides ($(\text{OH})^-$), Carbonates ($[\text{CO}_3]^{2-}$), and Phosphates ($[\text{PO}_4]^{3-}$).
"In the Dana System of Mineralogy, minerals are grouped primarily according to the dominant anion or anionic complex in their chemical formula:
• Halides: Contain halogen anions ($\text{Cl}^-, \text{F}^-, \text{Br}^-, \text{I}^-$) bonded to metals (e.g., halite $\text{NaCl}$, sylvite $\text{KCl}$, fluorite $\text{CaF}_2$).
• Hydroxides / Oxy-hydroxides: Contain hydroxyl $(\text{OH})^-$ groups (e.g., brucite $\text{Mg(OH)}_2$, manganite $\text{MnO(OH)}$, goethite $\text{FeO(OH)}$).
• Carbonates: Contain planar $[\text{CO}_3]^{2-}$ complex anions (e.g., calcite $\text{CaCO}_3$, siderite $\text{FeCO}_3$, dolomite).
• Phosphates: Contain tetrahedral $[\text{PO}_4]^{3-}$ complex anions (e.g., apatite $\text{Ca}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3(\text{F,Cl,OH})$, monazite $(\text{Ce,La,Th})\text{PO}_4$)."
— Dexter Perkins, Mineralogy, Chapter 2 (Classification of Minerals), pp. 47–55

🔍 Step-by-Step Chemical Matching

A. Sylvite ➔ 4. Halide [MATCH 1]

Formula & Chemistry: $\text{KCl}$ (Potassium chloride). Belongs to the isometric halite structure group. The dominant anion is chloride ($\text{Cl}^-$), making sylvite a primary member of the Halide class. It is the premier commercial ore of potash, precipitating in late-stage evaporite marine basins.

B. Manganite ➔ 3. Hydroxide [MATCH 2]

Formula & Chemistry: $\text{MnO(OH)}$ (Manganese oxy-hydroxide). Contains essential structural hydroxyl $(\text{OH})^-$ groups coordinated with $\text{Mn}^{3+}$ cations. In Dana's taxonomy, oxy-hydroxides are classified under Hydroxides (Oxides and Hydroxides class). It occurs in low-temperature hydrothermal veins and secondary manganese replacement deposits.

C. Siderite ➔ 1. Carbonate [MATCH 3]

Formula & Chemistry: $\text{FeCO}_3$ (Iron(II) carbonate). Belongs to the rhombohedral calcite group ($R\bar{3}c$). The fundamental anionic complex is $[\text{CO}_3]^{2-}$. Siderite is an important iron ore found in sedimentary iron formations (clay ironstone), coal beds, and hydrothermal veins.

D. Monazite ➔ 2. Phosphate [MATCH 4]

Formula & Chemistry: $(\text{Ce},\text{La},\text{Nd},\text{Th})\text{PO}_4$ (Rare-earth phosphate). Contains tetrahedral $[\text{PO}_4]^{3-}$ groups coordinating large light rare-earth elements (LREEs) and thorium. Monazite is the primary source of cerium, thorium, and rare earths, occurring in granites, pegmatites, and concentrated as heavy mineral beach sands (placers).

📊 Overview: Dana Chemical Mineral Classes

Mineral Class Defining Anion / Radical Key Examples Diagnostic Properties
Native Elements None (pure elements) Gold ($\text{Au}$), Diamond/Graphite ($\text{C}$), Sulfur ($\text{S}$) Metallic or covalent bonding
Sulfides $\text{S}^{2-}$ (Sulfide ion) Galena ($\text{PbS}$), Sphalerite ($\text{ZnS}$), Pyrite ($\text{FeS}_2$) High density, metallic luster, dark streak
Halides $\text{F}^-, \text{Cl}^-, \text{Br}^-, \text{I}^-$ Sylvite ($\text{KCl}$), Halite ($\text{NaCl}$), Fluorite ($\text{CaF}_2$) Ionic bonding, low hardness, salty/bitter taste, water solubility
Hydroxides $(\text{OH})^-$ (Hydroxyl ion) Manganite ($\text{MnO(OH)}$), Brucite, Goethite, Gibbsite Dehydrate upon heating to form oxides
Carbonates $[\text{CO}_3]^{2-}$ Siderite ($\text{FeCO}_3$), Calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$), Dolomite Effervesce in dilute $\text{HCl}$, high birefringence, rhombohedral cleavage
Phosphates $[\text{PO}_4]^{3-}$ Monazite ($(\text{Ce,La,Th})\text{PO}_4$), Apatite ($\text{Ca}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3\text{F}$) High refractive index, moderate-to-high density, radioactive when Th/U-rich

💡 Key Takeaway & Exam Strategy

Quick Matching Logic:
  • Sylvite ($\text{KCl}$): $\text{Cl}^- \rightarrow$ Halide (4).
  • Manganite ($\text{MnO(OH)}$): $\text{OH}^- \rightarrow$ Hydroxide (3).
  • Siderite ($\text{FeCO}_3$): $\text{CO}_3^{2-} \rightarrow$ Carbonate (1).
  • Monazite ($(\text{Ce,La,Th})\text{PO}_4$): $\text{PO}_4^{3-} \rightarrow$ Phosphate (2).
  • Direct matching: A-4, B-3, C-1, D-2 (Option a).
