DGMS 2019 • SECTION E • QUESTION 29

Deltaic Environments: Subaqueous Delta Zonation, Delta Front & Prodelta Architecture

Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) Junior Scientific Officer Examination 2019 | Sedimentology & Deltaic Depositional Systems Guide

📋 Question Statement

Q.29. The uppermost part of subaqueous delta that lies at water depth down to 10 m or so, is referred to as:
a (a) Upper delta plain
b (b) Lower delta plain
c (c) Prodelta
d (d) Delta front
✅ Correct Answer: (d) Delta front (Uppermost subaqueous delta, depth 0 to ~10 m)

📖 Official Textbook Figure (Sam Boggs Jr., Principles of Sedimentology & Stratigraphy)

Figure 1: Sam Boggs Jr., Chapter 9, Figure 9.12. Principal physiographic components and facies of a delta system. Note the major boundary between the Subaerial Delta Plain (Upper Delta Plain above high-tide level, Lower Delta Plain in the intertidal zone) and the Subaqueous Delta, which is subdivided into the Delta Front (uppermost part down to ~10 m depth; sand and silt mouth bars) and the Prodelta (deeper seaward slope down to 200–300 m; hemipelagic mud and silt).

🔍 In-Depth Option Evaluation & Petrographic Analysis

(d) Delta front — [CORRECT]

Verbatim Definition from Sam Boggs Jr. (p. 316):
"The subaqueous delta plain lies seaward of the lower deltaic plain below low-tide water level. The uppermost part of the subaqueous delta, lying at water depths down to 10 m or so, is commonly called the delta front."

Hydrodynamics & Sediments: The delta front is the high-energy seaward interface where river effluents meet standing basin water. Sediments consist dominantly of distributary-mouth-bar sands, sheet sands, and coarse silts with cross-bedding and ripple laminations, winnowed by waves and tidal currents.

(c) Prodelta — [INCORRECT]

Characteristics: The prodelta is the deeper seaward part of the subaqueous delta lying beyond the delta front. It extends from ~10–20 m down to water depths of 200–300 m where it merges into the open marine shelf.

Sediments: Lacks coarse sands; dominated by hemipelagic suspension deposition of fine clays, muds, and thin silt turbidites.

(a) Upper delta plain — [INCORRECT • SUBAERIAL]

Characteristics: Part of the subaerial delta, not the subaqueous delta. It lies completely above high-tide water level and is governed strictly by subaerial fluvial processes (braided/meandering channels, point bars, natural levees, backswamps, and freshwater marshes).

(b) Lower delta plain — [INCORRECT • INTERTIDAL SUBAERIAL]

Characteristics: Extends between the low-tide mark and the upper limit of tidal influence. While periodically inundated during high tide, it is subaerially exposed at low tide and features interdistributary bays, mangrove swamps, salt marshes, and tidal channels. It is not part of the continuously submerged subaqueous delta.

Subaerial Delta (Deltaic Plain) Upper Delta Plain Above high-tide level (Landward) Fluvial channels, overbank floods, crevasse splay Fluvial sands, gravels, floodplain muds, coal/peat
Lower Delta Plain Intertidal zone (Between low & high tide) Mixed fluvial and tidal currents, marine inundation Interdistributary bay muds, tidal creek sands, mangrove peat

Prodelta From ~10 m down to 200–300 m (Seaward slope) Low-energy suspension fallout, gravity slumps, turbidity currents Dark marine muds, clays, bioturbated silts, prodelta claystones

🎯 Exam Traps & Rapid Elimination Strategy

⚡ Rule of Thumb for Deltaic Questions in UPSC & DGMS:
  1. Subaerial vs. Subaqueous: If the question mentions subaqueous, immediately eliminate Upper Delta Plain and Lower Delta Plain.
  2. Delta Front vs. Prodelta:
    • Delta Front: Shallow (0 to ~10 m depth), high energy, sandy, mouth bars.
    • Prodelta: Deep (10 m to 300 m depth), low energy, muddy/clayey, prodelta slope.
