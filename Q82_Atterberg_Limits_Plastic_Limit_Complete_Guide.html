Question 82: Atterberg Limits & Soil Consistency: Plastic Limit Boundary

Official Examination Guide • Soil Consistency States, Phase Transitions & Geotechnical Indices
Official Examination Question
The Atterberg limits that define the boundary between the plastic and semi-solid states is
(a) Liquid limit
(b) Plastic limit CORRECT ANSWER
(c) Shrinkage limit
(d) Zero% water content and plastic limit
1. Executive Summary & Core Principle

In geotechnical engineering and soil mechanics (established by Swedish chemist Albert Atterberg in 1911 and standardized by Arthur Casagrande in 1932/1948), fine-grained cohesive soils (clays and silts) exhibit four distinct physical states of consistency depending on their moisture content ($w$):

  1. Liquid State: Soil behaves essentially as a viscous liquid, possessing negligible shear strength and flowing under its own weight or minimal shock.
  2. Plastic State: Soil can be deformed and molded into various shapes without developing tensile cracks or crumbling.
  3. Semi-Solid State: Soil can support substantial loads, but when deformed, it fractures, shears, and crumbles rather than deforming plastically. Further reduction in moisture produces a corresponding reduction in total soil volume.
  4. Solid State: Soil volume remains completely constant; further drying causes air to replace water in the pores with zero shrinkage.

The boundaries between these four states are quantified by critical water contents known as the Atterberg Limits:

  • Liquid Limit ($w_L$ or LL): The boundary between the Liquid state and the Plastic state.
  • Plastic Limit ($w_P$ or PL): The boundary between the Plastic state and the Semi-Solid state.
  • Shrinkage Limit ($w_S$ or SL): The boundary between the Semi-Solid state and the Solid state.

Therefore, the Atterberg limit defining the boundary between the plastic and semi-solid states is unequivocally the (b) Plastic limit.

2. Laboratory Determination of the Atterberg Limits
Atterberg Limit Boundary Demarcated Standard ASTM / IS Laboratory Method Engineering Significance
Liquid Limit ($w_L$ / LL) Liquid State $\leftrightarrow$ Plastic State Casagrande Cup (moisture at which standard groove closes along 12.7 mm after 25 drops) or Fall Cone Penetrometer (20 mm cone penetration). Evaluates soil compressibility ($C_c \approx 0.009(\text{LL} - 10)$) and settlement potential.
Plastic Limit ($w_P$ / PL) Plastic State $\leftrightarrow$ Semi-Solid State Rolling soil into a thread on a glass plate; moisture content at which the thread crumbles precisely at 3.2 mm (1/8 inch) diameter. Determines the lower threshold of plasticity and workability of cohesive soil.
Shrinkage Limit ($w_S$ / SL) Semi-Solid State $\leftrightarrow$ Solid State Mercury displacement method; moisture content below which drying produces no further volume decrease ($\Delta V = 0$). Evaluates swelling/shrinkage potential in expansive clay soils (e.g., Black Cotton Soils / Smectites).
3. Derived Geotechnical Consistency Indices

From the fundamental Atterberg limits, several crucial engineering indices are calculated:

Plasticity Index (Ip or PI):
Ip = wL - wP (Range of moisture over which soil exhibits plastic behavior)

Liquidity Index (IL):
IL = (w - wP) / (wL - wP) = (w - wP) / Ip
• IL < 0 : Semi-solid or solid state (brittle failure risk)
• 0 < IL < 1 : Plastic state (normal field consolidation)
• IL > 1 : Liquid state (sensitive clay quick failure hazard)

Consistency Index (IC):
IC = (wL - w) / Ip
4. Systematic Option Evaluation
  • (a) Liquid limit: INCORRECT. Defines the boundary between the liquid state and the plastic state.
  • (b) Plastic limit: CORRECT ANSWER. By definition, the plastic limit marks the moisture boundary separating the plastic state from the semi-solid state.
  • (c) Shrinkage limit: INCORRECT. Defines the boundary between the semi-solid state and the solid state.
  • (d) Zero% water content and plastic limit: INCORRECT. Zero% moisture represents an oven-dry state deep within the solid state, not a boundary of the plastic state.
Key Takeaway: Liquid Limit = Liquid/Plastic boundary; Plastic Limit = Plastic/Semi-solid boundary; Shrinkage Limit = Semi-solid/Solid boundary. Hence, (b) Plastic limit is the correct answer.
