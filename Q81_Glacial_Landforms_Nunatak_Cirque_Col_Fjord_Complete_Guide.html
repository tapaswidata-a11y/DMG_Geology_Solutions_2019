Question 81: Glacial Alpine Landforms: Arête vs. Col Differentiation

Official Examination Guide • Glacial Erosional Features, Terminology & Landform Identification
Official Examination Question
Which one of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?
(a) Nunatak : Unglaciated island of bedrock, formerly surrounded by ice
(b) Cirque : Steep-walled semicircular basin in mountain glaciers
(c) Col : Narrow, sharp-edged ridge in mountain glaciers CORRECT ANSWER (MISMATCHED PAIR)
(d) Fjord : U-shaped glacial trough below sea level
1. Executive Summary & Core Principle

Alpine and continental glaciation produce distinctive erosional topography characterized by sharp crests, amphitheater basins, and drowned troughs. In classic geomorphology (Bloom, Geomorphology; Thornbury, Principles of Geomorphology; Mahapatra, A Text Book of Geology):

  • Arête (French for "fishbone" or "ridge"): A sharp, steep-sided, knife-edged, serrated rock ridge that separates two adjacent glacial valleys or opposing cirques. It is formed when two adjacent valley glaciers actively scour and quarry the rock wall between them.
  • Col: A mountain pass, gap, or saddle-like depression carved across an arête or mountain divide. A col forms when two cirques on opposite sides of a divide erode backward (headward sapping and plucking) until their headwalls intersect and breach the ridge crest. A col is thus a pass/gap, NOT a narrow, sharp-edged ridge!
  • Cirque (Corrie / Cwm): A steep-sided, amphitheater-like, semicircular or horseshoe-shaped hollow excavated into high mountain slopes at the head of a glacial valley by freeze-thaw frost wedging, plucking, and rotational sliding of glacier ice. (Correctly matched in Option b).
  • Nunatak: An isolated peak or island of exposed bedrock projecting above surrounding glacial ice or an ice sheet, unmodified by glacial ice cover. (Correctly matched in Option a).
  • Fjord (Fiord): A deep, steep-walled, U-shaped glacial trough carved by valley glaciers below sea level, or submerged by post-glacial marine transgression. (Correctly matched in Option d).

Because Option (c) defines a Col using the morphological definition of an Arête, it represents the incorrectly matched pair and is the correct answer to the question.

2. Systematic Morphological Classification of Glacial Landforms
Landform Etymology / Origin Geomorphological Definition Formation Process
Cirque (Corrie / Cwm) Latin circus (circle); Scottish Gaelic coire (cauldron) Armchair-shaped, steep-walled, flat-bottomed rock basin at glacier head. Rotational ice slippage, plucking at headwall, nivation, and abrasion. Often holds a tarn lake.
Arête French arête (fishbone, ridge) Narrow, sharp-edged, serrated, knife-like ridge. Parallel erosion by two adjacent valley glaciers or headward erosion of parallel cirques.
Col Latin collum (neck); French col (pass) Saddle-shaped gap, breach, or pass along a ridge. Headward retreat and intersection of two opposing cirques, lowering the arête crest into a pass.
Horn (Pyramidal Peak) German Horn (Matterhorn type) Sharp, steep-sided, pyramidal peak with faceted faces. Intersection and backward erosion of three or more cirques converging from different sides.
Nunatak Inuit nunataq (lonely peak) Bedrock peak or ridge surrounded by glacier ice/ice sheet. Glacial nunataks project above the ice surface, undergoing intense periglacial frost shattering.
Fjord (Fiord) Old Norse fjörðr (passage) Deep, drowned U-shaped glacial valley with steep rock walls. Glacial valley excavated well below sea level (overdeepened by ice) subsequently flooded by post-glacial eustatic sea-level rise.
3. Evaluation of Options
  • Pair (a) [Nunatak] is CORRECTLY MATCHED: An unglaciated rock island or peak surrounded by an ice cap/glacier.
  • Pair (b) [Cirque] is CORRECTLY MATCHED: A steep-walled, semicircular armchair-shaped basin carved into mountain slopes.
  • Pair (c) [Col] is NOT CORRECTLY MATCHED (CORRECT ANSWER): Defines a col as a "narrow, sharp-edged ridge". That is the definitive definition of an arête! A col is the saddle or pass formed by the breach of an arête.
  • Pair (d) [Fjord] is CORRECTLY MATCHED: A submerged U-shaped glacial trough flooded by the sea.
Exam Summary: Arête = Sharp ridge; Col = Saddle/pass breach in the ridge; Horn = Pyramidal peak; Cirque = Semicircular basin; Nunatak = Ice-surrounded rock peak. Therefore, (c) is the mismatched pair.
