THEORETICAL FOUNDATION: THE Sm-Nd SYSTEM & EPSILON NOTATION
The Chondritic Uniform Reservoir, Derivation of the $\epsilon$ Formulation & The Terrestrial Mantle Array

1. The Sm-Nd Geochronometer & The CHUR Reference Reservoir

Samarium ($\text{Sm}$) and Neodymium ($\text{Nd}$) are adjacent Rare Earth Elements. The radioactive isotope $^{147}\text{Sm}$ decays to stable radiogenic $^{143}\text{Nd}$ by alpha ($\alpha$) emission:

The Sm-Nd Decay Equation
$^{147}_{62}\text{Sm} \longrightarrow {}^{143}_{60}\text{Nd} + \alpha + Q$

• Decay constant: $\lambda_{\text{Sm}} = 6.54 \times 10^{-12}\text{ yr}^{-1}$
• Half-life: $t_{1/2} = 1.06 \times 10^{11}\text{ years}$ ($106\text{ Ga}$)

Normalized to the non-radiogenic reference isotope $^{144}\text{Nd}$:
$\left(\frac{^{143}\text{Nd}}{^{144}\text{Nd}}\right)_{\text{sample}}(t) = \left(\frac{^{143}\text{Nd}}{^{144}\text{Nd}}\right)_0 + \left(\frac{^{147}\text{Sm}}{^{144}\text{Nd}}\right)_{\text{sample}} \cdot (e^{\lambda t} - 1)$
The Chondritic Uniform Reservoir (CHUR):
Because both $\text{Sm}$ and $\text{Nd}$ are refractory lithophile elements that condense at high temperatures in the solar nebula, their planetary ratio is not fractionated by volatilization. DePaolo & Wasserburg (1976) demonstrated that the Bulk Silicate Earth (BSE) possesses the exact same $\text{Sm/Nd}$ ratio and Nd-isotopic composition as pristine chondritic meteorites. This global planetary baseline is termed CHUR:
  • Present-day CHUR ratio: $\left(^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd}\right)_{\text{CHUR}}(0) = \mathbf{0.512638}$
  • Present-day CHUR parent/daughter ratio: $\left(^{147}\text{Sm}/^{144}\text{Nd}\right)_{\text{CHUR}} = \mathbf{0.1967}$

2. Epsilon ($\epsilon$) Notation: Origin and Mathematical Definition

Because the half-life of $^{147}\text{Sm}$ is exceptionally long ($106\text{ Ga}$), radiogenic growth of $^{143}\text{Nd}$ is very slow. Differences in measured $^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd}$ ratios between diverse terrestrial rocks occur only in the 4th and 5th decimal places (e.g., $0.5126$ vs. $0.5118$).

To eliminate cumbersome decimal fractions and highlight petrogenetic deviations, Donald J. DePaolo and Gerald J. Wasserburg (1976) invented the Epsilon ($\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$) parameter, defined as the fractional deviation of a sample's Nd-isotopic ratio from that of CHUR in parts per 10,000 (parts per myriad):

The Epsilon Neodymium ($\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$) Formulation
$\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}(t) = \left[ \frac{\left(^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd}\right)_{\text{sample}}(t)}{\left(^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd}\right)_{\text{CHUR}}(t)} - 1 \right] \times 10^4$

For present-day rocks ($t = 0$):
$\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}(0) = \left[ \frac{\left(^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd}\right)_{\text{sample}}(0)}{0.512638} - 1 \right] \times 10^4$
"Because variations in the $^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd}$ ratio are small, DePaolo and Wasserburg (1976) defined the $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ (epsilon) notation to express the deviation of the $^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd}$ ratio of a sample from that of the Chondritic Uniform Reservoir (CHUR) in parts per $10^4$ ($10,000$)... The beauty of this notation is that rocks derived from an undifferentiated reservoir have $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}} = 0$, while rocks from depleted mantle have positive values and ancient crustal rocks have negative values."
— William M. White, Geochemistry (2005), Chapter 8: Radiogenic Isotope Geochemistry, p. 325.

3. Petrogenetic Meaning: Positive vs. Negative $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ Values

Because $\text{Nd}$ has a larger ionic radius than $\text{Sm}$ (due to the Lanthanide Contraction), $\text{Nd}$ is more incompatible than $\text{Sm}$ during mantle partial melting ($D_{\text{Nd}} < D_{\text{Sm}}$). This creates an elegant geodynamic dichotomy:

$\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ Value Relative $\text{Sm/Nd}$ Ratio Geochemical Reservoir Representative Geological Rock Suite
$\epsilon_{\text{Nd}} = 0$ Identical to CHUR ($0.1967$) CHUR / Bulk Silicate Earth (BSE) Primitive, undifferentiated Earth mantle; chondritic meteorites.
$\epsilon_{\text{Nd}} > 0$ (Positive)
$(+2 \text{ to } +12)$		 High $\text{Sm/Nd}$ ($> 0.20$) Depleted Mantle (DM) Mid-Ocean Ridge Basalts (N-MORB). Ancient melt extraction stripped more Nd than Sm into the crust, leaving a mantle residue with high $\text{Sm/Nd}$ that evolved radiogenic, positive $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$.
$\epsilon_{\text{Nd}} < 0$ (Negative)
$(-5 \text{ to } -40)$		 Low $\text{Sm/Nd}$ ($< 0.18$) Enriched Continental Crust Granites, Shales, Ancient Cratons. Partial melts extracted into the crust captured preferential Nd, resulting in low $\text{Sm/Nd}$ and retarded radiogenic growth relative to CHUR.
Figure 1 (The Terrestrial Mantle Array): Plot of $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ versus $\epsilon_{\text{Sr}}$ for oceanic and continental igneous rocks. The inverse correlation forms the renowned Mantle Array trending from the upper-left (Depleted Mantle: positive $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$, negative $\epsilon_{\text{Sr}}$) through the origin ($\epsilon_{\text{Nd}} = 0, \epsilon_{\text{Sr}} = 0$, representing BSE/CHUR) down to the lower-right (Enriched Crustal Reservoirs: negative $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$, positive $\epsilon_{\text{Sr}}$).

4. Notation Conventions Across Isotope Geochemistry

Exam candidates must never confuse epsilon notation with delta notation or other physical constants:

Symbol Notation Name Scale / Multiplier Application & Domain
$\epsilon$ (The Answer) Epsilon Notation $\times 10^4$ (parts per $10,000$) Radiogenic Isotopes ($\text{Sm-Nd, Lu-Hf, Rb-Sr}$): Deviations from bulk planetary reservoirs (CHUR / UR).
$\delta$ Delta Notation $\times 10^3$ (parts per $1,000$, $\text{‰}$) Stable Isotopes ($\delta^{18}\text{O}, \delta^{13}\text{C}, \delta^{34}\text{S}, \delta\text{D}$): Equilibrium & kinetic mass-dependent fractionation relative to SMOW, PDB, CDT.
$\omega$ Omega Dimensionless / Ratio Cosmological density parameter ($\Omega$) or specific atomic frequency. Never used for isotopic reservoir deviation.
$\upsilon$ Upsilon Dimensionless / Kinematic Particle physics or fluid viscosity ($\nu$). Never used in isotope geochemistry.

EXAM QUESTION & RIGOROUS OPTION-BY-OPTION DISSECTION
DGMS 2019 Examination · Official Question Statement, Verified Answer & Detailed Distracter Analysis
DGMS 2019 · QUESTION 22
Which one of the following symbols denotes the deviation of the Nd-isotopic composition of a rock from that of the chondritic uniform reservoir?
(a) $\omega$ (Omega)
(b) $\varepsilon$ (Epsilon)
(c) $\delta$ (Delta)
(d) $\upsilon$ (Upsilon)
✓
Correct Option: (b) ε (Epsilon)
Verified via William M. White (Geochemistry, p. 325) & K. C. Misra (Introduction to Geochemistry, p. 248)
Incorrect Option (a): $\omega$ (Omega)

Detailed Refutation: The Greek letter $\omega$ (Omega) has no role in radiogenic isotope geochemistry. In cosmology and astrophysics, $\Omega$ denotes the critical density parameter of the universe; in structural geology, it may denote angular rotation. It is never used to define deviations in radiogenic isotope ratios from CHUR.

Correct Answer Option (b): $\varepsilon$ (Epsilon)

Detailed Validation: The lowercase Greek letter $\varepsilon$ (Epsilon) is the universally accepted, standard symbol introduced by DePaolo & Wasserburg (1976) to denote the parts-per-ten-thousand ($10^{-4}$) deviation of the $^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd}$ isotopic composition of a rock from that of the Chondritic Uniform Reservoir (CHUR). A positive $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ indicates extraction from a depleted mantle source with high $\text{Sm/Nd}$, while a negative $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ signifies derivation from or contamination by enriched, low-$\text{Sm/Nd}$ continental crust. This matches the exam question verbatim.

Incorrect Option (c): $\delta$ (Delta)

Detailed Refutation: The Greek letter $\delta$ (Delta) is used exclusively for stable isotope geochemistry (such as $\delta^{18}\text{O}$, $\delta^{13}\text{C}$, $\delta^{34}\text{S}$, and $\delta\text{D}$), where variations are reported as parts-per-thousand (permil, $\text{‰} = 10^{-3}$) deviations from arbitrary reference standards like V-SMOW, V-PDB, or Canyon Diablo Troilite (CDT). Delta notation is never used for Nd radiogenic deviations from CHUR.

Incorrect Option (d): $\upsilon$ (Upsilon)

Detailed Refutation: The Greek letter $\upsilon$ (Upsilon) is not used as an isotopic delta or deviation symbol anywhere in geochemistry. It serves solely as a distracter.

SYNTHESIS & HIGH-YIELD PRACTICE QUESTIONS
Curated Problems Covering Model Ages ($T_{\text{DM}}$), Mantle Array Trajectories & Cratonic Geochemistry

High-Yield Exam Practice Questions

Practice Q1 (Numerical / Calculation): Evaluating $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$

A modern basalt from the East Pacific Rise has a measured $^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd} = 0.513150$. Given that present-day CHUR has $^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd} = 0.512638$, calculate $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}(0)$ and interpret its mantle provenance.

Solution & Rationale:
$\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}(0) = \left[ \frac{0.513150}{0.512638} - 1 \right] \times 10^4 = [1.00099875 - 1] \times 10^4 = \mathbf{+9.99} \approx \mathbf{+10.0}$.
A value of $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}} = +10.0$ indicates derivation from the Depleted Mantle (DM) reservoir, which underwent ancient melt depletion that elevated its $\text{Sm/Nd}$ ratio relative to CHUR.
Practice Q2 (Geochronology): Nd Model Age ($T_{\text{DM}}$) Definition

What is a Neodymium Depleted Mantle Model Age ($T_{\text{DM}}$), and why is it referred to as a "crustal residence age"?

Answer: The $T_{\text{DM}}$ age is the calculated time in the past when the Nd-isotopic growth curve of a crustal rock intersected the Depleted Mantle evolution curve. It records the time when the rock's chemical precursors were originally extracted from the mantle to form continental crust, irrespective of later metamorphic or magmatic remelting events.
Practice Q3 (Scale Multiplier): Parts Per Ten Thousand vs. Permil

Why did DePaolo and Wasserburg select a multiplier of $10^4$ (parts per ten thousand) for epsilon notation instead of $10^3$ (permil, used in delta notation)?

Answer: Because the fractional differences in $^{143}\text{Nd}/^{144}\text{Nd}$ caused by $\text{Sm-Nd}$ decay are approximately an order of magnitude smaller than mass-dependent stable isotope fractionation. A multiplier of $10^4$ scales the subtle differences in the 4th and 5th decimal places into convenient, whole-integer numbers (e.g., $+10$ or $-15$) easily analyzed by geochemists.
Practice Q4 (Complementary Systems): Lu-Hf and Epsilon Hafnium ($\epsilon_{\text{Hf}}$)

Which other radiogenic isotope system is defined using an identical epsilon notation relative to a chondritic reservoir, and how does its behavior correlate with $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$?

Answer: The Lutetium-Hafnium ($\text{Lu-Hf}$) system ($^{176}\text{Lu} \to {}^{176}\text{Hf}$), expressed as $\epsilon_{\text{Hf}}$ relative to CHUR. Because $\text{Lu-Hf}$ behaves identically to $\text{Sm-Nd}$ during mantle melting ($D_{\text{Hf}} < D_{\text{Lu}}$), $\epsilon_{\text{Hf}}$ correlates positively with $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ along the "terrestrial mantle-crust array" ($\epsilon_{\text{Hf}} \approx 1.4\,\epsilon_{\text{Nd}} + 3$).
Practice Q5 (Crustal Provenance): Archean Craton Signatures

A granitic pluton intruded during the Proterozoic ($1.8\text{ Ga}$) displays an initial $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}(1.8\text{ Ga}) = -12.5$. What does this imply about the magma's origin?

Answer: It proves that the granite was not derived from juvenile Proterozoic mantle; rather, it formed by the remelting or heavy assimilation of ancient Archean continental crust that had existed for at least $1\text{ billion years}$ prior with low $\text{Sm/Nd}$, allowing its $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ to decay to highly negative values.
Practice Q6 (Sediment Resistance): Sm-Nd vs. Weathering

Why do Nd isotopes in clastic sedimentary rocks (e.g., shales and turbidites) accurately record the average crustal age of an entire eroded continent, whereas Sr isotopes do not?

Answer: Rare Earth Elements like $\text{Sm}$ and $\text{Nd}$ are insoluble and immobile during surface weathering and diagenesis, preserving their pristine parent-daughter ratio. Strontium ($\text{Sr}$), being a fluid-mobile alkaline earth element, is easily leached and exchanged with seawater and groundwaters, corrupting its original isotopic signature.
Practice Q7 (Mantle End-Members): Zindler and Hart (1986) Taxonomy

In the isotope taxonomy of oceanic basalts, what mantle component is characterized by extremely high $^{206}\text{Pb}/^{204}\text{Pb}$ but moderately positive $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$?

Answer: HIMU (High-$\mu$, where $\mu = {}^{238}\text{U}/^{204}\text{Pb}$). HIMU represents ancient subducted oceanic crust that was dehydrated (stripping lead relative to uranium) and recycled through the deep mantle for $1.5–2.0\text{ Ga}$.
Practice Q8 (Initial Ratio Evolution): Slopes of $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ vs. Time

On a plot of $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ versus geologic time ($4.55\text{ Ga} \to \text{Present}$), what is the slope of the CHUR reference reservoir line?

Answer: Zero (horizontal line at $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}} = 0$). Because $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}$ is defined by normalize-comparing a sample to CHUR at that exact same time $t$, CHUR by mathematical definition always has $\epsilon_{\text{Nd}}(t) \equiv 0$ at all times in Earth's history.
